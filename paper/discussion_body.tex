\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what would refute it.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove that any
transformer contracts, and our attempt to measure $q$ directly failed:
estimating it from boundary distances needs a monotone text read-head, and
these decoders' attention centroid advances on barely half of generation
steps --- self-defeating, since the flatter the attention the worse any such
localiser gets (supplement, S5). Nor is the count \emph{absent}: a probe
retains it at \ProbeRetRep{} of its early level on repeated text against
\ProbeRetCtl{} on controls, degradation rather than erasure. What we establish empirically
is the theorem's \emph{consequence}, not its antecedent.

A conditional account risks being unfalsifiable, so we state what would refute
it: a control with the same deficit as its repeated twin; a model with the
deficit whose capacity gain matches its control; or a probe reading the count as
well late as early. Our data could have shown any of the three.
\ErrBestModel{} is instructive --- no deficit yet still a capacity gap, so
capacity loss is necessary but not sufficient, as \eqref{eq:horizon} allows when
the readout margin is large --- though it is also, unhelpfully, the one
architecture whose attention is not separable, so the dilution premise cannot be
checked on the very model carrying this argument.

The closest prior result is representational
collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: as a sequence grows, distinct prefixes map to
arbitrarily close representations, which already implies counting errors. Our
control separates the two mechanisms empirically --- collapse-by-length predicts
that a $32$-word control and a $32$-word repetition fail alike, since both are
equally long, so Figure~\ref{fig:main}(b) is evidence against length alone being
sufficient, and~\eqref{eq:horizon} supplies a rate in repetitions that a
length-indexed bound does not. Related dynamical treatments index convergence by
depth or an abstract
temperature~\cite{dong2021attention,geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered};
ours indexes it by generation step. A larger literature bounds transformer
expressivity by circuit class, formal language and state tracking, periodic
languages included~\cite{strobl2024formal,merrill2024illusion}; those bound
architecture against worst case in one forward pass, where ours bounds a decoder
against its measured contraction over generation. A competing account holds that in text LMs
the count survives in the residual stream and only the output policy
fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}; our probes favour representational
degradation, but the two are not exclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Implications and limits.}
Existing mitigations act late~\cite{wang2025eliminating,xin2024ralle}, and under
this account they help only insofar as they raise $q$ or enlarge the readout
margin. We tested that rather than asserting it, sweeping XTTS-v2's repetition
penalty --- the most widely deployed such fix --- over
$\{\MitPenLo{},3,5,\MitPenHi{}\}$, the arms whose controls stay exact. The
deficit moves only from \MitErrBest{}\% to \MitErrWorst{}\%, a \MitSpan{}-point
span across a fourfold change in the penalty, sloping \MitSlope{} points per unit
--- slightly the \emph{wrong} way. Disabling the penalty entirely
($\!=\!\MitBrokenPen{}$) breaks the control too, so no setting both preserves
general quality and removes the deficit: this lever does not act on the quantity
that sets the horizon.
Limits: the panel is English-only and entirely codec-language models with softmax
attention over text, so the claim concerns that class, not AR TTS at large.
