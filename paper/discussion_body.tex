\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what the measurements do not show.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodicity plus contraction imply a
horizon. It proves nothing about any transformer, and two attempts to measure $q$ failed --- one estimator
needs a monotone read-head these decoders lack, and a perturbation
probe found no difference between the arms ($7/18$, $p=0.12$), its linearity
control failing (S5).

The theorem's distinctive prediction is a constant $N^\ast$, invisible where our
ladder stopped; $k=128$ found saturation of that kind, though not from a
measured $q$. A linear probe does not separate this
from the rival on which the count survives and only the output policy
fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}: it retains less on repeated text in
\ProbeNBelowCtl{} of \ProbeNCk{}, but the count stays decodable late
($R^2$ \ProbeLateMed{}).

Past the horizon the probe does test the theorem, which forbids a readout only
\emph{beyond} $N^\ast$. Measured against a predictor that ignores the states and
answers the mean, \PhAllNames{} reach \PhAllRatios{} of its error; below the
horizon \PhLostName{} reaches \PhBelowMae{} against \PhBelowConst{}. Range is
not why: over the \emph{same} $k$ the probe reads control states at
\PhCtlRatio{} --- which is exactly \PhClearName{}'s margin. So the prediction holds in
\PhNLost{} of \PhNCk{}, \PhTieName{} sitting within \PhTieBand{}\% of the
trivial predictor, and we quote the stricter count (S12).
What would refute it: a
control with its twin's deficit, a model whose capacity gain matches its control,
or a probe recovering the count past $N^\ast$, as one now does.

Closest is representational collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: distinct prefixes
converge with length, which our control separates from periodicity. Related
bounds~\cite{hahn2020theoretical,dong2021attention,geshkovski2023emergence,%
strobl2024formal,merrill2024illusion} treat one pass, state
tracking~\cite{li2025statetracking} many.
