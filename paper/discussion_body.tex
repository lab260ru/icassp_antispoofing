\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what the measurements do not show.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove any transformer
contracts, and two attempts to measure $q$ failed --- a boundary-distance
estimator needs a monotone read-head these decoders lack, and a perturbation
probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items
($7$ of $18$ pairs, $p=0.12$), its linearity control failing too (S5).

The theorem's distinctive prediction is a constant $N^\ast$, invisible where our
ladder stopped. Extending to $k=128$ found saturation of the predicted kind, but
not one derived from a measured $q$. One test could have separated our account from the rival on which the count
survives and only the output policy fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}. It does
not. A linear
probe retains less on repeated text than on its control in \ProbeNBelowCtl{} of
\ProbeNCk{} checkpoints --- but the count stays decodable late (median $R^2$
\ProbeLateMed{}), and retention exceeds one in \ProbeAboveOne{} of them. A state
that had lost the count could not support that, and the rival predicts exactly
such persistence. The relative effect is real; it discriminates nothing. Nor does
it contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}(iv), whose prohibition concerns counts
\emph{past} the horizon: the probe runs over $k\le32$, at or below the fitted
$\hat K_{\mathrm{rep}}$, where a Lipschitz readout may succeed.

A conditional account risks being unfalsifiable, so we say what would refute it:
a control with its twin's deficit; a model whose capacity gain matches its
control; or an AR decoder behaving like the non-AR baseline.

The closest prior result is representational
collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: as sequences grow, distinct prefixes map to
arbitrarily close representations, already implying counting errors. But
collapse-by-length predicts that equally long control and repeated items fail
alike, and they do not wherever the control carries no repetition of its own;
\eqref{eq:horizon} also gives a rate in repetitions that a length-indexed bound
does not. Related dynamical and expressivity bounds index
convergence within one forward pass~\cite{dong2021attention,%
geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered,strobl2024formal,merrill2024illusion};
ours indexes generation steps.
