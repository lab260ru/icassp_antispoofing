\noindent\textbf{Where the count lives, and the rival the evidence favours.}
A linear probe tests the bound's one distinctive prediction --- a readout should
fail \emph{beyond} $N^\ast$, not before. Against a predictor that ignores the
states and answers the mean, the \PhNCk{} checkpoints we could run past the
extension horizon (\PhAllNames{}) reach \PhAllRatios{} of its error: the
prediction holds in \PhNLost{}, \PhClearName{} plainly \emph{keeps} the count
past the horizon, which the bound forbids, and the third sits in a
\PhTieBand{}\% band where neither reading holds. Range is not the explanation
(controls read \PhCtlRatio{} over the same $k$). At $n=\PhNCk{}$ this is
inconclusive and we report it for transparency.

It does bear on the live rival. On the state-contraction account the count is
erased from the state; on the output-policy
account~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated} it survives and only the policy reading it
fails. Repetition does cost retention relative to the control in all
\ProbeNBelowCtl{} of \ProbeNCk{} checkpoints, as contraction predicts --- but
the count is not erased: it stays decodable late ($R^2$ \ProbeLateMed{}) and
repeated-item retention exceeds $1$ in \ProbeAboveOne{}, the probe reading it
\emph{better} deeper in. Deciding between them needs a manipulation of the
readout at fixed state, which we did not run; as things stand the output-policy
account is the better supported.

\noindent\textbf{Related work, and what the control decides.}
Closest is representational collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: distinct
prefixes converge with length, which our control separates from periodicity. The
counting-specific expressivity results reach a compatible conclusion by another
route --- transformers count only under conditions on precision, depth and
positional encoding~\cite{yehudai2024when,salzer2025counting}, tokenisation
shifting the limit~\cite{zhang2024counting}. The two predict differently, which
is the point of the control: an inductive-bias limit is a property of the model
and holds at fixed periodicity as length grows, while a contraction limit is a
property of the \emph{conditioning}. Ours varies periodicity at fixed length,
and the result is the periodicity-specific one --- whether contraction is the
reason remains open. Other
bounds~\cite{hahn2020theoretical,dong2021attention,strobl2024formal} treat one
pass, state tracking~\cite{li2025statetracking} many.

\noindent\textbf{What the design does not separate.}
Our manipulation varies periodicity and \emph{verbatim token identity} together,
so ``periodicity'' here should be read as the pair. Neither is separated from
\emph{training-set frequency}: $k$-fold verbatim repetition is far rarer in
speech corpora than a length-matched sentence of distinct words, so an
out-of-distribution account predicts the same dissociation. The duration story
about VITS and F5-TTS rests on one model per strategy and is a hypothesis, not
a contrast. Exclusions are rule-based and applied by script without reference to
arm or score; no per-item judgement was made.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
The panel is \NFamilies{} families and English the only language tested. The
judge is validated against concatenative ground truth with known counts, not
against human listeners on generated audio; the \SampleN{}-clip sample ships so
any scoring decision can be checked by ear, and we report no human agreement
statistic because we ran no listening study. What would refute the periodicity
claim is a control arm carrying its twin's deficit at matched length, or a
checkpoint whose exact-rate gap runs the other way. Neither appeared.
