\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what would refute it.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove that any
transformer contracts, and our attempt to measure that premise directly failed
(Section~\ref{sec:negative}). What we establish empirically is the theorem's
\emph{consequence}, not its antecedent.

A conditional account risks being unfalsifiable, so we state what would refute
it: a control showing the same deficit as its repeated twin (blaming length, not
periodicity); a model with the deficit whose capacity gain matches its control
(severing mechanism from behaviour); or a probe recovering the count from late
states as well as early ones (placing the failure in the readout). Our data could
have shown any of the three. \ErrBestModel{} is instructive: no count deficit yet
still a capacity gap, so capacity loss is necessary but not sufficient ---
consistent with \eqref{eq:horizon}, where a large readout margin buys horizon
even under contraction.

The closest prior result is representational
collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: as a sequence grows, distinct prefixes map to
arbitrarily close representations, which already implies counting errors. Our
control separates the two mechanisms empirically --- collapse-by-length predicts
that a $32$-word control and a $32$-word repetition fail alike, since both are
equally long, so Figure~\ref{fig:main}(b) is evidence against length alone being
sufficient, and~\eqref{eq:horizon} supplies a rate in repetitions that a
length-indexed bound does not. Related dynamical treatments index convergence by
depth or an abstract
temperature~\cite{dong2021attention,geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered};
ours indexes it by generation step. A competing account holds that in text LMs
the count survives in the residual stream and only the output policy
fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}; our probes favour representational
degradation, but the two are not exclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Implications and limits.}
Existing mitigations act late~\cite{wang2025eliminating,xin2024ralle}. Under this
account they help only insofar as they raise $q$ or enlarge the readout margin,
and \eqref{eq:horizon} says the levers are unequal: the horizon is logarithmic in
the margin but inversely logarithmic in $q$. XTTS-v2 is a case in point: its shipped
repetition penalty of $5.0$ acts directly against the behaviour we measure, and
disabling it degrades both conditions so far that the ablation is uninformative,
leaving the penalised model the conservative panel member. Limits: the panel is
English-only and all six checkpoints are
codec-language models with softmax attention over text, so the claim concerns
that architecture class, not AR TTS in general; capacity ratio and count error
correlate in the predicted direction across the panel (Spearman $+0.49$) but
$n{=}6$ cannot establish it; text attention is unreachable where text and
acoustic embeddings are additively fused (Qwen3-TTS); and the residual
$O(\delta + 1/k)$ term of Lemma~\ref{lem:dilution} is not propagated through the
theorem, so the machine-checked bound applies to the idealised periodic map.
