\noindent\textbf{What is and is not proved.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove that any
transformer contracts. That premise is empirical, and where a model fails it the
theorem is simply silent --- a distinction the formalisation forced us to keep
explicit, and one that descriptive spectral accounts of hallucination tend to
blur.

\noindent\textbf{Relation to prior collapse results.}
That deep transformers lose rank and over-squash is
known~\cite{dong2021attention,barbero2024glasses}, and self-attention has been
analysed as a dynamical system with attracting
clusters~\cite{geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered}. Those results index
convergence by \emph{depth} or by an abstract temperature; ours indexes it by
\emph{generation step under periodic conditioning}, which is what turns the
consequence into a statement about counting and yields a horizon in units a
practitioner can act on. Repetition inevitability has also been derived at the
level of decoding rules~\cite{fu2021theoretical,xu2022learning};
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} is decoding-rule agnostic, constraining any Lipschitz
readout of the state. A competing account deserves note: in text LMs, repeated-token
counts remain linearly decodable from the residual stream even where the wrong
answer forms, locating the failure in the output policy rather than the
representation~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}. Our probes recover the count only
coarsely, and better from non-repetitive text, so our data favour representational
degradation --- but a stronger probe could shift that, and the two accounts are not
mutually exclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Implications for mitigation.}
Existing fixes act late: attention supervision, alignment priors, contrastive
decoding~\cite{wang2025eliminating,xin2024ralle}. Under this account they help
only insofar as they raise $q$ or enlarge the readout margin, and
\eqref{eq:horizon} says the two levers are not equal --- the horizon is
logarithmic in the margin but inversely logarithmic in $q$. Doubling the margin
adds a constant number of repetitions; moving $q$ from $0.9$ to $0.95$ roughly
doubles the horizon. Interventions that keep the per-repetition map from
contracting should therefore dominate decoding-time patches.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
The panel is English-only. Text-attention instrumentation is unavailable in
models whose text and acoustic embeddings are additively fused (Qwen3-TTS), and
we report which method produced each estimate. XTTS-v2 ships a repetition
penalty of $5.0$ on acoustic tokens, which acts against the behaviour we measure
and makes it a conservative rather than neutral panel member; we therefore rerun
it with the penalty disabled. Finally, $\hat q$ summarises a map that is only
approximately autonomous, and we do not propagate the residual
$O(\delta + 1/k)$ term of Lemma~\ref{lem:dilution} through the theorem.
