\noindent\textbf{Where the count lives, and the rival the evidence favours.}
A linear probe tests the bound's one distinctive prediction --- a readout should
fail \emph{beyond} $N^\ast$, not before. Against a predictor that ignores the
states and answers the mean, the \PhNCk{} checkpoints we could run past the
extension horizon (\PhAllNames{}) reach \PhAllRatios{} of its error: the
prediction holds in \PhNLost{}, \PhClearName{} plainly \emph{keeps} the count
past the horizon, which the bound forbids, and the third sits in a
\PhTieBand{}\% band where neither reading holds. Range is not the explanation
(controls read \PhCtlRatio{} over the same $k$), but at $n=\PhNCk{}$ this is
inconclusive and reported for transparency.

This bears on the live rival. On the state-contraction account the count is
erased from the state; on the output-policy
account~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated} it survives and only the policy reading it
fails. Repetition does cost retention relative to the control in all
\ProbeNBelowCtl{} of \ProbeNCk{}, as contraction predicts --- but the count is
not erased: it stays decodable late ($R^2$ \ProbeLateMed{}) and
repeated-item retention exceeds $1$ in \ProbeAboveOne{}, the probe reading it
\emph{better} deeper in. Deciding between them needs a manipulation of the
readout at fixed state, which we did not run; as things stand the
output-policy account is better supported.

\noindent\textbf{Related work, and what the control decides.}
Closest is representational collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: distinct
prefixes converge with length, which our control separates from periodicity.
Counting-specific expressivity results reach a compatible conclusion another way
--- transformers count only under conditions on precision, depth and positional
encoding~\cite{yehudai2024when,salzer2025counting,zhang2024counting}, with
single-pass bounds~\cite{hahn2020theoretical,strobl2024formal} and state
tracking~\cite{li2025statetracking} bracketing it. The two predict differently,
which is the point of the control: an inductive-bias limit is a property of the
model and survives at fixed periodicity, a contraction limit is a property of
the \emph{conditioning}. Ours varies periodicity at fixed length, and the
result is periodicity-specific.

\noindent\textbf{What the design does not separate.}
Our manipulation varies periodicity and \emph{verbatim token identity}
together, so ``periodicity'' here is the pair; and neither is separated from
\emph{training-set frequency}, since $k$-fold verbatim repetition is far rarer
in speech corpora than a length-matched sentence of distinct words, so an
out-of-distribution account predicts the same dissociation. Within the repeated arm the
requested count and the amount of text are one variable, so a probe decoding $k$
from decoder states cannot be said to decode a \emph{count} rather than a
length; only the control separates those, and behaviourally. The VITS/F5-TTS duration story rests on one model per strategy
and is a hypothesis, not a contrast. Exclusions are rule-based, applied by script
without reference to arm or score.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
The panel is \NFamilies{} families and English the only language tested. The
judge is validated against concatenative ground truth, not against human
listeners on generated audio, and we report no agreement statistic because we
ran no listening study. What would refute the claim is a control arm carrying
its twin's deficit at matched length, or a checkpoint whose gap runs the other
way; neither appeared.
