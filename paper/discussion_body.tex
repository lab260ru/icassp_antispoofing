\noindent\textbf{What is and is not proved.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove that any
transformer contracts. That premise is empirical, we measured it as $\hat q$, and
we report the measurement rather than assuming it. Where a model were to show
$\hat q \ge 1$ the theorem would simply be silent about it, and we would report
that as a second regime rather than as a refutation --- a distinction the
formalisation forced us to keep explicit, and one that descriptive spectral
accounts of hallucination tend to blur.

\noindent\textbf{Relation to prior collapse results.}
That deep transformers lose rank and over-squash is
known~\cite{dong2021attention,barbero2024glasses}, and self-attention has been
analysed as a dynamical system with attracting
clusters~\cite{geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered}. Those results index
convergence by \emph{depth} or by an abstract temperature. Ours indexes it by
\emph{generation step under periodic conditioning}, which is what makes the
consequence a statement about counting rather than about representation quality,
and what yields a horizon in units a practitioner can act on. Repetition
inevitability has also been derived at the level of decoding rules and output
probabilities~\cite{fu2021theoretical,xu2022learning}; Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}
is decoding-rule agnostic, since it constrains any Lipschitz readout of the
state, greedy and sampled alike.

\noindent\textbf{Implications for mitigation.}
Existing fixes act late: attention supervision, alignment priors, contrastive
decoding~\cite{wang2025eliminating,xin2024ralle}. Under this account they help
only insofar as they raise $q$ or enlarge the readout margin. The bound in
\eqref{eq:horizon} says the two levers are not equal --- the horizon is
logarithmic in the margin and in the orbit scale, but inversely logarithmic in
$q$. Buying a factor of two in margin adds a constant number of repetitions;
moving $q$ from $0.9$ to $0.95$ roughly doubles the horizon. Architectural or
training interventions that keep the per-repetition map from contracting should
therefore dominate decoding-time patches, and $\hat q$ gives a cheap way to
measure whether a candidate intervention actually does so, without generating a
single evaluation set.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
Boundaries are located from text attention, which is unavailable in models whose
text and acoustic embeddings are additively fused (Qwen3-TTS, where those items
fall back to uniform placement); we report which method produced each estimate.
The panel is English-only and covers \NModelsNum{} checkpoints, so the $k^\ast$
regression rests on few points. XTTS-v2 ships a repetition penalty of $5.0$ on
acoustic tokens, which acts directly against the behaviour we measure and makes
it a conservative rather than a neutral member of the panel. Finally, $\hat q$ is
a scalar summary of a map that is only approximately autonomous; the residual
non-autonomy is exactly the $O(\delta + 1/k)$ term Lemma~\ref{lem:dilution}
bounds but that we do not propagate through the theorem.
