\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what the measurements do not show.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove that any
transformer contracts, and our attempt to measure $q$ directly failed ---
estimating it from boundary distances needs a monotone text read-head, and these
decoders' attention centroid advances on barely half of generation steps, which
is self-defeating (S5).

The theorem's distinctive prediction is a \emph{constant} $N^\ast$, and we do not
observe it: to $k=32$ the count stays proportional (Section~4), as a decoder
still tracking $k$ would. Three readings survive and we cannot separate them. The
horizon may lie past our ladder, since $q$ near $1$ makes $N^\ast$ large and the
fit places it at \ShapeNStarMin{}--\ShapeNStarMax{}; the contraction premise may
fail here, making the theorem inert; or the horizon may govern stopping rather
than counting, which this measurement cannot see. What the data support is weaker
and independent of the theorem: a periodicity-specific proportional deficit and a
state budget growing at \CapRatio{} of the control rate while staying positive
--- approach toward the collapse regime, not arrival. A probe finds the count
degraded, not erased (\ProbeRetRep{} against \ProbeRetCtl{} on controls).

A conditional account risks being unfalsifiable, so we state what would settle
it. Saturation past our ladder is the direct test: if median counts at $k=64$ and
$128$ level off together, the horizon is real and merely distant; if they keep
tracking $k$, contraction is not the operative limit and the theorem, though
proved, does not describe these models. Three observations would refute the
account outright: a control with its twin's deficit; a model with the deficit
whose capacity gain matches its control; or a probe reading the count as well
late as early. Our data could have shown any of the three.
\ErrBestModel{} is instructive: no deficit yet still a capacity gap, so capacity
loss is necessary but not sufficient.

The closest prior result is representational
collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: as a sequence grows, distinct prefixes map to
arbitrarily close representations, already implying counting errors. But
collapse-by-length predicts that a $32$-word control and a $32$-word repetition
fail alike, so Figure~\ref{fig:main}(b) is evidence against length alone, and
\eqref{eq:horizon} supplies a rate in repetitions that a length-indexed bound
does not. Related dynamical treatments index convergence by depth or an abstract
temperature~\cite{dong2021attention,geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered};
ours indexes it by generation step. A larger literature bounds transformer
expressivity by circuit class and state
tracking~\cite{strobl2024formal,merrill2024illusion}, but against a worst case in
one forward pass where ours bounds a decoder over generation. A competing account
holds that in text LMs the count survives in the residual stream and only the
output policy fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}; our probes favour
representational degradation, though the two are not exclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Implications and limits.}
Existing mitigations act late~\cite{wang2025eliminating,xin2024ralle}, helping
under this account only insofar as they raise $q$ or enlarge the readout margin.
We tested that rather than asserting it, sweeping XTTS-v2's repetition penalty
--- the most widely deployed such fix --- over $\{\MitPenLo{},3,5,\MitPenHi{}\}$,
the arms whose controls stay exact. The deficit moves only from \MitErrBest{}\%
to \MitErrWorst{}\%, a \MitSpan{}-point span across a fourfold change, sloping
\MitSlope{} points per unit --- slightly the \emph{wrong} way. Disabling it
entirely ($\!=\!\MitBrokenPen{}$) breaks the control, so no setting both
preserves quality and removes the deficit.
Limits: the panel is English-only and entirely codec-language models with softmax
attention over text, so the claim concerns that class. The
judge has a limit that runs in our favour and so must be stated: CTC
blank-collapse can merge adjacent copies of a word, depressing counts on repeated
items but not on controls (S9). The gap survives --- controls are
perturbation-stable and a second non-AR recogniser agrees within \HubertDiff{}
counts --- but high-$k$ magnitudes are approximate.
