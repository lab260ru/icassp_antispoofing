\noindent\textbf{The rival the evidence favours.}
With contraction refuted, the live question is where the count fails: erased
from the state, or surviving there while the policy that reads it
fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}. Our evidence points the second way: repetition costs probe retention against the
control in all \ProbeNBelowCtl{} of \ProbeNCk{}, as contraction predicts, but
the count is not erased --- it stays decodable late ($R^2$ \ProbeLateMed{}). Past the extension horizon the
\PhNCk{} checkpoints reach \PhAllRatios{} of a mean-answering predictor:
inconclusive at $n=\PhNCk{}$, and on the two Qwen checkpoints no evidence about
the representation at all, since trajectory length alone beats the state probe
there. Deciding between them needs a manipulation of
the readout at fixed state, and we ran two. Splicing whole donor states is too disruptive
to read (an \emph{unrelated} donor moves the count $1.3\times$ as much as one
differing in $k$). A rank-1 patch transferring only
the probe's count coordinate removes the damage (\CausalDegen{}\% degenerate,
self-patch bitwise-identical \CausalNoop{}/\CausalNoop{}) and moves the count
not at all ($\CausalShift{}$ log-count $[\CausalShiftLo{},\CausalShiftHi{}]$,
$n=\CausalN{}$); steering along it gives no dose-response. The count coordinate
transplants cleanly and the decoder ignores it (S26).

\noindent\textbf{Related work, and what the control decides.}
Closest is representational collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: distinct
prefixes converge with length, which our control separates from periodicity.
Counting-specific expressivity results reach a compatible conclusion another way
--- transformers count only under conditions on precision, depth and positional
encoding~\cite{yehudai2024when,salzer2025counting,zhang2024counting}, with
single-pass~\cite{hahn2020theoretical,strobl2024formal} and state-tracking
bounds~\cite{li2025statetracking} bracketing it. The two predict differently,
which is the point of the control: an inductive-bias limit is a property of the
model and survives at fixed periodicity, a contraction limit is a property of
the \emph{conditioning}. Ours varies periodicity at fixed length, and the
result is periodicity-specific.

\noindent\textbf{What the design does not separate.}
Periodicity and \emph{verbatim token identity} vary together here, so
``periodicity'' is the pair; neither is separated from \emph{training-set
frequency}, $k$-fold verbatim repetition being far rarer in speech corpora than
a matched sentence of distinct words, so an out-of-distribution account predicts
the same dissociation. Inside the repeated arm the requested count and
the amount of text are one variable, so a probe decoding $k$ cannot be said to
decode a \emph{count} rather than a length; only the control separates those,
behaviourally. The VITS/F5-TTS duration story rests on one model per strategy.
Exclusions are rule-based, applied without reference to arm or score.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
The panel is \NFamilies{} families and English the only language tested. The
judge is validated against concatenative ground truth, not human listeners, and
we ran no listening study. A control arm with its twin's deficit at matched
length would refute the claim; none did.
