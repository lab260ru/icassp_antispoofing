\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what the measurements do not show.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodic conditioning plus
contraction implies a finite counting horizon. It does not prove that any
transformer contracts, and two attempts to measure $q$ failed. A
boundary-distance estimator needs a monotone text read-head, which these
decoders do not provide --- their attention centroid advances on barely half of
generation steps. A finite-difference perturbation probe then found no
difference in local contraction between repeated items and their controls
($7$ of $18$ pairs in the predicted direction, $p=0.12$), with its own linearity
control failing (S5). The premise remains unestablished.

The theorem's distinctive prediction is a constant $N^\ast$, and where our
original ladder stopped we could not see one --- the count still tracked $k$.
Extending to $k=128$ found it, at $N^\ast_{\mathrm{rep}}=\HzNRep{}$ against
$\HzNCtl{}$ for length-matched controls. We read this as locating these decoders
just below the collapse regime at $k\le32$ rather than as confirming the bound:
the extension covers \HzNModels{} checkpoints, and the theorem's premise is still
unestablished, so what is shown is a horizon of the predicted kind, not one
derived from a measured $q$. Consistent with that, the state budget grows at
\CapRatio{} of the control rate while staying positive, and a probe finds the
count degraded rather than erased (\ProbeRetRep{} against \ProbeRetCtl{}).

A conditional account risks being unfalsifiable, so we state what would refute
it: a control with its twin's deficit; a model with the deficit whose capacity
gain matches its control; a probe reading the count as well late as early; or
counts still tracking $k$ past the ladder. The last was live until we ran it; the
others remain open to anyone with the released code.
\ErrBestModel{} is instructive: no deficit yet a capacity gap, so capacity loss
is necessary, not sufficient.

The closest prior result is representational
collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: as a sequence grows, distinct prefixes map to
arbitrarily close representations, already implying counting errors. But
collapse-by-length predicts that a $32$-word control and a $32$-word repetition
fail alike, so Figure~\ref{fig:main}(b) is evidence against length alone, and
\eqref{eq:horizon} supplies a rate in repetitions that a length-indexed bound
does not. Related dynamical treatments index convergence by depth or an abstract
temperature~\cite{dong2021attention,geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered};
ours indexes it by generation step, and expressivity bounds by circuit class and
state tracking~\cite{strobl2024formal,merrill2024illusion} constrain one forward
pass where ours constrains a decoder over generation. A competing account holds
that in text LMs the count survives in the residual stream and only the output
policy fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}; our probes favour representational
degradation, though the two are compatible.

\noindent\textbf{Implications and limits.}
Existing mitigations act late~\cite{wang2025eliminating,xin2024ralle} and, under
this account, help only insofar as they raise $q$ or enlarge the readout margin.
Sweeping XTTS-v2's repetition penalty fourfold moves the deficit from
\MitErrBest{}\% to \MitErrWorst{}\%, the \emph{wrong} way, and disabling it
breaks the control (S7).
Limits: the panel is English-only codec-language models with softmax attention
over text, and CTC blank-collapse merges adjacent word copies, so high-$k$
magnitudes err our way on repeated items only.
