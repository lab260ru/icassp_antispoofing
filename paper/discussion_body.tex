\noindent\textbf{What is proved, and what the measurements do not show.}
The Lean artifact certifies an implication: periodicity plus contraction imply a finite counting horizon. It does not prove any transformer
contracts, and two attempts to measure $q$ failed --- a boundary-distance
estimator needs a monotone read-head these decoders lack, and a perturbation
probe found no difference between repeated and control items ($7/18$ pairs,
$p=0.12$), its linearity control failing (S5).

The theorem's distinctive prediction is a constant $N^\ast$, invisible where our
ladder stopped; extending to $k=128$ found saturation of the predicted kind,
though not from a measured $q$. A linear probe does not separate this
from the rival on which the count survives and only the output policy
fails~\cite{venkatesh2026repeated}: it retains less on repeated text in
\ProbeNBelowCtl{} of \ProbeNCk{} checkpoints, but the count stays decodable late
($R^2$ \ProbeLateMed{}), as that rival predicts.

Past the horizon the probe does test the theorem, which forbids a Lipschitz
readout only \emph{beyond} $N^\ast$. On $k=48\ldots128$ states it reaches MAE
\PhPastMae{} in $\log_2 k$ against \PhPastConst{} for a predictor that ignores
them and answers the mean; below the horizon, \PhBelowMae{} against
\PhBelowConst{}. Range cannot explain this: over the \emph{same} $k$ it still
reads the count off control states (\PhCtlMae{}, $R^2$ \PhCtlRTwo{}), what it loses is
periodicity, not range. The theorem's prediction tested where it
applies --- \PhPastN{} items, one checkpoint: one observation, not a panel (S12).
To keep this falsifiable, we say what would refute it:
a control with its twin's deficit, or a model whose capacity gain matches its
control.

Closest is representational collapse~\cite{barbero2024glasses}: distinct prefixes
converge with length; our control separates that from periodicity. Related
bounds~\cite{dong2021attention,geshkovski2023emergence,gao2026clustered,%
strobl2024formal,merrill2024illusion} constrain one pass.
