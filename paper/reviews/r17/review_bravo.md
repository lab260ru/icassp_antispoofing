### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive (AR) TTS models' well-known tendency to miscount repeated phrases is explained by a formal mechanism: that softmax attention over k identical spans flattens (Lemma 1, "attention dilution"), that the resulting per-repetition state update is approximately autonomous (Assumption 1), and that if this map contracts (Assumption 2, unverified for any real model) then no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can recover the repetition count past a computable horizon N* (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six checkpoints across three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), scored by a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the AR bias of Whisper, are shown to undercount repeated phrases (median error −12.5%/checkpoint at k≥6, 18.2% exact) while length-matched non-repetitive controls are essentially exact (94.3%); the deficit is proportional rather than saturating up to k=32, and only a post-hoc extension to k=128 (4/6 checkpoints) recovers something like the saturating horizon the theorem predicts, with a linear state probe supporting the prediction in only 1 of 3 testable checkpoints. The authors are unusually candid that the theorem's premise (contraction) was never established and that a direct test of the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The panel's decoding hyperparameters (temperature, top-p, and especially repetition penalty) covary with checkpoint/architecture identity and are not held constant or fully swept, so the "periodicity, not length" pattern is confounded with family-specific anti-repetition heuristics that have nothing to do with the state-contraction mechanism the theorem posits.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Sampling uses shipped defaults... making Qwen3-TTS the most stochastic family"), Section 4.3 penalty-sweep paragraph (S15).
   **Why it matters** — Repetition penalty is a decoding-level mechanism that mechanically suppresses previously-emitted tokens; it can produce exactly the observed dissociation (suppress repeats, leave distinct words alone) with no reference to a contracting hidden-state map. The penalty sweep (S15) covers only 2 of 6 checkpoints (XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B); the three Llasa scale variants and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B — which ship no penalty or a different one — are never varied. The claim that "the limit tracks periodicity... in every checkpoint and family" is therefore drawn from a between-subjects comparison in which a plausible alternative driver (decoding-time repeat suppression) is uncontrolled for 4 of 6 checkpoints.
   **What would address it** — Run the penalty/temperature/top-p sweep on all six checkpoints (or hold sampling settings constant across the panel and report defaults as a separate ablation), and report how much of the repeated-vs-control gap survives when the decoding-level suppression pathway is neutralized architecture-by-architecture, not just for the two already swept.

2. **Issue** — The theorem's load-bearing premise (Assumption 2, contraction) was never established for any checkpoint, and the one dose-response test that was run came out sign-reversed, yet the paper's framing repeatedly implies the theorem explains the measured effect.
   **Where** — Section 4.4 ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately... its dose-response prediction fails outright... flatter attention goes with better counting"); Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... a perturbation probe found no difference between the arms (7/18, p=0.12)").
   **Why it matters** — Under an adversarial reading, a reversed dose-response and a null perturbation test are not merely "unmeasured" quantities — they are the two closest attempts at direct tests of the causal chain (dilution → contraction → collapse), and both point away from it. The abstract and intro nonetheless lean on theorem-flavored language ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and family") that a reader could easily take as confirmation of the mechanism rather than of a correlational behavioral pattern that a formally unconnected theorem happens to gesture at.
   **What would address it** — Either produce a checkpoint where q is measured and quantitatively predicts N*, or consistently frame the empirical contribution (periodicity-driven undercounting) as independent of, not explained by, the Lean-verified bound, removing language that implies the two are currently linked.

3. **Issue** — The CTC judge is validated for counting accuracy only on synthetic concatenative (spliced) audio with known ground truth, not on genuinely model-generated repeated speech, leaving the central instrument's accuracy on the actual measurement target unverified.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Behavioural measurement" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — Concatenated repeats lack the coarticulation, boundary-merging, and looping artifacts the paper's own introduction attributes to AR TTS ("it stops early, runs on, or locks into a loop"). If real model-generated repeats blend acoustically more than spliced ones as k grows, the CTC judge could systematically undercount genuine repeated audio in a k-dependent way that is indistinguishable from the model itself under-rendering — which would inflate or even manufacture the −12.5% error and the periodicity/length dissociation that the paper calls its "sharpest test." The released 165-clip "checked by ear" sample verifies scoring decisions, not an independent recount against a blinded human ground truth.
   **What would address it** — Have blinded human annotators independently count repetitions in a sample of genuinely model-generated (non-concatenative) audio across the k range and report CTC agreement with that ground truth, not just with spliced audio.

4. **Issue** — The empirical panel (n=6 checkpoints, 3 architecture families) is explicitly too small for inferential statistics, and multiple key analytic choices (functional form of the fit, extension range, probe checkpoint selection, exclusion rules) are made after inspecting the data, without pre-registration.
   **Where** — Sections 4.1–4.2 (switch from linear to saturating-exponential fit only after k≤32 proportionality contradicted Theorem 1(iv)), Section 4.2 (k=128 extension explicitly "post hoc," S16), Section 5 (probe run on only 3 of 6 checkpoints, no stated reason), S18 (exclusion sensitivity).
   **Why it matters** — The authors' own honesty about this ("The panel is too small for significance testing... so we rest on effect sizes") is commendable, but the paper still states strong qualitative universals ("in every checkpoint and family," "general in direction, not magnitude") built on a sequence of post-hoc branch points. Commit-log dating (S16) documents when decisions were made, but documenting post-hoc choices is not the same as constraining them in advance; the reader cannot tell how many alternative fits, ranges, or exclusion rules were tried before the reported ones.
   **What would address it** — Separate confirmatory from exploratory analyses explicitly, pre-register the functional form and extension protocol for a follow-up sample, and justify (or fix) which checkpoints receive the probe analysis rather than reporting "1 of 3" without explaining why the other 3 weren't attempted.

5. **Issue** — The length-matched control itself carries a residual periodicity confound above k=8 (its filler pool cycles every 8 items), and the correction for this confound is not carried through to the headline figures/table.
   **Where** — Section 4.3 ("Above k = 8 the control's fillers cycle a pool of eight... Re-generating k = 12...32 from a never-cycled 146-word pool leaves the dissociation at 71.2 points... where cycled controls give 84.6").
   **Why it matters** — By the authors' own accounting, ~13.4 of the ~84.6-point dissociation is attributable to this confound at k=12–32 — not negligible for a paper whose central rhetorical move is "the curves would coincide [under alternative explanations]; they do not." Figure 1(a)/Table 1, which anchor the abstract's headline numbers (94.3% vs. 18.2%), still use the cycled-pool controls throughout the full ladder, not the corrected uncycled version.
   **What would address it** — Regenerate the full control ladder (not just k=12–32) from a non-cycling pool and report headline statistics from that version, or explicitly flag in Figure 1/Table 1 that the reported control values include a residual confound of known, non-trivial size.

6. **Issue** — The one checkpoint (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) that directly disconfirms the k=128 horizon-lowering claim is explained away post hoc using the panel's own outcome ranking rather than treated as a test the theory failed.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation within k ≤ 128 and inverts the ratio. The exception is not arbitrary: it is the weakest checkpoint on all 3 panel measures... This is where a small effect becomes none, not the theorem behaving conditionally").
   **Why it matters** — This reasoning is unfalsifiable as structured: the criterion for "exception" (worst performer on the very panel measures under test) is derived from the same dataset used to grant the exception, so no possible pattern of results could count against the theorem — any disagreeing checkpoint can be relabeled "weak" after the fact. This directly bears on whether the headline horizon effect would replicate on a genuinely held-out checkpoint.
   **What would address it** — State an a priori criterion (e.g., derived from architecture, training data, or a pre-measured proxy for q) for which checkpoints should show the saturation-lowering effect before observing the k=128 data, or acquire an independent replication checkpoint to test whether the "weakest checkpoint" account generalizes.

### Minor concerns

- The paper cites recent work on formal/expressivity limits of counting in transformers (Hahn 2020, Merrill et al. 2024) but omits closely related counting-specific formal results such as "Counting and Algorithmic Generalization with Transformers" [2310.08661] and "Counting Like Transformers: Compiling Temporal Counting Logic Into Softmax Transformers" [2404.04393], which would usefully contextualize (or complicate) the dilution lemma's claim about why counting fails.
- Two of the citations underpinning key argumentative moves — the companion spectral-hallucination paper [7] and the linear-probe rival hypothesis [13] — are very recent (2026), evidently unreviewed preprints, one apparently by overlapping authors; readers cannot yet assess their reliability independently.
- The abstract quotes the per-checkpoint median error (−12.5%) without mentioning the pooled-item estimate (−8.3% [−12.5, −6.2]) reported in 4.1, even though the text itself flags the pooled interval as narrower only because it "ignores clustering" — worth stating explicitly why the larger, not the pooled, number was chosen for the headline.
- Figure 1's axis tick labels and captions, as rendered in the submitted text, are difficult to parse (e.g., "(a) periodicity, not length ... 22 25 k 100 0") — likely a formatting/OCR artifact, but should be checked in camera-ready.
- The Whisper audit numbers appear in two different forms (0.27–0.65 undercounting ratio in one place, 0.19 in another) without a clear statement of whether they refer to the same audio condition; a brief clarifying sentence would help.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" column is introduced but its quantitative relationship to the count-error columns (e.g., correlation across checkpoints) is not reported, despite both being framed as tests of the same theorem.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length empirical dissociation and its transparency/reproducibility package are genuinely strong, but uncontrolled decoding-hyperparameter confounds, an unvalidated (and in one direct test, contradicted) theorem premise, an unvalidated judge on the actual measurement target, and post-hoc handling of disconfirming data (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) mean the paper's central claim — that the Lean-verified theorem explains the observed collapse — is not yet supported by the experimental design as presented.
