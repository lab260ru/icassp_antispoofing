### Summary

The paper studies why autoregressive (AR) TTS models mis-render text containing many repetitions of a phrase. It proves, and formalises in Lean 4, a conditional theorem: if repeated-phrase conditioning collapses to an autonomous map on decoder state (via an attention-dilution lemma) and that map is a q-Lipschitz contraction, then no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* that shrinks with the contraction rate. Empirically, six TTS checkpoints across three families are run on a repetition ladder (k up to 32, later extended post hoc to 128) and scored with a CTC recogniser chosen specifically because the more obvious Whisper judge is itself shown to be repetition-biased. The headline findings are: (1) undercounting is periodicity-specific, not length-specific — length-matched distinct-word controls are essentially always exact (94.3%) while repeated items are not (18.2%); (2) within k ≤ 32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating, contradicting the constant-horizon prediction of the theorem, but a post-hoc extension to k=128 on 4 checkpoints finds a saturation-scale reduction of 3.5×; and (3) direct attempts to measure the theorem's own premise (contraction, and the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction) either failed or came out sign-reversed, so the paper explicitly does not establish that its proven implication is the operative mechanism behind the observed effect.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The two experiments designed specifically to test the theorem's causal mechanism either failed to produce a usable estimate or produced a result opposite to the theorem's prediction, yet the paper still frames the empirical undercounting as if it instantiates Theorem 1.
   **Where** — Section 4.4 / Discussion ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately... its dose-response prediction fails outright: within a (model, k) cell, flatter attention goes with better counting (r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75])"; "a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items (7 of 18 pairs, p = 0.12)").
   **Why it matters** — This is not a minor caveat; it is a failed confirmatory design. The dilution lemma predicts that more attention flattening (higher dilution) should track worse counting; the measured correlation goes the other way. The contraction-measurement experiment returned a null with a minority of pairs (7/18) even trending in the predicted direction. Together these mean the paper has run the experiments that would validate its own mechanism and gotten either no signal or the wrong sign — the theorem and the empirical phenomenon remain two separate findings connected only by narrative, not by measurement. The title's own hedge ("a horizon it does not yet explain") should extend to the whole causal chain, not just the horizon shape.
   **What would address it** — Either redesign the contraction/dilution probes with adequate power and a pre-specified analysis plan (the current probe uses only 18 pairs), or reframe the paper's contribution as two independently supported but as-yet-unlinked results: a conditional theorem, and an unexplained periodicity-specific behavioural deficit — dropping language that implies the theorem explains the data.

2. **Issue** — The k=128 extension that produces the paper's only evidence for a horizon (matching Theorem 1's qualitative shape) is explicitly a post-hoc design decision made after the pre-extension ladder (k ≤ 32) failed to show a horizon.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc (S16 dates every analysis decision against the commit log)... Because ˆc = 0.950k fit the pre-extension data better than any constant, no horizon lies inside this ladder" — implying the extension followed that null result).
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook HARKing risk: the range of k tested, the choice of which 4 (of 6) checkpoints to extend, and the specific saturating functional form ˆc(k) = K̂(1 − e^{−k/K̂}) fitted to it were all chosen after the initial hypothesis-consistent result failed to materialize. The commit-log dating (S16) is a good instinct but is not shown in the reviewed text, so the reader cannot verify that the extension protocol (which k values, which checkpoints, which fit family) was fixed before inspecting the k=48–128 outcomes rather than tuned to produce a saturation curve.
   **What would address it** — Report the extension as clearly exploratory (which the paper already partly does) but also report the results of alternative functional-form fits (e.g., a second saturating family, or a null hypothesis of continued proportionality with heavier tails) to show the saturation conclusion is not an artifact of the chosen parametric form; ideally pre-register the extension protocol and hold out a subset of checkpoints for confirmation.

3. **Issue** — Cross-family comparisons and the resolution of the one outlier checkpoint's null result depend on an uncontrolled confound: each model family runs its own "shipped default" sampling parameters (temperature, top-p, repetition penalty), which differ by up to an order of magnitude in penalty and non-trivially in temperature.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Sampling uses shipped defaults (XTTS-v2 T=0.75/0.85/50/penalty 5.0; Llasa T=0.8, top-p 1.0; Qwen3-TTS T=0.9/1.0/50/1.05), making Qwen3-TTS the most stochastic family, so its larger checkpoint's null is not an artifact of conservative decoding").
   **Why it matters** — The claim that Qwen3-TTS-1.7B's null result (no deficit, no saturation, "inverts the ratio") is not due to conservative decoding rests on an argument from stochasticity level, not from a controlled comparison. High stochasticity could just as plausibly *inflate* variance in ways that mask a genuine deficit as rule it out. Because sampling settings are never equalized across families, every between-family claim in the paper (e.g., "in every checkpoint and every family," Table 1's per-model comparison) is partly confounded with decoding-parameter differences that were not manipulated experimentally, only swept within two architectures for the narrower repetition-penalty question (Section 4.3).
   **What would address it** — Run at least one matched-decoding condition (same T/top-p/penalty) across all families, or explicitly model sampling parameters as covariates in the cross-family analysis rather than asserting their irrelevance by proxy argument.

4. **Issue** — Exclusion criteria that plausibly differ in incidence between the repeated and control arms are applied and pooled without reporting the per-condition breakdown, risking a biased between-condition comparison.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Of 2052 panel generations we exclude 342 in one template whose fillers the judge never transcribes, 132 stopped by our token budget rather than by the model, and 19 degenerate, leaving 1559"); Section 4.2 ("Of 240 generations 20 hit our budget and 10 were degenerate, leaving 21–26 per cell").
   **Why it matters** — The 342-item exclusion is described as affecting "fillers" specifically — a construct that by design only exists in the control condition (repeated items don't have fillers). If this exclusion removes a systematically hard-to-transcribe subset of control stimuli, it could inflate the reported 94.3% control exact-match rate relative to what a full, unbiased sample would show, directly affecting the paper's sharpest reported contrast (18.2% vs. 94.3%). The paper never reports exclusion counts broken out by condition (repeated vs. control) or by model, so this potential asymmetry is unauditable from the text as given.
   **What would address it** — Report exclusion counts and rates separately by condition (repeated/control) and by model, and show the headline contrast is robust to including the excluded items under a worst-case scoring assumption (e.g., score them as errors).

5. **Issue** — The audio-degeneracy classification step that removes 19 (and later 10) generations from analysis has no stated procedure: it is unclear whether it is automated, manual, or manual-and-blinded to condition.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Audio-level degeneracy is detected separately") — no method is given anywhere in the main text.
   **Why it matters** — If a human rater classifies "degenerate" audio while aware of which condition (k-repeated vs. control) or which model a clip belongs to, this is a classic unblinded-rating confound: expectations about which condition should fail could bias exclusion decisions, especially since degenerate items are dropped from the denominator rather than scored as failures, which can only help whichever condition disproportionately loses degenerate items.
   **What would address it** — State the degeneracy-detection procedure explicitly (rule-based vs. human), and if manual, confirm raters were blind to condition/model and report inter-rater reliability.

6. **Issue** — The F5-TTS duration intervention, used to test and largely rule out a duration-based alternative explanation for the "severe" deficit, supplies repeated items with a duration derived from the matched *control* condition, an unvalidated cross-condition transfer.
   **Where** — Section 4.3 ("supply the duration a correct rendering takes, read off its own controls — and the answer splits: below k=12 it lifts exact renderings from 43.3% to 76.7% (n=30), at or above it the gain is 0.0 (n=60)").
   **Why it matters** — Repeated-word speech and distinct-word speech of equal word count are not guaranteed to take equal duration when rendered correctly (prosodic compression/lengthening under repetition is a known phenomenon in both human and synthetic speech). If "correct" repeated-condition duration is systematically different from the control-condition duration used as the intervention target, the null result at k ≥ 12 could reflect a mis-specified target duration rather than a genuine architectural inability to "place thirty-two repetitions" in the right span — undermining the paper's claim that this rules out a duration failure at the severe end. The n=60 cell for the k≥12 condition is also small for a zero-effect claim.
   **What would address it** — Validate the assumption directly: measure the duration of ground-truth (human or reference) correct renderings of the *repeated* stimuli themselves, rather than importing the control's duration, and repeat the intervention with that in-condition target.

7. **Issue** — The paper's rival-hypothesis test against Venkatesh's representation/output dissociation account [13] (arXiv:2605.09239) relies on a post-hoc, unstated decision threshold for what counts as "the count stays decodable."
   **Where** — Discussion ("A linear probe retains less on repeated text than on its control in 6 of 6 checkpoints — but the count stays decodable late (median R2 0.48), and retention exceeds one in 2 of them... It does not contradict Theorem 1(iv) either (S12)").
   **Why it matters** — [13]'s own arXiv record (confirmed via search) reports linear probes decoding count "with near-perfect accuracy at every post-embedding layer" for text LLMs — a much stronger decodability claim than this paper's R²=0.48. No criterion is given in the main text for what R² or retention-ratio value would have counted as confirming vs. refuting the rival account, so "does not discriminate" is an ex post interpretive call on an ambiguous number, not a pre-specified test outcome, despite the paper elsewhere insisting (correctly) that a conditional account "risks being unfalsifiable" and offering falsification criteria for the *main* theorem. The same discipline is not applied here.
   **What would address it** — State in advance (or in a clearly separated pre-registration-style passage) what probe R²/retention values would count as support for vs. against each account, and report effect sizes with uncertainty intervals rather than a single median R².

### Minor concerns

- "exact-match scores 30-of-32 and 3-of-32 alike" (Section 3) is ambiguous — unclear what quantity is 30-of-32 vs 3-of-32 without more context.
- Reference [1] is cited approvingly for its alignment analysis despite being flagged in the bibliography itself as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a sentence justifying continued reliance on a retracted preprint.
- Reference [7], the paper's own framing anchor ("here the attractor is what the theorem is about"), is a same-author 2026 preprint not established as peer-reviewed; the paper leans on it for conceptual continuity without independent validation.
- Many load-bearing methodological details (audit sample sizes for the Whisper/CTC judge comparison, the definition of "capacity gain" dNeff/d log k, which "deep layers" were probed, the linear-probe protocol) are deferred to supplementary sections (S5, S8, S10–S16) not included in the reviewed text, making several central claims unverifiable from the manuscript as submitted for review.
- Table 1's "capacity gain dNeff/d log k" column has no defining equation in the main text.
- No explanation is given for why Llasa-3B is excluded from the k=128 extension panel while Llasa-1B and Llasa-8B are included.
- Figure 1's axis labels as rendered in the extracted text ("22 25" for panel (a)'s k-axis, "26 27" for panel (b)) appear to be extraction artifacts; illegible in the version reviewed.
- The phrase "no horizon lies inside this ladder" and later "so it is why k ≤ 32 looked proportional" reads awkwardly and could be tightened for clarity.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuinely well-controlled and interesting empirical result, and the formal verification is a real methodological strength, but the paper's central narrative link between the Lean-proved theorem and the observed deficit rests on experiments (contraction measurement, dilution dose-response) that the authors themselves report as failed or sign-reversed, compounded by a post-hoc horizon search and several under-reported exclusion/confound risks that need to be resolved or transparently bounded before the theorem-to-data connection can be trusted.
