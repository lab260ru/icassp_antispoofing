LENS: Experimental design (controls, confounds, blinding, pre-registration)
STANCE: Adversarial (assume the headline result is false; what would falsify it?)

### Summary

The paper argues that autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated phrases not because of generic exposure bias or attention-span limits, but because softmax attention over k near-identical spans dilutes toward Θ(1/k), pushing the per-repetition decoder update toward an approximately autonomous, contractive map; if that map contracts, a Lean-4-verified theorem shows any Lipschitz readout (including the stop head) loses the ability to distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon N*. The authors test this on a 180-item repetition ladder (k up to 32, later extended post hoc to 128) across six checkpoints from three AR families, scored with a CTC recognizer chosen after auditing the more obvious Whisper judge as itself repetition-biased. The headline result is a periodicity-specific deficit (median count error −8.3% pooled / −12.5% per-checkpoint at k ≥ 6, 18.2% exact vs. 94.3% for length-matched non-repeating controls) that is proportional rather than saturating up to k = 32, and only shows the theorem's predicted saturating "horizon" once the ladder is extended post hoc to k = 128 on 4 of the 6 checkpoints — one of which shows no such effect. The paper explicitly reports that its own attempts to measure the theorem's premise (the contraction factor q) failed, and that a dose-response test of the attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem's signature prediction (a saturating horizon) fails inside the pre-specified experimental design and is only recovered by a post-hoc extension of the design after the null result was observed, which is close to textbook HARKing even though the authors log the decision.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("Within k ≤ 32 that deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts") vs. §4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc… to k ∈ {48,64,96,128}").
   **Why it matters** — The pre-registered-in-spirit ladder (k up to 32, the range described in Methods as *the* stimulus set) directly contradicts the paper's central theoretical claim. Extending the range until a saturating pattern appears is exactly the design move that will produce apparent confirmation regardless of whether the mechanism is real: any generation process bounded by a token/duration budget must saturate eventually, and the paper concedes as much for the *control* condition too ("these decoders have a general utterance-length ceiling"). A ratio-based rescue (K_rep vs. K_ctl) doesn't establish that the two saturating curves share a cause — it only establishes that both are bounded, which is unsurprising. Compounding this, the extended arm has only 4 checkpoints, 21–26 items per cell, and one of the four (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B — not coincidentally the checkpoint with no k ≤ 32 deficit either) shows the opposite pattern entirely. A "3.5-fold" headline number built from 3 of 4 tested checkpoints, with no connection to the theorem's actual free parameter q, is weak support for a quantitative theorem.
   **What would address it** — Pre-register the extended ladder (k up to 128) as the primary design before collecting data, run it on all six checkpoints rather than the four that happen to admit long utterances, and report the saturation fit as confirmatory only if it holds out-of-sample on checkpoints not used to select the extension range. Absent that, the extension should be presented strictly as exploratory, not as evidence that "past the ladder, the count does level off" (§4.2 header).

2. **Issue** — The primary length-matched controls are constructed by cycling a pool of only 8 fillers above k = 8, which reintroduces the very periodicity the theorem's mechanism is supposed to be sensitive to, and the cleaner, non-cycled control was only run on a subset of the design.
   **Where** — §3 Stimuli ("cycled above k = 8 (S10)"); §4.3 ("Above k = 8 the control's fillers cycle a pool of eight… Regenerating k = 12…32 from a never-cycled 146-word pool leaves the dissociation at 71.2 points… where cycled controls give 84.6").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own "sharpest test" (periodicity vs. length) is only cleanly interpretable if the control genuinely lacks periodic structure. The authors' own follow-up shows the cycled-pool confound is worth 13.4 points out of ~85 — not negligible for a paper whose entire argument rests on razor-thin dissociation between two curves. Critically, this correction was applied only to k = 12–32 in what appears to be a limited re-run, not to the full panel (n = 525) used for the abstract's 94.3%/18.2% headline numbers, nor to the k ≥ 6 window that anchors the main effect-size claims in Table 1.
   **What would address it** — Use the non-cycled 146-word pool as the *primary* control across the entire panel and all k, not as a post-hoc patch on a subrange; report the headline contrast (94.3% vs. 18.2%) under that design, and only then argue the residual gap is attributable to periodicity rather than filler exhaustion.

3. **Issue** — The two direct attempts to measure the theorem's operative premise (Assumption 2, contraction) are statistically underpowered nulls that are reported as informative rather than inconclusive, and the dilution lemma's dose-response test is treated as merely "unexplained" despite being sign-reversed.
   **Where** — §5 Discussion ("a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items (7 of 18 pairs, p = 0.12)… its linearity control failing too"); §4.4 ("flatter attention goes with better counting (r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75])").
   **Why it matters** — n = 18 pairs gives very low power to detect a plausible effect size; a non-significant p = 0.12 under that sample size is not evidence of no contraction difference, it's an underpowered test that cannot distinguish "no effect" from "effect too small to detect here." Worse, the paper's own linearity control for this probe reportedly failed, which should disqualify the null result from being cited as informative at all rather than folded into "two attempts to measure q failed." Separately, the dose-response check for Lemma 1 (which the theory needs to actually be doing causal work) came back with the *opposite* sign from what dilution predicts — flatter attention correlates with *better* counting, not worse. That is a directional falsification of the mechanism as stated, not a footnote; the paper treats it as leaving "what selects which generations fail" merely open, which understates how much this undercuts the causal story linking Lemma 1 to Theorem 1's premise.
   **What would address it** — Report a power analysis for the perturbation probe and either scale up n or drop the "found no difference" framing; treat the sign-reversed dose-response result as active evidence against the stated dilution→collapse causal chain, and revise the paper's framing of what has and hasn't been shown accordingly (the abstract already partially does this — "a dose-response test of the dilution lemma came out sign-reversed" — but the body text is softer than the abstract).

4. **Issue** — The architectural (AR vs. non-AR) comparison and the follow-up "duration hypothesis" test are built on an asymmetric, single-model causal design: only F5-TTS receives the duration-supplying intervention, VITS is never subjected to an equivalent manipulation, and the whole architectural story is inferred post hoc from a 2-system split.
   **Where** — §4.3 ("The last rival is architectural... VITS... shows no dissociation... F5-TTS... does show it... What plausibly separates them is where the count must live"); the duration intervention paragraph (S13).
   **Why it matters** — With only two non-AR systems and no pre-specified hypothesis about which one would dissociate, "VITS is immune, F5-TTS is not, therefore duration representation is the plausible mediator" is a post-hoc explanation invented to fit an unexpected 2-point split, then partially tested only on the system that already showed the effect. This is not a controlled test of the duration hypothesis; it's a demonstration compatible with it. No manipulation was attempted on VITS (e.g., degrading its per-token duration assignment to see if that reproduces the deficit) that would falsify the account from the other direction.
   **What would address it** — Either add a third non-AR architecture to break the n = 2 tie, or design a bidirectional intervention (impair duration-per-token access in VITS as well as supply total duration to F5-TTS) before treating "where the count must live" as an explanation rather than a post-hoc redescription of the observed split.

5. **Issue** — The CTC judge is validated only against synthetic concatenative audio with known ground truth, not against the naturalistic degenerate/looped audio that the impaired models actually produce, which is precisely the regime where a CTC recognizer's own well-known weakness to repeated near-identical acoustic segments could align with (and inflate) the effect under study.
   **Where** — §3, Behavioural measurement ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth... ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper").
   **Why it matters** — Concatenative ground-truth audio is clean and constructed, not the actual model output distribution (which includes loops, truncations, and degenerate spans that the paper separately flags and excludes 19+10 items for). CTC decoders are themselves susceptible to blank-collapse and repeat-collapse on genuinely repetitive or degraded acoustic input — the paper even invokes this possibility ("blank-collapse would have depressed [VITS's] repeated items too") as a *reassurance* for one result, which implicitly concedes the judge's own repeat-sensitivity is a live concern the audit doesn't rule out for the main AR panel.
   **What would address it** — Extend the judge audit to a stratified sample of actual (human-verified) model outputs, ideally with double human transcription and inter-rater agreement statistics, not just an "audio can be checked by ear" offer via the 165-clip sample. Report human/CTC agreement (e.g., Cohen's κ) on genuinely model-generated degenerate audio, not only on synthetic ground truth.

6. **Issue** — The panel size (n = 6 checkpoints, 3 families) is explicitly acknowledged as too small for significance testing, yet the paper's title and abstract make general claims about "autoregressive text-to-speech models" as a class.
   **Where** — §4.2 ("The panel is too small for significance testing at either level... so we rest on effect sizes and their consistency"); Abstract/Title.
   **Why it matters** — This is commendably transparent, but it means every headline number (−8.3% pooled error, 18.2% vs 94.3% exact-match, 3.5-fold horizon shrinkage) is a descriptive statistic over an n too small for inference, with one of six checkpoints behaving as a flat exception throughout. The generalization implied by the title ("Counting Collapse in Autoregressive Text-to-Speech") outruns what a 6-checkpoint, 3-family convenience sample (with no stated basis for family/checkpoint selection) can support.
   **What would address it** — Either soften title/abstract claims to explicitly scope them to the tested families, or add checkpoints/families sufficient to support the effect-size consistency argument statistically (even a pre-registered stopping rule for "how many checkpoints will be tested" would help distinguish confirmatory from exploratory reporting).

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is cited for its alignment analysis but is noted as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — leaning on a withdrawn preprint's analysis, even narrowly, should be flagged more prominently or replaced.
- The abstract reports −8.3% (pooled) as the headline count-error figure while §4.1 states the per-checkpoint median is −12.5% and explicitly argues the checkpoint should be the unit of replication (§4.2); leading with the smaller pooled number in the abstract is inconsistent with the paper's own stated preference for checkpoint-level aggregation.
- Figure 1's axis labels/values render ambiguously in the extracted text (e.g., "22 25 k 100 0"), likely a PDF-extraction artifact of log-scale exponents — worth double-checking the camera-ready figure renders k-axis tick labels unambiguously.
- The repetition-penalty sweep is run on only 2 of 6 architectures (S15); the claim that "the deficit stays put" under decoding-rule variation would be stronger if swept on more checkpoints rather than relying on Llasa's zero-penalty default as an implicit control.
- Self-citation of a companion paper [7] by the same authors for the "attractor regime" framing is fine but is the paper's only cited empirical precedent for the dynamical-systems framing in speech; a broader related-work pass connecting to non-self-authored dynamical/attractor analyses of attention (already partially done via [15–19]) would situate the contribution more independently.
- "exact-match scores 30-of-32 and 3-of-32 alike" (§3) is a compressed aside whose referent is unclear without closer reading; consider spelling out what the two numbers represent.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core dissociation (periodicity vs. length) is a genuinely interesting and mostly well-controlled result, and the paper is unusually candid about its own failures, but the pivotal "horizon" claim is recovered only via a post-hoc, underpowered extension after failing in the pre-specified design, the primary controls carry an only-partially-corrected periodicity confound, and the theorem's premise (contraction) is not just unconfirmed but actively contradicted in direction by the paper's own dose-response test.
