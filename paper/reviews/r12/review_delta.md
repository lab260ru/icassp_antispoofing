### Summary

The paper makes three claims: (1) a Lean-verified corollary — if a decoder's per-repetition update contracts, no Lipschitz readout can distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon; (2) six AR TTS checkpoints across three families show a repetition-specific undercount that tracks periodicity rather than utterance length, judged by a CTC recognizer chosen because the obvious AR judge (Whisper) is itself biased in the same direction as the effect; and (3) the theorem's signature prediction — a finite horizon — is invisible on the pre-specified ladder (k ≤ 32, where the deficit is proportional) and only appears after the ladder is extended post hoc to k = 128, at which point the paper's own attempts to verify the theorem's premise (measuring the contraction factor q) failed, and the companion dilution lemma's dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed. Treating the result as artefactual until proven otherwise: the periodicity-vs-length dissociation (§4.3) is a genuinely well-controlled, distinguishing experiment and I believe it. The theorem is real math, competently formalized, but narrow (a Banach fixed-point corollary for parts i–iii). The causal story wiring the theorem to the data — contraction ⇒ collapse ⇒ observed horizon — is not established by anything in the paper; the one piece of evidence offered for it (the k=128 saturation) was obtained by extending the experiment specifically because the pre-registered range failed to show it, and the paper says so itself. That is the paper's own admission of the mechanism I'd have gone looking for anyway.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The audit validating the CTC judge doesn't state it covers the k=128 extension range, leaving ASR-artifact as an unaddressed alternative explanation for "leveling off." `[Evidence — alternative explanation]`
   **Where** — §3 ("Behavioural measurement...") vs §4.2.
   **Why it matters** — The headline extension finding — "the repeated median sits at 25 and 25 at k=48 and 128 as the control climbs 46 to 66" — is the paper's only direct evidence for a horizon. CTC recognizers are known to degrade on long, highly repetitive/monotonous audio (the paper itself invokes an analogous failure mode, "blank-collapse," to explain VITS's null result in §4.3). If the judge's own transcription accuracy sags on 128-repetition audio the way Whisper's does on shorter repeated audio, the flattening reported in Fig. 1(b) could be partly judge failure, not model failure — and this would selectively hit the *repeated* condition, since control audio at matched length isn't periodic.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly that the concatenative-audio audit (or an equivalent) was run at k up to 128, with the resulting counted/true ratio, not just at the original ladder's range.

2. **Issue** — Lemma 1's dose-response prediction is falsified by the paper's own data, decoupling the two formally-verified results from each other empirically. `[Evidence — distinguishing test failure]`
   **Where** — §4.4, last paragraph: "flatter attention goes with better counting (r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75])."
   **Why it matters** — The introduction's causal chain is: dilution (Lemma 1) flattens attention → the per-repetition map becomes approximately autonomous → if it contracts (Assumption 2), counting collapses (Theorem 1). If more dilution predicted *better* counting rather than worse, within-cell, that's not a minor caveat — it says the mechanism the paper spends two sections building isn't the one selecting which generations fail. The paper reports this honestly but doesn't revise the causal narrative in the abstract/intro to match; it still reads as "dilution → contraction → collapse" everywhere except the one paragraph that reports the reversal.
   **What would address it** — Either propose and test a revised mechanism consistent with the reversed sign, or downgrade Lemma 1 in the abstract/intro from "the mechanism" to "a formally verified but empirically unconfirmed candidate mechanism."

3. **Issue** — No engagement with the substantial existing "can transformers count" literature, leaving the novelty claim uncalibrated. `[Novelty]`
   **Where** — References [1]–[19]; absent from all of them.
   **Why it matters** — Searching arXiv turns up direct, on-point theoretical/empirical work the paper never cites or distinguishes itself from: "When Can Transformers Count to n?" [2407.15160], "The Counting Power of Transformers" [2505.11199], and "Language Models Need Inductive Biases to Count Inductively" [2405.20131]. The paper's cited "related dynamical and expressivity bounds" [15–19] are about rank collapse, clustering, and formal-language expressivity generally — not about counting per se, which is exactly the topic of the three papers above. Given the paper's own framing ("we argue that much of this behaviour has a simpler cause"), a reader can't tell whether the periodicity-specific deficit here is a speech-domain instance of an already-established general limitation, is in tension with existing counting-capacity results, or genuinely adds something those papers don't cover. The narrower claim ("first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact") may survive, but that's a much thinner claim than the paper's framing suggests.
   **What would address it** — Cite and position against this literature; state precisely what's new relative to it (formalization? the periodic-conditioning framing? the TTS domain?).

4. **Issue** — All headline contrasts rest on an n = 6 checkpoint panel the authors state cannot support significance testing, and the one disconfirming checkpoint is narratively excluded rather than integrated. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — §4.2 ("the exact signed-rank floor at n = 6 is 0.031... so we rest on effect sizes") and the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B treatment throughout §4.2–4.4.
   **Why it matters** — Every quantitative claim advertised in the abstract (3.5-fold saturation-scale reduction, -8.3%/-12.5% error, 0.46× capacity gain) is an effect size over 6 units with no valid test, by the authors' own statement. That's a defensible thing to report, but it means the paper cannot distinguish "a real, general effect" from "5 of 6 available checkpoints happened to go one way." Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no k≤32 deficit, no k≤128 saturation, and an inverted ratio — a genuine three-way disconfirmation — yet it's handled as "an unexplained exception" rather than folded into the effect-size confidence interval as a data point that pulls hard against the claim. Separately, the F5-TTS duration-intervention result that "settles" the duration question (§4.3, "43.3% to 76.7%, n = 30" vs "gain is 0.0, n = 60") is reported as flat point estimates with no bootstrap interval, unlike the K̂ fits and capacity-gain figure elsewhere in the paper — an inconsistent standard of evidence for a result doing real argumentative work.
   **What would address it** — Report the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B exception inside the same CI machinery used for the other five (does the 3.5-fold or 0.46× figure survive leave-one-out?), and give a CI for the duration-intervention percentages.

5. **Issue** — The paper's central quantitative confirmation of the theorem — the k=128 horizon — was obtained by extending the pre-specified ladder specifically because it failed to show a horizon, and the same post-hoc pattern recurs in how the VITS/F5-TTS architectural split is explained. `[Evidence — post-hoc-shaped hypothesis]`
   **Where** — §4.1 ("not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts... no horizon lies inside this ladder") → §4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc... to k ∈ {48, 64, 96, 128}").
   **Why it matters** — This is as clean a case of search-until-it-fits as I've seen stated in a paper's own words. The Method section pre-specifies a ladder up to k=32. That ladder returns a result inconsistent with the theorem's distinctive prediction (proportional, not saturating). The authors then collect new data at a range chosen after seeing that the first range didn't work, on only 4 of 6 checkpoints (the ones physically capable of it), fit a phenomenological saturation curve the paper itself concedes is "not the theorem's N∗, a function of the unmeasured q," and report the resulting 3.5-fold ratio as the paper's headline confirmatory number — all while the theorem's actual load-bearing premise, contraction (Assumption 2), was never measured (two attempts failed) and the companion lemma's prediction reversed sign (concern #2 above). Given that, "the count stays proportional rather than saturating [within k≤32]... a post-hoc extension... finds one" is not independent confirmation of the theorem; it's evidence that if you keep extending an experiment until a qualitative pattern (any saturating shape — which a generic decode-length ceiling would also produce, as the authors themselves note in the same paragraph) appears, it eventually does. The same move recurs at a smaller scale in §4.3: VITS turning out immune and F5-TTS turning out not is used to retroactively construct a new hypothesis ("what plausibly separates them is where the count must live") that is tested only after the split was observed, on the same F5-TTS runs that motivated it. The paper is commendably transparent about doing this (S16 timestamps decisions against the commit log), and I'd rather see this disclosed than hidden — but disclosure doesn't neutralize the inferential problem: nothing in the paper is a genuinely held-out confirmatory test of "the horizon exists and its location is periodicity-dependent." A fresh k∈{48...128} run on independently-selected checkpoints/prompts, decided before looking at the extension data, would be that test; it isn't run.
   **What would address it** — Either reframe the extended-ladder result explicitly as exploratory (not confirmatory of Theorem 1), or run a genuinely pre-registered confirmatory extension on held-out checkpoints/stimuli and report whether the 3.5-fold figure replicates.

- Table 1's "capacity gain... measured at deep layers" never defines which layer(s) "deep" refers to.
- The abstract quotes only the pooled -8.3% error figure; the larger -12.5% per-checkpoint median (§4.1) is mentioned but a reader who stops at the abstract gets the smaller number.
- Reference [1] is cited despite being "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — flagged transparently, but leaning on a withdrawn preprint's alignment analysis at all is worth a stronger caveat than a footnote.

- No qualitative/cherry-picking check applies here in the usual sense — the paper shows no hand-picked example transcripts or spectrograms in the text itself; the released 165-clip stratified sample is a reasonable substitute, but nothing in-paper lets a reader eyeball a single failure case.
- The GitHub repo linked for code/data/stimuli is named "icassp_antispoofing," topically mismatched with a TTS-counting paper — likely a shared monorepo, but worth flagging since it could confuse someone trying to locate the artifact from the paper text alone.

### Belief update

Real, modest update: I now put more weight on "AR TTS decoders undercount specifically on periodic text, not merely long text" as a robust behavioral fact — the length-matched control design in §4.3 is the one experiment here I'd call a genuine distinguishing test, and it's hard to explain away as length, exposure bias, or attention span. I put close to zero updated weight on "this is because the per-repetition state map contracts" as the explanatory mechanism — the paper's own attempts to measure that premise failed, its companion lemma's prediction reversed sign, and the one piece of evidence offered for the theorem's distinctive signature (a horizon) was obtained by extending the experiment after the pre-specified design failed to show it. I'd read this paper as establishing a phenomenon in search of a mechanism, with a nicely executed but not-yet-load-bearing formal artifact attached to it.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a real, well-controlled finding worth publishing, but the paper's central confirmatory evidence for its own theorem (the k=128 horizon) was produced by extending a failed pre-specified experiment until a qualitative match appeared, while the theorem's actual premise (contraction) remains unmeasured and its companion lemma's prediction is empirically reversed — the causal narrative needs to be either re-tested on a genuinely held-out design or explicitly downgraded to exploratory before the "Lean-verified bound explains this" framing is warranted.
