### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech models miscount repeated phrases, and proposes a mechanistic account: softmax attention dilutes evenly over k repeated spans (Lemma 1), making the decoder's per-repetition update approximately autonomous; if that update map contracts (Assumption 2), a Banach-style argument (Theorem 1) shows no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can separate repetition counts beyond a horizon N* set by the contraction factor, orbit scale, and decision margin. Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six AR checkpoints across three families are scored by a CTC recognizer (chosen because Whisper's own AR decoder is shown to be repetition-biased) on a parametric repetition ladder with length-matched non-repetitive controls: repeated items show a median count error of roughly -8 to -12% at k≥6 and 18.2% exact-match versus 94.3% for controls, a dissociation the authors argue tracks periodicity rather than length. Within k≤32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating (contrary to Theorem 1's prediction); a post-hoc extension to k=128 on four checkpoints finds saturation with a 3.5-fold lower scale for repeated versus control speech. The authors report, however, that two direct attempts to measure the theorem's contraction premise failed, and a dose-response test of the dilution lemma came out sign-reversed, and one checkpoint (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows no deficit at all.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem's mechanistic premises are not merely "unconfirmed"; the paper's own direct tests of them fail or point the opposite direction, so the causal story linking the formally verified bound to the observed behavior is undermined by the paper's own evidence, not just unestablished.
   **Where** — Section 2 (Assumption 2), Section 4.4 ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately... its dose-response prediction fails outright: flatter attention goes with *better* counting"), Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's rhetorical spine is "we proved a bound, and the data show a horizon of the predicted shape." But the one sub-mechanism that was actually tested (dilution → worse counting) reverses sign, and the central parameter (q) could not be measured by either of two attempts. What remains is a periodicity-specific behavioral deficit and a saturating curve shape at k=128 — both compatible with contraction dynamics but not distinctively produced by them, since the length-matched control also saturates (Section 4.2: control counts level off from 46 to 66 between k=48 and 128). A saturating-count curve is a generic property of long-utterance AR/NAR generation; observing it in the repeated condition does not confirm Assumption 2 specifically. The paper's title already flags "a horizon it does not yet explain," which is honest, but Discussion language such as "found saturation of the predicted kind" still overstates how much the k=128 finding validates the mechanism versus merely being consistent with many possible mechanisms (including a plain capacity ceiling).
   **What would address it** — Either instrument a genuine estimate of q (the paper notes the missing ingredient is a monotone read-head; propose or attempt one) and report it, or explicitly demote the theorem to "a formally verified conditional result that motivates, but is not empirically supported by, the measured periodicity-specific deficit," removing phrasing that implies mechanistic confirmation.

2. **Issue** — The abstract's claim that "the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family" is contradicted by the results section's own admission of a null checkpoint.
   **Where** — Abstract vs. Section 4.2 ("the median-error gap is positive in 5 of 6, zero in Qwen3-TTS-1.7B... an unexplained exception") and Table 1 (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B: count err. rep. 0.0%, ctl. 0.0%).
   **Why it matters** — By the paper's own stated unit of replication (the checkpoint, Section 4.2), 1 of 6 checkpoints is a flat null on the headline metric. Calling this "every checkpoint" in the abstract is an overclaim a careful reader will catch once reaching Section 4.2, and it matters for external validity: the effect's generalization across architectures/scales is weaker than advertised.
   **What would address it** — Qualify the abstract ("in 5 of 6 checkpoints") and move the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B exception into the headline framing rather than only the results/discussion.

3. **Issue** — F5-TTS, used as a load-bearing "rival architecture" result that falsifies the paper's autoregressive-specificity hypothesis and motivates the duration-based sub-explanation, is never described in Methods.
   **Where** — Absent from Section 3 (Models); introduced only in Section 4.3 ("But F5-TTS, contemporary and flow-matching rather than recurrent, does show it") and the subsequent duration-intervention paragraph (n=30, n=60).
   **Why it matters** — This is one of the paper's most consequential findings ("we withdraw the architectural claim"), and it is directly germane to the paper's own cited rival account [13] (Venkatesh, [2605.09239] — "Repeated-Token Counting Reveals a Dissociation Between Representations and Outputs," which argues counting failures are output-policy artifacts rather than representational loss). F5-TTS is not an AR decoder, so Theorem 1 does not formally apply to it at all; if a non-AR, non-recurrent, flow-matching model shows the same periodicity-specific deficit, the paper needs to explain why its state-contraction story — built specifically around AR decoding boundaries — should be expected to say anything about F5-TTS's failure mode, rather than simply noting the phenomenological similarity. Without seeds, sampling settings, item counts, and the duration-supplying protocol in Methods, this comparison cannot be audited or reproduced to the standard applied to the six-checkpoint panel (S10 exclusions, seeds, etc.).
   **What would address it** — Add F5-TTS methodology (version, sampling params, seeds, full item counts, and the duration-intervention protocol) to Section 3, and directly address whether/how the AR-specific contraction mechanism could apply to a non-recurrent flow-matching decoder, or explicitly state that F5-TTS's deficit is left mechanistically unexplained by Theorem 1.

4. **Issue** — The 342-item exclusion ("one template whose fillers the judge never transcribes") is structurally tied to the control condition and could inflate the central periodicity-vs-length contrast.
   **Where** — Section 3, exclusion accounting (342 of 2052 panel generations); Section 4.3's "sharpest test" (94.3% vs. 18.2% exact match).
   **Why it matters** — Fillers exist only in the control arm; if this judge-transcription failure disproportionately drops hard/ambiguous control items, the reported near-ceiling control performance (and hence the size of the periodicity/length dissociation, the paper's headline claim) could be partly an artifact of differential exclusion rather than a pure behavioral effect.
   **What would address it** — Report the 342 exclusions broken down by condition (repeated vs. control) and k-band, and show the dissociation survives a conservative worst-case inclusion of them.

5. **Issue** — The k=128 extension is explicitly post-hoc and was undertaken specifically because the k≤32 data contradicted the theorem's predicted saturating shape, a textbook setup for confirmation-seeking that only 4 of 6 checkpoints ever entered, with one omission (Llasa-3B) left unexplained.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc... on the 4 checkpoints admitting utterances that long (Llasa-1B, Llasa-8B, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B); XTTS-v2's 602-mel-token ceiling censors every k≥48 item"); Llasa-3B's absence from this list is never justified.
   **Why it matters** — Combined with the explicit admission that "the panel is too small for significance testing at either level" and "no multiplicity correction clears 0.05," the paper is reporting a precise, quotable statistic (3.5-fold horizon reduction) from an exploratory, small-n (21–26 items/cell), non-preregistered analysis chosen after seeing the disconfirming k≤32 trend. The decision-log transparency (S16) is commendable but does not substitute for either a held-out replication or explicit "this is exploratory, unreplicated, and should not be read as confirmatory" language in the Discussion, where instead it is described as finding "saturation of the predicted kind."
   **What would address it** — State why Llasa-3B is missing from the extension; explicitly label the k=128 finding exploratory/unreplicated in the Discussion; ideally replicate on an independent stimulus set before it anchors the horizon claim.

6. **Issue** — The paper poses a genuine critical test against the leading rival account [13] and reports that the test fails to discriminate, yet still frames the contraction account as the operative explanation in the abstract and title.
   **Where** — Section 5: "One test could have separated our account from the rival on which the count survives and only the output policy fails [13]. It does not... The relative effect is real; it discriminates nothing."
   **Why it matters** — The rival account (representations retain the count; only the output/decoding policy fails) is not excluded — indeed the paper's own linear-probe check finds count information remains decodable late (median R² 0.48, with retention *exceeding* the control in 2 of 6 checkpoints), which is at minimum in tension with Theorem 1(ii)'s premise that a Lipschitz readout should lose count-discriminating power past N*. Two mechanistically distinct accounts of the same phenomenon are left unresolved, but the paper's title and abstract present the state-contraction account as the operative one.
   **What would address it** — Either design a test that actually discriminates the two accounts (e.g., intervening on the readout rather than the state, or testing whether count information is recoverable by a *different* Lipschitz probe than the one used), or reframe the claim as "a candidate mechanism among at least two live ones, empirically underdetermined by current tests."

### Minor concerns

- The code/data repository is named `lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing`, unrelated to the paper's stated topic (TTS repetition/counting collapse) — worth clarifying whether this is a repurposed repo, and confirming the correct, topic-matched URL ships with the camera-ready version.
- Reference [1] is cited as a live source for the "documented stability problem" framing but its own entry notes it was "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026"; the reason for withdrawal isn't given, and leaning on a withdrawn preprint without further caveat is risky.
- Figure 1's reported axis tick values in the extracted text ("22 25" for the k-axis, "26 27" for rendered count) look malformed, possibly a PDF-extraction artifact rather than an authoring error — worth double-checking the camera-ready figure renders correctly.
- Table 1's "ratio" column (0.21–0.80) has a simple mean of ≈0.49, while Section 4.4's text quotes "a factor 0.46," which is the ratio-of-means rather than the mean-of-ratios; both are legitimate statistics but the paper mixes aggregation conventions across sections (checkpoint-median vs. item-pooled in 4.1; mean-of-differences vs. ratio-of-means in 4.4) without flagging which is which, making the numbers hard to audit at a glance.
- The vivid "six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six" example in the Introduction is never revisited with its own quantitative result; it reads as illustrative framing rather than a tested stimulus.
- Theorem 1 is stated as decoding-rule-invariant ("no property of the decoding rule enters"), yet Section 4.3 spends a paragraph empirically ruling out repetition-penalty as a rival explanation; a sentence connecting why a theorem-level invariance still needs empirical rule-out would help readers.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length dissociation and the CTC-judge methodology are a solid empirical contribution, but the paper's own reported test failures (unmeasured contraction factor, sign-reversed dilution dose-response, an undiscriminating test against the leading rival account) mean the formally verified theorem is not yet shown to explain the measured phenomenon, and this gap needs to be reflected more consistently in the abstract/discussion framing, alongside filling in the missing F5-TTS methods and the abstract/body universality inconsistency.
