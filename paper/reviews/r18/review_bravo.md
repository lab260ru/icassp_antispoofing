### Summary
The paper asks whether neural TTS models miscount repeated words because repetition makes text *periodic* rather than merely *long*, and separates the two by pairing each k-fold word repetition with a length- and carrier-matched control in which the repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), repeated items are rendered exactly 18.2% of the time versus 94.3% for controls, a gap the authors argue survives decoding-rule, judge, and exclusion-rule sweeps, and is present in one non-autoregressive baseline (F5-TTS) but absent in another (VITS). A CTC recognizer replaces Whisper as the scoring instrument after Whisper is shown to under-count periodic ground truth. The paper also states, and formally verifies in Lean 4, a conditional bound on how long a contracting per-repetition state map can support count-separating readouts, but explicitly reports that the bound's central parameter (q) could not be measured and that an auxiliary dose-response prediction came out reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The formal Lean 4 result is framed as a headline contribution ("the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact") despite explicitly failing to connect to the empirical phenomenon it is introduced to explain.
   **Where** — Section 1 (contribution list), Section 4 throughout, and the admission that "part (i) specializes [mathlib's] Banach machinery, the rest is proved from scratch."
   **Why it matters** — By the authors' own account, the theorem's premise (q < 1 contraction) was never measured, two independent estimators of q failed, and the companion dilution lemma's dose-response prediction is sign-reversed (flatter attention correlates with *better* counting, r = 0.59). What remains formally verified is a generic Banach-style contraction/Lipschitz-readout argument that holds for any contracting dynamical system — it is not specific to speech synthesis, to neural networks, or even to sequence models. Novelty framed around "first formal artifact in speech hallucination" attaches domain specificity to a result whose actual domain content is unestablished; the paper is claiming priority for formalizing a phenomenon it says it cannot show is present. This inflates the paper's contribution count without inflating its evidentiary content — the reader is left with a discussion section (5) that finds the *output-policy* account, not the contraction account, better supported by the paper's own probe data.
   **What would address it** — Either demote the formal result to an explicitly labeled negative/exploratory appendix result ("we attempted to formalize and test a contraction account; the premise resists measurement and the auxiliary prediction fails"), or hold the contribution claim until q can be estimated by some means (e.g., fitting F in Theorem 1's sense directly to hidden-state trajectories via a learned Koopman-style operator, rather than via an attention read-head or finite-difference probe that the paper itself argues is structurally biased against detecting q).

2. **Issue** — The related-work engagement with the cited counting-expressivity theory (Yehudai et al. [15], Sälzer et al. [16], Zhang et al. [17]) is asserted as "a compatible conclusion by another route" without checking whether those theorems' preconditions (precision, depth, positional encoding regime) actually hold for the tested checkpoints.
   **Where** — Section 5, "Related work, and what the control decides" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — These are precision/depth-conditioned expressivity results for idealized transformer counting; asserting compatibility with an entirely different empirical setup (TTS decoders operating on codec/duration tokens rather than text token counts) without any instantiation of the theorems' hypotheses for Llasa/Qwen/XTTS is a category-level citation rather than an engaged one. It leaves the reader unable to tell whether the cited theory actually predicts anything about this paper's architectures, or is invoked for rhetorical cover.
   **What would address it** — State the precision/depth/positional-encoding regime of each tested backbone and note explicitly whether it falls inside or outside the conditions under which [15]/[16]/[17] guarantee (or forbid) counting, or drop the "compatible conclusion" framing.

3. **Issue** — The paper's TTS-robustness related work (Section 1) cites only four papers on the "stability problem" [1–4] and none of the monotonic-alignment / duration-supervision literature (e.g., guided-attention losses, forward-sum alignment, hard-duration predictors used in the FastSpeech/Glow-TTS/VITS lineage) that plausibly already explains why one non-autoregressive baseline (VITS) is immune while another (F5-TTS) is not.
   **Where** — Section 1, and the discussion of VITS vs. F5-TTS in Section 3.2.
   **Why it matters** — The paper itself identifies the mechanism distinguishing the two non-AR baselines as "where the count must live": VITS assigns each token its own duration (a form of hard monotonic alignment), F5-TTS estimates one total duration. That is exactly the design axis the monotonic-alignment TTS literature (which the paper never cites) was built around, and which claims to solve or mitigate the very failure mode under study. Omitting this literature makes the periodicity-vs-length dissociation look like a newly discovered structural fact about TTS decoders, when it may be a restatement, in a controlled paradigm, of a known duration-modeling design tradeoff.
   **What would address it** — Cite and discuss the monotonic-alignment/duration-supervision literature, and explicitly test whether architectures using hard per-token duration allocation (not just VITS) are systematically immune, which would strengthen — or falsify — the causal story rather than leaving it as a two-model anecdote.

4. **Issue** — The judge-validation claim ("CTC scores 1.00 on concatenative ground truth, therefore it is trustworthy for this study") is validated on spliced/concatenative audio, not on the actual generated (looping, glitchy) audio the recognizer is then used to score.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Behavioural measurement" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — Under an adversarial reading, this is the single largest threat to the headline result: if the CTC recognizer's behavior on genuinely generated loop artifacts (blank-collapse, repeated codec frames, glottal glitches at loop boundaries) differs from its behavior on clean concatenative splices, the judge could still be systematically biased in the direction of the studied effect on the actual test items, even though it passes the concatenative audit. The paper's strongest defense against judge bias — the CTC vs. Whisper contrast — is thus not tested on the distribution it is applied to.
   **What would address it** — Report the CTC recognizer's ratio (or the 165-clip by-ear-checked sample's agreement rate) specifically on the sub-population of items with the input's own hallmark generation artifacts (loops, truncations), not only on concatenative ground truth; ideally with a matched non-periodic distortion condition to separate "recognizer fails on any glitchy audio" from "recognizer specifically fails on periodicity."

5. **Issue** — Theorem 1's per-repetition recurrent state map sm+1 = F(sm) is a framework built for architectures with an explicit step-wise state recursion at repetition boundaries, but the paper applies its narrative loosely to explain F5-TTS's failure (a parallel flow-matching duration model with no such repetition-indexed recursive state) via "duration under-allocation," without establishing that a comparable contracting-map structure exists there.
   **Where** — Section 3.2 (F5-TTS duration-supply experiment) and Section 4 (Theorem 1 statement).
   **Why it matters** — The paper is careful elsewhere to say "we withdraw the architectural claim an earlier draft made" once F5-TTS turned out to also fail — commendable transparency — but then reuses the *same* formal contraction vocabulary ("where the count must live") to narrate F5-TTS's failure without any formal or even semi-formal argument that F5-TTS's duration predictor admits an analogous sm+1 = F(sm) recursion. This risks laundering an unformalized, architecture-specific duration-budget story through the language of the (already unmeasured) formal bound, making the paper's evidence base look more unified than it is.
   **What would address it** — Either state plainly that the F5-TTS account is a separate, informal hypothesis unconnected to Theorem 1, or attempt to instantiate an analogous state map for the duration predictor and test its q.

### Minor concerns
- The n = 3 architecture-family generalization claim in the abstract ("75.4 points [71.1, 78.9] by architecture family (n=3)") is stated with more confidence than the body's own Bayesian partial-pooling analysis, which reports that under the widest prior on group-level scale the interval touches zero (−1.6, P = 0.956) — the abstract should flag this sensitivity, not just the headline interval.
- Self-citation [7] to a same-author, same-year (2026) companion arXiv preprint is used to frame this paper as part of a series without independent verification available; fine as a pointer, but readers should not treat it as external corroboration.
- "Exact-match scores 30-of-32 and 3-of-32 alike" under the relative-count-error metric — the paper acknowledges this collapses very different severities into one number but doesn't report a severity-weighted or ordinal alternative anywhere in the main text.
- Table 1's "cap. ratio" column (capacity gain ratio) is introduced without a self-contained definition in the main text; the reader must infer it from the Fig. 1(c) caption and Section 3.3 prose.
- The claim that F5-TTS is "contemporary" (Section 3.2) is doing rhetorical work (implying it isn't disadvantaged by age/training scale relative to the panel) without a citation or date comparison to substantiate it.
- Minor terminology drift: "capacity gain," "effective rank," and "d_eff/d log k" are used interchangeably across Fig. 1(c)'s caption and Section 3.3's text without a single canonical name.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the empirical periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuinely useful controlled result, but the paper's own framing overclaims the formal-verification contribution relative to what it admits is an untested and partially falsified premise, under-engages the TTS-specific duration/alignment literature that bears directly on its central mechanistic question, and leaves its strongest defense against judge bias unvalidated on the actual failure-mode audio.
