# Review (Reviewer alfa)

### Summary
The paper asks whether neural TTS models' well-documented tendency to mis-render repeated phrases is a length effect or a periodicity effect, and separates the two with a "repetition ladder" of 180 stimuli in which a target word repeated k times is paired with a length-matched control where the k copies are replaced by distinct filler words. Scored with a CTC recognizer (chosen after showing Whisper's autoregressive decoder is itself biased against repeated ground truth), six checkpoints across three TTS architecture families render repeated items exactly 18.2% of the time versus 94.3% for their length-matched controls, a gap the authors report survives decoding-parameter sweeps, exclusion-rule ablations, and holds for one of two non-autoregressive baselines (F5-TTS, but not VITS). The paper also states and Lean-4-formalizes a conditional "counting collapse" theorem (a contracting per-repetition state map bounds how many repetitions a Lipschitz readout can distinguish) but explicitly reports that its premise (contraction factor q<1) could not be measured and that a companion dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed, presenting it as an untested conjecture rather than a demonstrated mechanism.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The manipulation confounds "periodicity" with "verbatim token identity repetition"; the experiment as designed cannot distinguish a periodicity-specific limit from a literal-repeated-token limit, so the title's claim is broader than what was tested.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Stimuli" and Section 1 ("A length account predicts the two curves coincide. They do not.").
   **Why it matters** — The control replaces the k repeated tokens with k *distinct* filler words, which changes two things simultaneously relative to the repeated condition: (a) the sequence stops being periodic, and (b) the sequence stops containing an identical token attended to/predicted repeatedly (a condition under which attention keys/values, codec token distributions, or induction-head-like dynamics could collapse regardless of any abstract "periodicity" property). A genuine test of periodicity per se would need a condition that is periodic in structure but not in literal token identity (e.g., an alternating ABAB…AB pattern of two or more distinct words) versus an aperiodic sequence of the same words. Without that arm, "the limit tracks periodicity, not length" is not actually established — what is established is that literal token/word repetition, not length, drives the deficit. These are not the same claim, and the paper's own mechanistic story in Section 4 (spans "whose logits differ by at most δ") already smuggles in verbatim-identity, not abstract periodicity, as the operative variable.
   **What would address it** — Add a periodic-but-non-identical control arm (alternating distinct tokens in a fixed period) and show the deficit still tracks period length rather than literal repetition; alternatively, retitle and rescope the claim to "verbatim repetition," which is what the data support.

2. **Issue** — The CTC judge is validated only on synthetic concatenative ground truth, not on the actual model outputs it is used to score, so its measurement validity for the dependent variable of interest is asserted, not demonstrated.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Behavioural measurement" ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth... ratio 1.00 on the same ground truth").
   **Why it matters** — Concatenative splices lack the prosodic blending, partial-loop artifacts, and codec degeneration that characterize real generative failures (the very phenomenon under study). A CTC recognizer could behave very differently on a model output that trails into a garbled loop than on a clean synthetic splice of the same nominal repeat count. The paper's own falsification target ("what would refute the periodicity claim is a control arm carrying its twin's deficit at matched length, or a checkpoint whose exact-rate gap runs the other way") never considers "the judge systematically undercounts genuinely-rendered repeats in AR models specifically because of codec/prosody artifacts correlated with periodicity" — which is a live alternative explanation given that the paper itself demonstrates one ASR system (Whisper) is differentially biased by exactly this kind of periodicity-correlated artifact. Showing the *replacement* judge is unbiased on ground truth doesn't show it is unbiased on the harder, out-of-distribution failure cases it actually adjudicates.
   **What would address it** — Validate the CTC judge (agreement with careful human transcription) directly on a stratified sample of *actual model generations* spanning both arms and the full k range, not only on concatenative ground truth. The paper already ships a 165-clip human-checkable sample; report a quantitative human-vs-CTC agreement statistic on it rather than only offering it for spot-checking.

3. **Issue** — No statement that exclusion decisions (342 template items, 132 budget-stopped, 19 "degenerate") or the manual "checked by ear" audits were made blind to condition (repeated vs. control) or to the CTC score.
   **Where** — Section 2, exclusion counts; Section 2 "165-clip sample... so a judgement can be checked by ear."
   **Why it matters** — "Degenerate" and template-exclusion calls are exactly the kind of judgment calls that can be swayed by knowing which arm favors the hypothesis, especially when the effect itself is defined by degenerate-sounding output. The multiverse check in S18 (deficit persists with all exclusion rules off, 61.7 pts) mitigates this somewhat but does not establish that the *labeling* of individual items as degenerate/excluded was itself unbiased — a multiverse over rule choice is not the same as blinding the individual coding decisions.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly whether exclusion/degeneracy coding was performed blind to arm label (and ideally blind to model identity), or, if not, note this as a limitation alongside the S18 robustness check.

4. **Issue** — The confirmatory/exploratory split is asserted via "dated against the commit log" for Section 3.3 only; there is no equivalent pre-registration statement for the primary confirmatory comparisons in 3.1–3.2, nor for the 420-specification multiverse itself.
   **Where** — Section 3.3 ("Both analyses here were decided on after seeing the results above; S16 dates every analysis decision against the commit log"); Section 3.1 ("Across 420 specifications spanning every forking choice we made").
   **Why it matters** — Labeling some analyses "confirmatory" implies the primary metrics (median count error, exact-match rate, the specific mixed-effects specification) were fixed before data collection, but no registration timestamp or protocol document is cited for them — only for the post-hoc-flagged exploratory section. A multiverse analysis constructed after seeing which specification produces the reported headline number is a much weaker guarantee than a pre-registered primary analysis plus a multiverse as robustness check; the paper doesn't distinguish these two very different epistemic situations for the reader.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly (with commit-log timestamps, as done for 3.3) that the k≥6 exact-match/median-error comparison and mixed-effects specification in 3.1 were fixed before the ladder was scored, or acknowledge that they were not and temper the "confirmatory" framing accordingly.

5. **Issue** — The "where the count lives" argument (VITS immune, F5-TTS not) rests on n=1 model per duration-modeling strategy, conflating duration-prediction architecture with every other difference between VITS and F5-TTS (training data, parameter count, GAN vs. flow-matching, mel vs. codec representation).
   **Where** — Section 3.2, third and fourth paragraphs; Section 5 ("What plausibly separates the two is where the count must live").
   **Why it matters** — This is the paper's central mechanistic explanation for why "absence of an autoregressive decoder confers no immunity," yet it is drawn from a single contrast pair. The authors themselves partially acknowledge this ("S20 sets out three rivals we cannot exclude") but still present the duration-locus account as "the rival the evidence favours" in the Discussion, which overstates what a two-model, unreplicated comparison can support.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one more non-autoregressive model in each duration-strategy bucket (per-token vs. single-total duration) before asserting the duration-locus explanation is favored; until then, frame it explicitly as a single-pair observation, not a supported account.

6. **Issue** — The formal Lean 4 result is framed in the abstract and title machinery as a substantive contribution ("state and machine-check... a conditional bound"), but its only empirically tested prediction (dose-response between attention flatness and counting accuracy) came out sign-reversed, and the two independent attempts to measure its central premise (q<1) both failed.
   **Where** — Section 4, final paragraph; Abstract.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably transparent that the bound is "an untested conjecture" and that "the empirical result stands independently of it," which is good scientific practice — but the framing still positions the formalization as a headline contribution ("to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact") for a theorem whose only tested empirical consequence failed in the wrong direction. A reader skimming the abstract could reasonably come away crediting the paper with a validated mechanistic account it does not have.
   **What would address it** — Either demote the formal result to an appendix/discussion item explicitly labeled as a negative-leaning exploration (given the sign-reversed dose-response), or, if kept prominent, add a sentence in the abstract itself noting the dose-response test failed, not just that q was unmeasured — "untested" undersells that one prediction *was* tested and did not hold.

### Minor concerns
- Table 1's "cap. ratio" column is not clearly tied back to the Fig. 1(c) metric definition (fitted d(eff.rank)/d(log k)); state explicitly whether Table 1 reports the same quantity or a derived summary.
- The finite-difference probe for q is reported as n=18 pairs with p=0.12 while "failing its own linearity control" — this is a clearly underpowered null result being used to support "we could not measure q," which is fine as stated but borders on treating an underpowered test as informative; consider flagging its power explicitly.
- References [7] and [13] are companion/adjacent works with overlapping authorship and future-dated arXiv IDs (2604.xxxxx, 2605.xxxxx) relative to prior literature cited — worth a brief conflict-of-interest note given [7] is explicitly "a companion" paper by the same authors.
- Section 3.1's "76.7 points less often" phrasing for the checkpoint with a zero median-error gap is easy to misread on first pass; a clarifying clause distinguishing median-error gap from exact-match gap would help.
- The extension-ladder analysis (Section 3.3) runs on only "4 checkpoints admitting utterances long enough" — worth noting this is a non-random subset (likely correlated with model capability/context length), which could bias the reported 3.5-fold saturation-scale ratio.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the core dissociation (periodicity/repetition vs. length) is a genuinely useful and mostly well-controlled empirical contribution, but the periodicity-vs-verbatim-repetition confound, the out-of-distribution judge validation, and the single-pair duration-locus mechanism need to be fixed or explicitly rescoped before the paper's central claims are adequately supported by its design.
