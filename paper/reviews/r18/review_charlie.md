### Summary

The paper makes three claims: (1) autoregressive and some non-autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated words, and this deficit tracks *periodicity* rather than input *length* — established by pairing each repeated stimulus with a length-matched, word-count-matched control whose repeated token is swapped for distinct fillers; (2) the natural instrument for measuring this, Whisper, is disqualified because its autoregressive decoder shares the bias under study, so a CTC recognizer is used instead; (3) a Lean 4-verified theorem shows that *if* a decoder's per-repetition state map is a q-Lipschitz contraction, no Lipschitz readout can separate counts past a computable horizon — a premise the authors say they tried and failed to measure. The evidence is a 180-item stimulus ladder run across six checkpoints (three architecture families) plus two non-AR baselines, with an extensive battery of robustness checks (decoding temperature, repetition-penalty sweep, greedy vs. sampled, exclusion-rule ablation, cycled vs. never-cycled control fillers, a 420-specification curve). The control-vs-repeated dissociation itself is a genuinely well-designed distinguishing experiment and the paper is unusually candid about what it couldn't establish (Section 4's premise, the extension-ladder saturation claim). My steelmanned reading is that this is a well-instrumented, honestly-hedged benchmark paper. Pressed harder, though, the single number that anchors the abstract (94.3% vs. 18.2%, a 75.4-point gap) is quietly the *k ≥ 6* subset of the ladder rather than the full 180-item benchmark, and that threshold is visibly chosen because "below k=6 every model is essentially exact" — a textbook post-hoc breakpoint dressed as the headline result. Section 4 compounds this: the one falsifiable prediction of the formal mechanism came out sign-reversed, and the paper relabels that as "the premise holds only approximately" rather than "the mechanism's prediction failed."

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The abstract's headline statistics (94.3% vs. 18.2% exact-render rate, 75.4-point gap) are not the full-ladder result; they are the *k ≥ 6* subset, and this is never disclosed in the abstract or introduction. `[Narrative — overclaiming / undisclosed conditioning]`
   **Where** — Abstract; Section 3.1 ("Below k = 6 every model is essentially exact; above it..."); Table 1 caption ("Panel summary, k ≥ 6").
   **Why it matters** — A reader who stops at the abstract will treat the 75.4-point gap as characterizing the benchmark as a whole. It characterizes only the regime the paper itself identifies as the one where the effect is largest. That's a materially different claim than "TTS models can't render repeated text," and the gap between the two is exactly the effect size inflation a naive reader would miss.
   **What would address it** — State the k ≥ 6 restriction explicitly in the abstract, and report the unconditioned, full-ladder statistic (k = 1…32) alongside it so the reader can see how much of the effect is concentrated at high k versus present everywhere.

2. **Issue** — The mechanistic story offered for why VITS is immune and F5-TTS is not (per-token vs. single total duration prediction) is one of (by the authors' own count) four candidate explanations, but only this one gets developed in the main text. `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — Section 3.2, closing sentence: "S20 sets out three rivals we cannot exclude."
   **Why it matters** — The prose ("What plausibly separates the two is where the count must live...") reads as an explanation, but the reader has no way to judge how much of the probability mass it should carry relative to the three alternatives that are never named in the paper proper.
   **What would address it** — One sentence per rival hypothesis in-text, or a ranked table, so the duration-budget account isn't the only one visible to a reader who doesn't chase the supplement.

3. **Issue** — The generalization-to-unseen-architecture claim rests on a Bayesian interval that is explicitly reported to include zero under a wider (and not obviously less defensible) prior, but the abstract/summary anchors only on the friendlier construction. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "the posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel is 65.3 points [5.9, 93.3]... under the widest prior on the group-level scales that interval does touch zero (−1.6, P = 0.956)."
   **Why it matters** — With n = 3 architecture families as the clustering unit, the prior on between-family variance is doing real work, and the paper reports both outcomes without designating which is the primary inferential claim. This is exactly the situation where post-hoc selection of "the" confidence interval, from several computed after seeing the data, silently determines the take-home message.
   **What would address it** — Pick and pre-specify (or clearly flag as exploratory) one primary interval construction for the generalization claim; put the full prior-sensitivity range, not just the favorable end, in the abstract-level summary.

4. **Issue** — No alternative explanation is offered for why periodicity itself, rather than any state-space account, might be hard: k-fold verbatim word repetition is vanishingly rare in natural training corpora relative to length-matched sentences with distinct words, so an *out-of-distribution* account (the model has simply never seen "word word word word word word" as training data, independent of any contraction dynamics) is left completely unaddressed. `[Limitations / Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — absent (not discussed anywhere in Sections 2–5).
   **Why it matters** — This alternative predicts exactly the same periodicity-not-length dissociation the paper treats as diagnostic of a state-dynamics story, so the control experiment (Fig. 1a) doesn't distinguish it from the state-contraction/output-policy debate that occupies Section 4–5. If the effect is a training-distribution artifact, the Lean-formalized mechanism in Section 4 is answering a question the data doesn't actually motivate.
   **What would address it** — At minimum, name this as a live rival in the Limitations section (it currently lists panel size and language coverage, but not this); ideally, a stimulus arm using synthetically common repeated patterns (e.g., "ha ha ha," which is frequent in training data) vs. rare ones at matched k would separate frequency from periodicity.

5. **Issue** — The k ≥ 6 cutoff that generates every headline number is a post-hoc breakpoint chosen because that's where the curves visibly separate, and Section 4's one falsifiable auxiliary prediction (flatter attention → worse counting) came out sign-reversed, yet the paper reframes this as an "untested conjecture" with a premise that "holds only approximately," rather than reporting it as a failed prediction. `[Post-hoc-shaped hypothesis]`
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("Below k = 6 every model is essentially exact; above it..." — this line is the entire justification for the threshold that defines Table 1 and the abstract's headline gap); Section 4, dose-response result: "flatter attention goes with better counting (r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]...)" against a mechanism that predicts the opposite direction.
   **Why it matters** — Two things are happening at once. First, a threshold selected after seeing where the effect is cleanest becomes the population that every top-line statistic (18.2%/94.3%, 75.4 points, Table 1) is computed over, with no independent justification for k = 6 offered anywhere (the 420-specification robustness check in S11 does vary "k threshold," which shows the authors know this is a forking-path choice, but the sensitivity to it is buried in supplement rather than reflected in the primary reported number). Second, the one place the paper actually pre-specifies a directional prediction from its formal apparatus — Lemma 1's dose-response — gets it backwards, and the write-up (correctly, to its credit) reports the number but then launders the interpretation into "untested" language that lets the Lean-verified theorem keep top billing in the abstract ("we also state and machine-check in Lean 4 a conditional bound") even though the one test the paper ran of its qualitative prediction disconfirmed it.
   **What would address it** — Report the full-ladder (unconditioned on k) statistic as the primary headline number, with k ≥ 6 clearly labeled as a secondary, threshold-sensitive view; and change the framing of the sign-reversed dose-response from "untested premise" to what it is — evidence against the contraction account's qualitative prediction, alongside the output-policy account which the paper's own Section 5 discussion (correctly) treats as the better-supported rival.

### Minor concerns

- Section 3.1: "Across 420 specifications... and 0 reverse its sign" is a grammatical slip ("0 reverse" → "none reversed" or similar); worth fixing since it's describing a number readers will want to parse precisely.
- Fig. 1(c) axis label "d_eff/dlog k" is opaque without the caption pointing to S8 for what "effective rank" and "capacity gain" mean operationally; a one-clause in-caption definition would make the panel self-contained.
- The Whisper large-v3 audit doesn't state decoding configuration (temperature, beam width, whether the built-in repetition/no-repeat heuristics were active) — since the whole point is that Whisper's own decoding dynamics bias it, the exact decoding settings used in the audit matter and aren't given.
- "Qwen3-TTS-1.7B inverts the ratio" (Section 3.3) is mentioned once and dropped; given it's the one panel checkpoint that breaks the pattern being discussed, it deserves at least a sentence on why.
- Two of the paper's own citations ([7], the companion Whisper-hallucination paper, and [13], Venkatesh) are 2026 arXiv preprints from the same few months as this submission — i.e., unreviewed, very fresh work that the Discussion section leans on for its interpretive conclusion ("the output-policy account is the better supported"). Not a fatal issue, but worth flagging that a load-bearing part of the interpretation rests on unvetted contemporaneous preprints.
- No hardware/compute or determinism statement: "Audio and activations regenerate from the released seeds" is asserted but the paper doesn't say whether bit-exact reproduction was verified across GPU types, which matters for anyone trying to exactly reproduce Table 1.
- Table 1's "cap. ratio" column and its relation to the "0.46 median" figure quoted in Section 3.3 aren't reconciled at a glance — the six per-model ratios in the table (0.21–0.80) bracket 0.46 but the text's "median factor 0.46" isn't visibly one of the six numbers, so a reader has to compute the median themselves to check it.

### Belief update

Before reading, my prior was roughly "TTS repetition failures are a length/attention-span artifact, same as long-document degradation elsewhere." The matched-control design (Fig. 1a) is a genuinely good instrument and shifts me toward "periodicity is doing something length isn't" — that's a real, specific update, and it's the paper's strongest asset. But the update is smaller than the abstract wants me to make: once I noticed the headline gap is a k ≥ 6-conditioned statistic rather than an unconditioned one, and that the formal mechanism's only directional test came back reversed and got relabeled rather than reported as disconfirming, I trust the *size* of the effect less than the *existence* of the dissociation. Net: I now believe periodicity-specific miscounting in TTS is real and worth a benchmark; I don't yet believe the specific 75.4-point / 18.2%-vs-94.3% numbers are the right ones to quote, and I don't update at all toward the state-contraction mechanism in Section 4, which the paper's own data argues against.

### Verdict

**Minor revision** — the core dissociation experiment is sound and the paper is unusually forthright about its own limitations, but the abstract's headline numbers are silently conditioned on a post-hoc k ≥ 6 threshold and Section 4 keeps a Lean-verified theorem in the spotlight despite the one prediction test it ran coming back sign-reversed; both need to be fixed in the reporting, not the underlying experiment.
