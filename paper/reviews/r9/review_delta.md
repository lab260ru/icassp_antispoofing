### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' tendency to miscount long runs of a repeated phrase has a general, mechanistic explanation rather than being an artifact of exposure bias or attention span. It models one repetition boundary as an autonomous state map F and proves (with a Lean 4/mathlib formalization) that if F is a q-contraction, no Lipschitz readout of the decoder's state — including the stop-token logit — can separate repetition counts beyond a computable horizon N*. Using a CTC recognizer as judge (chosen because Whisper's own autoregressive decoder is shown to be biased against periodic material), the authors test six checkpoints across three TTS families on a repetition ladder (k up to 32, later extended post hoc to 128) against length-matched non-repeating controls, plus a non-autoregressive baseline (VITS). The headline result is a periodicity-specific undercounting deficit (median error ≈ −8.3% at k≥6, exact-match 18.2% vs. 94.3% for controls) that is absent in the non-AR model, together with a saturating count curve at very high k whose fitted scale is 3.5× smaller for repeated text than for controls. The paper is unusually candid that the theorem's premise (contraction) is not established — two attempts to measure the contraction factor q failed, and a dose-response test of the companion attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed — so what is empirically shown is a periodicity-specific deficit of roughly the predicted shape, not a validated instance of the proven bound.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's own tests of the causal mechanism linking Lemma 1 (attention dilution) to Theorem 1 (contraction-driven collapse) either fail or point in the wrong direction, so the framing that the empirical deficit *is* an instance of counting collapse is not supported by the paper's own evidence, only by a superficial resemblance.
   **Where** — Section 4.4 ("its dose-response prediction fails outright: … flatter attention goes with better counting (r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]) … We record that as disconfirming"); Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed"); Section 5's linear-probe test against the rival "count survives in the state, only the policy fails" account ([12]), which the authors themselves conclude "discriminates nothing."
   **Why it matters** — The abstract and title lead with the theorem and its Lean proof as the paper's central offering, but every experiment designed to confirm the specific mechanism (dilution → approximate autonomy → contraction → loss of separability) either failed to detect the premise or produced the opposite sign. What remains well-supported is a phenomenological regularity (periodicity, not length, predicts undercounting) that is compatible with several other mechanisms the paper does not rule out — including the rival hypothesis in [12] that the count survives in the representation and only the readout policy fails, which the paper's own probe cannot distinguish from its account. Presenting a rigorously proved implication alongside an unconfirmed (and partly contradicted) premise as if together they explain the data risks readers crediting the theorem with more explanatory power than the paper itself claims to have earned.
   **What would address it** — Either (a) find an operationalization of q or the dilution dose-response that actually predicts per-item failure, or (b) reframe the abstract/title to state plainly that the theorem is offered as a candidate explanation whose distinguishing predictions were tested and did not confirm it, rather than implying (via juxtaposition) that the Lean-verified bound explains the measured deficit.

2. **Issue** — The k=128 "horizon" is a post-hoc extension, motivated by failing to find the predicted saturation in the pre-specified k≤32 ladder, and the fitted saturation curve is a generic exponential, not a validated instance of the theorem's N* (which requires q, L, C, μ — none measured).
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc…"; "ˆK a saturation scale read off counts, not the theorem's N∗, a function of the unmeasured q"); Section 5 ("Extending to k = 128 found saturation of the predicted kind but not one derived from a measured q").
   **Why it matters** — Choosing a new measurement range after the original range fails to show the predicted effect, then reporting a curve of "the predicted shape" at the new range as evidence, is a form of range-selection that inflates confidence in a preregistration-free setting. The paper is transparent about this in Section 5's hedged language, but the abstract's "finds one" and the Results section's confident framing understate how thin this evidence is: only 4 of 6 checkpoints admit the longer utterances, and one of those four (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows no saturation and an inverted ratio — i.e., the "3.5-fold" headline number is carried by 3 checkpoints, not a validated general property.
   **What would address it** — Report the extension as clearly exploratory/hypothesis-generating (which the Discussion already half-does) and pre-register a follow-up ladder at a range chosen independently of this paper's own null result, ideally with enough checkpoints that a null result in 1/4 doesn't have to be explained away post hoc.

3. **Issue** — The experiment meant to rule out the strongest rival explanation the title stakes everything on ("perhaps any synthesiser mishandles long repeated text") rests on a single non-autoregressive model (VITS, a 2021 architecture), and that one model's own result is not fully consistent with the paper's narrative.
   **Where** — Section 4.3 ("The last rival is architectural, the one the title stakes… The same ladder through VITS… shows no dissociation: 65.6% of repeated items exact against 58.9% of controls, a gap of −6.7 points").
   **Why it matters** — A gap of −6.7 (repeated items scoring *better* than controls) is qualitatively different from "no dissociation" as stated — it is a reversed, smaller-magnitude dissociation, and the paper offers no account of why VITS's controls underperform its repeated items in the first place. Generalizing "architecture, not length" — a claim broad enough to anchor the paper's title — from n=1 non-AR model, with an unexplained sign flip in the tested direction, is weak support for what is presented as the definitive architectural control.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one more architecturally distinct non-AR system (e.g., a diffusion- or flow-matching-based TTS model) and explain the VITS repeated>control asymmetry, or soften the architectural-generality claim to match the n=1 evidentiary base.

4. **Issue** — The panel is explicitly underpowered for formal inference (n=6 checkpoints, signed-rank floor 0.031, no multiplicity correction), and the analysis contains numerous post-hoc branch points (item exclusions, a second control regeneration after discovering a confound, which 4/6 checkpoints get the extension ladder, a separate penalty-sweep ablation) that are individually disclosed but collectively constitute a large garden of forking paths for a 6-checkpoint study.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("the panel is too small for significance testing… so we rest on effect sizes and their consistency," S11); Section 4.3 (control regeneration with a 146-word pool after finding the cycled-pool confound worth 13.4 points).
   **Why it matters** — Effect-size consistency across a handful of correlated observations (checkpoints from only 3 families, sharing tokenizers/backbones within family) is a much weaker form of evidence than the paper's confident prose ("in every checkpoint and every family") suggests, especially once one checkpoint (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) is conceded to show a null median-error effect at k≤32. Readers should be told explicitly which comparisons were pre-specified versus discovered during analysis.
   **What would address it** — A pre-registered analysis plan (even informal, in supplementary material) distinguishing confirmatory from exploratory comparisons, and, ideally, additional checkpoints/families (e.g., other Llama-codec or diffusion-based TTS systems) to raise n beyond 6.

5. **Issue** — The CTC judge's validity is asserted via calibration on synthetic concatenative audio but its absolute error rate on genuine model output, and hence the noise floor around the reported ~8–16% count-error effect sizes, is never quantified.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Worse in absolute WER, right for this purpose… ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's central quantitative finding is a double-digit percentage undercount, not a qualitative failure (e.g., complete stalling). Without a quantified judge error rate on real (non-concatenative) model audio, it is not possible to assess how much of the reported -8% to -16% deficit is genuine model behavior versus recognizer noise, particularly since concatenative audio (spliced from separate renders) may differ acoustically (boundary discontinuities, prosodic discontinuity) from naturally generated repeated speech in ways that could differentially help or hurt a CTC recognizer.
   **What would address it** — Report the CTC judge's absolute WER/count accuracy on a held-out sample of genuinely model-generated (non-spliced) speech with independently verified ground truth, and propagate that uncertainty into the count-error confidence intervals.

6. **Issue** — Multiple statistics central to evaluating the above concerns (confound decomposition, dose-response correlation, significance testing, exclusion criteria, the boundary-distance estimator's failure mode) are relegated to supplementary sections (S4, S5, S8, S10, S11, S12) not included in the reviewed text.
   **Where** — Throughout Sections 3–5, via repeated "(S_)" pointers.
   **Why it matters** — Several of the concerns above (e.g., the 0.46 vs. ~0.49 discrepancy between the text's aggregate capacity-gain ratio and the mean of Table 1's per-checkpoint ratios) cannot be resolved without the supplement, meaning the main text's headline claims are not fully self-verifiable from the manuscript body — a problem for a paper whose central contribution is methodological rigor and formal verification.
   **What would address it** — For camera-ready, ensure the supplement is bundled with the submission for reviewers and that any statistic quoted in the main text (aggregation method for pooled ratios, exact test used for the dose-response correlation, etc.) states its computation inline or in an equation, not only by pointer.

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is cited for its alignment analysis despite being flagged as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a sentence on why the withdrawal doesn't undercut the specific analysis being cited.
- Table 1's column grouping is not self-explanatory from the caption alone; a reader must infer that the "18.2 / 56.7" style columns are capacity-gain figures rather than exact-match rates (which, confusingly, coincide numerically for Llasa-1B with the abstract's headline 18.2% exact-match figure purely by coincidence). Add explicit sub-headers.
- The abstract's "-8.3%" is the item-pooled statistic while the text also reports a "-12.5% per checkpoint" median; the abstract doesn't flag that a second, larger number exists for the same quantity under a different aggregation, which invites cherry-picking concerns even though both are reported in the body.
- The aggregate capacity-gain ratio "a factor 0.46" (Section 4.4) doesn't match the mean of Table 1's six per-checkpoint ratio values (~0.49); the aggregation method (mean of ratios vs. ratio of means) is unstated.
- "Effective rank" (Neff) used for the capacity-gain measurement is not defined in the main text (no threshold or estimator specified).
- VITS's repeated-item score exceeding its control score (65.6% vs 58.9%) is left unexplained and slightly undercuts the "no dissociation" characterization used to describe it.
- The number "666,666"-style example in the introduction is stylistically colorful but adds nothing evidential; not an issue, just a note that the paper leans on vivid framing in places where the empirical language is otherwise very precise.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the empirical periodicity-vs-length dissociation is well-evidenced and the formal verification is a genuine (if narrow) contribution, but the paper's own mechanism-testing experiments (dose-response, contraction measurement, the state-vs-policy probe) fail or contradict the theorem's premise, the k=128 "horizon" is a post-hoc-range finding not tied to a measured q, and the architectural control central to the title's claim rests on a single, imperfectly-behaved non-AR model — the headline framing needs to be brought into line with what the paper's own honest Discussion section already concedes.
