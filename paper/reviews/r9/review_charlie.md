### Summary

This paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-documented failure to count repeated words or phrases has a single mechanistic explanation, and answers with a two-part argument: (1) a Lean 4/mathlib-verified theorem showing that if per-repetition decoder dynamics contract (Assumption 2, unverified for real transformers) then no Lipschitz readout can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N*, built on an attention-dilution lemma; and (2) an empirical repetition-ladder benchmark across six checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) scored with a CTC recognizer chosen specifically because the natural judge (Whisper) is shown to share the bias under study. The headline finding is a periodicity-specific counting deficit — median count error −8.3% pooled at k≥6, 18.2% exact-match on repeated items versus 94.3% on length-matched non-repeated controls, absent in a non-autoregressive (VITS) baseline — with a proportional (not saturating) deficit up to k=32 that only becomes horizon-shaped in a post-hoc extension to k=128 on 4 of 6 checkpoints. The paper is unusually candid that the theorem's premise (contraction) was never directly confirmed, and that a direct dose-response test of the dilution lemma came out sign-reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The manuscript's quantitative claims depend on statistical and methodological detail (test procedures, item/template construction, probe protocols, bootstrap resampling units) housed in appendices (S4, S5, S8, S10, S11, S12) that are cited pervasively but not included in the reviewable text, and the underlying data (2.5 GB audio, 25 GB activations) are "available on request" rather than deposited at a permanent, citable location.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Code, data, settings"); recurring references to S4/S5/S8/S10/S11/S12 throughout Sections 4–5.
   **Why it matters** — Nearly every load-bearing number in the paper — the RSS fit favoring linearity over saturation in §4.1, the ˆK saturation fit and its 3.5-fold claim in §4.2, the r=0.59 dose-response correlation in §4.4, the bootstrap CIs in Fig. 1(c), the boundary-distance and perturbation-probe procedures in §5 — is asserted but not independently checkable from what is provided. "Available on request" is a well-documented failure mode for long-term data availability; without it, the empirical contribution reduces to trust in numbers the reader cannot recompute.
   **What would address it** — Submit the statistical/methods appendix as part of the paper (even if condensed), deposit audio and activations at a permanent archive with a DOI (e.g., Zenodo, HF datasets) rather than "on request," and pin exact checkpoint revisions/commit hashes and library versions alongside the release so "regenerable from released seeds" is a verifiable claim rather than an assertion.

2. **Issue** — The paper's own results actively disconfirm the mechanistic chain (attention dilution → contraction → collapse) that motivates the theorem, yet the theorem remains the paper's headline framing.
   **Where** — Section 5: "two attempts to measure [the contraction factor] q failed"; "a dose-response test of the dilution lemma came out sign-reversed" — flatter attention (more dilution) correlates with *better* counting (r=0.59 [0.35, 0.75]), the opposite of Lemma 1's implied direction. Section 4.1 additionally shows the observed deficit is proportional, not saturating, up to k=32 (RSS 485.3 for a constant fit vs. 2.6 for ˆc=0.950k), which is "not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts."
   **Why it matters** — Assumption 2 (contraction) is never established, and the one directly testable prediction of the mechanism (dilution dose-response) fails in the wrong direction, not merely non-significantly. What survives is a real, well-replicated phenomenological deficit tied to periodicity, wrapped in formal machinery whose premises the paper cannot confirm and whose most direct empirical test contradicts it. A reader relying on the title ("A Lean-Verified Bound") could reasonably conclude the mechanism is established when only the implication is.
   **What would address it** — Decouple the claims explicitly: state upfront that the theorem is a conditional (if-contraction-then-horizon) result whose antecedent is untested and whose one falsifiable corollary (dilution dose-response) failed, and discuss candidate alternative readings of the sign-reversed correlation (e.g., that attention flatness may track output diversity or model confidence rather than state contraction) rather than reporting it only as "disconfirming" without further diagnosis.

3. **Issue** — The k=128 extension that finally produces horizon-shaped behavior is explicitly post hoc, restricted to 4 of 6 checkpoints chosen for practical reasons (utterance-length ceilings), and fit with a saturation functional form selected only after the pre-specified k≤32 ladder failed to show saturation.
   **Where** — Section 4.2.
   **Why it matters** — Selecting the extension range, checkpoint subset, and curve family after observing that the original ladder falsified the theorem's naive prediction creates researcher-degrees-of-freedom risk: the reported "3.5-fold" horizon reduction (ˆK_rep=30 [24,46] vs ˆK_ctl=105 [94,114]) could partly reflect the chosen functional form rather than a pre-specified confirmatory test. This is compounded by the acknowledged censoring (XTTS-v2 dropped entirely; 20 budget-truncated and 10 degenerate generations excluded "conservatively" from the remainder).
   **What would address it** — Either badge the k=128 results explicitly as exploratory and excluded from the headline abstract claims, or report sensitivity of ˆK to alternative saturating functional forms, and model XTTS-v2's censored k≥48 data via a survival/censoring approach instead of excluding it outright.

4. **Issue** — With only 6 checkpoints as the true unit of replication (the paper itself notes "the panel is too small for significance testing" and that no multiplicity correction clears 0.05), several confidence intervals are nonetheless computed by bootstrapping over stimulus templates rather than over checkpoints, which estimates the wrong source of uncertainty for the paper's cross-model generalization claim.
   **Where** — Figure 1(c) caption ("95% bootstrap intervals over stimulus templates"); Section 4.2's signed-rank statistics (n=6).
   **Why it matters** — The paper's strongest claim is that the effect holds "in every checkpoint and every family" — a generalization claim over models. Bootstrapping over templates within a fixed set of 6 checkpoints characterizes stimulus-sampling noise, not whether a 7th checkpoint would show the effect; conflating the two overstates the precision behind the cross-model generalization the abstract advertises.
   **What would address it** — Report which resampling unit (item, template, checkpoint) backs each interval, and add checkpoint-level (leave-one-checkpoint-out) variability estimates specifically for claims framed as holding "across checkpoints/families."

5. **Issue** — The CTC judge's own accuracy is reported only qualitatively ("worse in absolute WER, right for this purpose"), and the concatenative-audio calibration audit (which underwrites both the Whisper-bias claim and the CTC judge's validity) is not described with enough detail — sample size, construction method, ground-truth verification — to reproduce.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong."
   **Why it matters** — Every count-error statistic in the paper is downstream of this judge. wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self's CTC blank-collapse behavior is invoked elsewhere in the paper as a real, non-negligible phenomenon (used to argue the VITS comparison rules out a recognizer artifact), so its potential residual bias on genuinely repetitive/monotonous acoustic input is a live concern that absolute WER numbers and audit sample sizes would let a reader assess directly rather than take on faith.
   **What would address it** — Report absolute WER for the CTC judge on both periodic and non-periodic ground truth, the number/construction of concatenative audit stimuli, and ideally a second, architecturally distinct ASR system as a cross-check on the headline count-error numbers.

### Minor concerns
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio 0.46" appears to be the ratio of per-checkpoint *means* (mean(rep)/mean(ctl) ≈ 0.455) rather than the mean of the six individual per-checkpoint ratios listed in the same table (which average ≈0.49) — worth stating the aggregation method explicitly.
- Section 4.2's generation accounting ("Of 240 generations 20 hit our budget and 10 were degenerate, leaving 21–26 per cell") isn't reconstructable from the stated design (4 checkpoints × 4 k-values × 2 conditions × 3 seeds = 96, not 240); a per-cell table would resolve the ambiguity.
- The released repository is named `icassp_antispoofing`, which doesn't match the paper's subject (TTS counting collapse) — likely a shared/repurposed repo, but this will confuse readers trying to locate the described artifact.
- Reference [1] is annotated "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" yet is cited for its alignment analysis without comment on whether the withdrawal bears on the cited claim.
- Several citations ([7] the companion paper, [12], [16]) carry very recent 2026 arXiv IDs that could not be corroborated via arXiv search in this review; given [12] is discussed at length in Section 5 as the paper's main rival account, its status (peer-reviewed vs. preprint) is worth noting for readers.
- Eq. (1) introduces N* as the theorem's central quantity, but it is never numerically estimated anywhere in the paper (q is unmeasured); flagging this limitation adjacent to Eq. (1), not only in the Discussion, would help readers calibrate expectations earlier.
- "Rendering very sixteen times" in Section 4.4 reads as a typo.
- Table 1's caption states capacity gain "is measured at deep layers," but Section 3's "State measurement" paragraph doesn't specify which layers or how "deep" is operationalized.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the empirical periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuine and carefully controlled finding, but the paper cannot currently be verified on its own terms (key statistical procedures and large data assets are unreviewable/undeposited) and its formal-theorem framing outruns evidence that, by the authors' own report, actively fails the theorem's one direct empirical test.
