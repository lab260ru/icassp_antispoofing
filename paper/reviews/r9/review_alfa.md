### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech decoders miscount how many times they render a repeated phrase, and proposes that the cause is dynamical rather than merely a training or decoding artifact: if softmax attention over $k$ near-identical spans dilutes to $\Theta(1/k)$ (Lemma 1) and the resulting per-repetition state map contracts with factor $q<1$ (Assumption 2), then no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can recover the repetition count past a computable horizon $N^*$ (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, across six AR checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) scored with a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the autoregressive bias of Whisper, repeated-phrase items are undercounted (median error −8.3% pooled, exact in 18.2% of generations) while length-matched non-repetitive controls are essentially exact (94.3%), and a non-AR baseline (VITS) shows no such dissociation. Within $k\le32$ the deficit is proportional rather than saturating (contra the theorem's prediction of a constant horizon); a post-hoc extension to $k=128$ on 4/6 checkpoints finds saturation with a 3.5-fold lower fitted scale for repeated vs. control text, though one checkpoint shows no such effect. The authors report that they could not confirm the theorem's premise: two independent attempts to measure the contraction factor $q$ failed, and the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's own linear-probe test contradicts the specific mechanism Theorem 1(iv) proposes as the explanation for the observed deficit, but this is downgraded to "discriminates nothing" rather than treated as disconfirming evidence.
   **Where** — Section 5, paragraph beginning "What is proved, and what the measurements do not show," discussing the linear probe (median $R^2$ 0.48, "retention exceeds one in 2 of them").
   **Why it matters** — Theorem 1(iv) is explicit and general: *"every $L$-Lipschitz readout $g$"* satisfies the margin bound, so past $N^*$ *no* Lipschitz function of the state — a linear probe is exactly such a function — should recover the count. The paper's own probe recovers a substantial and sometimes above-baseline fraction of count information at exactly the layers/checkpoints where output-level counting has already failed. That is direct evidence that whatever collapse is occurring in the observed regime is not the state-level informational collapse Theorem 1 describes, since the state manifestly still supports linear count decoding. The rival account the paper cites — Venkatesh [12], found via search at arXiv:2605.09239, reports that linear probes on the residual stream decode the correct count "with near-perfect accuracy at every post-embedding layer... even at the exact layers where the wrong answer crystallizes in the output" — is a considerably stronger and more specific version of the same pattern the paper's own probe reproduces at a weaker but qualitatively similar level (0.48 $R^2$, not near-zero). Calling this "real but non-discriminating" understates how much it favors the output-policy account over the state-contraction account for the tested checkpoints and horizon.
   **What would address it** — Report the probe-based decodability explicitly against the theorem's prediction (a Lipschitz readout should be *unable* to separate counts past $N^*$); if decodability stays well above chance at the horizon where output miscounting is already large, say plainly that Theorem 1's mechanism does not appear to be what's operating in the tested regime, rather than filing it as a wash. The abstract and title should not imply the theorem explains the measured deficit when the paper's strongest direct test of the mechanism points the other way.

2. **Issue** — Assumption 2 (contraction, the theorem's load-bearing empirical premise) is never established: both measurement attempts failed, so the Lean-verified theorem is a conditional whose antecedent is unconfirmed for any real decoder.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("two attempts to measure $q$ failed... a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items (7 of 18 pairs, $p=0.12$)"); Section 3 ("Section 5 says why we do not measure $q$ directly").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's rhetorical structure — title, abstract, and Section 2's "Formal verification" paragraph — repeatedly foregrounds the Lean proof as grounding for the empirical finding. But a machine-checked proof of "contraction $\Rightarrow$ horizon" says nothing about whether real decoders contract; by the authors' own account, the one attempt with an actual dose-response test (Lemma 1) came back *sign-reversed* (flatter attention associates with *better* counting, $r=0.59$ [0.35, 0.75]), which is evidence against, not for, the attention-dilution route into the contraction story. As it stands the formal verification certifies pure mathematics that is not shown to describe the system under study, and readers of the abstract alone would likely come away believing more has been demonstrated about actual TTS decoders than has been.
   **What would address it** — Either produce a working (even noisy) estimator of $q$ and report the resulting $N^*$ against the observed saturation scale as a genuine quantitative test, or restructure the abstract/title so the formal result is clearly flagged as unconfirmed-premise throughout, not just in the closing sentence.

3. **Issue** — The k=128 "horizon" is a post-hoc, exploratory follow-up run specifically because the pre-registered ladder ($k\le32$) failed to show the theorem's predicted saturation, and one of the four checkpoints tested is a direct counterexample.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc..."); "Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation within $k\le128$ and inverts the ratio."
   **Why it matters** — This is close to a textbook HARKing pattern: the theory failed its first test (Section 4.1: "not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts"), the ladder was extended until a pattern compatible with the theory appeared, and the abstract then reports "a horizon it does not yet explain" but still leads with "the count stays proportional... so no horizon is visible there; a post-hoc extension... finds one" — phrasing that reads as confirmatory despite the exploratory origin and despite one of four tested checkpoints outright contradicting the pattern. The fitted saturation curve $\hat c(k)=\hat K(1-e^{-k/\hat K})$ is also explicitly *not* derived from the theorem (the paper admits $\hat K$ is "not the theorem's $N^*$, a function of the unmeasured $q$"), so even where the curve fits, it is a phenomenological description, not a test of Theorem 1's specific functional form.
   **What would address it** — Treat the $k=128$ extension as a separate, clearly-labeled exploratory study with its own (ideally pre-registered) replication on fresh checkpoints/items; report the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B counterexample in the abstract, not only in Section 4.2's body text; avoid describing the fitted $\hat K$ ratio as evidence for the theorem's horizon when the paper itself says it isn't.

4. **Issue** — All headline percentages are pooled over only 6 checkpoints from 3 families, one of which already deviates from the claimed dissociation pattern at the k=128 horizon, and the paper concedes the panel is too small for significance testing.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("the panel is too small for significance testing... no multiplicity correction clears 0.05"); abstract ("median count error of −8.3%... exact in 18.2%... against 94.3%").
   **Why it matters** — The abstract states these numbers as if they are stable population-level facts about "autoregressive text-to-speech," but the true unit of replication (as the paper itself argues) is the checkpoint, $n=6$, and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B already shows zero median error at $k\ge6$ and an inverted saturation ratio at $k=128$. A reader relying on the abstract alone would not learn that 1 of 6 tested models is a partial counterexample to the paper's own sharpest claim.
   **What would address it** — Report checkpoint-level ranges alongside pooled point estimates in the abstract (as is already done in the body, e.g. "−12.5% per checkpoint... or −8.3% pooling"), and qualify the title/abstract's scope to the specific families and scale range actually tested.

5. **Issue** — Key quantitative claims depend on supplementary sections (S4, S5, S8, S10, S11, S12) that are not included in the reviewed text, so several load-bearing numbers cannot be checked.
   **Where** — Throughout Section 4 (e.g., residual sum of squares 485.3 vs. 2.6 for the linear-vs-constant fit, S10; signed-rank floor 0.031, S11; correlation CI for the dose-response test, S12; audit details, S4/S5/S8).
   **Why it matters** — For a paper whose central move is "we show the naive read is wrong, here is the more careful statistic," the careful statistics themselves are largely off-page. This is compounded by the fact that the paper's most consequential self-critical results (contraction-measurement failures, dose-response sign reversal) live in the same unseen supplement sections as the numbers used to support the headline claims, making independent verification of both the confirmatory and disconfirmatory evidence impossible from the main text alone.
   **What would address it** — Given ICASSP page limits, at minimum surface the specific supplementary numbers most load-bearing for the headline claims (RSS fit comparison, dose-response CI, contraction-probe details) in the main text or a clearly linked appendix; the repository is provided, which helps, but the review should be conducted against material that is actually inspectable.

6. **Issue** — The audit establishing the CTC judge's validity does not report the size or composition of the concatenative ground-truth set used to compute the audit ratios.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong" (ratios 0.27–0.65 for Whisper, 1.00 for CTC; "Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth").
   **Why it matters** — The entire measurement pipeline for every headline number rests on this audit's finding that the CTC judge tracks ground truth (1.00) where Whisper does not (0.19–0.65). Without knowing how many items, speakers, or phrase lengths the audit covered, it is impossible to judge whether "1.00" is a robust ratio or an artifact of a small or narrow audit set.
   **What would address it** — Report $n$, speaker/voice diversity, and $k$-range for the audit set, and ideally extend the never-cycled control pool (already used for the k=12…32 confound check in Section 4.3) to the audit itself.

### Minor concerns

- Section 4.4: "Rendering very sixteen times does produce monotonous audio" is garbled/ungrammatical and should be corrected (likely "rendering it sixteen times" or similar).
- Figure 1 axis labels as rendered in the extracted text ("22 25" / "26 27" for panels a/b) are illegible; verify these survive typesetting as intended log-scale tick marks.
- Ref [1] is cited as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" without stating the withdrawal reason; citing a withdrawn preprint for its alignment analysis is defensible but should note why the withdrawal doesn't undermine the cited claim.
- The repetition-penalty ablation (Section 4.3, third rival) is run only on XTTS-v2; the claim that Llasa "pass[es] no penalty at all yet show[s] the effect at full size" is an existence proof, not a systematic test of the penalty confound across families — a within-family penalty sweep on at least one more family would strengthen this.
- Companion paper [7] (arXiv:2604.08591, 2026) is a same-author, apparently very recent preprint used to motivate the framing ("attractor regime descriptively"); readers cannot yet independently check that characterization.
- The paper's own falsifiability statement (Section 5, "a control with its twin's deficit; a model whose capacity gain matches its control; or an AR decoder behaving like the non-AR baseline") is good practice, but by the paper's own numbers Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is arguably already close to the second condition (capacity-gain ratio 0.80 in Table 1, the highest of the six, and zero median error) — this near-miss deserves explicit discussion as a stress case rather than being folded into aggregate statistics.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuinely well-demonstrated empirical effect, but the paper's central theoretical apparatus (contraction, Theorem 1, and the Lean verification built on it) is not shown to be the operative mechanism — its premise measurement failed twice, its companion lemma's dose-response test is sign-reversed, and the paper's own Lipschitz-readout (linear probe) test is in tension with Theorem 1(iv)'s claim that no such readout can recover the count past the horizon — so the claim-to-evidence linkage needs to be substantially reframed before the formal result can be presented as explanatory rather than merely consistent-in-shape.
