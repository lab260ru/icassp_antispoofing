### Summary

The paper makes three linked claims: (1) a Lean-verified theorem that if a decoder's per-repetition state map contracts, no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon; (2) an empirical claim that six AR-TTS checkpoints across three families undercount repeated phrases in a way that tracks *periodicity* rather than *length* (94.3% exact-match on length-matched distinct-word controls vs. 18.2% on their repeated twins), and that this vanishes in a non-autoregressive baseline (VITS); and (3) an unusually candid admission that the theorem's premise — contraction — was never directly confirmed: two measurement attempts failed, and a dose-response test of the companion dilution lemma came out sign-reversed. Steelmanned, this is a paper that proves a conditional implication cleanly, designs the single sharpest test available for its stated rival (length vs. periodicity), and refuses to oversell the mechanism once the data didn't cooperate — that intellectual honesty is real and above the field average. Pressed harder, though, the paper's most totalizing claim — that this is an *architectural*, autoregressive-specific phenomenon rather than a general capacity or scale artifact — rests on a single, five-year-old, comparatively tiny non-AR baseline (VITS) standing in for "any synthesiser." That's the load-bearing control for the title's claim, and it's the weakest-supported part of the paper.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's "closest prior result" section leans on a same-author companion preprint and a same-year, unreviewed preprint as its two most load-bearing citations, thinning the independent-prior-art base. `[Novelty]`
   **Where** — §1 ("companion to a spectral study of hallucination in encoder–decoder ASR [7]") and §5 ("One test could have separated our account from the rival... [12]").
   **Why it matters** — Ref [7] (arXiv 2604.08591) is by the same three authors and is used to justify that an "attractor regime" was already identified — i.e., the paper cites itself as prior context establishing the phenomenon it now formalises. Ref [12] (confirmed via search: Venkatesh, arXiv 2605.09239, "Repeated-Token Counting Reveals a Dissociation Between Representations and Outputs") is a single-author preprint from the same month-range, about LLM counting, not TTS. Neither is a problem in isolation, but together they mean two of the paper's four "closest prior work" slots are not independently-vetted external validation — a reader shouldn't come away thinking this literature is more settled than it is.
   **What would address it** — Either find independent (peer-reviewed or older, more-cited) work making the same "attractor" observation, or state plainly that [7] is concurrent self-work rather than settled context.

2. **Issue** — The k=128 extension, which is the only place the theorem's *distinctive* prediction (a saturating horizon) is actually observed, is built on a post-hoc-selected sub-ladder with n=21–26 per cell, no significance testing, and one of four checkpoints inverting the direction. `[Evidence — statistical rigor / distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — §4.2.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is explicit that this ladder was constructed *after* the k≤32 data failed to show saturation ("So we extended it, post hoc"), which is a legitimate move to disclose but also means the choice of k∈{48,64,96,128} and of "the 4 checkpoints that admit utterances that long" was made with the answer already partly known. With 21–26 items per cell and no correction for the resulting multiple comparisons, and with Qwen3-TTS-1.7B showing "no repeated-side saturation... and invert[ing] the ratio," roughly a quarter of the extended panel contradicts the headline "repetition lowers the counting horizon 3.5-fold" claim. A ˆK ratio built from 3 supporting and 1 contradicting checkpoint, on a hand-picked sub-ladder, is compatible with an unrelated per-checkpoint length/budget ceiling that happens to differ in scale for reasons unrelated to periodicity.
   **What would address it** — Pre-register (or at least separately hold out) an extension ladder before looking at k≤32 residuals, and run it on all six checkpoints rather than the four that "admit" long enough utterances, so the excluded two aren't silently absent from the claim that's supposed to generalise across the whole panel.

3. **Issue** — The one direct mechanistic test the paper runs — the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction — comes back sign-reversed, and this tension is not reflected in the abstract/title framing. `[Evidence — alternative explanation]`
   **Where** — §4.4 ("flatter attention goes with better counting (r=0.59 [0.35,0.75])... We record that as disconfirming").
   **Why it matters** — Lemma 1 (attention dilution) is the mechanistic bridge the paper offers between "periodic conditioning" and "approximately autonomous map F" — it's in the abstract as a "companion attention-dilution lemma," formally verified alongside the main theorem, and the intro's causal story runs through it explicitly ("softmax attention cannot tell one copy from another... The decoder therefore meets approximately the same conditioning at every repetition boundary"). When the one testable consequence of that bridge reverses sign, the paper still keeps the lemma prominently in the abstract and title-adjacent framing without flagging that the specific causal story it tells has an empirically falsified link. The theorem's implication survives (it's proven), but the *explanatory* picture the introduction sells — dilution causes autonomy causes contraction causes collapse — took a real hit that a skimming reader of the abstract would not learn about.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the dilution→failure causal framing from the abstract/intro, or add a sentence there flagging that the dose-response link is disconfirmed, consistent with how honestly it's handled in §5.

4. **Issue** — Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB), the artifacts underlying the state-measurement and effective-rank results, are "available on request" rather than hosted, and several exclusion/threshold rules central to the reported numbers (item exclusions, S10/S11/S12 thresholds) are deferred to unshown supplementary material. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — §3, "Code, data, settings."
   **Why it matters** — Code, transcripts, and the Lean development are released, which is well above the field median — but "available on request" for the two largest artifacts is a soft commitment, not a reproducibility guarantee, and a grad student trying to replicate the effective-rank / capacity-gain figures (Table 1, Fig. 1c) specifically cannot do so from the paper alone without those activations or the exact probe-layer selection, which isn't specified in the visible text.
   **What would address it** — Host the activations (or a representative subset sufficient to reproduce Fig. 1c) rather than gating them behind a request, and state the probe layer(s) and effective-rank estimator in the main text, not only in code.

5. **Issue** — The paper's single non-autoregressive baseline (VITS) is a 2021, much smaller, non-scaled architecture asked to carry the entire "this is architectural, not just long-text degradation" claim against six 2024–2025 AR checkpoints up to 8B parameters — and VITS's own control accuracy (58.9% exact) is already far below the pooled AR-model control accuracy (94.3%), so architecture is confounded with capacity/vintage rather than isolated. `[Evidence — baselines]`
   **Where** — §3 ("VITS [10] is run as a non-autoregressive baseline"), §4.3 ("The last rival is architectural, the one the title stakes... shows no dissociation: 65.6%... against 58.9%... a gap of -6.7 points where the panel's is +76.1").
   **Why it matters** — This is the single experiment that licenses the paper's title claim (that the phenomenon is about the AR decoding loop specifically, not TTS synthesis in general) and the abstract's "vanishes in a non-autoregressive synthesiser." But n=1 architecture, with no scale ladder on the NAR side (contrast: Llasa gets a 1B/3B/8B ladder on the AR side) and no modern competitor, is a strawman-shaped comparison, not a tuned one. VITS (ICML 2021) predates every AR checkpoint tested by 3–4 years and is an order of magnitude or more smaller than the larger Llasa/Qwen3-TTS checkpoints; its control-item exact-match (58.9%) is already worse than *every single AR checkpoint's* pooled-control performance, meaning VITS is a substantially weaker TTS system on this stimulus set overall — plausibly because it's old and small, not because it's non-autoregressive. A fresher, comparably-invested non-AR competitor exists and would have been a much stronger stand-in: e.g. ZipVoice [2506.13053], a 2025 flow-matching zero-shot TTS system explicitly built for quality at compact size, is contemporaneous with the AR checkpoints under test. Without a second, modern, non-AR architecture (ideally at more than one scale), "all 6 AR checkpoints separate from it" is a claim about AR-vs.-one-old-small-NAR-model, not AR-vs.-the-NAR-architecture-class the title implies.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one modern (2023–2025), reasonably-scaled non-AR TTS system (flow-matching or diffusion-based) to the architectural-rival test, ideally with its own small scale ladder, so "architectural" isn't resting on n=1 and a capacity confound.

### Minor concerns

- Fig. 1's axis-label text renders as garbled fragments ("22 25", "26 27", "0 50") in the extracted text — likely a PDF-extraction artifact of log-scale tick labels (e.g. 2²…2⁵), but as given the figure isn't self-interpretable from the caption alone; worth double-checking the camera-ready render.
- The abstract's pooled 18.2%/94.3% exact-match figures don't obviously match either an unweighted mean of Table 1's per-checkpoint values (≈20.7%) or a stated weighting rule, unlike the -8.3% figure, which the text explicitly flags as item-weighted vs. checkpoint-weighted. State the pooling rule for these too.
- §4.4's "factor 0.46" summary ratio and Table 1's per-checkpoint ratio column (0.32–0.80) don't obviously reconcile (mean of the six ratios ≈0.49); clarify whether 0.46 is a ratio-of-means or mean-of-ratios.
- Reference [1] is cited as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a clause on why (correctness vs. priority dispute vs. other), since it's used for a substantive claim ("its alignment analysis") rather than just background colour.
- The number-phrase stimuli ("six hundred sixty-six thousand...") are a vivid hook in the intro but never get their own reported breakout in Results — either fold them into the ladder analysis or note they're qualitative colour only.
- "median count error of -8.3% at k≥6" in the abstract vs. "-12.5% per checkpoint" a paragraph into §4.1 — both are given, which is good practice, but the abstract only reports one of the two and a reader skimming just the abstract gets the smaller number.

### Belief update

I updated, modestly, on the proposition "AR-TTS repetition failures are substantially periodicity-specific rather than a generic length/attention-span effect" — the length-matched control design (§4.3) is genuinely the paper's strongest piece of evidence, it's consistent across all six checkpoints and three families, and it survives a targeted confound check (the cycled-filler-pool re-test, 71.2 vs. 84.6 points). I did not update on "AR-TTS repetition failure is caused by state-map contraction toward a fixed point," because the paper's own tests of that mechanism came back null or reversed (two failed q-measurements, a sign-reversed dose-response test, a probe that retains the count late in 6/6 checkpoints), and the one experiment meant to isolate architecture-as-cause from capacity/vintage-as-cause used a single, old, small non-AR baseline. The Lean proof is a valid conditional — I don't doubt the math — but a machine-checked implication with an unverified premise doesn't move my belief about the actual mechanism in real transformers; it moves my belief about what a sufficient mechanism *could* look like, which is worth less.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the phenomenon (periodicity-specific undercounting) is well-evidenced, but the paper's title-level claim that this is architectural rather than a capacity/vintage artifact rests on one 2021, small, unscaled non-AR baseline standing in for an entire model class, and that comparison needs a second, modern, comparably-invested NAR competitor before the "horizon" framing can be trusted at the scope the title claims.
