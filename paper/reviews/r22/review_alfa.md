### Summary

The paper asks whether neural TTS models' well-known failure to render repeated phrases correctly (looping, truncation, miscounting) is driven by *length* or by *periodicity*. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" — each repeated-word stimulus paired with a length-matched, word-count-matched control whose repeated token is swapped for distinct fillers — the authors test six checkpoints across three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus two non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a later-acquired fourth family (CosyVoice 2). The headline result is a ~75-point gap in exact-repetition-count accuracy between repeated and control conditions at k≥6, which survives decoding-parameter sweeps, exclusion-rule ablations, and re-scoring with four different ASR judges (motivated by a demonstration that Whisper's autoregressive decoder is itself biased against detecting the phenomenon). A second "period ladder" shows the deficit is graded with periodicity, not binary with verbatim adjacency. A formally verified (Lean 4) contracting-map account of *why* the failure should happen is then empirically measured and rejected — the boundary-to-boundary Jacobian is expansive (q ≫ 1) on every checkpoint tested, including the one checkpoint that counts correctly.

### Steelman

The paper's strongest form is genuinely good work: the length-matched control is the right control to isolate periodicity from length, the judge-selection argument (Whisper is autoregressive and shares the failure mode under study, so it must be disqualified and replaced with a CTC recognizer, then cross-validated against three more recognizers) is a real methodological contribution to this subfield, and the willingness to formally state a mechanistic hypothesis, pre-register it in Lean, and then report it dead is more intellectually honest than the median mechanistic-interpretability paper. The specification-curve section is also unusually forthcoming about researcher degrees of freedom. Taken at face value, this is a paper that did the controls right and let a favored mechanism die in public. The problems below are about whether the controls and the "pre-specified" framing hold up under the scrutiny the paper itself invites.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — Sections 3.1–3.2 are titled "Pre-specified," but the paper's own text admits the central k≥6 threshold defining the headline 75.4-point gap was chosen *after* seeing the data.
   **Where** — Section 3.2: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge, which is why the abstract gives the unconditioned figure too and the specification curve varies it."
   **Why it matters** — Calling a data-dependent threshold "pre-specified" is a direct terminological contradiction, not a nuance. It matters because the entire confirmatory register of the paper — "Pre-specified: the limit tracks periodicity, not length" — leans on readers taking these results as tests of an a priori hypothesis. A threshold selected by looking at where curves diverge is HARKing by definition, and the specification curve (420 post-hoc variants, none corrected) is offered as the remedy, but a robustness sweep over post-hoc choices is not equivalent to a genuine pre-registered confirmation. The word "pre-specified" should be reserved for what actually was fixed in advance (the ladder design, the control construction), not for the threshold that produces the abstract's flagship number.
   **What would address it** — Rename the sections to reflect what was actually pre-registered vs. exploratory (the paper already does this correctly for Fig. 1(b,c), dated as "exploratory" against S16 — the same discipline should apply to the k≥6 cut), and report the unconditioned (k=1) 46.8-point figure as the primary confirmatory statistic, with k≥6 explicitly framed as a post-hoc, robustness-checked refinement.

2. **Issue** — The abstract's headline gap (75.4 points, cycled-filler controls) is not the number the body text says the authors intend to report.
   **Where** — Abstract ("a gap of 75.4 points... by architecture family (n=3; 67.6 with never-cycled control fillers)") vs. Section 3.1 ("Never-cycled control fillers throughout put the gap at 69.6 by checkpoint and 67.6 by family against 76.7 and 75.4 cycled, positive throughout, and **we quote the smaller**").
   **Why it matters** — The body explicitly states the policy is to quote the smaller (never-cycled) number, presumably because it is the less confound-prone condition, but the abstract leads with the larger cycled number and demotes the smaller one to a parenthetical. This is exactly the kind of selective headline-reporting the paper's own specification-curve ethos is supposed to guard against, and it is directly checkable by the reader — which makes it worse, not better, that it's there.
   **What would address it** — Either make the abstract's headline number match the stated reporting policy, or explicitly justify why the abstract deviates from it (e.g., "we lead with the cycled figure because it uses the full k-range and quote the conservative one alongside it").

3. **Issue** — The control's filler pool is only 8 items and is "cycled above k=8," which reintroduces periodic structure (period 8) into the nominally aperiodic control arm at higher k — including across the entire k=48–128 extension ladder in Fig. 1(b), which uses exactly this control.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Stimuli"): "the repeated token replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k = 8"; Fig. 1(b) extension ladder.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's central logical move is that the control is a length-matched, *aperiodic* twin of the repeated condition, and the whole argument that "the limit tracks periodicity, not length" depends on the control genuinely lacking periodic structure. At k=16 each filler recurs twice; at k=32, four times; in the k=128 extension ladder, sixteen times. A "control" with an embedded period-8 cycle is not periodicity-free — it is a milder instance of the same manipulation under study, which would tend to *understate* the gap at high k and undermine the extension-ladder's saturation-curve analysis specifically at the range where the paper argues against a horizon existing.
   **What would address it** — Report the extension-ladder result (Fig. 1b, RSS 2.6 vs. 485.3) using never-cycled or randomly-sampled-without-replacement fillers, and quantify how much of the plateau/non-saturation finding is attributable to the cycled-control confound versus genuine periodicity-tracking in the repeated arm.

4. **Issue** — The CosyVoice 2 "held-out architecture" replication, presented as strong confirmatory evidence, is a single family, n=90, with a very wide predictive interval and posterior sensitivity that touches zero under a plausible prior.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel is 65.3 [5.9, 93.3], P=0.996... under the widest prior on the group-level scales it touches zero (−1.6, P=0.956). We then acquired a fourth architecture... a gap of 64.4, inside the predicted interval."
   **Why it matters** — The prose frames this as unusually strong evidence ("the agreement closer than it entitles us to expect"), but a predictive interval spanning [5.9, 93.3] is compatible with almost any positive result, and the authors' own sensitivity check shows the same model touches zero under a different, defensible prior. One point estimate landing inside a very wide interval, from a single additional family at n=90, is weak generalization evidence dressed in strong rhetorical clothing.
   **What would address it** — Report the CosyVoice result plainly as "consistent with, but not a strong test of" the cross-architecture claim, and acquire at least one more held-out family before asserting the panel-level gap generalizes beyond the three panel architectures.

5. **Issue** — Several secondary mechanistic claims generalize well beyond what their (very small, often single-checkpoint) samples support.
   **Where** — Section 4/5: causal rank-1 patch, n=36, single checkpoint (Llasa-1B) — "The patch is a single-checkpoint result on Llasa-1B" is acknowledged, but the surrounding prose still asserts "the decoder ignores it" and builds a discussion-level claim ("the count is not erased... the policy reading it fails") on top of it; Section 3.2, F5-TTS duration-supply result, n=60 with "any gain above 8.3 points excluded" as an ad hoc cell-count safeguard.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is admirably explicit about these limits in the final "what the design does not separate" paragraph, but the discussion section's rhetorical weight ("the rival the evidence favours," "the decoder ignores it") reads as more settled than a single-checkpoint, n=36 causal-patch result can support, especially set against a panel where q (the Jacobian expansion factor) itself varies by an order of magnitude across checkpoints (21.0–347.7).
   **What would address it** — Replicate the rank-1 patch on at least one additional checkpoint/architecture before using it to anchor the discussion's central causal claim, or explicitly downgrade the claim's status in the main text (not just the limits paragraph) to "suggestive, single-checkpoint."

6. **Issue** — The paper disclaims p-value-based inference while using p-values as pivotal evidence.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values" immediately follows a paragraph whose key generalization claim rests on "P=0.996" and "P=0.956."
   **Why it matters** — This is a direct methodological inconsistency: the stated inferential philosophy is invoked to justify not correcting 420 uncorrected specification-curve comparisons, yet the paper still leans on p-values exactly where they are convenient (the partial-pooling generalization argument). Readers cannot tell which standard of evidence is actually governing which claim.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the p-values from the partial-pooling paragraph and report only the posterior intervals (consistent with the stated effect-size philosophy), or acknowledge explicitly that different claims in the paper use different inferential standards and justify why.

### Minor concerns

- The 165-clip human spot-check ("so a judgement can be checked by ear") never states whether any manual review was performed blind to condition/model, nor reports inter-rater agreement if more than one listener was involved.
- Reference [1] "VALL-E" is rendered as "V ALL-E" (likely a PDF-extraction artifact, but worth fixing for camera-ready).
- The companion paper [7] and the representational-probing paper [13] (Venkatesh) are both dated 2026 and one is self-cited from the same author group; this is not disqualifying but the paper leans heavily on [13] for half of its causal argument ("Venkatesh established the representational half... we add the causal half") without much independent discussion of that paper's methodology.
- "audio and higher cap-hit rate both push that particular ratio up (S28)" and similar phrases repeatedly defer the actual robustness numbers to supplementary sections not included in the text under review, making several claims (the p=2 "surviving fraction of 0.502") unverifiable from the main text alone.
- The Whisper audit is n=12 (English) and unspecified n for the Spanish arm, described only as "unreportable" in the limits paragraph — this should be stated plainly wherever the Spanish result is first invoked (Section 2), not only in the limits section.
- The abstract's "94.3% for their controls" and Table 1's per-checkpoint "exactly right" control percentages (e.g., 87.8%, 100.0%) are consistent, but the table would benefit from also showing the never-cycled-control column given how much interpretive weight that comparison carries.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core design (length-matched control, judge-selection argument, formally falsified mechanism) is sound and the paper is unusually candid about its own limits, but the "pre-specified" framing around the k≥6 threshold, the headline-vs-stated-policy mismatch on the 75.4/67.6 numbers, and the residual periodicity embedded in the cycled control at high k are all concrete, textually verifiable problems that need to be fixed or explicitly justified before the paper's central quantitative claims can be taken at face value.
