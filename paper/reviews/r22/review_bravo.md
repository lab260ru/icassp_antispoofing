### Summary
The paper asks whether TTS models' well-documented tendency to lose count on repeated phrases is really about *length* (long inputs, exposure bias, duration budget) or about *periodicity* (a specific structural property of repeated spans), and separately asks whether a length-matched control design, a period-varying ladder, and multiple ASR judges can pin the effect down mechanistically. Using 180 stimuli with length-matched repeated/control pairs rendered by six checkpoints across three (plus a fourth, confirmatory) architecture families, the authors report a large gap in exact-repetition-count accuracy between repeated and control items (18.2% vs 94.3% at k≥6, ~75-point gap), show the gap survives decoding/exclusion/judge perturbations, and then propose and reject a Lean-4-verified contraction-map mechanism for the failure via measured Jacobian singular values (q ≫ 1 everywhere), concluding the deficit is a policy/readout failure rather than a representational or purely length-driven one.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central dichotomy ("periodicity, not verbatim identity") is drawn from a comparison (p=2 vs p=1) that the authors themselves describe as statistically thin and rule-dependent, and the dichotomy is not even logically clean: periodic recurrence of a target word cannot occur without repeating that word verbatim, so what the period ladder actually isolates is *adjacency*, not "periodicity vs. verbatim identity" as two independent constructs.
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "Periodicity, or verbatim identity?" paragraph: "a surviving fraction of 0.502 on the strictest of four rules and 0.69–0.81 on the recount rules, which is where we rest the claim: the strict margin over one half is thin."
   **Why it matters** — The abstract and title stake the paper's contribution on having isolated periodicity as *the* driver ("PERIODICITY, NOT LENGTH"), but the interior text quietly downgrades this to a coin-flip-adjacent effect (0.502) on the authors' own strictest rule, with the more favorable framing (0.69–0.81) reported under looser "recount rules" whose selection isn't independently justified. A reader taking the abstract at face value would not know the load-bearing statistic is this fragile. Combined with the conceptual conflation (periodicity necessarily entails repeated identical tokens), the claim that the design has cleanly separated "periodicity" from "verbatim identity" as competing explanations is not well supported — at most the data show adjacency is not necessary for a large deficit, which is a narrower and less headline-worthy result.
   **What would address it** — Reframe the claim around adjacency vs. non-adjacency explicitly, report the p=2 result with its full uncertainty in the abstract (not just the length-vs-periodicity framing), and justify why the "recount rules" are appropriate scoring choices rather than a second garden of forking paths stacked on top of the already-acknowledged 420-specification curve.

2. **Issue** — Confidence intervals and significance-adjacent framing are built from clusters as small as n=3 (architecture families) and n=6 (checkpoints), and 420 unadjusted specification variants are explicitly run without correction and characterized as a "robustness envelope, not a family of hypotheses."
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "the gap is 75.4 [71.1, 78.9] points, all three positive" (n=3); "Across 420 specifications... the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points and none reverses its sign... we correct across none of them."
   **Why it matters** — A 95% CI computed over three architecture-family point estimates is not a meaningful frequentist interval by any standard convention, and presenting it alongside genuinely more careful machinery (mixed-effects model, partial pooling with priors) risks lending it more credibility than it earns. The specification-curve framing ("not a family of hypotheses") is a reasonable philosophical stance, but it is asserted rather than defended, and it is doing a lot of work to pre-empt the reviewer objection that no correction was applied to 420 comparisons.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the naive n=3 interval in favor of the mixed-effects/partial-pooling estimates already reported, or explicitly justify (with citation to specification-curve-analysis methodology) why sign-consistency across an uncorrected multiverse is the right evidentiary standard here, and report what fraction of the 420 specifications would survive a conservative multiple-comparisons adjustment even under the authors' own "not a hypothesis family" framing, for readers who don't share that view.

3. **Issue** — The mechanism-refutation in Section 4 tests whether the measured Jacobian contracts (q<1), but does not verify that the antecedent of the theorem (attention flattening under a small logit-difference bound δ, "logits differ by at most δ") actually held in these models before or independent of testing the consequent.
   **Where** — Section 4, "The candidate mechanism, measured and refuted" and "Measuring q."
   **Why it matters** — The Lean-4 proof's conclusion (no Lipschitz readout beyond a horizon) is conditional on a specific derivation chain: bounded-δ logits → near-uniform attention weights → an autonomous boundary map F → contraction. The paper measures only the final link (q, the top singular value of F) and reports it is never <1, concluding the whole premise "was never a description of these decoders." But if δ was already large in these models (i.e., attention was never close to flat at repetition boundaries), the theorem's antecedent fails at the first link, and testing q<1 in isolation doesn't distinguish "the map exists but doesn't contract" from "the map as constructed by the derivation never applied to begin with." The dilution-lemma correlation (r=0.59) is offered as related evidence but is a correlational patch on a different question, not a check on δ itself.
   **What would address it** — Report the empirical distribution of attention-weight spread (or the effective δ) at repetition boundaries across checkpoints, alongside q, so readers can see whether the rejection is of the full chain or just its final step.

4. **Issue** — The behavioral judge's calibration — the finding that motivates using a CTC recognizer over Whisper at all — rests on very small ground-truth audits (n=12 over 6 donors for Whisper; n=24 for the CTC recognizer).
   **Where** — Section 2, "Behavioural measurement..." paragraph: "0.19 of the true count, n=12 over 6 donors" and "ratio 1.00 (n=24, mean 0.996)."
   **Why it matters** — Every downstream number in the paper (the 75.4-point gap, the period ladder, the specification curve) is filtered through this recognizer choice. A judge-selection decision this consequential deserves a larger and more diverse audit than 12–24 trials, particularly since the paper itself argues (correctly) that judge bias can manufacture the very effect under study — the same logic that indicts Whisper should raise the bar for how confidently the CTC alternative is validated.
   **What would address it** — Expand the concatenative ground-truth audit (more donors, more k values, ideally spanning the full k range used in the main ladder) and report per-k audit accuracy rather than a single pooled ratio, since judge reliability plausibly degrades exactly at the high-k region where the headline effect lives.

5. **Issue** — External validity is narrow and partly acknowledged as broken: the Spanish replication is explicitly reported as unusable, generalization rests on one confirmatory out-of-panel architecture, and the causal patching result generalizes from a single checkpoint.
   **Where** — Section 5, "What the design does not separate, and other limits": "a Spanish arm was unreportable, an exact-rate contrast needing a more accurate judge"; "the causal patch on one checkpoint."
   **Why it matters** — The paper's claims are pitched at the level of a general property of neural TTS decoders ("Nor is the failure peculiar to autoregressive decoding," "It is periodicity, not length" as an apparently architecture-general law), but the causal/mechanistic evidence (Section 4's readout-transplant experiment) is n=1 checkpoint, and the only non-English evidence is disclosed as unreliable. The generalization step from "true across the panel we tested" to language spanning claims implicit in the abstract's confident tone is larger than the evidence supports.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the generalization language in the abstract/introduction to match the disclosed single-checkpoint/single-language scope of the causal evidence, or extend the causal patching and the Spanish arm (with the improved judge the paper says is needed) before publication.

### Minor concerns
- The repository is named `icassp_antispoofing`, explicitly noted as predating the study ("name predates this study") — this is disclosed but still a friction point for reproducibility/discoverability; consider a redirect or renamed release tag.
- Table 1 shows Qwen3-TTS-1.7B has 0.0% median count error yet only 38.9% exact-match (vs. 98.9% control) — the paper uses this well to argue median error is not the right summary statistic, but the two metrics being this discordant elsewhere in the paper (both are reported throughout) invites confusion about which is the primary outcome measure; a single sentence early on flagging that median error and exact-match can dissociate would help.
- The "points" vs "%" units are used somewhat interchangeably across the abstract and Section 3 (e.g., "42.4 against 84.4 points" refers to gap magnitudes, not rates) — a brief notational note would reduce ambiguity for readers skimming numbers.
- Self-citation [7] ("From dispersion to attraction...") is described as a "companion" paper but the relationship (shared data? shared judge methodology?) isn't spelled out, which matters for assessing independence of the two contributions.
- The abstract's parenthetical "(67.6 with never-cycled control fillers)" is intelligible only after reading Section 3.1 in full; consider a one-clause gloss in the abstract.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the core dissociation (repeated vs. length-matched control) is well-instrumented and the judge-artifact and decoding-rule checks are genuinely thorough, but the paper's signature claim to have isolated *periodicity* from *verbatim identity* rests on an admittedly thin, rule-dependent statistic and a conflated conceptual distinction, and the mechanism-refutation in Section 4 tests only the consequent of its own theorem without validating the antecedent — both need to be tightened or the claims correspondingly narrowed before the paper's framing is fully earned by its evidence.
