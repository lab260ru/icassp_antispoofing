### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech models miscount repeated words or phrases, and proposes a mechanistic explanation: if softmax attention over k repeated spans dilutes to weight Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1) and the resulting near-autonomous per-repetition state map F contracts with factor q<1, then no Lipschitz readout of the decoder state — including the stop-token logit — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six TTS checkpoints across three families are evaluated on a repetition ladder (k∈{1,…,32}, later extended to 128) scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically to avoid the AR bias of the obvious ASR judge (Whisper). The headline finding is a strong dissociation between repeated and length-matched non-repeated ("control") stimuli — median count error ≈ −8.3% to −12.5% at k≥6 for repeated items versus ≈0% for controls, and 18.2% vs 94.3% exact-match — replicated in direction across all six checkpoints and, on a post-hoc extension to k=128, a 3.5-fold lower fitted saturation scale for repeated versus control speech. The paper is unusually candid that several of the theorem's own premises (the contraction assumption, the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction, and the probe-based test of the horizon) are only partially or inconsistently supported by the data.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's own significance-testing arithmetic shows the headline cross-checkpoint claim cannot reach conventional significance after correction, yet the abstract and running text assert the effect unconditionally ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family").
   **Where** — §4.2, penultimate paragraph ("The panel is too small for significance testing — the exact signed-rank floor at n = 6 is 0.031 and no multiplicity correction clears 0.05 (S11)"); Abstract; §4.3 opening claim.
   **Why it matters** — With n=6 checkpoints as the unit of replication, the authors themselves report that even the best-case (fully concordant) Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value (0.031) does not survive multiplicity correction. That is a direct, self-supplied statement that the panel-level test fails at conventional α after correction. Yet the abstract states the periodicity-vs-length dissociation as a categorical fact ("in every checkpoint and every family"), and §4.3 calls it "the paper's sharpest test" without qualifying that the cross-checkpoint claim is not statistically significant by the paper's own reported threshold. Readers who don't reach S11 will take the abstract's unconditional phrasing as an established result rather than a consistent-but-underpowered effect.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly, in the abstract and in §4.2/§4.3, that the cross-checkpoint effect is directionally consistent (6/6 or 5/6 by various measures) but does not clear a corrected significance threshold at n=6; frame the claim as an effect-size/consistency argument throughout, not just in the appendix-referenced caveat.

2. **Issue** — The paper mixes two incompatible units of analysis — checkpoint-level (n=6, explicitly treated as underpowered) and item-level (n=457, n=525, n=240, etc., treated as if items were independent and exchangeable) — without any mixed-effects or cluster-robust adjustment, so item-pooled confidence intervals are likely too narrow.
   **Where** — §4.2 ("−8.3% [−12.5, −6.2] pooling items (n = 457)"); §4.3 ("94.3% for controls against 18.2%... n=525"); Fig. 1(c) caption ("95% bootstrap intervals over stimulus templates"); Table 1.
   **Why it matters** — Items generated by the same checkpoint share model-specific idiosyncrasies (decoding defaults, tokenizer, training data) that make them correlated, not i.i.d. draws. Bootstrapping "over stimulus templates" (as stated for Fig. 1(c)) does not obviously account for checkpoint-level clustering either. Pooling 457 or 525 items into a single interval while separately conceding that the true independent sample size is 6 checkpoints creates two parallel, differently-powered narratives in the same paper: a fragile n=6 test that (per concern 1) fails after correction, and superficially precise item-pooled intervals that look far more decisive than the underlying independent-sample evidence supports. A reader can come away citing the tight item-pooled CI without realizing it rests on 6 non-independent generators.
   **What would address it** — Fit a mixed-effects model (checkpoint as random effect, k and condition as fixed effects) or report cluster-robust (checkpoint-clustered) standard errors/CIs for every pooled-item statistic, and reconcile these with the per-checkpoint effect sizes already reported in Table 1.

3. **Issue** — The extension to k∈{48,64,96,128} that produces the paper's only demonstration of an actual horizon (the 3.5-fold saturation-scale reduction) is explicitly post hoc, run on very small per-cell samples, and fit with a one-parameter model whose resulting ratio is then quoted with unwarranted precision.
   **Where** — §4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc..."; "leaving 21–26 per cell"; "K̂rep = 30 against K̂ctl = 105 [94, 114]... 3.5-fold"; "form-dependent — 2.7–4.3 across 3 saturating families").
   **Why it matters** — This is the paper's single piece of evidence for the theorem's most distinctive, falsifiable prediction (a finite horizon at all, since the k≤32 ladder was proportional and showed none). It comes from an exploratory decision made after seeing the k≤32 result fail to show a horizon (the paper itself dates this against the commit log in S16, an honest disclosure, but disclosure of post-hoc-ness does not confer confirmatory status). Fitting K̂ per checkpoint/condition on ~21–26 generations per cell, then computing a ratio between two noisy point estimates and further slicing that ratio "across 3 saturating families" (i.e., 3 numbers) as though it were a distribution, invites exactly the kind of small-sample overfitting the rest of the paper is careful to flag elsewhere.
   **What would address it** — Either report the extension as strictly exploratory/hypothesis-generating (removing "3.5-fold" and the "2.7–4.3" range from headline claims and figures) or collect a genuinely held-out confirmatory sample at k∈{48,…,128} on fresh items/seeds and re-fit, reporting whether the ratio replicates outside the sample that motivated the extension in the first place.

4. **Issue** — The strongest direct test of Theorem 1's conclusion (a linear probe recovering count below N* but not above it) succeeds in only 1 of 2 checkpoints it could be run on, and the dose-response test of the companion Lemma 1 comes out sign-reversed — yet both the abstract and §5 continue to lean on the mechanism as the leading explanation rather than treating these as substantial disconfirmation.
   **Where** — Abstract ("in 1 of the 2 checkpoints we could run it on"); §5 ("the prediction holds in 1 of 2 checkpoints, a failure to replicate"); §4.4 ("its dose-response prediction fails outright: ... flatter attention goes with better counting").
   **Why it matters** — n=2 for the probe test is not a "replication rate," it is a coin flip; treating it as "1 of 2" implicitly invites the reader to read 50% as a partial success rather than as statistically indistinguishable from the theorem making no prediction at all. Combined with the dilution lemma's dose-response coming out in the opposite direction to the theorem's mechanism (r=0.59 favoring more dilution → better counting, the reverse of what dilution-driven contraction predicts), the paper has two of its most theory-diagnostic tests actively cutting against the mechanistic story, not merely "not yet confirming" it. The framing in §5 ("what would refute it... the third has now happened once") acknowledges this but still positions the theorem as the operative account in the abstract and title.
   **What would address it** — Either run the probe test on more checkpoints before making claims about replication rate, or explicitly downgrade the theorem from "explanation" to "candidate mechanism with two of three direct empirical tests currently disconfirming or non-replicating" in the abstract/discussion, not just in an appendix-referenced hedge.

5. **Issue** — A closely related theoretical claim — that long-horizon breakdown in autoregressive execution is structural rather than complexity-driven, and holds even in unbranched, unambiguous tasks — appears in contemporaneous work on LLM reasoning and is not discussed.
   **Where** — Absent from §1 (Introduction) and the Related Work sketch in §5.
   **Why it matters** — [2602.06413] ("Intrinsic Stability Limits of Autoregressive Reasoning: Structural Consequences for Long-Horizon Execution") argues that AR breakdown over long horizons is intrinsic to the decoding process itself rather than attributable to task complexity — the same "not-a-complexity-problem, it's-a-mechanism-problem" move this paper makes for TTS repetition counting via contraction/Lipschitz-readout arguments. Whether or not the two papers' formal machinery coincides, the shared thesis (structural horizon in AR execution, independent of surface task difficulty) is close enough that its omission weakens the novelty framing ("the autoregressive-decoder companion to a spectral study...") and denies the reader a chance to compare the two accounts' horizons/mechanisms.
   **What would address it** — Discuss [2602.06413] in §5, clarifying whether the two results are compatible, competing, or orthogonal (e.g., different notions of "horizon," different necessary conditions), and whether the reasoning-domain result offers an independent estimate of q or N* that could substitute for the two failed attempts to measure q described in §5.

6. **Issue** — "Effective rank" of the hidden-state trajectory, the sole quantity underlying the §4.4 "capacity gain" result and Table 1's rightmost column, is never formally defined.
   **Where** — §3 ("State measurement" paragraph: "reporting the trajectory's effective rank against k"); §4.4; Table 1 caption ("capacity gain is measured at deep layers"); Fig. 1(c).
   **Why it matters** — Effective rank of a matrix/trajectory can mean several different things (participation ratio over singular values, entropy of the normalized singular spectrum, a hard threshold count, etc.), each with different sensitivity to noise floor and sequence length — exactly the kind of choice that could materially change whether "capacity gain" differs by a factor of 0.46 or something else between repeated and control conditions. Readers cannot check or reproduce Table 1's numbers or Fig. 1(c) from the text as given, only from the (promised but unreviewed) code.
   **What would address it** — State the exact effective-rank estimator (formula) in §3 or an appendix pointer, including which layers ("deep layers") and how ties/degenerate spectra are handled.

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is a withdrawn preprint (arXiv:2509.19852, "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026") cited for its "alignment analysis" without discussing why a withdrawn paper is still safe to rely on for that specific claim.
- The two count-error summaries in §4.1 (−12.5% median per-checkpoint vs −8.3% pooling items) are reported side by side without a clear statement of which is the "headline" number used in the abstract, and why the two differ so much (weighting by surviving item count) is stated but not intuitively motivated.
- Fig. 1(b)'s axis labels in the extracted text ("2⁶ 2⁷", "2⁵ 2⁶ 2⁷") suggest a log2 k-axis, but this isn't stated in the caption or main text — worth confirming the axis scale explicitly since the extension range (48–128) spans irregular, non-power-of-2 steps.
- Table 1's "ratio" column (0.21–0.80) and Fig. 1(c)'s "d Neff/d log k" bars are described somewhat inconsistently (fitted ratio vs. raw rep./ctrl. slopes) — a sentence tying the two together would help.
- The CTC-judge audit ("ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper") does not report the audit sample size or a CI, despite this measurement underwriting the validity of every downstream count-error number.
- The repo is described as living at `lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing`, with the parenthetical "(name predates this study)" — harmless, but worth a one-line footnote explaining why a TTS hallucination study lives in an antispoofing repo, since reviewers checking code/data provenance may otherwise wonder if material was mixed across unrelated projects.
- "of two non-autoregressive baselines, one is immune and one is not" (Abstract) is a strong compressed claim; the immune one (VITS) is explained post hoc via a duration-per-token architectural difference, but this explanation is not itself tested independently of the F5-TTS duration-conditioning experiment — it would help to flag this as an inferred, not directly tested, architectural distinction for VITS.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the theorem, its Lean formalization, and the qualitative periodicity-vs-length dissociation are a genuine and unusually transparently-reported contribution, but the paper's own statistics (an admittedly non-significant n=6 signed-rank result after correction, item-pooled CIs that ignore checkpoint clustering, a post-hoc small-cell horizon fit, and an n=2 probe "replication") do not yet support the unconditional framing used in the abstract and title, and need to be reconciled with the hedged, more accurate framing already present in the body text.
