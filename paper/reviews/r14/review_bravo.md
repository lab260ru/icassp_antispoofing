# Review (Reviewer bravo)

### Summary
The paper argues that autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated phrases because softmax attention over k identical spans dilutes to Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1, "attention dilution"), which makes the decoder's per-repetition update approximately autonomous; if that update map contracts (Assumption 2), a machine-checked theorem (Lean 4 / mathlib) shows no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N*. Across six checkpoints from three TTS families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B), a repetition-ladder benchmark scored by a CTC recogniser (chosen because Whisper's autoregressive decoder is itself shown to be biased against repetition) finds models undercount repeated content (median error −8.3% at k≥6, exact only 18.2% of the time) while length-matched non-repeating controls are essentially exact (94.3%); the deficit tracks periodicity rather than length. Within k≤32 the deficit looks proportional rather than saturating, contradicting the theorem's predicted plateau, but a post-hoc extension to k=128 on 4/6 checkpoints finds saturation with a 3.5-fold lower horizon for repeated vs. control text. The paper explicitly reports that the theorem's contraction premise was never confirmed (two measurement attempts failed) and that a dose-response test of the dilution lemma came out sign-reversed, while a linear-probe test of the theorem's readout-impossibility claim replicated in only 1 of 2 checkpoints tested.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The empirical population covered (six checkpoints, three families, English-only synthetic stimuli, one English-trained CTC judge) is far too narrow to support the generality the theorem and abstract claim ("no property of the decoding rule enters," "in every checkpoint and every family").
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Models," "Stimuli"), abstract, Section 4.1–4.3.
   **Why it matters** — The theorem is stated as a fully general fact about any decoder state space with a contracting per-repetition map, and the introduction motivates it by analogy to text-LM degeneration ([5],[6]) and cites TTS stability papers for systems (CosyVoice 2, RALL-E, ELLA-V) that are never actually tested here. Yet the entire empirical case rests on (a) one within-family scale ladder that confounds "scale" with a single fixed backbone+tokenizer (Llama over X-codec2), (b) a synthetic 180-item stimulus set of carrier sentences, tongue twisters, and number phrases — not naturalistic text — with fillers cycled from a pool of only 8 above k=8, and (c) a judge (wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self) trained on clean, English, read speech, applied to zero non-English material despite XTTS-v2 being explicitly "massively multilingual." Nothing here licenses extrapolation to other languages, other tokenization schemes, other TTS paradigms (e.g., the Tacotron-style attention models the cited stability literature is actually about), or to text-only AR LLMs, which is exactly the broader class the paper's introduction gestures at. A closely related literature exists on state-tracking limits in general language models — e.g. "(How) Do Language Models Track State?" [2503.02854], which studies exactly the kind of state-composition failure this paper's contraction argument predicts, using permutation-composition tasks in text LMs — and the paper does not engage with it or test whether its account transfers outside TTS at all, despite discussing "state," effective rank, and readouts throughout Section 4.4.
   **What would address it** — At minimum, test on a non-English stimulus set with a matched non-English (or multilingual) judge, given that several tested models are multilingual; add at least one architecturally distinct AR TTS family (attention-based, non-codec-LM) to break the family×tokenizer confound; and either explicitly scope the abstract's generality claims down to "codec-token language-model TTS, English, this stimulus family" or run at least a small text-LLM analogue of the repetition ladder to test the claimed AR-generality of the mechanism.

2. **Issue** — The theorem's load-bearing premise (Assumption 2, contraction with factor q<1) is never empirically established, and the one mechanistic prediction that was tested (Lemma 1's dose-response) came out sign-reversed.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("What is proved, and what the measurements do not show"), Section 4.4.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's title advertises "a Lean-verified bound," but a machine-checked implication is only as informative as its antecedent. The authors report that a boundary-distance estimator and a perturbation probe both failed to measure q, and that within-cell dose-response between attention flatness and counting accuracy is *positive* (r=0.59, flatter attention → *better* counting) — the opposite of what dilution-then-collapse predicts. That is not a null result; it is disconfirming evidence for the proposed causal chain (dilution → contraction → collapse) as stated. The paper's own conclusion — "whatever selects which generations fail is not the degree of dilution" — effectively retracts the mechanistic story while the abstract still frames the theorem as explanatory scaffolding for the main finding.
   **What would address it** — Either drop or substantially soften the claim that the theorem *explains* the observed collapse (versus merely being *consistent with* the count-error/control contrast under an unverified premise), or invest in a working q-estimator (e.g., a designed synthetic decoder with known contraction, used to validate the estimator before applying it to real checkpoints) before asserting the mechanism in the abstract.

3. **Issue** — Underpowered comparison (n=6 checkpoints) is paired with strong universal-sounding language, and the one genuine dissenting checkpoint is narratively minimized.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("panel is not unanimous"), abstract ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family").
   **Where 2** — Section 4.2 statistics paragraph ("too small for significance testing... no multiplicity correction clears 0.05").
   **Why it matters** — The authors are candid that n=6 cannot support significance testing, yet the abstract's "every checkpoint and every family" is a universal quantifier over a sample explicitly acknowledged as too small to test statistically. Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation at k≤128, an *inverted* ratio, and no median-error gap at k≤32 — three separate departures from the predicted pattern in the same checkpoint — yet is reported as "an unexplained exception, not as the theorem behaving conditionally." With n=6, one checkpoint behaving qualitatively differently on three separate measures is not a negligible outlier to wave off; it is 1/6 of the entire evidence base contradicting the horizon prediction in its strongest form.
   **What would address it** — Either scale the checkpoint panel (more families, more scales per family) to a size where the "every checkpoint" claim is statistically supportable, or rephrase the universal claims as majority/typical-case claims and give Qwen3-TTS-1.7B equal narrative weight as a genuine counterexample rather than a footnote.

4. **Issue** — The k=128 saturation result, which is the only evidence for the theorem's signature horizon prediction, is a single post-hoc extension whose "3.5-fold, just past where our ladder stopped" framing is exactly the kind of result that garden-of-forking-paths analysis should treat with suspicion.
   **Where** — Section 4.2.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably transparent that this is post hoc ("S16 dates every analysis decision against the commit log") and that the original k≤32 ladder showed no horizon at all. But an extension that happens to land just past the point needed to first reveal the predicted effect, run on only 4 of 6 checkpoints (with the 5th/6th excluded by a hardware ceiling and the remaining 4 including the one dissenting checkpoint discussed above), leaving 21–26 items per cell after exclusions, is a small and singular basis for the paper's central quantitative claim (the "3.5-fold" horizon reduction, "2.7–4.3 across 3 saturating families"). There's no independent replication ladder, no pre-registration of the extension range before running it, and no held-out confirmation set.
   **What would address it** — Pre-register (or at minimum, blind) a second, independently-drawn extension ladder and confirm the 3.5-fold figure replicates before treating it as the paper's headline quantitative result; report the excluded XTTS-v2/Qwen-checkpoint status more prominently rather than folding it into supplementary notes.

5. **Issue** — The probe test of the theorem's distinctive "no readout beyond N*" prediction is described in the abstract as surviving "1 of the 2 checkpoints we could run it on," which is a failure to replicate presented with success-leaning framing.
   **Where** — Abstract; Section 5 ("a failure to replicate (S12)").
   **Why it matters** — A theorem-specific falsification test that passes in one of two available checkpoints and fails in the other (Llasa-1B: probe MAE 0.50 past the horizon, statistically indistinguishable from a mean-only baseline at 0.50 — i.e., no information recovered, consistent with the theorem; Llasa-8B: MAE 0.43, matching the control's own margin — i.e., recoverable, contradicting the theorem) is a coin-flip outcome on n=2, not a "survives" result. The abstract's phrasing ("survives one direct test... in 1 of the 2 checkpoints") reads as though the theorem passed a test, when the more accurate summary is that the only two available checkpoints split evenly between confirming and disconfirming the theorem's most specific, falsifiable claim.
   **What would address it** — Run the probe on more checkpoints before making any claim about whether the readout-impossibility prediction holds; until then, state plainly in the abstract that the theorem's core testable prediction failed to replicate in the available data, rather than leading with "survives."

## Minor concerns
- Reference [1] is cited as evidentiary background for stability hallucinations but is flagged in the paper's own bibliography as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a sentence explaining why a withdrawn preprint is still safe to cite for its alignment analysis.
- Reference [7], the companion paper this work is positioned against/alongside, is a same-author preprint (arXiv:2604.08591) — reasonable as a companion citation, but its claims (the "attractor regime" this paper is said to give a mechanistic theorem for) are themselves unverified by an independent source; worth noting the two papers are mutually reinforcing self-citations rather than independent corroboration.
- Fig. 1 axis labels as rendered ("22 25," "26 27") are illegible/garbled in the manuscript text provided — likely log2 tick marks (2², 2⁵, 2⁶, 2⁷) lost in formatting; should be checked in the camera-ready.
- Table 1's "capacity gain dNeff/d log k" and Figure 1(c)'s "d eff/dlog k" use inconsistent notation (Neff vs. eff) for what appears to be the same quantity.
- Section 3's "State measurement" paragraph is very terse on how probe layers, effective rank, and head-averaging were chosen — more detail (or a pointer to the exact supplementary section) would help reproducibility, since these choices plausibly affect the capacity-gain numbers in Table 1 and Fig. 1(c).
- The bracketed numbers throughout (e.g., "−8.3% [−12.5, −6.2]") are never explicitly labeled as bootstrap CIs vs. ranges in the visible text; a one-line definition on first use would remove ambiguity.
- The prose style (very long, clause-dense sentences, heavy use of dashes and inline caveats) makes some quantitative claims hard to parse on first read (e.g., the Section 4.2 paragraph mixing per-checkpoint and pooled statistics); a results table summarizing the horizon/saturation numbers per checkpoint would aid clarity.
- "of two non-autoregressive baselines, one is immune and one is not" is a striking finding stated almost in passing in the abstract; given that it forces withdrawal of the paper's architectural claim, it deserves more prominent framing as a limitation rather than being folded into the abstract's periodicity sentence.

## Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal-verification contribution and the periodicity-vs-length contrast are genuine and unusually transparently reported, but the theorem's causal premise is unconfirmed, its one direct dose-response test is sign-reversed, its signature horizon prediction rests on a small post-hoc extension with one dissenting checkpoint, and the entire empirical base (6 checkpoints, English-only, synthetic stimuli, single-language judge) is too narrow to support the paper's "every checkpoint and every family" / general-AR-decoder framing.
