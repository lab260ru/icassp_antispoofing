# Review (Reviewer delta)

### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech models miscount repeated phrases, and proposes a mechanistic explanation: softmax attention over k identical repeated spans dilutes to O(1/k) per span (Lemma 1, "attention dilution"), so the decoder's per-repetition update becomes approximately a single autonomous map F on its hidden state; if F is a q-contraction, Banach fixed-point machinery implies that no L-Lipschitz readout of the state — including the model's own stop-head — can separate repetition counts beyond a horizon N* = log(µ/2LC)/log q (Theorem 1, "counting collapse"). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib with a clean axiom audit. Empirically, six checkpoints across three TTS families are evaluated on a repetition ladder (k up to 32, later extended post hoc to 128) with length-matched non-repetitive controls, scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically to avoid the AR bias of a Whisper judge. Repeated items show a median count error of −8.3% (pooled) at k≥6 and 18.2% exact-match versus 94.3% for controls, a periodicity-specific rather than length-specific deficit that survives sweeps of decoding rule, filler-pool cycling, and one non-AR baseline (F5-TTS) while another (VITS) is immune. Within k≤32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating — contrary to the theorem's prediction — and a saturating horizon only appears in a post-hoc extension to k=128, in 3 of 4 extended checkpoints. The theorem's premise (contraction) is never measured successfully, and its dose-response prediction from Lemma 1 comes out sign-reversed; a direct probe test of the theorem's conclusion replicates in only 1 of 2 checkpoints tested.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's headlined contribution is "a quantitative impossibility result... with an explicit horizon in the contraction factor, orbit scale and readout margin," but none of q, C, or µ is ever measured for any checkpoint, so N* is never numerically instantiated anywhere in the paper.
   **Where** — Contributions (Sec. 1); Discussion, "What is proved, and what the measurements do not show" (Sec. 5): "two attempts to measure q failed."
   **Why it matters** — This is the crux of a claim-vs-evidence gap: the abstract and introduction read as though the paper delivers a computable, checkpoint-specific bound on how many repetitions a model can count, but what is actually delivered is a conditional (if F contracts, then...) whose antecedent is never verified and whose parameters are never estimated. "Explicit" describes the formula, not anything measured about a real decoder. A reader skimming the abstract would reasonably believe the horizon was computed and checked against data; it was not.
   **What would address it** — Either (a) succeed at measuring q, C, µ for at least one checkpoint and report the resulting N* against the observed saturation point, or (b) rewrite the abstract/contributions to state plainly, up front, that the theorem's applicability to any specific model remains unverified and that the paper's contribution is the conditional bound plus indirect, partially-replicating behavioral evidence.

2. **Issue** — The theorem's signature qualitative prediction (saturating, non-proportional undercounting) is falsified on the paper's own primary dataset (k≤32: "not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts"), and a saturating pattern is recovered only via a post-hoc range extension chosen after that disconfirmation — and even then it fails to appear in 1 of the 4 checkpoints tested at k=128 (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B "shows no repeated-side saturation... and inverts the ratio").
   **Where** — Sec. 4.1 ("no horizon lies inside this ladder"); Sec. 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc..."; "The panel is not unanimous").
   **Why it matters** — A theory whose central quantitative prediction fails on the pre-specified test range, and is then rescued by extending the range until the prediction appears, is a textbook pattern for post-hoc confirmation rather than genuine falsification-resistant support — even with the commendable transparency of dating the decision (S16). The fact that one of four extended checkpoints still doesn't show the predicted shape, and is set aside as "an unexplained exception," means the paper's strongest quantitative test of Theorem 1 is 3-for-4 at best, on a a sample too small to draw a confident conclusion either way.
   **What would address it** — Report the extension as exploratory/hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory (the paper gestures at this but the abstract still states the saturation result as a finding: "a post-hoc extension to k=128 finds one"). Pre-register a k-range and saturation criterion for a follow-up study, and give a substantive account of why Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is exempt — right now it is simply excluded from the narrative.

3. **Issue** — The dose-response test of the mechanism linking Lemma 1 (attention dilution) to Theorem 1 (contraction-driven collapse) comes out sign-reversed: within a (model, k) cell, flatter attention is associated with *better* counting (r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]), not worse.
   **Where** — Sec. 4.4, final paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — This is direct evidence against the paper's own proposed causal chain (dilution → autonomous map → contraction → collapse). The paper's response — "whatever selects which generations fail is not the degree of dilution" — quietly abandons the mechanistic story that motivated the theorem's construction in the first place, while the theorem itself is still presented in the abstract and title as *the* explanation. If dilution doesn't predict failure within-cell, the paper has not shown that Lemma 1 is doing any explanatory work at all, only that it is mathematically true.
   **What would address it** — Either identify what does predict per-generation failure (and test whether it is consistent with *some* contraction-based account), or explicitly downgrade the claim from "Lemma 1 explains the mechanism" to "Lemma 1 is a plausibility argument for Assumption 1, unconnected to which generations fail."

4. **Issue** — The CTC judge is validated against concatenative (spliced) audio with a clean 1.00 counted/true ratio, but CTC decoding's own repeat-collapsing behavior (collapsing repeated consecutive symbols before removing blanks) is a specific, structural risk for exactly the phenomenon under study — undercounting acoustically similar, closely-spaced repeated units — and concatenated ground truth (which likely has clean splice boundaries) may not stress-test this the way continuous, prosodically smooth model-generated repetition would.
   **Where** — Sec. 3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong."
   **Why it matters** — The paper's whole measurement strategy rests on the CTC judge being free of the periodicity-specific bias it explicitly diagnoses in Whisper. If the CTC judge itself undercounts real (non-spliced) repeated speech more than it undercounts real non-repeated speech of matched length, part of the reported -8.3%/-12.5% deficit could be judge artifact rather than model behavior — and the audit performed does not rule this out because it wasn't run on genuinely continuous repeated TTS output with known ground truth (only concatenated audio).
   **What would address it** — Audit the CTC judge against non-concatenative repeated speech with independently verified ground truth (e.g., human-transcribed genuine repeated TTS renders, or repeated natural read speech), not only spliced audio.

5. **Issue** — F5-TTS, a non-autoregressive flow-matching model with no per-repetition-boundary recurrent state map of the kind Theorem 1 is built around, reproduces the periodicity-specific undercount (25.6% vs 85.6% exact, growing with k) that the theorem is supposed to explain via AR decoder-state contraction.
   **Where** — Sec. 4.3, "The last rival is architectural"; "Absence of an autoregressive decoder confers no immunity, and we withdraw the architectural claim."
   **Why it matters** — The paper is honest that it withdraws the "AR-specific" framing, but doesn't follow through on the implication for the theorem's explanatory scope: if a mechanism with no AR state-recurrence produces the same behavioral signature, then Theorem 1 — which is specifically a statement about AR decoder state maps — cannot be the (or even *a*) explanation for the F5-TTS result, and the paper has not shown it is the correct explanation for the AR results either, only that it is *consistent* with them. The alternative the paper raises itself in Discussion (representational collapse, ref. [14]) is a more general phenomenon that would explain both AR and non-AR cases uniformly, and the paper does not test whether that account fits the AR data any better or worse than Theorem 1 does.
   **What would address it** — Either extend the formal framework to cover NAR duration-based decoders (the paper takes a first step here by testing F5-TTS's duration-conditioning) and show it predicts the same qualitative pattern, or explicitly frame Theorem 1 as *one sufficient* mechanism among several that could produce the observed behavioral signature, rather than as the paper's title and abstract's throughline suggest.

6. **Issue** — Statistical power is explicitly acknowledged as insufficient (n=6 checkpoints; "the exact signed-rank floor at n=6 is 0.031 and no multiplicity correction clears 0.05"), yet the abstract and Sec. 4 report the headline numbers (−8.3%, 18.2% vs 94.3%, the 3.5-fold saturation-scale ratio) as flat, unhedged findings.
   **Where** — Sec. 4.2, penultimate paragraph; Abstract.
   **Why it matters** — Readers who don't reach Sec. 4.2's admission will take the abstract's percentages as established results rather than as effect sizes computed over an underpowered, non-randomly-assembled panel of convenience (six checkpoints from three families, not a sampled population of TTS architectures). The "checkpoint is the unit of replication" framing is reasonable, but n=6 (n=3 by family) is a genuinely thin base for claims like "the limit tracks periodicity... in every checkpoint and every family."
   **What would address it** — Carry the power caveat into the abstract, or reframe the family/checkpoint-level consistency claims as descriptive rather than as a tested generalization.

7. **Issue** — The one experiment framed as a direct test of the theorem's conclusion (a linear probe that should read out count below N* and fail above it) succeeds in only 1 of 2 checkpoints tested (Llasa-8B: yes; Llasa-1B: no, "MAE 0.50... against 0.50 for a predictor that ignores the states").
   **Where** — Sec. 5, "Past the horizon the probe does test the theorem... a failure to replicate."
   **Why it matters** — This is the paper's most direct empirical purchase on Theorem 1 itself (as opposed to on the softer behavioral undercounting pattern, which several alternative mechanisms could also produce), and it fails on the majority of the (admittedly tiny) sample tested. A 1-of-2 replication rate, with no diagnosis of what differs between the two checkpoints, is not strong support and should not be characterized in the abstract as the theorem's conclusion having "survived" a test — surviving in half of two trials is barely distinguishable from chance given the sample size.
   **What would address it** — Run the probe test on more than two checkpoints before treating it as evidence either way; if resource-constrained, say explicitly that n=2 is too small to interpret and drop the "survives" framing from the abstract.

### Minor concerns

- Ref. [1] (Wang et al., "Eliminating stability hallucinations...") is cited despite being noted as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — using a withdrawn preprint for motivating background claims, even flagged, is a soft-spot in the related-work grounding.
- Table 1 tabulates count error and capacity gain per checkpoint but not the exact-match rates (18.2% vs 94.3%, and the per-family breakdowns like "14.0% against 92.9%" for Llasa) that the text and abstract treat as the paper's "sharpest test" — these numbers are scattered through prose rather than tabulated, making the headline contrast harder to audit at a glance.
- The paper mixes checkpoint-mean and item-pooled statistics without a single consistent convention (e.g., "-12.5% per checkpoint, or -8.3% pooling items... so we quote both") — helpful in spirit, but it is easy for a reader to conflate the two when they reappear later without the qualifier.
- "To our knowledge, the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact" is a "first-to-do-X" claim that is plausible but unverified against the broader (non-speech) formal-verification-of-neural-network literature; a narrower, defensible phrasing ("first in TTS specifically") would be safer.
- Effective rank as a measure of "distinct internal states" (Sec. 4.4) is not formally defined (threshold, normalization, layer selection are not specified in the visible text); given it backs a headline figure (Fig. 1c, Table 1's capacity-gain column), a definition belongs in the method section rather than being left implicit.
- The GitHub repository name ("icassp_antispoofing") is unrelated to the paper's topic, with a parenthetical "(name predates this study)" — harmless, but worth a footnote clarifying provenance so reviewers/readers aren't left wondering whether code from an unrelated project is entangled with this release.
- No conflict-of-interest or funding statement is present in the visible text.
- The manuscript is not anonymized (full author names/affiliations and a named GitHub org appear in-line) — worth confirming this matches the venue's review-track expectations.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the empirical and formal work is unusually transparent about its own gaps, but the central "explicit horizon" claim is never instantiated, the theorem's own predicted shape is disconfirmed on the primary dataset and only recovered post hoc, the proposed causal mechanism's dose-response test is sign-reversed, and the theorem's most direct test replicates in just 1 of 2 checkpoints — collectively too wide a gap between what the abstract asserts and what the evidence, by the paper's own account, supports.
