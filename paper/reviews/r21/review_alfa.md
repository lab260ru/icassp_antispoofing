### Summary

The paper's steelmanned claim is genuinely interesting: TTS repetition-collapse is driven by periodicity, not sequence length, isolated via a length-matched-control design that is honestly the strongest single piece of evidence here (Fig. 1a, replicated across four recognisers to rule out a judge-side artifact). It then does something unusually disciplined — proposes a Lean-4-verified contraction mechanism for *why* periodicity would matter, measures the premise directly, and reports it false in every checkpoint tested, refusing to rescue the theory. That's good epistemic hygiene. The trouble is in how confidently the paper's quantitative apparatus is dressed: the headline "75.4 [71.1, 78.9] points" gap, the "P=0.996" Bayesian confirmation, and the "no map is a contraction... in any checkpoint" universal claim all rest on 3–6 checkpoints from 3–4 architecture families, and the paper's own sensitivity analysis shows the favourable posterior is largely prior-driven — a fact stated once, in passing, and never foregrounded. My overall stance: the core periodicity-vs-length claim is credible and well-designed; the statistical dressing around the generalisation claims overclaims precision the design cannot deliver, and one live alternative explanation (training-corpus frequency) is named by the authors but never tested.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The training-frequency/out-of-distribution alternative is named but never tested, and it is at least as parsimonious as "periodicity." `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — Discussion, "What the design does not separate": *"neither is separated from training-set frequency: k-fold repetition is far rarer in speech corpora than a matched distinct-word sentence, and an out-of-distribution account predicts the same dissociation."*
   **Why it matters** — This isn't a minor caveat; it's a rival hypothesis that predicts the *same* result as the paper's Fig. 1a. A model that has simply never seen "word word word word word word" in training could fail on it for reasons that have nothing to do with periodic attention dynamics — the entire mechanistic apparatus in Section 4 (contraction bound, Jacobian estimation) would then be answering the wrong question.
   **What would address it** — A frequency-matched control: synthetic fine-tuning or corpus-frequency-matched carrier phrases that are periodic but not rare, vs. rare-but-aperiodic carriers, to separate the two confounds the authors themselves flag as entangled.

2. **Issue** — The VITS/F5-TTS split and the duration-splice follow-up look shaped after the data was seen, not predicted in advance. `[Evidence — post-hoc]`
   **Where** — §3.2, "VITS shows no dissociation... F5-TTS does show it... What plausibly separates the two is where the count must live" and "with any gain above 8.3 points excluded."
   **Why it matters** — The mechanistic story ("duration budget vs. per-token duration") is constructed *after* observing that one non-AR baseline dissociates and the other doesn't, on n=2 architectures. That's exactly the shape of a post-hoc narrative — a story invented to explain a 2-item split, then treated as if it generalises. The specific "8.3 points" exclusion threshold in the follow-up experiment, introduced with no stated justification, is a classic post-hoc-tell.
   **What would address it** — State this explicitly as exploratory (the paper does this for Fig. 1b/c but not for this claim) and test the duration hypothesis on a third non-AR architecture with yet another duration-modelling strategy before asserting it as "what plausibly separates the two."

3. **Issue** — The main text is not self-contained, and the linked repository's own history shows the exact archival-pointer failure mode this creates. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — "Code, data, settings" paragraph, and (verified directly against `github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing`, the repo cited in this exact paragraph) commit `1790187` ("paper: the availability line pointed at a tag that predates the paper"): the tag the paper had been pointing readers to was *"cut before the judge replication, CosyVoice 2, the q measurement, the causal intervention, the probe extension... A reviewer following that link would have found none of the work the paper describes."* That commit's own message notes the check that would catch a recurrence — `verify_submission.sh`'s clean-tree gate — does not actually detect *which* snapshot the tag points to; only manual vigilance caught it this time.
   **Why it matters** — The main text offloads essentially all methodological detail to ~20 supplement sections (S1–S27) referenced almost every paragraph; the paper cannot be sanity-checked from its own body. That makes the pointed-to repository snapshot load-bearing for reproducibility in a way the automated submission gate does not actually protect. This is a process fragility, not a currently-broken link (the tag in the text I reviewed, `v1.2-locating`, appears current) — but it is evidence the failure mode is real and only caught by luck/manual review, not tooling.
   **What would address it** — Add an automated check that diffs the tagged commit against the numbers/claims actually cited in `main.tex` (not just a clean-tree check), so a future re-tagging slip is caught mechanically rather than by a reviewer or author noticing.

4. **Issue** — The formal-verification centrepiece is sold in the title and abstract but, by the authors' own account, explains none of the empirical results. `[Novelty — overselling a null component]`
   **Where** — Abstract: *"A Lean-4-verified bound says that if the per-repetition state map contracts, no Lipschitz readout can separate counts... We measure that premise... and reject it... so it never described these decoders at all."* §4: *"but it explains nothing in Section 3."*
   **Why it matters** — This is honest, but it means the actual positive contribution of the paper is the periodicity/length isolation (Section 3) plus the judge-methodology point (Whisper vs. CTC); the Lean-verified theorem contributes a formally clean *negative* result about a mechanism the authors themselves proposed and then discarded. Foregrounding "Lean-4-verified" in the title/abstract risks a reader coming away thinking the paper delivers a validated mechanistic account, when it delivers the opposite — a well-executed rejection of one candidate mechanism, with no replacement mechanism offered (Section 5 explicitly leaves "where the count fails" undecided, calling the causal-patch evidence inconclusive at n=5).
   **What would address it** — Reframe the abstract to lead with the periodicity/length result and present the Lean bound explicitly as "the mechanism we ruled out," rather than implying (via title placement) that formal verification is delivering positive explanatory content.

5. **Issue** — Headline effect sizes are reported with a precision the underlying sample sizes cannot support, and the Bayesian machinery's favourable output is foregrounded while its own prior-sensitivity check (which shows the effect "touches zero") is buried. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — Abstract and §3.1: *"a gap of 75.4 points [71.1, 78.9] by architecture family (n=3)"*; *"the posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel is 65.3 [5.9, 93.3], P=0.996... and under the widest prior on the group-level scales it touches zero (−1.6, P =0.956)."*
   **Why it matters** — A 95%-style interval computed from **n=3** architecture-family clusters ([71.1, 78.9]) communicates a precision that three data points structurally cannot deliver — the interval width is almost entirely a function of the parametric/prior assumptions of whichever of the "six interval constructions" was used, not of the data. The paper reports six different interval constructions for the same 3-cluster quantity and states "all six... exclude zero," which reads as robustness but is also consistent with a garden-of-forking-paths move: run every reasonable interval method on an underpowered stat and report that none of them happens to flip. The Bayesian partial-pooling result is worse: the authors' own "widest prior" sensitivity check shows the confirmatory P=0.996 result is largely an artefact of the assumed prior, not the n=3 data (P drops to 0.956 that it's positive under a wider prior, and the point estimate flips sign to −1.6) — yet the 0.996 figure is what appears in the flowing narrative and implicitly in the abstract's confident framing, while the near-null alternative is a subordinate clause. The CosyVoice-2 "confirmation" that follows is diagnostically weak for the same reason: a posterior-predictive interval of [5.9, 93.3] is wide enough that almost any positive, non-tiny gap would have "confirmed" it — the authors admit as much ("the agreement closer than it entitles us to expect") but this admission does not reach the abstract, where the CosyVoice-2 result functions rhetorically as validation. Separately, the paper never states whether the n's quoted for its aggregate stats (e.g., n=525 in §3.1) treat the three seeds per item as independent observations; if so, this is pseudoreplication that further inflates apparent precision.
   **What would address it** — Report the family-level interval as a range rather than a false-precision CI given n=3 (e.g., "3 of 3 families positive, magnitude 65–86 points" rather than [71.1, 78.9]); lead with the wide-prior sensitivity result rather than subordinating it; and state explicitly whether per-seed measurements are treated as independent in each reported n, with a variance decomposition if not.

### Minor concerns

- Table 1 gives point estimates only (median error, exact-rate %) with no per-cell n, SD, or CI — the reader can't tell how noisy any individual checkpoint's numbers are.
- "P=" is used for both what appears to be a Bayesian posterior probability (§3.1) and, implicitly, ordinary descriptive percentages elsewhere — worth a clarifying notation change so readers don't read these as frequentist p-values.
- Fig. 1(b)'s "y=x" reference line and the "control" curve are both described as sitting near the diagonal (controls near-exact); the caption doesn't clarify how visually distinguishable these two near-identical lines actually are in the printed figure.
- Fig. 1(c)'s y-axis label "d eff/dlog k" is unexplained in the caption — a reader needs the main text's "effective rank is the exponentiated entropy of the normalised singular-value spectrum" sentence to parse it, and even then the derivative-w.r.t.-log-k framing isn't spelled out.
- The intro's vivid hook ("six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six") references the "number-phrase families" in the stimulus set (§2), but no quantitative result for that sub-family appears in the body — the hook is never cashed out.
- Precision is inconsistent across the paper (some quantities to 3 significant figures, e.g. "0.996," others to 1, e.g. "76.7") without an evident rule.
- The paper cites its own companion piece [7] (arXiv:2604.08591) as already published/dated 2026 in a submission whose own review process presumably hasn't concluded — worth double-checking that self-citation dating is accurate at camera-ready time.

### Belief update

Modest, and directionally specific. I moderately update toward "TTS repetition-collapse is better described as a periodicity-tracking failure than a raw length/attention-span failure" — the length-matched control plus 4-recogniser replication is a real distinguishing test and the best evidence in the paper. I update much less on the mechanistic story, because there isn't one left standing: the paper's own Section 4 correctly talks itself out of the contraction account, which I credit as honest science rather than a negative result to be embarrassed about. I do **not** update on "architecture family X is more/less susceptible than Y in general" — the n=3-family statistics that would support that claim are reported with more confidence than 3 clusters can bear, and I'd treat the CosyVoice-2 "confirmation" as weak evidence at best given how wide its predictive interval was. Nothing here shifts my belief about the underlying contraction-theorem literature itself (Lean proof correctness isn't something I can or need to re-derive; I take the "no sorry" claim as verifiable in principle and did confirm no `sorry` appears in the linked repo's Lean files).

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-isolation result and judge-methodology contribution are solid, but the paper's generalisation claims (architecture-family gap, Bayesian confirmation, CosyVoice-2 validation) are reported with a statistical confidence that an n=3-family design cannot support, and the paper's own prior-sensitivity check — showing the effect "touches zero" under a wider prior — needs to be surfaced in the main narrative rather than left as a subordinate clause the abstract doesn't reflect.
