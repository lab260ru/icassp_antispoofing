### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech decoders miscount repeated phrases, and answers with a two-part argument: a Lean-4-verified theorem stating that if the per-repetition state update contracts (factor q<1), no Lipschitz readout of the decoder state — including its own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* set by q, the orbit scale, and the readout margin; and an empirical study across six AR TTS checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) showing a periodicity-specific undercounting deficit (median relative count error −8.3% at k≥6, 18.2% exact-match vs. 94.3% for length-matched non-repeating controls) that is absent in a non-autoregressive baseline (VITS) and only becomes saturating, rather than proportional, once the stimulus ladder is extended post hoc from k≤32 to k≤128. The paper is explicit that the theorem's premise (contraction) is not established — two direct attempts to measure it failed, and a dose-response test of the supporting attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed — so the claimed contribution is an implication (formally proved) alongside a phenomenologically matching but mechanistically unconfirmed empirical deficit.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem's one distinctive, falsifiable prediction (a horizon) only appears after a post-hoc range extension with no pre-registered stopping rule, run on a cherry-selectable subset of checkpoints, one of which contradicts it.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("Past the ladder, the count does level off"), contrasted with 4.1 where the pre-planned ladder (k≤32) explicitly does *not* show a horizon ("not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own framing is that "no horizon lies inside this ladder" under the design that was actually planned, and only after extending to k∈{48,64,96,128} — described as "post-hoc" by the authors themselves — does a saturation curve emerge. That extension (i) is run on only 4 of 6 checkpoints, with the exclusion of XTTS-v2 justified on ceiling grounds that are themselves outcome-contingent (its mel-token budget censors exactly the range where the effect would show), and (ii) among the 4 remaining, one (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows no saturation and an inverted ratio, which is folded into the narrative as "a conditional theorem behaving conditionally" rather than treated as a threat to the horizon claim. Without a pre-specified extension protocol and inclusion rule fixed before the k≤32 data were seen, this is structurally indistinguishable from extending measurement until the desired qualitative shape appears, and the reported ratio (K̂rep=30 vs K̂ctl=105, a 3.5-fold gap) inherits that uncertainty even though it is stated with a numerical confidence interval.
   **What would address it** — Report the k=48–128 extension explicitly as exploratory/hypothesis-generating (not confirmatory), and collect an independent replication ladder under a pre-registered protocol (fixed k range, fixed inclusion criteria, and ideally a redesigned stimulus/chunking scheme that lets XTTS-v2 be evaluated too) before treating the horizon estimate as a confirmed result of the theorem.

2. **Issue** — The mechanistic bridge from Lemma 1 (attention dilution) to Assumption 2 (contraction) is not merely unverified but actively contradicted by the paper's own dose-response test, and this is understated in the framing.
   **Where** — Section 4.4 / Discussion: "Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately (δ≤1.23 nats) and its dose-response prediction fails outright: within a (model, k) cell, flatter attention goes with better counting (r=0.59 [0.35, 0.75])."
   **Why it matters** — r=0.59 is not a null result; it is a moderate-to-strong correlation running opposite the direction the dilution mechanism requires (more dilution should impair counting, not improve it). Combined with the admission that "two attempts to measure the contraction factor failed," every empirical link in the causal chain Lemma 1 → Assumption 1 → Assumption 2 → Theorem 1 is either unmeasured or measured pointing the wrong way. The paper still treats the aggregate behavioral pattern (a periodicity-specific, roughly Theorem-1-shaped deficit) as evidence the theorem applies to real decoders. An alternative account — e.g., that repeated n-grams are simply out-of-distribution relative to training data and trigger a length/frequency-based stopping bias unrelated to any state-contraction dynamics — is at least as consistent with the reported data and is not experimentally excluded.
   **What would address it** — An intervention, not just an observational correlation: artificially flatten or sharpen attention over the repeated spans (temperature/masking manipulation) and measure the causal effect on counting, holding k and output length fixed, rather than relying on a within-cell correlation that is confounded by output length and degeneracy status.

3. **Issue** — The decoding-rule "rival explanation" (repetition penalty) is ruled out with an unbalanced manipulation that skips exactly the checkpoint where it would be most diagnostic.
   **Where** — Section 4.3, penalty-sweep paragraph ("Swept on 2 architectures the deficit stays put").
   **Why it matters** — The penalty ablation is run only on XTTS-v2 and Qwen3-TTS-0.6B, not on Qwen3-TTS-1.7B — the single checkpoint that shows *no* counting deficit at all (Table 1: 0.0% both conditions). That is precisely the case where a penalty manipulation is most informative: does disabling/varying its penalty ever induce a deficit, or is the null robust regardless of decoding rule? Leaving it untested weakens the claim that the decoding-rule rival is ruled out "in general," since the one discordant checkpoint is also the one never subjected to the control manipulation.
   **What would address it** — Extend the penalty/temperature sweep to all six checkpoints as a full factorial, prioritizing Qwen3-TTS-1.7B.

4. **Issue** — Pooled statistics (n=457, n=525, n=240, and the bootstrap CIs in Fig. 1) appear to mix non-independent observations — three seeds × items × checkpoints — without a stated clustering correction, while the paper simultaneously argues that only n=6 (checkpoints) is the valid unit for significance testing.
   **Where** — Sections 4.1 ("pooling items (n=457)... pooling weights checkpoints by surviving item count"), 4.3 (n=525, n=240), and Fig. 1(c) bootstrap intervals.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is admirably careful about power at the checkpoint level (explicitly noting the exact signed-rank floor at n=6 is 0.031 and that no multiplicity correction clears 0.05), but that scrutiny is not extended to the item/seed-pooled numbers that readers will treat as the paper's primary quantitative evidence (the −8.3% headline figure, the 94.3%/18.2% contrast). If three seeds of the same item on the same checkpoint are pooled as independent draws, the reported n's and their derived confidence intervals overstate precision.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly how seeds, items, and checkpoints are nested in each pooled statistic, and recompute intervals with cluster-robust or mixed-effects treatment of the nesting structure.

5. **Issue** — The "architecture, not periodicity" rival hypothesis is ruled out using a single non-AR model at a single size, not a matched panel.
   **Where** — Section 4.3, VITS paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — The AR panel comprises 6 checkpoints across 3 families; the counter-evidence for the architectural rival is one checkpoint of one non-AR family. A single model's null is weak evidence that *no* non-AR architecture exhibits the effect — VITS's own idiosyncrasies (duration predictor, flow vocoder, training corpus) could drive its null independent of the AR/non-AR distinction the paper wants to isolate, and the paper itself notes VITS is "smaller" than the panel, limiting even the within-model comparison it draws.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one more non-AR baseline (a second flow/diffusion-based TTS system) so the architectural contrast is a small panel rather than an n=1 comparison.

6. **Issue** — No statement of when exclusion criteria were fixed relative to seeing the data, and no statement of blinding for the manually-audited 165-clip sample.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Generations stopped by our token budget rather than the model, and one template whose fillers the judge never transcribes, are excluded throughout (S10)"; the by-ear audit sample).
   **Why it matters** — Exclusion rules that materially affect the numerator/denominator of the count-error statistics (truncated generations, one excluded template) are stated as procedural facts without saying whether they were decided before or after the effect was observed. Similarly, the 165-clip sample "so a judgement can be checked by ear" implies a manual/human step in the scoring pipeline, but the paper does not say whether that step was blind to condition (repeated vs. control) or model identity. Given that the central contrast in the paper is exactly repeated-vs-control, an unblinded or post-hoc adjudication step — however small — is a channel for the very confirmation bias that an otherwise objective CTC-WER pipeline was designed to avoid.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly that exclusion criteria were fixed prior to scoring (or disclose if they were not), and report whether the by-ear audit was blinded to condition and model.

### Minor concerns

- Table 1 shows Qwen3-TTS-1.7B has a 0.0% behavioral deficit yet still a capacity-gain ratio of 0.80 (below 1, i.e., some representational effect persists); this tension between "no behavioral deficit" and "reduced capacity gain" in the same checkpoint is noted in passing (Section 4.4) but not reconciled — worth a sentence explaining why the internal-state signature and the behavioral signature dissociate specifically in this checkpoint.
- Reference [1] is cited as documentation of the "stability problem" but is flagged as withdrawn (13 Feb 2026); the paper should clarify what, if anything, is still being relied on from a withdrawn source.
- The companion paper [7] (self-citation, same authors, same year) is used to motivate the "attractor regime" framing; since it appears contemporaneous and not independently validated by the field yet, the reliance on it for conceptual grounding should be flagged as such rather than cited as settled prior art.
- The abstract is extremely dense and syntactically compressed ("the count stays proportional rather than saturating, so no horizon is visible there; a post-hoc extension... finds one... though not in the one checkpoint that shows no deficit either"); this reads more like a set of internal notes than an abstract and would benefit from simplification for a general ICASSP audience.
- The δ≤1.23 nats bound (attention-logit spread) is reported without stating how it was estimated (over what population of items/positions), which affects reproducibility of the "premise holds only approximately" claim.
- The GitHub link (github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing) is not tied to a frozen commit, tag, or archival DOI, so "build.sh regenerates every number here" is not itself a stable claim if the repository changes after publication.
- The sentence "exact-match scores 30-of-32 and 3-of-32 alike and flips on ±1 of slop" is awkwardly phrased and its point (that exact-match is a noisy/brittle summary statistic) would land better stated directly.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-specific deficit itself is credibly demonstrated with reasonable confound controls (length-matched controls, a partial penalty sweep, a CTC-vs-Whisper judge audit), but the paper's two most theory-relevant claims — the existence of a horizon matching Theorem 1's shape, and the applicability of the contraction mechanism to real decoders — rest respectively on a post-hoc, non-preregistered range extension with a discordant checkpoint explained away after the fact, and on a dose-response test that the authors' own data reverses in sign; both need to be either strengthened with pre-registered follow-up data and a completed ablation matrix, or explicitly downgraded from "supported" to "not excluded" in the paper's framing.
