LENS: Statistical methodology (power, multiple comparisons, effect-size reporting, p-hacking)
STANCE: Skeptical-but-fair

### Summary
The paper asks whether TTS models' well-known tendency to lose count when a phrase is repeated many times is driven by input *length* or by *periodicity* per se, and answers with a parametric "repetition ladder" (180 stimuli, k ∈ {1…32}) in which each repeated-word item is paired with a length- and word-count-matched control whose repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus two non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a post-hoc fourth architecture (CosyVoice 2), repeated items are rendered exactly 18.2% of the time versus 94.3% for length-matched controls at k ≥ 6, a gap the authors report as robust to decoding strategy, repetition-penalty settings, exclusion rules, and choice of ASR judge (they show the "obvious" judge, autoregressive Whisper, is itself biased in the direction of the effect and substitute a CTC recognizer). A Lean-4-verified theoretical bound predicting the failure via state-map contraction is then measured on one checkpoint (Llasa-1B) and found not to hold (q = 38.1, expansive rather than contractive), so the authors conclude the proposed mechanism is refuted rather than confirmed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper runs a very large family of statistical comparisons (six checkpoints × four ASR judges × multiple k-thresholds × 420 "specification-curve" analytic choices × per-language replications × per-lag/per-sub-stack Jacobian measurements) without ever applying or mentioning a multiple-comparisons correction, and its headline uncertainty statement is highly sensitive to an unstated prior choice.
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("A mixed-effects linear probability model… puts the gap at 76.4 points; all six interval constructions exclude zero… under the widest prior on the group-level scales that interval does touch zero (−1.6, P = 0.956)"); Section 3.2's 420-specification robustness sweep (S11); Section 3.2's four-recognizer replication.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own text discloses that its central inferential quantity — probability that the true architecture-level gap exceeds zero — swings from P = 0.996 to a credible interval that *touches* zero (P = 0.956 the other way) purely as a function of prior width on group-level scales, and this is with only n = 3 architecture families, the actual unit of generalization the authors themselves identify ("we model the clustering rather than count checkpoints"). Three clusters gives essentially no information to separate a genuine architecture-level random effect from sampling noise in a hierarchical model; reporting a single headline P-value from this fit, and relegating the sensitivity analysis to a subordinate clause, risks presenting an unstable estimate as a settled one. Similarly, running 420 specifications and reporting only that "none reverses its sign" is a form of specification-curve/multiverse analysis, but without the formal aggregate test (e.g., a joint permutation p-value across the specification curve, à la Simonsohn et al.) that the multiverse-analysis literature treats as the actual inferential step rather than optional. A recent methodological critique argues multiverse analyses of exactly this shape can function as an "abdication of responsibility" for a single primary estimate when no formal aggregate test or pre-declared primary specification is given [2607.14623] — a criticism this paper is structurally exposed to, since Sections 3.1–3.2 report ranges and "positive in all N" counts rather than a single pre-registered confirmatory statistic with a corrected error rate.
   **What would address it** — Report the hierarchical model's prior specification and a prior-sensitivity table in the main text (not just "S16"/"S11" pointers); designate one specification (ideally the pre-registered one) as primary and treat the other 419 as a supplementary robustness display with a formal joint test, not just a sign count; and explicitly acknowledge that n = 3–4 architecture families cannot support strong claims about between-architecture variance regardless of how the within-architecture data are pooled.

2. **Issue** — The exact-match outcome is modeled with a *linear* probability model (not logistic/binomial GLMM) for a rate that is near a boundary (94.3% for controls, close to 100%), which is known to produce miscalibrated variance estimates and out-of-range fitted probabilities near ceiling/floor.
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "A mixed-effects linear probability model with architecture as a random effect… puts the gap at 76.4 points."
   **Why it matters** — With control accuracy pinned near 100% in several checkpoints (Table 1: Qwen3-TTS-0.6B controls 100.0%, Qwen3-TTS-1.7B controls 98.9%), an LPM's homoskedastic-Gaussian residual assumption is badly violated exactly where the effect is largest, and reported point estimates/CIs for "percentage-point gap" from an LPM are not directly comparable to (and can diverge from) what a mixed-effects logistic model would yield for the same data, especially under nesting with few top-level clusters.
   **What would address it** — Re-fit with a mixed-effects logistic/binomial model (or Firth-corrected variant given near-boundary rates) and report both the odds-ratio-scale and marginal-probability-scale effect sizes, or justify the LPM choice explicitly and show it doesn't change the qualitative conclusion.

3. **Issue** — The mechanism-refutation section (Section 4) generalizes a single-checkpoint, small-n result to a paper-wide "reject" claim in the abstract, without a stated correction for the several sub-analyses run on the same n = 29 items (per-lag, per-sub-stack, paired sign test, dose-response correlation).
   **Where** — Abstract ("We measure that premise on one checkpoint with a validated estimator and reject it there"); Section 4 ("q = 38.1 [32.9, 51.4] against 31.8… q < 1 in 0 of 29 items, in every lag and sub-stack measured… paired p = 0.017… r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]").
   **Why it matters** — Running the contraction test across "every lag and sub-stack measured" and a paired sign test and a correlation on the same 29-item sample is a multi-endpoint analysis on a small sample from a single checkpoint; the paper does report this scope caveat once in the Discussion ("Both are single-checkpoint results on Llasa-1B"), but the abstract's phrasing ("no Lipschitz readout can separate counts… we reject it there… it never described that decoder at all") reads as a general dismissal of the theoretical mechanism rather than a bounded, single-checkpoint, likely-underpowered rejection. A q estimate with CI [32.9, 51.4] from n=29 teacher-forced trajectories is informative but the multiple related statistics drawn from the same 29 items (lag-wise, sub-stack-wise, sign test, correlation) inflate the appearance of converging evidence without a joint test or correction.
   **What would address it** — State a single pre-specified primary contraction test and correction for the secondary tests (lag/sub-stack breakdowns), or explicitly frame all of them as one robustness display for a single q estimate rather than independent confirmations; soften the abstract's "reject it there" to match the single-checkpoint scope stated in the Discussion.

4. **Issue** — Several supporting sub-analyses have very small samples used to support definitive causal language, with no power analysis or minimum-detectable-effect statement anywhere in the paper.
   **Where** — Whisper audit (n = 12 over 6 donors, Section 2); CTC audit (n = 24, Section 2); rank-1 patch experiment (n = 36, Section 5: "removes the damage… moves the count not at all (0.00 log-count [−0.09, 0.09], n = 36)"); self-patch check (18/18, Section 5); probe-past-horizon test ("inconclusive at n = 5", Section 5); F5-TTS duration-splitting sub-experiment (n = 30 and n = 60, Section 3.2).
   **Why it matters** — Claims like "removes the damage" and the decoder "ignores" the transplanted count coordinate are strong causal conclusions built on n = 36 (and a null result read as informative, not merely underpowered) and n = 5 (explicitly flagged as inconclusive, which is appropriate, but its presence alongside a run of confidently-worded n≈30 results in the same section makes the overall evidentiary weight of Section 5 hard to calibrate from the text alone).
   **What would address it** — Report a power/sensitivity analysis (or at minimum the smallest effect the sample size could reliably detect) for the rank-1 patch and duration-splitting experiments, and distinguish in-text between "no effect detected" and "effect ruled out" language accordingly.

### Minor concerns
- Table 1's per-checkpoint control exact-match values (91.5–100.0%) are somewhat higher on average than the headline "94.3%" figure quoted in the abstract/Section 3.1; the aggregation method (unweighted mean across checkpoints vs. pooled-item mean) isn't stated, so the two numbers can't be reconciled from the text alone.
- The abstract's companion-paper citation [7] is dated "2026" with an arXiv ID (2604.08591) that would place it after this manuscript's own apparent writing window; if that paper is unpublished or a preprint-in-progress at submission time, its status should be flagged as such rather than cited as a settled companion result.
- Figure 1(b)'s axis label text ("48 64 96 128 requested k") suggests missing tick spacing in figure extraction/typesetting; verify the camera-ready figure renders these as separate, legible tick labels.
- Section 2 states the CTC judge "skip[s] unrecognised" tokens "rather than stopping at the first miss," but the false-negative rate this scoring rule introduces (crediting a match that appears later in the transcript than the model actually produced) isn't separately quantified — only the aggregate ratio-to-truth validation is given.
- "P(gap > 0) = 0.996" and the alternate "P = 0.956" (touching zero) are reported without stating which credible-interval type (equal-tailed vs. HPD) or which specific prior was used for each, making the two numbers not independently checkable from the main text.
- The claim that CosyVoice 2's prediction "committed before the checkpoint's first waveform existed" is a nice piece of design (a genuine held-out prediction), but the paper doesn't state whether the *outcome metric* used to score the prediction (exact-match rate specifically, vs. count error or another candidate metric) was also fixed in advance, or chosen post hoc among several candidate correspondence metrics.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the core periodicity-vs-length manipulation and multi-judge robustness work are genuinely careful, but the paper's uncorrected multiplicity across dozens of tests/specifications, a headline hierarchical-model estimate that the text itself shows is prior-sensitive down to touching zero, and single-checkpoint/small-n mechanism claims stated in unqualified language in the abstract need to be resolved before the strength of the causal and mechanistic claims matches the evidence presented.
