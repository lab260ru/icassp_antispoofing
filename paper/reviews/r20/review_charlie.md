# Review by charlie

### Summary

Steelmanned, this is one of the more statistically self-aware papers I've seen come out of a TTS lab: it separates periodicity from length with a genuine length-matched control, treats judge selection as a result rather than an assumption, runs a real specification curve (420 forking-path variants, none sign-reversing), makes an out-of-sample architecture prediction and reports the miss-or-hit honestly, and — rarest of all — takes its own proposed mechanism (a Lean-verified Lipschitz-contraction bound) out behind the shed and shoots it when the data say `q ≫ 1`. That is exactly the epistemic move Nanda-style reviewing wants to reward: a paper that could have quietly dropped the falsified theory section instead reports it as a finding.

Pressing on that steelmanned version, though: the confirmatory backbone of the paper — the mixed-effects/partial-pooling machinery that produces the headline "75–76 point gap" and the "65.3-point predicted gap for an unseen architecture" — rests on **three architecture-family clusters**, a sample size at which hierarchical variance components are known to be unstable, and the paper's own reported intervals show this (the group-level-prior-sensitivity interval touches zero at −1.6). The mechanism-refutation section (§4), which is the paper's most novel and most quotable result, is a single-checkpoint, n=29 result with a paired p=0.017 — a value the paper elsewhere explicitly tells the reader to distrust ("Be openly skeptical of .01<p<.05" is this reviewer's framework, but the paper's own repeated insistence on pre-specification and multi-recognizer robustness shows it holds itself to exactly that standard elsewhere, and doesn't apply it here). The abstract's claim to be "ruling out the judge-side artifact" leans on judge-validation audits of n=12 and n=24 concatenative trials. None of these are fatal individually, but they cluster on the same small set of decisive numbers, and the paper's confident language doesn't downgrade to match the tiny denominators. Overall: real, careful work, oversold in its most quotable places by exactly the standard the authors set for themselves.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The title's claim ("periodicity, not length") is not actually isolated from a plausible confound the paper itself names and never tests. `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — Discussion, "What the design does not separate": *"neither is [periodicity] separated from training-set frequency, k-fold repetition being far rarer in speech corpora than a matched distinct-word sentence, so an out-of-distribution account predicts the same dissociation."*
   **Why it matters** — This is the paper's central, title-level claim. An OOD-rarity account is not a minor caveat; it predicts the identical dissociation pattern reported in Fig. 1(a) without any special role for periodicity per se. A reader who reads only the title and abstract ("The limit tracks periodicity, not length") comes away with a stronger claim than the body — which admits it can't distinguish periodicity from corpus rarity — actually supports.
   **What would address it** — A control that holds token-frequency-in-corpus roughly constant while varying periodicity (e.g., a *periodic but individually common* filler pattern vs. the current *aperiodic, individually common* fillers), or at minimum retitling/rehedging the headline claim to "periodicity (confounded with rarity), not length."

2. **Issue** — The paper generalizes a "the deficit tracks periodicity" claim to a "TTS" level, but the internal baseline results actively contradict any single mechanistic story, and this isn't reconciled cleanly with the headline framing. `[Evidence — distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — §3.2, VITS/F5-TTS comparison.
   **Why it matters** — VITS shows *no* dissociation (65.6% vs 58.9%, gap −6.7) while F5-TTS shows a large one (+60.0). The paper's own proposed resolution ("what plausibly separates the two is where the count must live") is introduced post-hoc after seeing exactly two data points (VITS, F5-TTS) and is not itself tested against a third non-AR architecture with a different duration strategy. Two baselines is not enough to support a mechanistic distinction that is then treated as settled in the Discussion ("Absence of an autoregressive decoder confers no immunity").
   **What would address it** — A third non-AR architecture with yet another duration-allocation strategy (e.g. token-level with global length correction, or diffusion-based duration prediction) to see whether the "must-represent-total-duration" story predicts its behaviour out of sample, the same way the CosyVoice 2 test was used for the periodicity-gap prediction.

3. **Issue** — Baseline/comparison breadth for the "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" claim is thin: two non-AR baselines, no reported tuning effort, and CosyVoice 2 enters only as a single targeted confirmatory test, not as a fully profiled arm. `[Evidence — baselines]`
   **Where** — §2 ("Models"), §3.2.
   **Why it matters** — The claim that the failure "is not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" is a strong, generalizable-sounding claim built on n=2 non-AR systems, run presumably at default settings (no mention of decoding/duration-model tuning comparable to the repetition-penalty sweep given to the AR models). If F5-TTS's flow-matching implementation were under-tuned for this task relative to how carefully the AR checkpoints' decoding was swept, the asymmetry in effort would manufacture exactly this contrast.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly whether F5-TTS/VITS were run at tuned or default settings, and ideally include one more diffusion/flow-based TTS system to avoid a claim resting on one system per side.

4. **Issue** — The count-probe R² (0.48) that anchors the "count is not erased, it stays decodable" causal-half claim in the Discussion may be optimistically biased by post-hoc layer selection. `[Statistical rigour / Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — §2 ("State measurement": "the layer chosen by leave-one-template-out R²"), §5 ("it stays decodable late (R² 0.48)").
   **Why it matters** — Selecting the reporting layer by maximizing leave-one-template-out R² and then reporting that same R² as the headline number is a classic winner's-curse setup unless the final number comes from a further, independent held-out fold. The supplement (S6) is not in the text provided, so this can't be checked, but as described the procedure is ambiguous on this point, and it's load-bearing for the paper's most interesting causal claim (probe-transplant "decoder ignores it").
   **What would address it** — A nested/double cross-validation report: layer chosen on inner folds, R² reported on a disjoint outer fold, stated explicitly rather than left to a supplement.

5. **Issue** — The paper's two most decisive, most quotable results — the mechanism refutation (q=38.1, §4) and the abstract's claim to have "ruled out" a judge-side artifact — rest on samples (n=29 single-checkpoint; n=12/n=24 judge audits) and a borderline p-value (p=0.017) that the paper's own evidentiary standard elsewhere (extensive robustness sweeps, explicit "pre-specified" vs "exploratory" labeling, four-recognizer cross-checks) would treat with skepticism, but here does not. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — §4 ("Measuring q"): *"paired p = 0.017, repeated lower in 8 of 29 pairs"*; §2 ("Behavioural measurement"): Whisper audit "n=12 over 6 donors," CTC audit "n=24"; Abstract: *"ruling out the judge-side artifact."*
   **Why it matters** — Three separate rigor problems stack on the same load-bearing claims. (a) *Cluster count*: the headline 75.4/76.4/65.3-point gaps come from a mixed-effects model with only **three** top-level (architecture-family) clusters; variance-component estimates from hierarchical models with this few groups are notoriously unstable and prone to both over- and under-coverage — the concern isn't hypothetical here, since the paper's own "widest prior" interval touches zero (−1.6, P=0.956), a fact buried mid-paragraph rather than propagated into the headline framing. Eager & Roy's "Mixed Effects Models are Sometimes Terrible" [1701.04858] documents exactly this failure mode for small-cluster random effects in the same generalized-linear-mixed-model family the paper uses, and its central lesson (report and stress-test the random-effects structure, don't just cite the point estimate) is not visibly applied here. (b) *The mechanism section has no pre-registration label.* §3.1/3.2 are explicitly headed "Pre-specified" and §3.3 "Exploratory" — the paper clearly has this taxonomy in mind — but §4, where q is measured and the theorem's antecedent is "measured and rejected," carries no such label, and no timestamp analogous to the CosyVoice-2 "S16 timestamps it" evidence is offered for the q protocol (three gates, teacher-forcing choice, p=0.017 threshold). Given the paper's own demonstrated willingness to timestamp pre-registration when it strengthens a claim, its absence here is itself informative. (c) *Small-n judge validation carries a big rhetorical load*: "ruling out the judge-side artifact" in the abstract is asserted off a 12-trial and a 24-trial concatenative audit — clean synthetic ground truth, but a tiny n from which to certify the instrument that scores all 1558 downstream generations. None of this means the refutation is wrong — q=38.1 vs. a q<1 requirement is a large effect, and "expansive on repeated and control text alike" is a genuinely clean qualitative signature — but the paper states it with more certainty ("we reject it there," "decisive rather than marginal") than n=29-on-one-checkpoint plus p=0.017 licenses, and doesn't flag the small-cluster fragility of its own headline mixed-effects numbers the way it flags almost everything else.
   **What would address it** — Report the q result explicitly as exploratory pending replication on a second checkpoint; run a small-sample-corrected variance estimator (e.g. Kenward-Roger or a fully Bayesian model with weakly-informative priors reported as primary, not the flat-prior version) for the 3-cluster mixed model and lead with that instead of point estimates; expand the judge-validation audit past n=12/24 given how much of the paper's evidentiary chain depends on the CTC-vs-Whisper contrast being reliable.

### Minor concerns

- Fig. 1(c)'s "capacity gain" (`d_eff/d log k`) is never defined in the main text — the reader is sent to S17 to understand the y-axis of a headline figure, which fails the "interpretable from caption alone" bar.
- Results prose is extremely numerically dense (e.g., the "by family... by checkpoint... including the checkpoint whose median-error gap is zero..." sentence in §3.1) — several sentences pack 3–4 distinct statistics with different denominators into one clause, which will cost most readers a second and third pass to parse correctly rather than clarifying the finding.
- The self-citation to a companion paper [7], dated 2026 and evidently contemporaneous work by the same authors, is used to establish general credibility for the judge-audit methodology ("companion to a spectral study of hallucination... in encoder–decoder ASR") without independent verification available to the reader — not wrong, but worth flagging as a soft circularity.
- "S9, S27" and similar supplement pointers for load-bearing claims (e.g., the Spanish-language replication) mean several claims can't be checked from the paper text alone; the Limitations section itself admits the Spanish arm "came out unreportable rather than replicated" (S27), which is a slightly confusing gloss on an earlier abstract-level claim ("Nor is the contrast an English artefact... the same audit in Spanish gives 1.00 against 0.19") — the abstract-adjacent text and the limitations section read in slight tension.
- The rank-1 patch experiment's "0.00 log-count [−0.09, 0.09], n=36" and "self-patch bitwise-identical 18/18" are reported without units/definition of "log-count" change scale for a reader unfamiliar with the probing setup.
- CosyVoice 2's repetition-aware sampling rule is mentioned ("does not prevent that") without describing what the rule actually does, leaving the reader unable to judge whether it was a fair test of that mitigation.

### Belief update

I moved my belief that "TTS repetition failures are just a length/attention-span problem" down substantially — the length-matched control in Fig. 1(a) is a genuinely well-designed distinguishing test and the control-median-at-zero result is hard to explain away. I also updated toward "the field's default explanation (contraction / representational erasure) for these failures is probably wrong, at least sometimes" — a Lean-verified bound being empirically falsified on its own premise, cleanly and with a stated lower-bound argument for why the falsification is conservative, is a genuinely informative negative result, and I don't see an easy way to rescue the contraction story from `q=38.1`. I did *not* update much on the specific magnitude of "the periodicity effect" (75–76 points) or on "the count is causally read but ignored" (the patching claim) — both rest on samples and cluster counts too small to trust the precise numbers, even though the qualitative direction is probably right.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core manipulation and the mechanism-refutation are worth publishing, but the headline mixed-effects gap estimates rest on a 3-cluster hierarchical model whose own reported prior-sensitivity interval touches zero, and the paper's most decisive claims (q refutation, "ruling out" the judge artifact) lean on small-n, borderline-p evidence stated with more confidence than the paper's own stated standards for p-values and pre-registration would grant elsewhere in the same manuscript `[Statistical rigour]`.
