# Peer Review — "Periodicity, Not Length: Isolating the Repetition-Counting Failure in Neural Text-to-Speech"

## Summary

The paper asks whether TTS models' well-documented tendency to loop, truncate, or lose count on repeated phrases is driven by input length (attention span, exposure bias, duration budget) or by periodicity per se. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" that pairs each repeated-word stimulus with a length-matched control (same carrier, same word count, but non-repeating fillers), the authors test six checkpoints across three TTS architectures (Llasa, Qwen3-TTS, XTTS-v2) plus two non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS). They report exact-count rendering in 18.2% of repeated-text generations versus 94.3% of length-matched controls at k≥6, a gap that a companion "period ladder" (fixing carrier/word-count/total-count and varying only the recurrence period p) shows scales monotonically with periodicity rather than jumping at a length threshold. They further argue the standard judge (Whisper) is itself biased against detecting the phenomenon and substitute a CTC recognizer, and they formally derive (in Lean 4) and then empirically refute a contraction-based explanation for why counting fails past a horizon.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The default control condition is not actually non-periodic at the k values (16, 24, 32) that drive the largest reported gaps, because its filler pool is "cycled above k=8," reintroducing exactly the periodic structure the control is supposed to remove.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Stimuli" ("replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k = 8"); Section 3.1 headline statistics (18.2% vs. 94.3%, Fig. 1a, Table 1, which run to k=32).
   **Why it matters** — The paper's central logic is a subtraction: repeated text minus a length-matched, non-periodic control isolates periodicity. But for k>8 under the default (cycled) filler pool, the control text is itself periodic with period 8 (word A…H, A…H, A…H). The authors' own secondary analysis quantifies that this matters — the family-level gap shrinks from 75.4 (cycled) to 67.6 points (never-cycled) — an 8-point swing attributable purely to a design artifact the paper elsewhere treats as diagnostic in its own right (e.g., CTC blank-collapse is flagged as suspect precisely because it "has the effect's own periodicity signature"). Yet the headline 18.2%/94.3% figures and Table 1 are not clearly labeled as coming from the never-cycled subset, so the number the abstract leads with may itself be partly a period-vs-period comparison rather than a period-vs-non-period one at the highest, most dramatic k values.
   **What would address it** — Either (a) report the primary statistics (Fig. 1a, Table 1, abstract headline numbers) exclusively from the never-cycled control arm, or (b) expand the filler pool to ≥32 unique items so no control stimulus at any k is periodic, and state explicitly which pool backs every headline number.

2. **Issue** — The pivotal k≥6 threshold was chosen after inspecting where the two curves diverge, and the subsequent "420 specifications, none reversing sign" robustness analysis is computed entirely on the same fixed dataset that motivated the threshold, so it demonstrates robustness to analytic choices, not out-of-sample replication.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "The k≥6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge, which is why the abstract gives the unconditioned figure too... Two exploratory analyses, dated in S16, are reported in text alone."
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook researcher-degrees-of-freedom setup: a threshold selected post hoc, defended by a specification curve run over the same corpus. A specification curve controls for garden-of-forking-paths *within* one dataset; it does not substitute for pre-registration or held-out replication. The one genuine pre-registered extrapolation the paper offers — CosyVoice-2, tested from "a fit committed before the checkpoint's first waveform existed" — is commendable but is a single additional checkpoint, not a systematic held-out validation of the whole ladder.
   **What would address it** — Pre-register (or timestamp before data collection) the primary threshold and outcome definition for any future checkpoints, and treat the CosyVoice-2-style held-out test as the norm rather than the exception — ideally with 2–3 more out-of-panel checkpoints, not one.

3. **Issue** — The mechanism test in Section 4 estimates the contraction constant q under teacher-forcing (feeding back the model's own already-generated tokens through a controlled Jacobian computation), which is a different dynamical regime from the free-running autoregressive generation whose failure is under study.
   **Where** — Section 4, "Measuring q": "teacher-forcing the generated tokens so the map is well defined... Teacher-forcing removes the token channel, so these are lower bounds."
   **Why it matters** — The paper's strong claim — "we reject it: q = 21.0–347.7... it never described any decoder we measured" — is a claim about the process that actually generates the looping/miscounting behavior. Teacher-forcing changes the input distribution the boundary-to-boundary map sees (it never has to condition on its own errors, near-repeats, or degenerate loop states), and the authors' own caveat that this yields only a "lower bound" concedes the mismatch. A lower bound on q under a proxy regime does not establish non-contraction of the regime that actually produces the phenomenon; it is possible the free-running trajectory enters a different, contracting basin that teacher-forcing never visits.
   **What would address it** — Either estimate q (or a suitable local analogue) along free-running rollouts, with an explicit treatment of the ill-definedness this introduces, or soften the claim from "refuted in every decoder we measured" to "refuted along the teacher-forced trajectory in every decoder we measured," and discuss what the discrepancy could hide.

4. **Issue** — Cross-architecture and cross-checkpoint comparisons of the periodicity gap are not decoding-setting matched; only two of six checkpoints receive a repetition-penalty/temperature sweep, and the paper's single "immune" counter-example for the mechanistic story (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) is exempted from this control.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Sampling uses each system's shipped defaults... they make Qwen3-TTS the most stochastic family, so its larger checkpoint's null is not an artifact of conservative decoding" — asserted, not swept); Section 3.2 (penalty sweep run on XTTS-v2 and Qwen3-TTS-0.6B only).
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own ablation shows repetition penalty alone moves exact-match rates by several points within an architecture, i.e., decoding configuration is a real confound with the gap magnitude. Cross-checkpoint claims about "the gap by family/checkpoint" (Table 1, the mixed-effects estimate of 76.4 points) therefore compare architecture *and* decoding defaults simultaneously. This bites hardest exactly where the paper leans most on a single checkpoint's qualitative uniqueness — Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is the one checkpoint with zero median count error and is central to the Section 4 argument that q doesn't track counting ability — yet its decoding-independence is asserted rather than demonstrated.
   **What would address it** — Run the penalty/temperature sweep on all six checkpoints, or at minimum on Qwen3-TTS-1.7B specifically, before treating its near-zero deficit as an architecture-level fact rather than a decoding-configuration artifact.

5. **Issue** — Several analyses that the paper leans on for pivotal qualitative claims (judge validation, F5-TTS "no immunity" result, VITS non-dissociation) use small, uneven sample sizes and at least one undisclosed-timing post hoc exclusion rule.
   **Where** — Section 2 (Whisper audit n=12 over 6 donors; CTC audit n=24); Section 3.2 (F5-TTS duration-supplied test, n=60, "any gain above 8.3 points excluded"; VITS comparison n=90).
   **Why it matters** — The Whisper-vs-CTC judge-validation claim (0.19 vs. 1.00 ratio) — which the entire measurement methodology pivots on — rests on n=12 and n=24 respectively; no confidence interval is given for these ratios despite CIs being reported elsewhere in the paper (e.g., the mixed-effects gap). The F5-TTS duration experiment excludes any gain above 8.3 points without stating whether this rule was set before or after seeing the data, which is exactly the kind of undisclosed analytic choice the paper is otherwise scrupulous about flagging (cf. its extensive treatment of exclusion-rule sensitivity in Section 3.2 for the main ladder).
   **What would address it** — Report bootstrap or exact binomial CIs for the judge-validation ratios and the F5-TTS split, and state explicitly when the 8.3-point exclusion threshold was chosen relative to seeing the results (or drop it and report the raw distribution).

## Minor concerns

- The "obvious judge is wrong" methodological contribution would be strengthened by citing existing Whisper-hallucination literature (e.g., work on Whisper hallucination induced by non-speech/degenerate audio, and internals-based Whisper hallucination detection) rather than presenting the autoregressive-bias diagnosis as if arising purely from this study's own audit.
- The paper states "our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values" while simultaneously reporting multiple bootstrap/interval constructions and treating "all six interval constructions exclude zero" as load-bearing evidence — the stated philosophy and the actual practice pull in different directions and should be reconciled in one paragraph.
- The GitHub repository name ("icassp_antispoofing") is flagged as pre-dating the study but not explained; for a reproducibility-focused paper this deserves one clarifying sentence so reviewers/readers aren't confused about scope.
- Fig. 1's caption and the prose throughout are extremely dense (near one statistic per clause); this materially slows verification of specific numbers against specific claims and would benefit from a companion results table separating "headline," "robustness," and "mechanism" numbers.
- "n=3" is reused for both "architecture families" and, elsewhere in the audit description, small donor/audio counts — worth disambiguating on first use each time.
- The self-citation of a companion paper (arXiv:2604.08591) should be double-checked for public availability at submission/camera-ready time.

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the core periodicity-vs-length dissociation is well-motivated and partially self-audited, but a genuine, fixable confound in the control-arm filler cycling contaminates the headline statistics at exactly the k values driving the largest reported effect, and the mechanistic refutation in Section 4 tests a teacher-forced proxy rather than the free-running process it is used to rule out.
