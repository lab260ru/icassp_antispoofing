## Summary

The paper asks whether neural TTS models' well-documented tendency to lose count on repeated phrases is driven by input *length* or by *periodicity* per se. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" (a target word repeated k times, paired with a length-matched control in which the repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers) and a companion "period ladder" (fixed carrier/word-count/requested-count, varying only the recurrence period p), the authors test six checkpoints across three architecture families plus two non-autoregressive baselines. They report a large gap in exact-count accuracy between repeated and control stimuli (18.2% vs 94.3% at k≥6), show the gap is graded with period rather than a step function, argue it survives decoding, exclusion, and judge-choice perturbations, and then test — and reject — a Lean-4-verified contraction-based mechanistic account of the failure, concluding the count is representable but not reliably readable out.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The period ladder confounds "period" with the number of distinct word types cycling through the repeated span, so the graded p=1→2→4→8 accuracy curve is equally consistent with a local-predictability (entropy-of-next-token) account as with a "periodicity" account.
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "Periodicity, or verbatim identity?" and Fig. 1(b), where rungs p=2, 4, 8 "share one 8-word pool," meaning p=2 draws on 2 of 8 types and p=8 on all 8.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's second-strongest claim (that the deficit tracks periodicity, not verbatim identity) rests entirely on this ladder. But as p increases, so does the local surprisal of the upcoming token (harder to predict the next word when more types are in rotation), which is a distinct construct from recurrence structure. A model that is bad at suppressing a highly-predictable-but-not-immediately-adjacent repeat (low local entropy) would show exactly this monotone curve without any sensitivity to "periodicity" in the sense the paper wants to claim. The p=2 test rules out *pure adjacency* (no token is next to itself), which is good, but it does not rule out this local-entropy alternative, because p=2 already has lower type diversity/lower entropy than p=4 or p=8.
   **What would address it** — Add a rung that holds period fixed while varying the type pool size (e.g., p=4 with a 4-word vs. an 8-word pool), or an aperiodic-but-low-entropy control (a fixed non-periodic ordering of the same k tokens) to separate "periodic recurrence" from "predictable next token."

2. **Issue** — The primary result has essentially one blinded/pre-registered confirmatory test (CosyVoice2, n=90, committed before the run); everything else — the k≥6 threshold, the exclusion rules, the choice of recognizer, the 420-specification curve — is acknowledged by the authors themselves to be chosen or explored after seeing the data.
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("The k≥6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge… Across 420 specifications… we correct across none of them") and the CosyVoice2 paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — The authors are commendably transparent about this, but transparency does not neutralize the risk: a specification curve run *after* seeing the data characterizes robustness of an already-observed effect, it does not validate the effect out-of-sample. The one true held-out test (CosyVoice2) uses n=90 and, by the authors' own admission, its partial-pooling prediction interval "touches zero" under a wide prior — i.e., the hierarchical model with only 3 architecture-family groups barely constrains between-family variance. Treating CosyVoice2 landing "nearer its 65.3 mean than an interval that wide entitles us to expect" as confirmatory is a soft, almost narrative standard of evidence for what is otherwise presented as the paper's central generalization claim.
   **What would address it** — Register (before running) a second, independent held-out architecture/checkpoint with a pre-specified threshold for what would falsify the claim, and report calibration (how often would a null hypothesis of "no periodicity effect" also land in that range given n=3 architecture groups?).

3. **Issue** — The control stimuli are not actually length-matched-and-aperiodic once k>8: the filler pool is "cycled above k=8," so a k=32 control item repeats an 8-token cycle four times, i.e., it is itself periodic (with period 8) — the very property the manipulation is supposed to remove from the control arm.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Stimuli"): "the repeated token replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k=8"; Section 3.1 acknowledges this via the never-cycled-vs-cycled comparison (67.6 vs 75.4 points) and asserts "most of that 7.1 points is vocabulary rather than periodicity."
   **Why it matters** — For every item at k>8 (a non-trivial fraction of the ladder, since k reaches 32), the "length-matched control" contains genuine period-8 structure, undermining the clean length-vs-periodicity dissociation the paper's headline rests on precisely in the regime (large k) where the effect is largest. The authors do report the never-cycled comparison as a robustness check and prefer the more conservative number, which is good practice — but the claim that the residual 7.1-point difference is "vocabulary rather than periodicity" is asserted without a stated method (no decomposition procedure, model, or statistic is given for how "vocabulary" and "periodicity" contributions were separated).
   **What would address it** — Report results restricted to k≤8 (where no cycling confound exists) as a standalone robustness check, and specify the statistical procedure used to attribute the 7.1-point gap between "vocabulary" and "periodicity" components.

4. **Issue** — Sample sizes underlying several load-bearing measurement-validation claims are very small, and the causal-intervention null result in Section 5 reports no n and no multiple-comparison correction.
   **Where** — Judge audit: "n=12 over 6 donors" for Whisper, "n=24, mean 0.996" for the CTC recognizer (Section 2); readout-intervention test: "6–20% of a full transfer on three checkpoints… indistinguishable from a sampler re-roll on all 3 readouts (p ≥ 0.38)" (Section 5).
   **Why it matters** — The entire 2052-generation study depends on the CTC judge being validated as accurate; that validation itself rests on n=12–24 concatenative-audio trials, an unusually thin foundation for an instrument used to underwrite every downstream percentage in the paper. Separately, the Section 5 null (patching the count coordinate doesn't move the count) is used to support a substantive conclusion ("the bound bounds an intervention the decoder does not register") but is reported with p-values ≥0.38 on only 3 readouts and no stated n per condition — a null this weakly powered cannot distinguish "no effect" from "underpowered test," yet it is treated as informative.
   **What would address it** — Report per-condition n and a power/precision analysis for the intervention experiment; expand the judge-validation audit set or bound the CTC accuracy estimate with a confidence interval rather than a point estimate.

5. **Issue** (secondary, related-work angle within the design lens) — The paper does not engage with the broader length-generalization literature that has already developed controlled manipulations to separate "count" from "operand/sequence length" in transformers, despite this being methodologically the closest prior art to the paper's central design move.
   **Where** — Absent; not cited in Related Work (Section 5) or Introduction.
   **Why it matters** — [2410.15787] ("Arithmetic Transformers Can Length-Generalize in Both Operand Length and Count") explicitly studies transformers' ability to generalize over operand *count* independent of operand *length* in arithmetic tasks, which is conceptually the same disentanglement problem this paper poses for TTS. Not situating the periodicity/length dissociation relative to this line weakens the novelty framing of "we contribute…a controlled manipulation isolating periodicity from length" — the manipulation is genuinely useful for TTS specifically, but the general strategy (decouple item-count from sequence-length via a matched-length control) is not new to this paper's home discipline, and the omission makes the contribution claim read stronger than it should.
   **What would address it** — Cite and contrast with the count-vs-length generalization literature; clarify what is novel to TTS/audio versus what is a reapplication of a known disentanglement design.

## Minor concerns

- "n=12 over 6 donors" — "donors" is undefined (presumably voice-cloning reference speakers); should be spelled out on first use.
- The "five rules" used to compute the p=2 "surviving fraction" (0.547 strict, 0.69–0.86 "recount rules") are never enumerated in the main text, only referenced to S28 — impossible to evaluate the robustness claim without the supplement.
- The RSS comparison ("2.6 against 485.3") used to argue the deficit is "proportional, not saturating" is given without degrees of freedom, model complexity, or a formal test (F-test/AIC/BIC) — two bare RSS numbers are not self-interpreting.
- CosyVoice2 is not introduced in the Method/"Models" paragraph (which lists Llasa, Qwen3-TTS, XTTS-v2, VITS, F5-TTS) but appears in Results as an "architecture outside the panel"; the stimuli subset used for its n=90 test (which k-values, how many templates) is not specified in the main text.
- ˆc's "target unit" (word-level? phoneme-level?) is not explicitly defined at first use.
- Self-citation [7] (arXiv:2604.08591) is a companion paper from the same authors and cannot be independently verified from the text given; flag as a dependency worth noting for readers checking claims.
- The abstract markets the Lean-4 proof as a headline result ("A Lean-4-verified bound says…") but the paper's own conclusion is that this bound "explains nothing in Section 3" — the framing risks overstating the formal-verification contribution relative to what survives empirically.
- "q = 21.0–347.7" is reported without units/comparability context for readers unfamiliar with the Lipschitz-contraction formalism; a one-line intuition (what would q<1 vs q≫1 mean physically) would help general TTS-audience readability.

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the core periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuinely useful and unusually self-critical piece of work, but the period ladder's confound between period and lexical/entropy diversity (Concern 1), the residual periodicity in cycled controls at high k (Concern 3), and the thinness of the only true held-out generalization test (Concern 2) each need to be addressed or explicitly bounded before the "periodicity, not length" claim can be accepted at face value.
