**Steelman, stated up front:** the strongest reading of this paper is that it delivers a mechanistic, formally verified impossibility result (Lean 4, no `sorry`, audited axioms) that makes a sharp, falsifiable prediction — periodicity, not length, kills counting — and then subjects that prediction to an unusually adversarial empirical program: a length-matched control condition, a repetition-penalty sweep, a non-autoregressive check, and (rare in this literature) an explicit report of the two experiments that *failed* to establish the theorem's own premise. That is a genuinely higher bar than most ICASSP submissions clear. The question this review presses is whether the empirical panel that carries the paper's generality claims ("every checkpoint and every family," "no property of the decoding rule enters") was ever given a real chance to be falsified — or whether it was assembled from systems that were never going to test the theorem's most interesting rival explanations.

Ran one arXiv query against the paper's own citation trail (`RALL-E`, `CosyVoice2`, monotonic-alignment AR TTS) to check whether stronger, directly-relevant baselines exist and are publicly available. They do: **RALL-E** [2404.03204] is confirmed on arXiv and is cited by the paper itself (ref [3]) as a chain-of-thought / attention-supervision fix to "unstable prosody... due to the autoregressive prediction style," and **CosyVoice 2** [2412.10117] (paper's own ref [4]) is likewise cited as a streaming AR system with an explicit fix at the decoding-rule level. Both are exactly the kind of system the theorem should have something to say about, and neither appears in the empirical panel.

### Summary

The paper claims: (1) a Lean-verified theorem that periodic conditioning plus a contracting per-repetition state map implies a finite, computable horizon past which no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can recover repetition count; (2) an empirical demonstration, across six AR TTS checkpoints in three families, that repeated-phrase counting fails specifically under periodicity (median error −8.3%, 18.2% exact) while length-matched non-periodic controls survive almost perfectly (94.3% exact); and (3) an honest accounting of where the theory is *not* yet supported — the contraction premise itself was never measured successfully, and one supporting dose-response test came out sign-reversed. The periodicity-vs-length dissociation is well-controlled and is the paper's strongest single result. But the theorem's actual load-bearing premise (Assumption 2, contraction) is unestablished, the extension to k=128 that finds a "horizon" is explicitly post hoc, and the panel used to license the paper's broadest claims — "every checkpoint and every family," "no property of the decoding rule enters" — omits the AR systems most likely to break those claims (RALL-E, CosyVoice2) while leaning on only two non-AR systems evaluated at a single, untuned configuration each. My overall stance: real, carefully-caveated empirical phenomenon; theoretical mechanism dressed in more certainty (via the Lean artifact) than the data behind it currently supports; the baseline set is too narrow to bear the generality language actually used.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — the abstract's "the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family" is not quite what §4.2 shows. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Abstract; §4.2, Qwen3-TTS-1.7B ("shows no repeated-side saturation within k ≤ 128, inverts the ratio, and is also the checkpoint with no deficit at k ≤ 32").
   **Why it matters** — a reader who stops at the abstract (which is where most citations of this paper will originate) comes away believing the effect is universal across the panel; it is universal in *direction* for 5/6 checkpoints and genuinely absent, by the authors' own account, in the sixth. The abstract's own hedge ("though not peculiar to autoregressive decoding") already shows the authors know qualification is needed elsewhere in the same sentence — the "every checkpoint" clause doesn't get the same treatment.
   **What would address it** — soften to "in five of six checkpoints" or fold the caveat into the abstract sentence itself, the way the NAR caveat already is.

2. **Issue** — the theorem's premise (contraction, Assumption 2) is never established, so the entire apparatus (N\*, the k=128 saturation fits, the "3.5-fold" horizon-lowering headline number) is a set of predictions from an unverified hypothesis, propped up by one thin confirmatory test. `[Evidence — distinguishing test failure]`
   **Where** — §5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items (7 of 18 pairs, p = 0.12)"); §4.4 (Lemma 1's dose-response prediction "fails outright... r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]" in the *wrong* direction); the one supporting result is "12 items and one checkpoint."
   **Why it matters** — a saturating count curve at k=128 is consistent with *many* mechanisms besides contracting-state dynamics (a hard-coded max-length prior, KV-cache degradation, generic exposure-bias drift). The paper's own dilution dose-response test — the most direct test of the causal chain Lemma 1 → Theorem 1 — points the *opposite* way from the theory. That's not "premise unmeasured," that's one active piece of evidence against the mechanism, and it's stated but not weighted appropriately in the framing ("we do not establish the theorem's premise" undersells a result that actively cuts against it).
   **What would address it** — either drop the causal-mechanism framing to "consistent with, not established by" throughout (the Discussion does this; the Abstract and Title do not — "A LEAN-VERIFIED BOUND" in the title implies the bound is operative, not merely proved-but-unlinked to data), or run a third, higher-power contraction estimator before claiming the mechanism.

3. **Issue** — the k=128 extension and its two-parameter saturation model were chosen after seeing that k≤32 was proportional, and while the paper transparently timestamps *when* the decision to extend was made (S16), that doesn't establish the functional form, cell boundaries, or exclusion rule weren't shaped by the same post-hoc look at the k≤32 data. `[Evidence — pre-registered vs post-hoc]`
   **Where** — §4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc... S16 dates every analysis decision against the commit log"); the exponential saturation form ĉ(k) = K̂(1 − e^{−k/K̂}) is introduced only after the extension data exist.
   **Why it matters** — a commit-log audit of *when* a decision was made is a genuine and unusually rigorous safeguard, but it doesn't rule out that the specific functional form (why exponential saturation rather than, say, a power law or logistic) was selected because it fit well, nor does it protect the exclusion rules (budget-truncation, degenerate-drop) from the same hindsight. Cell sizes after exclusion are 21–26 generations — small enough that functional-form choice can matter a lot.
   **What would address it** — report the pre-registered (or at least pre-specified-before-fitting) functional form, or show the fit is robust to at least one alternative saturating family.

4. **Issue** — nearly every load-bearing number in the Results section is deferred to a supplement (S5, S8, S10–S16) not included in the text under review. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — throughout §4 (e.g., exclusion rates "in S10," significance floor "S11," dose-response "S12," penalty sweep "S15," post-hoc audit trail "S16").
   **Why it matters** — this review can only evaluate what's in front of it; several of the paper's most consequential caveats (the exact multiplicity-correction procedure that supposedly fails to clear 0.05, the "template rule falls evenly on both arms" claim) are asserted but not shown here, so I can't independently verify them and neither can a typical ICASSP reader without chasing down the linked repo.
   **What would address it** — for a 4–5 page ICASSP paper this is somewhat structurally forced, but at minimum the exclusion-rate table (342/132/19 out of 2052) and the multiplicity-correction procedure are central enough to the headline numbers that they'd benefit from at least a one-line in-text summary of method, not just a pointer.

5. **Issue** — the two non-AR systems used to test (and ultimately partially refute) the architectural hypothesis are each run at a single, apparently untuned configuration, in contrast to the extensive tuning-adjacent probing (repetition-penalty sweep, k-ladder extension, effective-rank analysis) given to the six AR checkpoints. `[Evidence — baselines]`
   **Where** — §4.3 ("VITS... n=90 each at three seeds"; F5-TTS "25.6% against 85.6%"); no report of any hyperparameter variation for either system, unlike the AR panel's §3 penalty ablation.
   **Why it matters** — the paper draws a specific causal story from this n=2 comparison ("what plausibly separates them is where the count must live... F5-TTS lets that be tested"), including withdrawing an entire claim category ("we withdraw the architectural claim") on the strength of it. Two systems, one configuration each, is a thin base for a claim that reshapes the paper's central thesis about what does and doesn't cause the failure. If VITS's default duration-predictor settings happen to sit in a regime immune to the effect for reasons unrelated to "monotonic vs. estimated duration" (e.g., a different training-data distribution, a different degenerate-output detector), the paper has no way to distinguish that from its preferred explanation.
   **What would address it** — at minimum, one config variation per NAR system (e.g., duration-predictor temperature/scale for VITS, a coarser vs. finer flow-matching step count for F5-TTS) to check the effect isn't an artifact of the specific stock checkpoint.

6. **Issue** — the AR panel excludes the systems specifically built to fix the failure mode under study, despite both being cited in the paper's own introduction as the field's existing answer to this exact problem — RALL-E [2404.03204] (paper's ref [3], confirmed via arXiv search) and CosyVoice 2 [2412.10117] (paper's ref [4]). `[Evidence — baselines, primary axis]`
   **Where** — §1 ("with fixes proposed at the level of attention supervision or decoding rules [3, 4]"); §3 Models (six checkpoints: Llasa ×3, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS ×2 — none with explicit alignment supervision or CoT duration decomposition); Abstract ("no property of the decoding rule enters, so the bound covers greedy, sampled and beam decoding alike").
   **Why it matters** — this is the single most informative missing experiment in the paper. The theorem's claim is architecture-and-decoding-rule-agnostic: it should apply to *any* AR system whose per-repetition map contracts, including ones with attention-supervision fixes, unless those fixes specifically change the contraction factor q (which the theorem's own framework predicts they should, if they work). Testing RALL-E or CosyVoice 2 would be a genuine distinguishing test: if they still collapse at large k (just with a larger N\*), that's strong support for the theorem's generality; if they don't collapse at all, that either falsifies the "no decoding rule matters" claim or shows the fix works by changing q, either of which the paper would want to know and report. Right now the panel can't tell these apart because it never included a system built to break the pattern. "No property of the decoding rule enters" is stated as established when the one class of models designed to test it wasn't run.
   **What would address it** — add at least one publicly available alignment-supervised or CoT-decomposed AR TTS system (RALL-E, CosyVoice 2, or a comparable open system) to the panel, even at reduced scale (e.g., the existing 10-item k-subladder), and report whether N\* shifts or the collapse disappears.

### Minor concerns

- The withdrawn citation [1] (arXiv:2509.19852, "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026; cited for its alignment analysis") is flagged transparently, which is good practice, but citing a withdrawn preprint at all for a load-bearing claim in the intro's motivation is worth a second look — withdrawal reasons aren't stated.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" column (0.21–0.80) has no confidence interval in the table itself; the CIs live only in Fig. 1(c), forcing cross-referencing to judge whether e.g. XTTS-v2's 0.69 and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B's 0.80 are distinguishable from each other.
- Fig. 1's extracted axis labels ("22 25" / "26 27" / "0 50") read as garbled OCR of what are presumably log-scale tick marks — I can't fully assess figure legibility from the text provided and flag this as a review-input limitation rather than a confirmed authoring flaw, but the caption alone (per the stated standard of figures being interpretable from caption + figure) would need actual axis values to verify.
- The CTC judge (`wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self`) is a 2020-era, LibriSpeech-only, English-only checkpoint; no newer or multilingual CTC ASR system is tried as a cross-check that judge choice itself doesn't interact with k (a modest ask given XTTS-v2 is explicitly multilingual, though the stimuli appear English-only).
- §4.2's phrase "the panel is not unanimous... we report it as an unexplained exception rather than as evidence of the theorem behaving conditionally" is doing real epistemic work but is dense enough (double negative, three clauses) that a first-time reader may need two passes; consider splitting into two sentences.
- A contemporaneous 2026 preprint, "Intrinsic Stability Limits of Autoregressive Reasoning: Structural Consequences for Long-Horizon Execution" [2602.06413], argues a structurally similar claim (fixed-point-style bounds on long-horizon autoregressive execution) in the LLM-reasoning domain rather than TTS. Given the closeness in publication window this may simply be contemporaneous and unavailable at submission time, but worth the authors checking whether the domains' formalisms overlap enough to warrant a footnote in a future revision.
- Novelty and prior-art attribution otherwise checked out: the intro's citation of [1]–[6] as decoding/attention-level fixes and the Discussion's distinction from representational collapse [14] and from single-forward-pass rank-collapse bounds [15–19] are accurate characterizations of what those works show, as far as I can verify from the paper text and confirmed arXiv IDs.
- Limitations section (embedded in §5 rather than separated out) is honest and specific — it names the failed contraction measurement, the sign-reversed dose-response test, and states falsification conditions outright ("a control with its twin's deficit, a model whose capacity gain matches its control, or a probe that recovers the count past the horizon"). This is a genuine strength; no major limitation appears to be conspicuously omitted, aside from the baseline-panel composition issue already raised as a major concern.

### Belief update

Modest, targeted update: I raise my credence that autoregressive TTS decoders (and at least one flow-matching non-AR system, F5-TTS) have a failure mode specifically triggered by *periodic* repetition rather than raw generation length — the length-matched control design (94.3% vs 18.2% exact) is the paper's strongest piece of evidence and is hard to explain away with generic exposure-bias or context-length stories. I do **not** update meaningfully toward the specific mechanistic story (contracting per-repetition state map via attention dilution) — the direct test of that mechanism (Lemma 1's dose-response prediction) came out sign-reversed, and the two attempts to measure the contraction factor itself failed. If anything, that's mild evidence *against* the dilution-driven-contraction account being the operative cause, even though it's consistent with some contraction mechanism existing that these particular measurement instruments couldn't detect. I make essentially no update on "no property of the decoding rule enters" or "the bound is architecture-general," because the systems that would most directly test those claims (RALL-E, CosyVoice 2) weren't run.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the paper's most generalizable claims ("every checkpoint and every family," "no property of the decoding rule enters") are asserted from a panel that omits the AR TTS systems specifically built to fix this failure mode (RALL-E, CosyVoice 2 — both cited by the authors themselves as the field's existing answer) and leans on two non-AR baselines run at a single untuned configuration each to adjudicate a central causal question; until at least one alignment-supervised AR system is added to the panel, the generality language in the abstract outruns what the evidence panel was actually built to test.
