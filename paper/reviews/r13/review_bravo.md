LENS: Experimental design (controls, confounds, blinding, pre-registration)
STANCE: Adversarial (assume the headline result is false; what would falsify it?)

### Summary
The paper argues that autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated phrases because softmax attention over k identical spans dilutes to weight Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1), pushing the decoder's per-repetition state update toward an approximately autonomous, contracting map whose fixed-point dynamics erase the repetition count from any Lipschitz readout past a derivable horizon N* (Theorem 1); both results are machine-checked in Lean 4. Using a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the autoregressive bias of Whisper, the authors run six AR-TTS checkpoints across three families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) on a repetition ladder (k = 1…32, extended post hoc to 128) with length-matched distinct-word controls, finding a median count error of −8.3% at k ≥ 6 (vs. 0.0% for controls, 18.2% vs. 94.3% exact-match) that tracks periodicity rather than length. Within k ≤ 32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating, contradicting the theorem's constant-horizon prediction; a post-hoc extension to k = 128 finds saturation (fitted horizon 3.5× lower for repeated vs. control text), and a single-checkpoint linear-probe test shows count decodability collapsing near the extended horizon. Two direct attempts to measure the contraction premise fail, and a dose-response test of the dilution lemma reverses sign, leaving the theorem's conclusion supported by one held-out probe test and its premise empirically unconfirmed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem's headline quantitative prediction (a finite counting horizon) is confirmed only after a post-hoc, data-contingent extension of the test range chosen because the pre-specified range falsified it.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("Within k ≤ 32 that deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts") followed by §4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc… to k ∈ {48,64,96,128}").
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook optional-stopping design: the confirmatory test (k ≤ 32) came back negative for the theory's distinctive prediction, and the authors then chose new data collection informed by that failure until a horizon appeared. Logging the decision against a commit timestamp (S16) documents *when* the choice was made but does not neutralize the statistical problem — a decision rule of "extend the range until saturation appears" will emit false positive "horizon" findings from ordinary decoder length-ceilings alone, which the authors' own control-saturation observation ("these decoders have a general utterance-length ceiling") shows is a live alternative explanation, not merely a nuisance to divide out. The paper does not report what a pre-registered stopping rule, or a held-out replication ladder collected before the extension was decided, would have shown.
   **What would address it** — Either pre-register the full k-range (including the extension) before any model is run, or treat the k=128 finding explicitly as exploratory/hypothesis-generating and report a fresh, independently collected replication ladder at the extended range as the confirmatory test, with its own pre-specified stopping point.

2. **Issue** — Disconfirming evidence is systematically re-classified as an "unexplained exception" rather than counted against the theory, while the two direct attempts to verify the theorem's premise (Assumption 2) and the dose-response test of Lemma 1 all fail or reverse sign.
   **Where** — §4.2 ("Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation… we report it as an unexplained exception rather than as evidence of the theorem behaving conditionally"); §5 ("two attempts to measure q failed… its dose-response prediction fails outright: … flatter attention goes with better counting").
   **Why it matters** — Under the paper's own stated falsification criteria ("a model whose capacity gain matches its control… would refute it," §5), Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is close to exactly that case (no k≤32 deficit, no k≤128 saturation, inverted control/repeat ratio) yet is not scored as a refutation. Combined with a mechanism (contraction) that has never been positively measured and a companion lemma whose predicted dose-response is empirically reversed, the causal chain the paper argues for — dilution → contraction → collapse — has zero confirmed empirical links and at least one that points the wrong way. The "operative" test that does survive (linear probe decodability collapsing past N*) is run on 12 items and one checkpoint, which the authors themselves call "one clean observation, not a panel result." A theory this hard to falsify by design, where every disconfirming checkpoint becomes an anomaly and every confirming test is n=1, cannot yet be said to have been empirically stress-tested.
   **What would address it** — Pre-specify, before data collection, what pattern of results in Qwen3-TTS-1.7B (or any checkpoint) would count as a refutation and report the base rate of "exceptions" across a larger checkpoint pool; extend the single-checkpoint probe test to the other five checkpoints before treating it as evidence for the theorem's conclusion.

3. **Issue** — The n = 6 "checkpoint as unit of replication" design pools non-independent checkpoints (three Llasa scales sharing architecture, tokenizer, and training pipeline) as if they were independent trials.
   **Where** — §4.2 ("The checkpoint, not the generation, is the unit of replication. The exactly-correct rate favours the control in all 6… positive in 5 of 6").
   **Why it matters** — Three of six "replicates" are a within-family scale ladder, not draws from independent model populations; a systematic property of the Llasa/X-codec2 pipeline would inflate the "5 of 6" and "6 of 6" tallies without adding independent evidence. The paper does separately report a family-level grouping (3 of 3 positive) — appropriately more conservative — but the checkpoint-level framing is what carries the abstract's "every checkpoint and every family" claim and the signed-rank floor discussion, overstating the effective sample size.
   **What would address it** — Lead with the family-level (n = 3) statistic as the primary claim, and present the checkpoint-level count explicitly as a secondary, non-independent breakdown; consider a mixed-effects treatment (family as random effect) instead of pooling checkpoints as exchangeable units.

4. **Issue** — The length-matched control itself becomes periodic (period-8) for k > 8 because its filler pool is cycled, confounding "periodicity" with "reduced lexical variety" exactly in the k range where the periodicity/length dissociation is reported as sharpest.
   **Where** — §3 ("filler tokens… cycled above k = 8"); §4.3 ("Above k = 8 the control's fillers cycle a pool of eight, making that contrast period-1 against period-8").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's sharpest claimed result (94.3% vs. 18.2% exact match, "the limit is periodicity, not length") is partly built on a comparison between a fully periodic sequence and a partially periodic one, not a genuinely non-periodic one, for k ∈ {12,16,24,32}. The authors' own remedial re-run with a 146-word pool reduces the gap by 13.4 points (84.6% → 71.2 points of dissociation), i.e., by the authors' own accounting roughly a fifth of the k>8 dissociation is attributable to this confound rather than to "periodicity per se." It is not stated whether the headline 94.3%/18.2% figures reported in the Abstract and §4.3 already reflect the corrected pool or the confounded one — as written it reads as if the original cycled-pool numbers are what is being headlined.
   **What would address it** — Report the periodicity/length dissociation using the never-cycled 146-word pool as the primary figure (not a secondary check), across all checkpoints and the full k-range, and state explicitly which pool underlies every summary statistic in the Abstract and §4.3.

5. **Issue** — 24% of all panel generations (493/2052) are excluded post hoc using criteria that appear to have been formulated after observing judge and model failure modes, without a pre-registered exclusion protocol or sensitivity analysis.
   **Where** — §3 ("Of 2052 panel generations we exclude 342 in one template whose fillers the judge never transcribes, 132 stopped by our token budget… and 19 degenerate, leaving 1559").
   **Why it matters** — The authors assert the exclusions do not favor their hypothesis ("the template rule falls evenly on both arms and the budget rule works against the contrast"), but this is an analytic claim, not a demonstrated one — no results are shown with the excluded items retained (even coded as failures/censored) to verify that the reported medians and exact-match rates are exclusion-robust. A quarter of the data being dropped by rules chosen after seeing which items misbehaved is a substantial degree of freedom in an unblinded pipeline.
   **What would address it** — Report headline statistics both with and without each exclusion category (or with truncated items coded as censored failures per the paper's own conservative-truncation logic used elsewhere), and pre-register exclusion criteria for any follow-up study.

6. **Issue** — The claim that periodicity-driven collapse is "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" rests on a comparison of exactly two non-autoregressive systems that give opposite answers.
   **Where** — §4.3 ("VITS… shows no dissociation… But F5-TTS… does show it… Absence of an autoregressive decoder confers no immunity, and we withdraw the architectural claim").
   **Why it matters** — With n = 2 non-AR architectures split 1–1, the paper cannot actually establish generality across non-AR designs; it can only report that AR-ness is not sufficient for the effect. The proposed explanation (duration-per-token vs. single total-duration estimation) is plausible but is itself tested only within F5-TTS's own outputs (§4.3, S13), not against a third independent NAR architecture that would let the duration-representation hypothesis be pitted against a competing account. A recent line of work on transformer counting failures being tied to architectural/positional factors more broadly (e.g., [2310.08661]) is not engaged with as an alternative explanation for why VITS and F5-TTS diverge.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one more architecturally distinct NAR system (e.g., another duration-predictor design and another per-token duration design) before generalizing about which architectural property confers immunity, and explicitly discuss alternative accounts of the VITS/F5-TTS split (e.g., [2310.08661]'s positional/architectural analysis of transformer counting failures) rather than settling on the duration-representation account from a single case.

### Minor concerns
- Reference [1] (Wang et al., "Eliminating stability hallucinations…") is cited as motivating prior art despite being marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a sentence on why a withdrawn preprint is still being relied on for its alignment analysis.
- The companion paper [7] and the competing-account paper [13] are both very recent (2026) preprints from adjacent/overlapping author circles (self-citation for [7]); this is not disqualifying but should be flagged as a possible circularity/coordination point when reading the "closest prior result" framing in §5.
- The 165-clip "audibility check" sample (so a judgement can be checked by ear) is described but no inter-rater agreement statistic (e.g., agreement rate between human spot-check and CTC-judge score) is reported.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" and Figure 1(c)'s effective-rank measure are introduced in §4.4 without stating how many probe layers/heads were tried before settling on "deep layers" — another place where an unreported researcher degree of freedom could affect the fourfold cross-checkpoint variability noted in the text.
- The Qwen3-TTS sampling settings are noted as "the most stochastic," used to argue its null result isn't due to conservative decoding — but only 3 seeds are used across the whole panel, which is a thin basis for characterizing stochastic variability at the highest-temperature setting.
- Minor prose ambiguity: "no multiplicity correction clears 0.05 (S11)" doesn't state which multiplicity correction(s) were attempted (Bonferroni, BH, etc.).

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal (Lean-verified) contribution and the periodicity-vs-length contrast are genuine and the paper is unusually candid about its own null results, but the central empirical case for the theorem's premise and horizon rests on a post-hoc extended test range, single-checkpoint confirmatory tests, and control confounds that the authors themselves partially quantify but do not fully correct in the headline figures.
