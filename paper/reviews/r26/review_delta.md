### Summary

Steelmanned, this is a genuinely well-designed empirical isolation: the authors notice that "repeat a word k times" confounds length with periodicity, build a length-matched control (same carrier, same word count, non-repeating fillers) to split the two, and show across six checkpoints from three architecture families that the repeated arm is rendered exactly 18.2% of the time versus 94.3% for its twin control — a gap that survives greedy decoding, a penalty sweep, exclusion-rule removal, and four different scoring recognisers. They also catch and fix a real methodological trap (Whisper, being autoregressive, undercounts the very phenomenon it's meant to measure) and honestly kill their own leading mechanistic hypothesis (the Lean-verified contraction bound: q is always ≫1, never <1). That's a paper with real epistemic hygiene — an admitted post-hoc threshold, a disclosed multiverse analysis, a killed hypothesis kept in the paper anyway. Pressing on that steelmanned version, though: the paper's "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" and "duration ownership, not architecture" claims rest on exactly two non-autoregressive models, tested off-the-shelf rather than against the purpose-built baseline (an explicit per-unit-duration NAR system) that their own post-hoc explanation calls for; several of the confirmatory tests (CosyVoice2 n=90, the F5-TTS duration ablation n=60, the Whisper audit n=12) are thin enough that "we tested it" oversells what was actually a single external checkpoint or a small ablation with a suspiciously specific post-hoc exclusion rule. The core repetition-not-length effect is credible; several of the paper's more totalizing claims ("not peculiar to AR decoding," "confirmed the predicted interval") are not as load-bearing as the prose implies.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The abstract's "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" claim, and the body's causal story that duration-ownership (not AR-vs-NAR) is what matters, are both built on a two-model NAR sample, and the paper never builds the baseline its own hypothesis calls for. `[Evidence — alternative explanations / Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Abstract ("Nor is the failure peculiar to autoregressive decoding: of two non-autoregressive baselines one is immune, one is not"); §3.2 ("What plausibly separates the two is where the count must live: VITS gives each token its own duration, F5-TTS estimates one total").
   **Why it matters** — The abstract frames this as an AR-vs-NAR result (n=2), which a reader will remember; the body then quietly reframes it as a duration-ownership result, discovered after seeing VITS survive and F5-TTS fail. Two off-the-shelf models differing in a dozen ways (scale, training corpus, vocoder, tokenizer) besides "has explicit per-token duration" is a weak basis for either framing.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the abstract to "duration ownership, not decoding paradigm" (matching the body's actual conclusion), or add a third NAR arm that isolates duration-ownership cleanly against the same confound the paper already fixed for length.

2. **Issue** — The "confirmatory" CosyVoice2 test and the F5-TTS duration-supply ablation are single small-n add-ons dressed in confirmatory-experiment language, not independent replications of the claimed effect. `[Evidence — pre-registered vs post-hoc]`
   **Where** — §3.1 ("a fit committed before the checkpoint's first waveform existed... checkable rather than asserted"); §3.2 ("Supplying F5-TTS that duration lifts exact renderings 43.3% to 76.7% below k=12 and 0.0 at or above it (n=60, gains above 8.3 points excluded)").
   **Why it matters** — n=90 (one external checkpoint) and n=60 (one retrofitted ablation, with an oddly specific post-hoc trim of "gains above 8.3 points") are each doing outsized rhetorical work — "checkable rather than asserted," "committed before... existed" — that a single data point per claim doesn't earn. The 8.3-point exclusion rule in particular is exactly the kind of after-the-fact threshold the framework asks reviewers to distrust.
   **What would address it** — Report the F5-TTS ablation's full distribution (not just the trimmed subset) and state the trimming rule's pre-registration status explicitly; add a second out-of-panel alignment-supervised checkpoint before calling the CosyVoice2 result a confirmed prediction rather than a single corroborating anecdote.

3. **Issue** — The headline instrument-validation numbers (Whisper vs. CTC audit) rest on n=12 and n=24 concatenative-audio trials, far too small to carry the weight the paper puts on them ("the obvious judge is disqualified"). `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — §2 ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth it transcribes the repeated word exactly once in every trial that returns anything (0.19 of the true count, n=12 over 6 donors)... ratio 1.00 (n=24, mean 0.996)").
   **Why it matters** — This audit is load-bearing for the entire paper's scoring methodology (every subsequent number depends on trusting the CTC judge over Whisper), yet it's validated on a dozen synthetic trials. A systematic bias this consistent probably is real, but n=12 doesn't rule out an audit-set artifact (e.g., all 6 donors sharing a recording setup).
   **What would address it** — Expand the concatenative audit to more donors/conditions, or at minimum report per-donor variance rather than only the pooled mean.

4. **Issue** — No qualitative example (transcript, spectrogram, or audio excerpt shown in-text) of a repeated-arm failure versus its control pair anywhere in the reviewable material — the reader must take every claim on numeric faith. `[Evidence — cherry-picking / Presentation]`
   **Where** — absent throughout §3.
   **Why it matters** — For a paper about a perceptual/behavioral phenomenon (models "loop, truncate and lose count"), not showing a single concrete example of what "18.2% exact" versus "94.3% exact" actually sounds/looks like makes it impossible for a reader to sanity-check the scoring pipeline by eye, and makes it impossible to assess whether the shown examples (if any exist in the supplement) were cherry-picked, since none are visible here. The stratified 165-clip sample is claimed to exist for exactly this purpose, but it's referenced, not shown.
   **What would address it** — Include at least one worked example (word sequence in, transcript out, exact-match decision) inline, ideally one "typical" and one "hard" case.

5. **Issue** *(axis focus — weak baselines)* — The paper's field-mitigation tests (repetition-penalty sweep, greedy decoding, exclusion-rule removal) are run on a subset of checkpoints over a narrow parameter range, and the NAR "baselines" (VITS, F5-TTS) are never tuned or purpose-selected the way the panel's own decoding ablations imply a fair fight would require. `[Evidence — baselines]`
   **Where** — §3.2 ("Swept on two architectures the deficit stays put (XTTS-v2 15.6–21.1% exact over a 4-fold range, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B 7.8–16.7% over 3-fold)"); §2 ("VITS... and F5-TTS... run as non-autoregressive baselines... neither group is pooled into the panel").
   **Why it matters** — A 3–4× repetition-penalty sweep on 2 of 6 checkpoints is a weak test of "the field's standard mitigations don't fix this" — practitioners routinely push repetition penalties, no-repeat n-gram blocking, or beam search much harder than an order-of-magnitude sweep implies, and none of those stronger tools (beam search, explicit n-gram blocking, the attention-supervision techniques from RALL-E/ELLA-V that the intro itself cites as the field's proposed fixes) are tested at all — only CosyVoice2, an already-trained checkpoint, stands in for "alignment supervision as a fix," with training data, scale and tokenizer all confounded with the one variable the authors want to attribute the difference to. Likewise, once the paper's own analysis lands on "duration ownership" as the operative variable (§3.2), the natural strong baseline is a NAR architecture *built around* explicit per-unit duration prediction (e.g., a FastSpeech-style or Matcha-TTS-style duration predictor) — not VITS (stochastic, not deterministic per-token duration) or a doctored F5-TTS given oracle ground-truth duration after the fact. The paper essentially discovers its real independent variable late and never constructs the baseline that variable calls for; it retrofits instead.
   **What would address it** — Add a native explicit-duration NAR baseline (not a retrofit), widen the repetition-penalty sweep to the range practitioners actually use and run it on more than 2 checkpoints, and either test one of the cited attention-supervision fixes (RALL-E/ELLA-V) directly or drop the intro's implication that they're being evaluated.

### Minor concerns

- The abstract crams roughly 15 distinct statistics into one paragraph; a first-time reader cannot easily identify which 2–3 numbers are the actual headline versus supporting detail (e.g., is the claim "18.2% vs 94.3%" or "46.8 points from k=1" or "76.4 points mixed-effects"? All three are given as if equally central).
- Repo name "lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing" is flagged by the authors themselves as pre-dating the study, which is fine, but combined with an ICASSP-antispoofing name for a TTS-counting paper it's genuinely confusing on first read and worth a footnote earlier than the Code paragraph.
- "S19," "S16," "S13," "S26," etc. are referenced roughly 20 times in the main text with no indication of what document contains them or how to access it inline — for a reviewer without the supplement, these read as unverifiable assertions-by-citation.
- The Lean-4 proof section (§4) is presented in full formal detail for a hypothesis the paper spends the whole section refuting; given the result is negative, this could be compressed substantially without losing anything, and would improve readability.
- "our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values (S11)" is stated once and then the paper does report one p-value (P=0.010) elsewhere (§3.1 shuffle test) — mildly inconsistent framing.
- Units are inconsistent in a few places — "points" is used for percentage-point gaps but not always distinguished from plain percentages in adjacent sentences (e.g., §3.1's "76.4 points" vs "18.2%" in the same paragraph requires the reader to do the subtraction themselves to check consistency).
- The dose-response correlation r=0.59 for the attention-flatness-vs-counting relationship in §4 is reported with no n, no CI, and no significance — inconsistent with the otherwise careful interval reporting elsewhere.

### Belief update

I came in with a strong prior that this class of claim ("we isolated the true cause of an ML failure mode and ruled out N alternative explanations") is usually overstated relative to what the experiments show. This paper moved me less than its exhaustive numerics suggest it should. The core distinguishing test (length-matched repeated-vs-control ladder) is genuinely good and I now put real weight on "AR-TTS counting failures track periodic/repeated structure, not raw length" — that's a real, useful update, maybe from "plausible but unconfirmed" to "probably true for the architectures tested." I did *not* update on "this generalizes across decoding paradigms" or "duration-ownership, not AR-ness, is the true variable" — those rest on an n=2 NAR comparison and a small retrofit ablation, not a real test. I also did not update much on the mechanistic story, but that's the authors' own doing — they refuted their candidate mechanism and said so, which is exactly the right move and the strongest part of the paper's epistemics.

### Verdict

**Minor revision** — the central repetition-vs-length isolation is solid and worth publishing, but the paper should not ship the "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" framing on a two-model, off-the-shelf NAR baseline set when its own analysis has already identified explicit duration-ownership as the operative variable and never built that baseline.
