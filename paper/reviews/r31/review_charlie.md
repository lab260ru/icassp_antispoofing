# Review (Reviewer: charlie)

### Summary
The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' documented tendency to miscount repeated phrases is driven by repetition per se or is simply a symptom of longer/harder inputs. Using a "repetition ladder" of 180 stimuli (a target word repeated k times vs. a length- and word-count-matched control with distinct fillers), evaluated across six checkpoints spanning three architectures (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a held-out fourth architecture (CosyVoice 2), the authors report a large and robust exact-match gap between repeated and control items (18.2% vs 94.3% at k≥6), that the gap survives decoding/penalty/exclusion ablations and different ASR judges, and that a period-manipulation ladder shows the deficit is graded with periodicity rather than a step function tied to verbatim adjacency. They further formalize a candidate mechanism (a Lean-4-verified theorem: if per-repetition state maps contract, no Lipschitz readout can separate counts past a horizon) and then empirically measure the contraction constant, finding it is never <1 in any checkpoint or condition — refuting their own proposed mechanism. The headline claim is that the counting failure tracks repetition structure, not sequence length, and that its cause remains mechanistically open despite the ruled-out contraction account.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central quantitative claim (75.4-point gap at k≥6) rests on a threshold chosen after inspecting the data, and the paper explicitly declines any multiple-comparison correction across its 420-specification robustness sweep.
   **Where** — Abstract ("a threshold set after seeing the data, so we quote both"); §3.2 ("The k≥6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge"); §3.1 ("Across 420 specifications... we correct across none").
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook post-hoc/HARKing pattern: pick the breakpoint that maximizes separation, then report the maximized statistic as the headline number. Reporting the unconditioned 46.8-point figure alongside it is honest, but it does not neutralize the inflation — the abstract and Table 1 still foreground the post-hoc-optimized value, and the "no correction" stance means every one of the six interval constructions, the mixed-effects estimate, and the partial-pooling posterior are all built downstream of a data-dependent design choice. Without a pre-registered analysis plan (none is described anywhere in §2), readers cannot distinguish "real threshold effect" from "threshold chosen because it happened to separate the arms in this dataset."
   **What would address it** — Either pre-register the k threshold (or use a threshold-free summary, e.g., an area-between-curves statistic across the whole ladder as the primary reported quantity) and relegate the k≥6 figure to a secondary/exploratory analysis explicitly labeled as such, rather than leading with it in the abstract.

2. **Issue** — The control condition is not length-matched in the periodicity sense for a large fraction of the ladder: above k=8, the filler pool is cycled, so the "control" itself becomes period-8 text, confounding the very comparison the paper calls "the sharpest test" for exactly the high-k region where the effect is largest.
   **Where** — §3.1 ("Above k=8 the cycled control is itself period-8, so that contrast is p=1 against p=8, not aperiodic text"); Fig. 1 caption ("Rungs p=2,4,8 share one 8-word pool").
   **Why it matters** — The stimulus ladder runs k up to 32, and the biggest between-arm gaps are claimed at the top of that range. But for k∈{12,16,24,32} — 4 of the 10 k-values, and the ones driving the largest reported deltas — the control is not the acyclic, length-matched foil the design narrative promises; it is itself periodic. The paper's own "never-cycled" analysis shows the gap shrinks from 76.7/75.4 (cycled) to 69.6/67.6 (never-cycled) and that "most of that 7.1 points is vocabulary, not periodicity" — but this correction is reported as a footnote-level robustness check, not folded into the primary reported statistic, and the never-cycled condition itself is only evaluated on a subset (S9/S27-style spot checks), not the full ladder.
   **What would address it** — Run the full ladder (all k, all checkpoints) with never-cycled, sufficiently large filler pools as the primary control, and report the cycled version as the ablation, inverting the current emphasis.

3. **Issue (adversarial framing — what would falsify the headline claim)** — The paper's own extrapolation and ordering experiments produce a result that, taken at face value, is inconsistent with a single unified "repetition" mechanism: shuffling token order at fixed multiset composition *helps* on Qwen checkpoints (+17.8) but *hurts* on XTTS-v2 (−30.0), and the posterior-predictive gap for an out-of-panel architecture has a wide interval that "touches zero" under the paper's own widest prior.
   **Where** — §3.1 ("Whether ordering or composition carries the ladder is unresolved"); §3.1 ("under the widest prior on it that gap touches zero (−1.6)").
   **Why it matters** — If the mechanism the paper is isolating is genuinely "repetition" as a unitary construct, a manipulation that holds repetition-count and multiset composition fixed while only reordering tokens should not produce opposite-signed effects across architectures. Two architectures moving in opposite directions under the same manipulation is exactly the pattern predicted by "there are at least two distinct failure modes being conflated under one label," which undercuts the paper's framing of a single isolated phenomenon. Only three architecture families are represented in total, so this three-way disagreement (Qwen up, XTTS down, others untested at this cell) is a large fraction of the entire architecture sample — not a minor residual.
   **What would address it** — Either expand the shuffle ablation to all six checkpoints (currently reported only for "both Qwen checkpoints" and XTTS-v2) before claiming the mechanism is unresolved-but-general, or explicitly narrow the paper's claim to "a family of related but architecture-dependent failure modes" rather than treating unification as the default and disunification as an open footnote.

4. **Issue** — No blinding or independent adjudication protocol is described for the scoring pipeline, and the counting rule itself (skip unrecognized tokens rather than stop at first miss) is a researcher-chosen design decision that is not validated against an alternative rule in the main text.
   **Where** — §2 ("ĉ is obtained by matching the target unit... skipping unrecognised ones rather than stopping at the first miss... The judge and the counting rule are varied in S14 and S28, not here").
   **Why it matters** — Automated ASR scoring removes human-annotator blinding concerns, but the counting rule was chosen by the authors specifically because the alternative ("stop at first miss") "would void credit for every later filler in a control" — i.e., the rule was selected, at least partly, with awareness of how it would affect the repeated-vs-control asymmetry. That may well be the right choice, but because the sensitivity analysis is deferred entirely to supplementary material not reproduced here, the main-text reader cannot evaluate how much of the 18.2%/94.3% split depends on this specific scoring convention versus a stricter alternative.
   **What would address it** — Report the exact-match rate under at least the "stop at first miss" alternative rule directly in Table 1 or Fig. 1, not only in supplementary text, given how central the counting rule is to the central metric.

5. **Issue** — Several pivotal falsification/confirmation checks rest on very small samples relative to the strength of the claims drawn from them (e.g., Whisper audit n=12 over 6 donors; CTC audit n=24; F5-TTS duration-injection n=60; rank-1 causal patch n=36; CosyVoice-2 confirmation n=90 generations from a single held-out architecture).
   **Where** — §2 (Whisper/CTC audits); §3.2 (F5-TTS, n=60; CosyVoice 2, n=90); §5 (rank-1 patch, n=36).
   **Why it matters** — These are not peripheral numbers — the Whisper-bias claim (0.19 vs 1.00 exact ratio) is used to justify discarding the "obvious" judge entirely, and the CosyVoice-2 result is used as the single confirmatory test of the out-of-panel extrapolation. With n as low as 12–36, no confidence intervals or variance estimates are given for these specific comparisons (unlike the main gap, which does report bootstrap/mixed-effects intervals), so the reader cannot assess whether, e.g., the Whisper ratio of 0.19 would remain stable under resampling.
   **What would address it** — Report interval estimates (bootstrap or exact binomial) for every n<100 comparison used to support a categorical claim (disqualifying Whisper, confirming CosyVoice-2, validating the causal patch).

6. **Issue** — The formal-verification framing ("A Lean-4-verified bound...") is presented as a headline contribution in the abstract despite the paper's own conclusion that the bound's antecedent is false for every decoder measured, making the theorem's role in the paper closer to a motivating-but-refuted hypothesis than a result.
   **Where** — Abstract; §4 ("We keep the bound because it motivated the manipulation... but it explains nothing in Section 3").
   **Why it matters** — This is transparently handled in the body text, but the abstract's structure (state the bound, then in the next sentence say the premise is measured and rejected) risks readers who skim the abstract coming away thinking a verified mechanistic explanation exists, when the paper's actual epistemic position is "we falsified our own candidate mechanism and don't have a replacement." This is a presentation/claims-emphasis issue as much as a design one.
   **What would address it** — Lead the abstract with the empirical finding and state the mechanism as tested-and-rejected up front, rather than presenting the Lean result before its refutation.

### Minor concerns
- The abstract's clause "It favours the control in all 6 checkpoints, where models render repeated text exactly in 18.2%... against 94.3%" is grammatically ambiguous on first read (the pronoun "it" needs to resolve back to "the repeated-versus-control gap," and "where" as a connector is unusual for framing conditional statistics).
- §3.1's sentence "the widest being [63.2, 89.2]" for "all six interval constructions" doesn't specify what the six construction methods are in the main text, forcing the reader into the supplement to evaluate robustness.
- The paper cites its own companion paper [8] with an arXiv ID (arXiv:2604.08591) dated 2026; readers checking this citation before the ID resolves/becomes public will be unable to verify it independently — worth flagging that it is a concurrent/companion submission, not established prior work.
- Table 1 reports "count err." as 0.0 for both arms of Qwen3-TTS-1.7B despite the checkpoint still showing a 60-point exact-match gap (38.9% vs 98.9%) — this apparent contradiction (median error indistinguishable, exact-match rate very different) is explained qualitatively in text but the table itself would benefit from a footnote pointing to this dissociation, since a reader scanning only the table would miss it.
- The GitHub repository name is noted as "predates this study" — a small transparency note, but it's unclear whether the repo's commit history prior to this study is publicly distinguishable from the study's own commits, which matters for verifying "regenerates every number here from pinned revisions" claims.
- The Spanish-language check is described as needing "a better judge" (§5) yet is still used as supporting evidence in §2 for the English-artifact rebuttal — the paper should be consistent about whether this cross-lingual result is trustworthy enough to cite as evidence or is acknowledged as under-validated.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the core repetition-vs-length manipulation is a genuinely valuable and mostly well-controlled design, but the post-hoc threshold selection feeding the headline statistic, the cycled-filler confound at exactly the high-k region driving the largest reported effects, and the unresolved cross-architecture sign-flip in the ordering/composition ablation need to be resolved or explicitly downgraded in emphasis before the paper's central quantitative claims can be taken at face value.
