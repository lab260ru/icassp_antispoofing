### Summary

The paper makes three claims: (1) TTS models undercount repeated words because of *repetition*, not sequence *length* — demonstrated via a repeated-vs-length-matched-control ladder across six checkpoints/three architectures; (2) the standard instrument for measuring this (Whisper) is itself biased toward manufacturing the effect, so a CTC recognizer is used instead, with the choice validated against concatenative ground truth; (3) a Lean-4-verified "contraction" mechanism that the authors themselves proposed to explain the failure is measured directly and refuted (q = 21–348, never < 1, on both repeated and control text). The repeated-vs-control gap is real and survives a genuinely impressive gauntlet of robustness checks (decoding strategy, penalty sweep, exclusion rules, four different recognizers, an out-of-panel architecture held out for a pre-registered extrapolation test). That is the paper's strength, and it is a real one. My problem is that essentially every one of these robustness claims — the Lean proof's soundness, the exact exclusion criteria, the bootstrap/mixed-effects procedures, the seeds, the audit methodology behind the Whisper-vs-CTC judge decision — is asserted to live in one of ~29 supplementary sections (S1–S29) and in a GitHub repository that, when I fetched it, returned HTTP 404. The organization (`lab260ru`) is real and has real, related repositories (`AASIST3`, `speech_spoof_bench`), but the specific repo named in the paper (`icassp_antispoofing`, tag `v1.3-isolating`) does not currently resolve. A paper this dense with unverifiable-in-text quantitative claims, all routed through one link that doesn't work, is not currently checkable by anyone who isn't the authors.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The one experiment designed to distinguish "periodic ordering" from "repeated composition" as the driver of the deficit gives architecture-dependent, sign-flipping results, and the paper resolves this by declaring it "unresolved" rather than investigating further. `[Evidence — distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — §3.1, "Does the ordering carry it?": shuffling gains 17.8 points on both Qwen checkpoints, is null on one Llasa checkpoint (implied), and *loses* 30.0 points on XTTS-v2.
   **Why it matters** — This is the paper's only attempt at a mechanistic distinguishing test beyond "repetition vs. length," and it fails to distinguish anything: two architectures move in opposite directions on the same manipulation. The paper's own duration-modeling argument for VITS vs. F5-TTS (per-token duration vs. single-total duration) is sitting right there as a candidate explanation for why XTTS-v2 (which, like F5-TTS in spirit, needs to place a total duration) might behave oppositely to Qwen — but this connection is never made or tested.
   **What would address it** — Either extend the p=4 shuffle test to more checkpoints per architecture so "architecture-dependent" isn't confounded with "n=1 per architecture," or explicitly test the duration-allocation hypothesis as the moderator, the way it's done for VITS/F5-TTS elsewhere in the paper.

2. **Issue** — The entire "use CTC, not Whisper" methodology — arguably the paper's second headline contribution — is certified on audit samples of n=12 and n=24 items, and no other cell in the paper carries an error bar, standard deviation, or interval on Table 1's point estimates. `[Evidence — statistical rigor]`
   **Where** — §2 ("Behavioural measurement..."): "n=12 over 6 donors" for the Whisper audit, "n=24, mean 0.996" for CTC; Table 1 reports point estimates only, no CIs, for cells as small as n=90 elsewhere in the text (e.g., CosyVoice 2, F5-TTS).
   **Why it matters** — A 6-donor audit is not enough to certify a scoring instrument that every downstream number in the paper depends on. If the audit's confidence interval on 0.19 vs 1.00 is wide, the "Whisper is the wrong judge" conclusion — used to justify discarding an entire scoring approach in favor of the one that happens to show the effect more starkly — is weaker than the crisp point estimates suggest.
   **What would address it** — Report CIs on the audit ratios, and put standard errors or bootstrap intervals on every Table 1 cell, not just the aggregate "panel" numbers that get intervals in the prose.

3. **Issue** — The paper's own framing oscillates between "isolating" a clean effect and admitting, in the same section, that its mechanistic follow-ups are inconclusive — creating a gap between what the title/abstract assert and what the body actually nails down. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Title: "Isolating the Counting Failure"; abstract: "It favours the control in all 6 checkpoints" (confident) vs. body, §3.1: "Whether ordering or composition carries the ladder is unresolved" and §3.2/Discussion: "at one model per strategy that is suggestive rather than isolating," "we could not build one" (re: the readout-vs-state manipulation).
   **Why it matters** — A reader who stops at the abstract gets a clean "repetition causes counting failure, mechanism basically pinned down" story. The actual paper is honest that the *mechanism* — ordering vs. composition, state-erasure vs. readout-failure, why NAR baselines split 1-1 — is still open. The title oversells what's actually isolated: the *phenomenon* (repetition vs. length) is isolated; the *mechanism* is not, and the title doesn't say that.
   **What would address it** — Retitle or add a subtitle qualifier that scopes the claim to the phenomenon-level dissociation, since that's what's actually supported; move "mechanism unresolved" language into the abstract, not just section 3/4.

4. **Issue** — The k≥6 threshold, the choice to quote "the smaller" of two cycled/never-cycled numbers, and the 420-specification robustness curve are all explicitly post-hoc constructions dressed as a robustness analysis. `[Evidence — pre-registered vs post-hoc]`
   **Where** — §3.2, final paragraph: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge"; §3.1: "we quote both ... and we quote the smaller"; "Across 420 specifications over six analysis axes ... the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points ... none reverses its sign."
   **Why it matters** — The authors deserve real credit for disclosing the post-hoc-ness explicitly rather than hiding it — that's unusually honest. But disclosure doesn't neutralize the statistical issue: a specification curve run *after* seeing that the effect is robust is not the same evidentiary object as a pre-registered one, and "median 70.5, sign never flips" is exactly the kind of summary a garden-of-forking-paths analysis produces even when the underlying effect is smaller or more contingent than it looks. The CosyVoice-2 held-out test (§3.1, "S16") is the one piece of real pre-registration in the paper and is by far its strongest evidentiary component — it's a shame the rest of the analysis isn't built the same way.
   **What would address it** — Foreground the CosyVoice-2 held-out prediction test as the paper's central evidence (it already is, informally) and downgrade the 420-specification curve to what it is: a robustness-of-a-post-hoc-finding check, not confirmatory evidence.

5. **Issue** — The GitHub repository the paper cites as the sole location for verifying its Lean 4 proof, exclusion criteria, bootstrap/mixed-effects procedures, seeds, hyperparameters, and "every number" in the paper (via `build.sh`) does not resolve. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — §2, "Code, data, settings": `https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing`, tag `v1.3-isolating`.
   **Why it matters** — I fetched `https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing` and `https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing/tree/v1.3-isolating` directly; both return HTTP 404. The GitHub organization `lab260ru` itself is real and active — it lists 19 repositories including genuinely related work (`AASIST3`, an anti-spoofing project; `speech_spoof_bench`) — which is consistent with the paper's own aside that "name predates this study," i.e., this may be a renamed/private fork of an existing antispoofing repo that simply isn't public yet. But that means, as submitted, **none** of the following claims are independently checkable: the Lean 4 development being "sorry-free... on Lean's three standard axioms" (§4); the exact stimuli set (180 items) and per-template filler pools; the precise exclusion rule that drops 342+133+19 of 2052 generations; the mixed-effects model specification and its six interval constructions; the "S1"–"S29" supplementary sections that are cited roughly 25 times across the paper for load-bearing methodological detail (audit protocols, Lean proof text, bootstrap procedures, per-condition rate tables, worked examples); and the specific seeds and sampling defaults referenced as "S19." A paper whose entire evidentiary architecture is "trust the aggregate number, the details are in a link" is not currently reproducible by a reader, referee, or "competent grad student" as the framework asks — it's reproducible in principle, by the authors, once they push the tag.
   **What would address it** — Push the tagged commit before or at submission, verify the link resolves publicly (not just to org members), and include at least the Lean theorem statement, the exact exclusion-rule pseudocode, and the seed list inline or in a genuinely-attached appendix rather than behind an S-numbering scheme that isn't reachable from the manuscript itself.

### Minor concerns

- The formal bound in §4 introduces `N* = log(µ/2LC)/log q` with µ, L, C never defined in the main text — readers can't evaluate the bound's derivation without the (currently unreachable) supplement.
- Table 1 has no error bars or n per row; "n" for the panel-level numbers appears only in prose, inconsistently (sometimes n=525, sometimes n=90, sometimes omitted).
- "the widest [interval] being [63.2, 89.2]" and similar interval-heavy prose is hard to parse without knowing which of the "six interval constructions" produced which number — a table would help far more than embedding six numbers in one sentence.
- Fig. 1 caption promises interpretability "on their own," but panel (b)'s "hollow markers are the p = k arm, matched to neither in vocabulary nor length" is not explained anywhere near where a reader first encounters period p — this needs a forward pointer or a sentence in the main text.
- The abstract's information density (≥15 distinct quantitative claims in one paragraph) makes it very difficult to hold the paper's actual claim structure in mind; a shorter, claim-first abstract with numbers moved to the body would read better under the "inform, not persuade" standard this venue expects.
- Novelty attribution check produced nothing adverse: the cited prior art ([1]–[7], [14], [16]–[22], and the two arXiv IDs I could independently verify, Venkatesh 2605.09239 and the authors' own companion paper 2604.08591) is real, correctly dated, and not obviously mischaracterized in the text provided.
- Limitations section (§5, closing paragraph) is reasonably honest — it names the training-frequency confound, the Spanish-arm judge weakness, and the audio-only validation of the judge — and isn't purely a fig leaf, though it doesn't mention the repo-availability gap that undercuts its own reproducibility claims.

### Belief update

I came in expecting the "repetition, not length" dissociation to collapse once I looked for a leak — either a judge artifact or a baseline that wasn't actually controlled for length. It didn't collapse: the design (fixed carrier/word-count, filler-substituted control) is genuinely well-constructed, and the four-recognizer robustness check in §3.2 is a real answer to the most obvious objection. So my belief in *"the TTS repeated-word counting deficit is driven by repetition specifically, not sequence length, in at least the six checkpoints tested"* moved up, moderately. My belief in *"the contraction mechanism the authors originally proposed explains the deficit"* moved to essentially zero — but that's the authors' own conclusion, stated plainly, so it's not really a belief they were pushing me toward. What did **not** move is my confidence in any of the paper's specific numerical values (the 46.8-point gap, the 76.4-point mixed-effects estimate, q = 21.0–347.7) as verified facts rather than authors' self-reports, because the one channel provided for verifying them is currently dead.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the phenomenon-level finding is credible and the robustness work is real, but the paper cannot be evaluated as "reproducible" or its statistical machinery independently checked while its cited GitHub repository returns a 404, `[Reproducibility]`.
