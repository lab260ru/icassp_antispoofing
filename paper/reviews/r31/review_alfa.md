STANCE: Adversarial (assume the headline result is false; what would falsify it?)

### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated words because the text is *long* or because it is *repetitive*, and tries to separate the two with a "repetition ladder": items where a word recurs k times, each paired with a length-matched control in which the k copies are replaced by distinct filler words. Across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa, Qwen3-TTS, XTTS-v2), plus held-out VITS, F5-TTS and CosyVoice 2, repeated items show a large, robust exact-match deficit relative to controls (median gap ~75 points at k≥6), which survives penalty sweeps, greedy decoding, exclusion-rule removal, and re-scoring with four different recognisers. A companion periodicity ladder shows the deficit is graded with period rather than a step function, and a Lean-4-verified "contraction" mechanism that the authors themselves proposed to explain the failure is measured and empirically rejected across five checkpoints (Jacobian expansion factor q ≫ 1 everywhere).

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The one causal manipulation offered for *why* some architectures dissociate (VITS immune, F5-TTS not) confounds per-token duration information with total duration/length, reopening the very length confound the paper's central design was built to rule out.
   **Where** — Section 3.2, the F5-TTS paragraph ("Supplying F5-TTS that duration lifts exact renderings 43.3% to 76.7% below k=12…").
   **Why it matters** — The proposed explanation is "where the count must live: VITS gives each token its own duration, F5-TTS estimates one total." The test given is supplying F5-TTS with (apparently) the correct per-token duration schedule, which simultaneously supplies a more accurate *total* duration. Without a control condition that gives F5-TTS an equally generous total-duration budget but *without* per-token boundaries, the result is equally consistent with "more accurate overall length/duration budget fixes counting" — i.e., a length effect — as with "per-token duration granularity fixes counting" — i.e., a repetition-specific mechanism. Given the whole first half of the paper hinges on ruling out length as an explanation, a single two-condition n=60 comparison that cannot separate these two accounts is a serious gap in an otherwise carefully controlled study.
   **What would address it** — Add a third arm: F5-TTS given the ground-truth *total* duration only (no per-repetition breakdown), matched in total length to the oracle condition. If that arm recovers the same gain, the "duration must live per-token" story collapses into a length story.

2. **Issue** — The headline k≥6 threshold, on which the sharpest contrast figures (75.4 points, 18.2% vs 94.3% exact) are built, is explicitly chosen after inspecting the data.
   **Where** — Section 3.2: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge, which is why the abstract gives the unconditioned figure too."
   **Why it matters** — This is textbook post-hoc threshold selection (HARKing). The authors' mitigation — a 420-specification robustness curve (34.4–86.4 points, "none reverses sign") — shows the *direction* of the effect is robust, but it does not restore the confirmatory status of the specific 75.4-point headline number, nor does it address that many of the "420 specifications" are themselves researcher degrees of freedom chosen after the fact (exclusion rules, judge choice, counting rule are stated to vary "in S14 and S28, not here," i.e., outside the robustness envelope). A paper that leans this heavily on a single, non-pre-registered, exploratory threshold for its most-quoted number needs to be much more explicit that the confirmatory claim is only the sign, not the magnitude.
   **What would address it** — Report the sign-only claim as the confirmatory result, and present the 75.4-point (and 76.7-point per-checkpoint) figures explicitly as exploratory/descriptive, ideally pre-registering the threshold and effect-size band on a held-out checkpoint (as was in fact done for CosyVoice 2 — that practice should be the norm here, not the exception).

3. **Issue** — The "six checkpoints, three architectures" generalization claim rests on a cluster size of 3 for the architecture random effect, which the authors themselves acknowledge is too small to identify a group-level scale.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "three groups barely identify a group-level scale and under the widest prior on it that gap touches zero (−1.6)."
   **Why it matters** — With n=3 clusters, any mixed-effects or partial-pooling estimate of between-architecture variance is essentially unidentified; the reported posterior predictive interval [5.9, 93.3] is enormous precisely because of this, and the point estimate should not be read as evidence of architecture-general robustness. The paper's own remedy — testing the extrapolation on a fourth, held-out architecture (CosyVoice 2) — is a genuinely strong design move and I don't dispute it landed inside the interval. But the interval is so wide it would have "confirmed" almost any outcome above chance; a single successful extrapolation against a near-uninformative prior is weaker validation than the prose implies.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the formal between-architecture variance estimate (report only the fixed effects and per-checkpoint results, which are well identified) or add at least 1-2 more genuinely independent architecture families before quoting group-level intervals.

4. **Issue** — The length-matched control is not a clean, single-factor manipulation: filler vocabulary is drawn from an 8-item pool cycled above k=8 (reintroducing periodicity into the "control" arm at high k), and the authors concede part of the never-cycled gap is a vocabulary confound rather than a repetition effect.
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "Above k = 8 the cycled control is itself period-8… Never-cycled fillers throughout remove it: the gap is 69.6 by checkpoint and 67.6 by family against 76.7 and 75.4 cycled… though most of that 7.1 points is vocabulary, not periodicity."
   **Why it matters** — This is a direct admission that roughly a tenth of the never-cycled/cycled discrepancy is attributable to something other than the repetition manipulation itself (word identity/frequency/phonetic difficulty differences between the fixed target word and the filler set), and it is disclosed only as an aside rather than isolated with a dedicated control (e.g., a control using a *single* repeated non-target filler word matched in frequency to the target). Without that, some non-trivial fraction of the "repetition, not length" gap could really be "this particular content word vs. these particular filler words," a lexical confound orthogonal to repetition.
   **What would address it** — Run a frequency/complexity-matched single-word control (one filler word repeated non-adjacently the same number of times, chosen to match the target word's phonotactic and corpus-frequency profile) to bound the vocabulary confound directly, rather than inferring its size indirectly from the cycled/never-cycled contrast.

5. **Issue** — The judge-selection audit (Whisper vs. CTC) is calibrated on a domain — concatenative/spliced audio with known ground truth — that does not match the domain being judged (natural TTS-model output), so the calibration may not transfer.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth…", n=12 over 6 donors) and the Spanish replication (S9, S27).
   **Why it matters** — Concatenated clips have different coarticulation, prosody-continuity and boundary artifacts than model-generated repeated speech; if the CTC recogniser's advantage over Whisper is partly an artifact of how cleanly spliced audio segments, the ratio-1.00-vs-0.19 comparison used to justify CTC as the primary instrument may not fully hold on the actual experimental stimuli. The subsequent four-recogniser cross-check on real model output (Section 3.2) is reassuring but addresses a different threat (blank-collapse) than domain mismatch in the initial audit does.
   **What would address it** — Repeat the ground-truth audit using genuine model-generated audio with an independently verified (e.g., forced-aligned or hand-counted) ground truth, rather than spliced concatenative audio, to confirm the CTC-over-Whisper advantage holds in the actual measurement domain.

6. **Issue** — Several pivotal sub-claims rest on very small samples with no positive control demonstrating the method can detect an effect when one is present, most notably the causal state-patching experiment used to argue the "rival hypothesis" is unresolved rather than refuted.
   **Where** — Section 5 (Discussion): "A rank-1 patch transplanting the probe's count coordinate moves the count not at all on three checkpoints… on Qwen3-TTS-1.7B neither does a carrier direction it decodes perfectly (1.46%)… on Llasa-8B nothing is detectable at all (n=36; S26)."
   **Why it matters** — If a direction the model "decodes perfectly" (the carrier control) also fails to move behavior when patched, this is evidence the patching methodology itself may be behaviorally inert on this model/setup, not evidence that the count representation is causally inert. With n=36 and no condition that successfully demonstrates the intervention can move *any* readout, the paper cannot distinguish "the state doesn't drive the policy" from "our intervention doesn't work here." The paper is appropriately cautious in labelling this "unresolved," but it is then used rhetorically to frame the rank-1 patch findings as informative null results throughout the discussion.
   **What would address it** — Include a manipulation with a known, strong behavioral effect (e.g., patching a direction known from other work to change speaker identity or prosody) as a positive control on the same checkpoints, to establish the intervention has power before drawing conclusions from its nulls on the count coordinate.

### Minor concerns

- Only three seeds per item; given the paper's own note that Qwen3-TTS's sampling defaults make it "the most stochastic family," three seeds is thin for estimating per-item exact-match variance, especially in small cells (e.g., n=90 for CosyVoice 2, n=60 for F5-TTS oracle-duration).
- No formally blinded human perceptual validation is reported — the "165-clip sample… checked by ear" is described only as spot-checkable, not as an inter-rater-reliability-scored validation study; all headline numbers are ASR-mediated.
- The equivalence bound used for the F5-TTS oracle-duration test (8.3 points) is not justified or pre-specified elsewhere in the text.
- The paper does not compare against or cite existing attention-alignment-constrained decoding methods aimed specifically at word skip/repeat failures in decoder-only TTS (e.g., [2404.19723], "Attention-Constrained Inference for Robust Decoder-Only Text-to-Speech"), which would be a natural mitigation baseline to test whether the counting deficit survives a known alignment fix, not just a penalty/sampling sweep.
- Typography/OCR artifacts throughout ("V ALL-E 2", spacing irregularities) should be cleaned up for camera-ready.
- The abstract's number "67.6 if the control's fillers are never cycled, and we quote the smaller" is stated without first defining "cycled," which is only explained in Section 3.1; consider a one-clause forward gloss.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core repetition-vs-length manipulation and multi-recogniser robustness checks are unusually careful, but the single causal test underpinning the VITS/F5-TTS mechanistic story reopens the length confound the paper otherwise rules out, the headline effect size rests on a post-hoc threshold, and the architecture-generalization statistics are built on an admittedly under-identified 3-cluster random effect.
