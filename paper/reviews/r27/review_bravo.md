Coverage note before the write-up: I ran two arXiv queries — (1) non-autoregressive TTS with explicit duration modelling, (2) modern CTC/ASR benchmarking — targeting the baselines axis. Neither surfaced a specific 2024–2026 paper that unambiguously supersedes either of this paper's baseline choices (VITS, wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self), but the fact that even a targeted search for "non-AR TTS explicit duration" returns mostly 2019–2022 systems plus one 2024 outlier is itself informative: the field has few contemporary, well-resourced non-AR alternatives, which makes the choice of a single 2021 model (VITS) as *the* representative of "non-AR, per-token duration" more defensible than it first appears, but does not rescue the ASR-judge choice, discussed below.

---

### Summary

The paper claims: (1) autoregressive TTS models miscount literal word repetitions, and this tracks repetition per se rather than sequence length (shown via a length-matched control ladder); (2) the deficit is driven by periodicity more than raw token-identity recurrence (a period ladder shows a graded, monotone effect that survives even where no token is adjacent to itself); (3) a Lean-4-verified contraction bound that the authors themselves proposed as the mechanism is measured directly and found false in every checkpoint tested. The evidence is unusually numerous and self-scrutinising — a specification curve, four recognisers, a formal proof plus an empirical refutation of its own premise, honest admission that a follow-up dissociation experiment ("is the count erased or just unreadable?") failed to resolve anything. That said, the entire empirical edifice — the effect sizes in the abstract, Figure 1, and Table 1 — rests on one primary ASR judge (a 2020-vintage wav2vec2 CTC model with no reported strength/WER context) and a two-model non-AR "baseline" comparison spanning a five-year capability gap. Given how load-bearing the judge is, and how visibly the authors know it (an entire subsection is devoted to defending the judge choice against Whisper), the corresponding effort to characterise or strengthen the *positive* choice — why wav2vec2-960h-lv60-self rather than a more capable 2024–2026 CTC system — is thin. My stance here is maximally adversarial: until the judge and the non-AR comparison are shown to be as strong as anything a competent skeptic would reach for, I treat the headline gap sizes as upper bounds contingent on instrument choice, not settled facts.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue:** The abstract and title ("Isolating the Counting Failure") claim more resolution than the paper delivers — the actual generative mechanism is explicitly *not* isolated; only one candidate (contraction) is ruled out. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where:** Abstract ("so it never described any decoder we measured"); Discussion, "The rival we could not decide" ("we could not build" a fixed-state readout manipulation).
   **Why it matters:** A reader who stops at the abstract believes the paper explains *why* TTS models miscount. It only explains one thing they don't do. The rejected-contraction result is a genuine (if modest) contribution, but "isolating the counting failure" oversells a paper whose own discussion section ends in an unresolved coin flip between "erased" and "erased-but-unreadable."
   **What would address it:** Retitle or reframe the abstract's closing clause to state plainly that the causal locus (state vs. readout) remains open; the refutation result should be framed as "one mechanism eliminated," not as the paper's terminal contribution.

2. **Issue:** Multiple robustness checks that the paper leans on heavily — the four-recogniser cross-validation, a "second CTC model with different pretraining," a "robust-finetuned" model — are named only generically in the main text, with no model IDs, sizes, or training data disclosed. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where:** §3.2, "Nor is it the recogniser…" paragraph.
   **Why it matters:** This is exactly the section meant to rule out judge-side artifact, i.e., the paper's single most important robustness claim ("ruling out the judge-side artifact that would otherwise explain it," per the abstract). An unnamed "robust-finetuned" ASR model could be a lightly fine-tuned variant of the same wav2vec2 checkpoint, in which case the four "independent" judges are not independent at all.
   **What would address it:** Name each recogniser (checkpoint, training corpus, param count) inline or in a table, not deferred wholesale to a supplement not present in the reviewed text.

3. **Issue:** The single significant p-value reported in the paper (P=0.010, shuffle-reintroduces-adjacency test) is computed by pooling two checkpoints with opposite-signed, large effects (Qwen +17.8 points, XTTS-v2 −30.0 points), which is a textbook setup for a pooled significance test that means nothing about any individual system. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where:** §3.1, "Does the ordering carry it?" paragraph.
   **Why it matters:** The paper elsewhere is admirably p-value-skeptical ("our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values," §3.1) — which makes it more notable that the one p-value it does foreground is the one place a pooled test actively obscures a sign-flipping, architecture-dependent result. Readers skimming for "the" statistically significant finding could easily walk away thinking ordering effects are a settled, unidirectional phenomenon.
   **What would address it:** Drop the pooled p-value or report it per-checkpoint; state directly in the main text (not just S28) that the two checkpoints disagree in sign, which the paper does eventually say but only after the p-value has already done its rhetorical work.

4. **Issue:** No alternative-explanation experiment addresses whether the "counting" failure is actually a training-distribution rarity effect (models rarely see k≥6 verbatim adjacent repeats in training corpora) rather than an architectural/representational limit — the one attempt (an independent LM's likelihood proxy) is explicitly conceded to be "weakened, not closed." `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where:** Discussion, "What the design does not separate."
   **Why it matters:** This is the most natural rival hypothesis to the entire paper's framing, and the length-matched control does not touch it — a length-matched control text with non-repeated fillers is *also* more probable/frequent under any reasonable LM than "word word word word word word," so the manipulation is confounded with training-corpus typicality in exactly the direction that would explain the whole result without invoking any interesting counting mechanism.
   **What would address it:** A direct manipulation of corpus-frequency-matched vs. -unmatched repeated strings (not just an LM-likelihood correlation check), or synthetic fine-tuning exposure to repeated-token text to see if the deficit is trainable away — which would be dispositive against a pure distributional account and is conspicuously the kind of thing a Lean-proof-writing team would be equipped to run.

5. **Issue [AXIS — Evidence, weak baselines]:** The paper's entire quantitative backbone (abstract numbers, Fig. 1, Table 1, the specification curve) is scored by a single, dated ASR judge — wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self, an English-only, LM-free CTC model released in 2020 — with no argument for why this, rather than a materially stronger 2024–2026 CTC/streaming recogniser, is the right default instrument; and the non-AR "one is immune, one is not" architecture claim rests on exactly two models, VITS (2021) and F5-TTS (2025), a four-year capability and design gap that confounds "architecture strategy" with "which decade the model is from."
   **Where:** §2 ("Behavioural measurement…"); §3.2 (VITS vs. F5-TTS paragraph); Table 1 caption.
   **Why it matters:** The paper is unusually careful about ruling out one weak-instrument failure mode (Whisper's autoregressive bias) but applies none of that scrutiny to its own chosen instrument's absolute strength — a CTC model six years old by the time of writing, whose blank-collapse behavior is independently flagged in the same section as a *known* confound direction ("CTC blank-collapse merges adjacent identical words… an artifact with the effect's own periodicity signature"). The paper answers "does blank-collapse manufacture the effect" but never answers "would a stronger, more modern CTC/streaming recogniser (with better handling of repeated speech, larger training data, or a Conformer-class acoustic model) produce the same 65–77 point gap, or a smaller one?" Likewise, VITS is the one non-AR model checked and found "not immune"; the paper explicitly flags this comparison as "suggestive rather than isolating" (§3.2) — a fair hedge, but that hedge appears once, deep in results, while the abstract synthesizes the finding as a settled empirical fact about "two non-autoregressive baselines."
   **What would address it:** Re-score the headline Fig. 1 / Table 1 numbers (not just a robustness footnote) with at least one current, strong, non-blank-collapsing recogniser as the *primary* judge, and add a third non-AR model contemporaneous with F5-TTS (e.g., a 2024–2025 explicit-duration flow/diffusion NAR system) so the architecture-strategy claim isn't resting on a two-point, era-confounded comparison.

### Minor concerns

- Fig. 1 caption reports medians/rates but the figure itself shows no visible error bars or CI bands for the per-checkpoint curves — text-reported intervals (e.g., "[63.2, 89.2]") don't obviously map onto anything visualisable in the figure as described.
- "Qwen3-TTS" as a model name should be double-checked against actual released Qwen TTS model naming/versioning before publication — a wrong version number in a headline table is an easy, embarrassing catch for reviewers.
- Companion reference [7] is a same-author 2026 preprint (arXiv:2604.08591) cited to support methodological choices; readers can't independently verify claims resting on an unreviewed self-citation.
- Reference [13] (Venkatesh, arXiv:2605.09239) is used to corroborate "it stays decodable late" — worth flagging this is also a 2026 preprint, i.e., two of the paper's load-bearing citations are unreviewed same-year work.
- CosyVoice 2 is introduced mid-Results as an out-of-panel prediction test without ever appearing in the "Models" list in §2 — a forward pointer in §2 would help orientation.
- The prose density (e.g., the §3.1 paragraph running "76.7 [68.4, 84.4]... 76.4... [63.2, 89.2]... 65.3 [5.9, 93.3]... −1.6...") packs 8+ numbers into single sentences with no table to anchor them, which makes the claims hard to audit by eye even though the underlying rigor is real — this is a presentation cost the paper pays for its own thoroughness.
- The Lean-verified bound section states the proof is "free of sorry and with an axiom audit" but the paper text gives no pointer to where in the linked repo this proof or its audit log actually lives, beyond the general "public at tag v1.3-isolating."

### Belief update

I moderately update toward "TTS repetition-counting failure is a real, repetition-specific phenomenon, not a length or exposure-bias artifact" — the length-matched control and the period ladder are genuinely well-designed distinguishing tests, and the four-recogniser cross-check (even under-specified as it is) makes pure judge-artifact a less likely full explanation than I'd have assumed walking in. I update much less on the mechanism: a priori I already thought a single global-contraction story was too simple to survive contact with real decoders, so watching it get formally refuted mostly confirms rather than surprises. I do not update toward "the effect size (65–77 points) is a stable, instrument-independent number" — that number is only as trustworthy as a dated, uncharacterized CTC judge, and I hold that specific figure as provisional pending a stronger primary recogniser.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the empirical design (repetition-vs-length, period ladder, formal-bound refutation) is sound and the honesty about what wasn't resolved is a real strength, but the paper's headline numbers all pass through one dated, uncharacterized ASR judge and a two-model, era-confounded non-AR comparison, and until those baselines are strengthened or the effect is shown to hold under a demonstrably stronger recogniser, the reported gap sizes should be read as instrument-contingent.
