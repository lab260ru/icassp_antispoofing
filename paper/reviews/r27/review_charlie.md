LENS: Experimental design (controls, confounds, blinding, pre-registration)
STANCE: Adversarial (assume the headline result is false; what would falsify it?)

### Summary
The paper asks whether neural TTS models fail to render repeated phrases correctly because the input is *long* or because it is *periodic*, and argues for the latter using a 180-item "repetition ladder": items where a word repeats k times, each paired with a length-matched control where the k repeats are replaced by distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2) scored by a CTC recogniser (justified as superior to Whisper, whose autoregressive decoder shares the failure mode), repeated items are rendered exactly correct in 18.2% of generations versus 94.3% for controls at k≥6. A second "period ladder" shows the deficit is graded with period (10.4→93.1% exact as p goes 1→8) and that roughly half the deficit survives even at period 2, where no token touches itself. The authors then propose and formally verify (in Lean 4) a contraction-based explanation for a Lipschitz-readout counting bound, measure the relevant contraction factor q across five checkpoints, and find q is always >>1 (21.0–347.7) — i.e., the mechanism they built the study around is empirically false in every decoder tested.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The controls the headline comparison depends on are not actually aperiodic once k exceeds the filler pool size, so the central "repetition, not length" contrast is partly a repetition-vs-repetition (period 1 vs period 8) comparison, not a repetition-vs-aperiodic-text comparison.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("cycled above k=8"), Section 3.1 ("Above k=8 the cycled control is itself period-8... Never-cycled fillers throughout remove it: the gap is 69.6 by checkpoint and 67.6 by family against 76.7 and 75.4 cycled").
   **Why it matters** — The abstract and Figure 1(a)/Table 1 report figures built on the ships-with-the-ladder (cycled) condition — 18.2% vs 94.3%, the number readers will remember — while the cleaner, confound-free version (never-cycled fillers) is relegated to a parenthetical correction that shows a materially smaller gap (67.6 vs 75.4 points). For a paper whose entire contribution is *isolating* repetition from a confound, running the flagship analysis on a control that reintroduces periodicity at exactly the k range (≥8) where most of the dramatic figures live is a serious design lapse. It does not fully undermine the finding — the never-cycled analysis still shows a large gap — but it means the paper's most-quoted numbers overstate the isolation the design is supposed to deliver.
   **What would address it** — Report the never-cycled analysis as primary (or expand the filler pool so no k in the ladder needs cycling), and re-derive the abstract's headline percentages from that condition.

2. **Issue** — The k≥6 threshold, the choice of which exclusion rules to apply, and the decision to run a specification curve at all were all made after inspecting where the arms diverge in this same fixed 180-item stimulus set — there is no independent, pre-registered replication ladder generated blind to the result.
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("The k≥6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge... Two exploratory analyses, dated in S16, are reported in text alone (S8, S10, S17)").
   **Why it matters** — The 420-specification robustness sweep is a genuinely good practice, but it tests sensitivity to *analytic* choices on the *same* 180 items, not sensitivity to *sampling* a new set of carriers/fillers. A design that overfits which carrier sentences and filler pools happen to produce a clean effect would pass this specification curve trivially, since the curve never resamples stimuli. The paper's own honesty about post-hoc threshold selection is commendable, but honesty about a practice doesn't neutralize its effect on effect-size inflation.
   **What would address it** — Generate a second, independently authored stimulus ladder (different carriers/fillers/templates) after the k threshold and exclusion rules are frozen, and report whether the gap replicates at a similar magnitude out-of-sample. The CosyVoice-2 out-of-panel prediction test (Section 3.1) is a good instance of exactly this discipline applied to *architectures*; the same logic is missing for *stimuli*.

3. **Issue** — The judge-validity audits that license discarding Whisper and trusting the CTC recogniser are conducted on tiny samples of "concatenative audio with known ground truth," not on the actual failure-mode audio (loops, truncations, garbled repeats) the recogniser must score in the real experiment.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("n=12 over 6 donors" for Whisper; "n=24, mean 0.996" for CTC; Spanish audit "S9, S27").
   **Why it matters** — n=12 and n=24 are far too small to establish, with any real precision, that a recogniser is unbiased in the specific regime that matters (repeated/looping/truncated speech). More importantly, "concatenative audio" (presumably artificially spliced clean clips) is not the same acoustic distribution as a model's actual degenerate output — loops and truncations may sound different (mumbled, unstable prosody, cut-off phonemes) from cleanly concatenated ground truth, and a recogniser validated on the latter is not thereby validated on the former. The disagreement-direction argument in S14 (four-recogniser replication) partially compensates for this, but it is an indirect check, not a direct validation on hard cases.
   **What would address it** — Validate the CTC judge (and Whisper) directly on a sample of actual model-generated degenerate audio with human transcription as ground truth, not only on synthetic concatenative controls, and report confidence intervals around the audit ratios given the small n.

4. **Issue** — The one experiment designed to test whether *periodic ordering itself* (as opposed to token identity/composition) drives the deficit gives architecture-contradictory results, yet the paper's title and abstract assert a single, unified "repetition, not length" story.
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "Does the ordering carry it?" ("that pool averages two checkpoints gaining 17.8 points, one null, and XTTS-v2 losing 30.0, intervals excluding zero both ways... Periodic ordering drives Qwen, not the panel").
   **Why it matters** — This is a genuine confound in the causal story: two of the paper's three architecture families show opposite responses to the same shuffle manipulation. That is not a robustness footnote — it means the mechanism differs by architecture, and the paper cannot currently say whether "repetition" is one phenomenon or two coincidentally overlapping ones. The abstract's framing ("The limit tracks repetition, not length") implies a unified account that the ordering experiment does not support.
   **What would address it** — Either scope the "ordering matters" claim explicitly to Qwen in the abstract, or run the shuffle test on more checkpoints per family (currently one shuffle result per architecture) before treating architecture-level disagreement as established rather than as a single-model idiosyncrasy.

5. **Issue** — The non-autoregressive comparison (VITS vs. F5-TTS) and the "duration-conditioning rescues F5-TTS" follow-up rest on exactly one model per architectural strategy, which the authors themselves flag as "suggestive rather than isolating," yet the discussion still draws a mechanistic conclusion ("what plausibly separates the two is where the count must live") from it.
   **Where** — Section 3.2 (VITS/F5-TTS comparison, n=90 and n=60) and Discussion ("The duration story rests on one model per strategy").
   **Why it matters** — With one exemplar per non-AR strategy, there is no way to separate an architecture-level effect (per-token duration vs. global duration estimate) from idiosyncratic properties of VITS or F5-TTS specifically (training data, duration predictor quality, alignment mechanism). The duration-conditioning experiment (giving F5-TTS the oracle ground-truth duration) is an oracle intervention that could leak count-correlated information through channels other than "knowing how long to be" — duration length is itself informative about token count — so "still cannot place thirty-two repetitions in it" is consistent with the paper's story but doesn't uniquely confirm it.
   **What would address it** — Add a second model per non-AR strategy (e.g., another duration-predicting NAR model, another flow-matching model) before generalizing to "duration vs. no duration" as an architecture-level explanatory axis, and report whether the oracle-duration boost holds when duration is perturbed to be uninformative about count but still correct in total length.

6. **Issue** — The hierarchical/partial-pooling estimate of the repetition-length gap for architectures outside the panel is built from only three architecture-family groups, which the authors acknowledge barely identifies a group-level variance, yet the number (65.3 [5.9, 93.3]) is quoted prominently in Section 3.1 before the single confirmatory CosyVoice-2 test is introduced.
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("three groups barely identify a group-level scale and under the widest prior on it that gap touches zero (−1.6)... but we tested it").
   **Why it matters** — This is a case where the authors flag the fragility themselves, which is good practice, but the paper still leans on the number rhetorically ("nearer its 65.3 mean than an interval that wide entitles us to expect") before conceding elsewhere that with n=3 groups the effect could be zero under a different prior. One successful out-of-panel replication (CosyVoice-2) is reassuring but is still a single data point; it does not resolve whether the group-level variance estimate is trustworthy in general.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the point estimate in favor of reporting the interval alone, or add a second out-of-panel architecture test before treating the extrapolation as validated.

### Minor concerns
- The related-work citation to Venkatesh [13] (arXiv:2605.09239) is confirmed to exist and match the described finding (linear probes decoding counts even where output is wrong) — but the analogy from *text* LLM state-tracking to *TTS decoder* hidden-state counting is applied without discussing whether the text and speech decoding stacks are architecturally comparable enough for the analogy to transfer; this should be stated as an assumption, not an established parallel.
- "ˆc" and other math notation render as raw Unicode artifacts (likely a PDF-extraction issue) rather than proper LaTeX math — check the camera-ready render.
- The abstract packs an unusually large number of numeric results (67.6, 75.4, 46.8, 10.4/47.9/73.6/93.1, 17.8/30.0, 61.7, 65.1–76.7, 0.19/1.00, 21.0–347.7) with little connective explanation; a reader without the body text cannot tell which numbers are primary vs. robustness checks.
- Section 3.2's claim "not for want of competence (S13)" for VITS is asserted but the supporting detail is not in the visible text — say explicitly what independent evidence rules out VITS simply being a weak model overall.
- The "gains above 8.3 points excluded" criterion in the F5-TTS duration experiment (S13, S20) is stated without explaining why that particular exclusion threshold was chosen or what it excludes — this reads as another post-hoc analytic choice inside an already small (n=60) sample.
- The claim that Qwen3-TTS is "the most stochastic family" so its null result "is not an artifact of conservative decoding" is asserted as a settled fact but no direct entropy/temperature comparison across families is shown in the main text.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the core manipulation and the paper's transparency about its own limitations are genuinely strong, but the flagship comparison partially rests on controls that are not aperiodic at the k range being showcased, the analytic choices (threshold, exclusions) were fixed after seeing the same data used throughout, and the one experiment meant to nail down the causal mechanism (shuffle/ordering) contradicts itself across architectures — each must be fixed or clearly re-scoped before the headline claims can stand as currently framed.
