### Summary
The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-documented failure to render a repeated phrase the correct number of times is driven by input *length* or by input *repetition/periodicity*, and separately whether a formally derivable "contraction" mechanism explains it. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" (a target word repeated k times, paired with a length-matched control whose repeated slot is filled with distinct filler words) rendered by six checkpoints across three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus VITS and F5-TTS as non-autoregressive baselines, the paper reports that repeated text is rendered exactly in 18.2% of generations versus 94.3% for controls at k≥6, that the deficit is graded with the period of repetition (not a step function), survives greedy decoding, a repetition-penalty sweep, exclusion-rule removal, and rescoring with four different recognisers, and that Whisper (the "obvious" judge) is itself biased toward manufacturing part of the effect, motivating use of a CTC recogniser instead. A companion Lean-4-verified "contraction" bound that would explain the failure via a shrinking per-repetition state map is then measured directly and rejected (estimated contraction factor q ranges 21.0–347.7, i.e. expansive, not contractive) on every checkpoint tested.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The design cannot separate "the text repeats" from "the produced acoustic output is self-similar/periodic," so the paper's title claim of *isolating the counting failure* to repetition-in-text is not actually established relative to the alternative that autoregressive audio-frame self-similarity (an acoustic-domain phenomenon) is doing the work.
   **Where** — Throughout Section 2 (Stimuli) and Section 4 (mechanism, "boundary states," "attention over k repeated spans"); implicit in the framing against Venkatesh [13] and the text-counting literature [15–20] in Section 5.
   **Why it matters** — In a text-only LLM, you can present a token sequence that repeats without the model having produced any of it yet, cleanly isolating input repetition from output dynamics. In TTS, asking a model to say the same word k times *necessarily* also makes it *produce* near-identical consecutive audio frames — the input manipulation and an output-side confound (repeated codec/acoustic state) are yoked by construction. The mechanism analysis in Section 4 explicitly operates on "boundary states" between repetitions — i.e., decoder-internal audio-domain dynamics — which is really testing an acoustic-repetition hypothesis, not a text-repetition hypothesis, even though the behavioral framing (Section 3, abstract, title) sells the result as demonstrating a text-repetition-specific counting failure analogous to LLM token counting. The period-2 result (no token adjacent to itself, "half the deficit remains") narrows but does not close this: it rules out *literal acoustic adjacency* of one rendered word to itself, but not periodicity of the underlying acoustic pattern across the utterance, which is still forced by periodic text.
   **What would address it** — A manipulation where the *text* is non-repetitive but the model is forced to *produce* repetitive/self-similar acoustic frames (e.g., via forced/duplicated codec-token continuations, or teacher-forcing repeated audio for non-repeated text), or conversely repeated text rendered with enforced acoustic diversity (e.g., prosodic perturbation between repetitions), to see whether the deficit tracks the text manipulation or the acoustic one when they are decoupled.

2. **Issue** — Architecture-level generalizations are drawn from extremely small numbers of systems while being reported with statistical machinery (mixed-effects models, posterior predictive intervals) that implies more power than 2–3 clusters can support.
   **Where** — "By family the gap is 75.4 [71.1, 78.9]... by checkpoint 76.7..."; the CosyVoice 2 "out-of-panel" prediction (Section 3.1, posterior interval [5.9, 93.3] under the default prior, touching −1.6 under the widest prior); the non-AR baseline claim ("of two non-autoregressive baselines one is immune, one is not... suggestive rather than isolating"); the ordering/shuffle result ("two checkpoints gaining 17.8 points, one null, and XTTS-v2 losing 30.0").
   **Why it matters** — A mixed-effects model with architecture as a random effect fit on three clusters barely identifies a group-level variance (the authors say so themselves), so the CosyVoice "confirmation" is one new data point landing inside a very wide interval — this is weak evidence dressed as a pre-registered confirmatory test. Likewise, the claim that ordering "drives Qwen, not the panel" and the claim that the failure is "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" both rest on 2–3 systems with disagreeing signs, which is not enough to support architecture-general claims stated as confidently as they are in the abstract and discussion.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the architecture-generalization language throughout (abstract and discussion) to explicitly flag n=2–3 per claim, or add more architectures/checkpoints per condition (especially for the non-AR and shuffle-ordering claims) before asserting directional, architecture-specific conclusions.

3. **Issue** — Controls are built from a small, cycled filler pool (8 items per template), and the paper does not describe randomization/counterbalancing of filler assignment, filler order, or whether the "target word" is held fixed within (or across) templates.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Stimuli": "the repeated token replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k = 8"; the "never-cycled fillers" analysis in Section 3.1.
   **Why it matters** — If filler assignment is deterministic/ordered rather than randomized per trial, a systematic difference between "the target word" and "the filler set" (phonetic difficulty, frequency, prosodic salience) could partly explain the gap independent of repetition per se. The paper's own "never-cycled" check reduces but does not eliminate this ("most of that 7.1 points is vocabulary, not periodicity"), and the Discussion explicitly concedes the frequency confound is "weakened, not closed." There is also no report of how many distinct target words exist across the 180-item ladder or whether results replicate across word identity (only template-level leave-one-out is reported, for the probe, not the main behavioral effect).
   **What would address it** — Report the randomization procedure for filler assignment explicitly; report the main repetition-vs-control gap broken out by target-word identity (or at minimum a leave-one-word-out robustness check analogous to the leave-one-template-out check already used for the probe).

4. **Issue** — The headline k≥6 threshold is explicitly chosen post-hoc after inspecting where the arms diverge, and this data-dependent threshold is what is foregrounded in the abstract's marquee numbers (18.2% vs 94.3%, the 67.6/75.4-point family gaps), with an accompanying 420-specification robustness check that is explicitly uncorrected for multiple comparisons.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge"; "Across 420 specifications... we correct across none of them."
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably transparent about this (it also reports the unconditioned 46.8-point gap from k=1), and the specification-curve framing is a reasonable way to report robustness without p-hacking one number. But readers and future citers will likely quote the post-hoc-threshold numbers (18.2%/94.3%) as the paper's central result, and the paper does not clearly separate "confirmatory" from "exploratory" analyses anywhere except a single footnoted commit hash (S16) for the CosyVoice prediction. There is no external, timestamped pre-registration (e.g., OSF) for any other analysis — only in-repo git commits, which the authors themselves control and could in principle have amended before "committing."
   **What would address it** — Lead the abstract/results with the unconditioned (k=1) or a pre-specified threshold number rather than the post-hoc-selected one, and use an external pre-registration mechanism (OSF timestamp, or similar third-party attestation) rather than a self-controlled git commit for any claim described as "committed before" evidence existed.

5. **Issue** — The recogniser "audit" that licenses trusting the CTC judge over Whisper is itself based on very small samples of synthetic, concatenative ground-truth audio that may not resemble the actual pathological (looping/truncated) audio being scored.
   **Where** — Section 2: "Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth... (0.19 of the true count, n=12 over 6 donors)... CTC recogniser... ratio 1.00 (n=24, mean 0.996)."
   **Why it matters** — Concatenated clean audio is not the same signal class as audio actually produced by a model that is looping, truncating, or otherwise degenerating — such audio may contain pitch resets, energy discontinuities, or coarticulation artifacts at repeat boundaries that a recogniser's error profile on clean concatenation would not predict. The entire justification for preferring the CTC judge over Whisper rests on n=12/n=24 synthetic-audio trials, which is a thin empirical base for a methodological choice that the whole downstream 1558-generation analysis depends on.
   **What would address it** — Validate the recogniser choice on actual model-generated looping/repetition failures with known (human-verified) ground truth, not only on clean concatenative audio, and report a larger validation sample.

### Minor concerns
- The "165-clip stratified sample... so a judgement can be checked by ear" is offered as a check on scoring validity, but no inter-rater agreement statistic or formal listener study is reported — it reads as spot-checking rather than a validated human baseline.
- Reference [7] ("From dispersion to attraction...", arXiv:2604.08591) is a same-group, very recent companion preprint cited as established context; its findings are not independently verifiable within this review and its status (peer-reviewed vs. preprint) should be made explicit.
- The abstract is dense with numeric callouts (67.6, 75.4, 46.8, 65.1–76.7, 21.0–347.7, etc.) to the point of being difficult to parse on a first read; consider moving some to the body and keeping the abstract to 2–3 headline numbers.
- "Cycled" vs. "never-cycled" control terminology is introduced without a clear upfront definition before it is used quantitatively in Section 3.1 — a brief explicit definition earlier in Section 2 would help.
- The claim "not for want of competence (S13)" regarding VITS is unsupported in the main text (deferred entirely to supplement) despite being used to rule out a substantive alternative explanation.
- Table 1's "count err. (%) ctl." column is uniformly 0.0 for every model, which is only informative if control variance is also reported (not present in the main table).

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the core repetition-vs-length dissociation is supported by an unusually thorough (and admirably self-critical) battery of robustness checks, but the paper's strongest framing claims — that it *isolates* text repetition (as opposed to acoustic self-similarity) and that the effect generalizes cleanly across architectures — rest on confounds and small-n generalizations the authors themselves partly flag but do not close, and the headline numbers derive from a post-hoc threshold that should not be the primary quoted statistic.
