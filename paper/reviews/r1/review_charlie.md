### Summary

The paper's steelmanned case is genuinely appealing: repetition hallucination in AR-TTS is reframed not as a decoding accident but as a consequence of a contracting per-repetition state map, the implication is machine-checked in Lean (a real and unusual level of rigor for a speech paper), and the periodicity-vs-length control (Fig. 1b) is a sharp, well-designed distinguishing test — matched carriers, matched word counts, only the repetition structure changed, and the two curves diverge exactly where the theory says they should. The authors are also commendably honest in §4.4 about a failed measurement attempt. That's the best version of this paper. Having stated it, I don't think it survives contact with its own numbers. The theorem's proof is sound as an implication (contraction ⇒ horizon), but its operative empirical premise — that the model's per-repetition map actually contracts with a measurable q — is never delivered: the boundary-based q̂ estimator described in Methods is explicitly retracted in §4.4 as "unsound," no numeric q̂ ever appears anywhere in the paper, and the abstract's claim that "the horizon implied by q̂ tracks the observed k∗" is consequently unsupported by anything in the results. On top of that, the empirical panel is far smaller than advertised — Table 1, the only place with per-model numbers, has exactly two rows (Llasa-1B, 1B; XTTS-v2, 0.4B), not the "1B–8B scale ladder" and "two families" plus Qwen3-TTS the abstract and Methods describe, and several scope claims (e.g. "the within-family Llasa ladder does not order k∗ monotonically with scale") are asserted about a ladder for which only one data point is ever shown. The result is a paper whose theoretical spine is fine and whose best experiment is genuinely good, wrapped in an empirical narrative that oversells what was actually measured.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The periodicity-vs-length control doesn't rule out a naturalness/perplexity confound as the real driver of the dissociation. `[Evidence — alternative explanation]`
   **Where** — §3 Method ("Crucially, every word- and sentence-repetition item at k≥2 has a length-matched control...the repeated token replaced by k distinct fillers") and Fig. 1(b).
   **Why it matters** — Twelve identical repetitions of one word is a far more out-of-distribution string than twelve distinct filler words in a natural-sounding sentence, independent of periodicity per se. The observed divergence (10.6% vs 56.9% at k≥6) is consistent with "the model fails on inputs unlike its training distribution" just as well as with "the model fails on periodic inputs because its per-repetition map contracts." These are not the same hypothesis, and the paper's sharpest test doesn't distinguish them.
   **What would address it** — Report perplexity (or an LM likelihood proxy) of repeated vs. control items under a reference text model, and ideally add a third condition that is periodic but drawn from natural corpora (e.g., real anaphora-heavy text) to hold naturalness roughly constant while varying periodicity.

2. **Issue** — A closely related, contemporaneous theoretical account of attractor/clustered fixed-point structure in self-attention is acknowledged only via a dangling internal-file citation, and the paper's only outside corroboration (Table 2, Ref. [4]) is a self-citation that also breaks anonymity. `[Novelty]`
   **Where** — Reference list, entry [10]: "Contemporaneous statistical-mechanics account of clustered fixed points in self-attention; see literature/gaps.md for how our claim differs" and the sentence "This work is the autoregressive-decoder companion to a spectral study of hallucination in encoder–decoder ASR [4]" (§1).
   **Why it matters** — [2608.08922] ("Clustered Attractor Manifolds and Dynamical Condensation in Self-Attention," Gao/Yang/Chen, 2026) derives attractor structure in self-attention dynamics from first principles via an "overlap gap" governing cluster formation — the same phenomenon class (fixed points arising from attention dynamics) this paper claims to be first to formalize for hallucination. The differentiation is promised but not delivered in the submitted text: a reviewer literally cannot check whether the claimed contribution is novel relative to it. Separately, [4] gives full author names (Viakhirev, Borodin, Mkrtchian) for a paper explicitly called "the companion" to this "anonymous" submission — this both deanonymizes the authors and means Table 2's cross-domain corroboration is not independent evidence, just the same group's other paper.
   **What would address it** — Inline discussion of how Theorem 1 differs from [2608.08922] (e.g., decoding-horizon vs. depth-indexed convergence, as the Discussion gestures at for [9]), and proper anonymization of self-citations for the review copy.

3. **Issue** — No code, data, generation hyperparameters, or repository link is given anywhere, despite Lean formalization being described as "part of our release." `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — §2.3 ("The check is one command and is part of our release") and throughout §3 (no sampling temperature/top-p/top-k, no exact prompt templates, no chance-level formula for the "chance-adjusted 0.5" threshold that defines k*).
   **Why it matters** — A competent grad student could re-derive the Lean proof from the paper's math, but could not reproduce a single number in Table 1 or Figure 1/2: the decoding parameters that materially affect looping/truncation behavior in AR generation are never stated, and "chance-adjusted 0.5" is used to define the headline k* statistic without ever being defined.
   **What would address it** — An anonymized code/data link, explicit decoding hyperparameters per model, and a formula (or worked example) for the chance-adjustment.

4. **Issue** — Lemma 1 (attention dilution) and Theorem 1 (state contraction) are both invoked to explain the same observation (effective-rank saturation, Fig. 2), and no experiment separates their contributions. `[Evidence — missing ablation]`
   **Where** — §2.1–2.2 (Assumption 1 derived "from" Lemma 1) and §4.3 (effective-rank collapse presented as confirming Theorem 1(ii)).
   **Why it matters** — A flattening attention profile mechanically reduces the diversity of information entering the decoder at each boundary regardless of whether the recurrent update F is a genuine contraction (q<1). The effective-rank result is consistent with "dilution alone lowers input diversity" without any dynamical contraction being present at all — which would leave Theorem 1's counting-horizon machinery doing no explanatory work beyond Lemma 1. Nothing in the paper isolates which one is load-bearing.
   **What would address it** — A condition where Assumption 1 (dilution) holds but Assumption 2 (contraction) is suppressed or absent — e.g., an intervention that measurably keeps q near 1 while attention still dilutes — and check whether effective-rank saturation and counting failure survive.

5. **Issue** — The abstract's headline empirical claim, and the reported scope of the study, are not supported by the paper's own tables. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — Abstract ("boundary-state distances decay geometrically with measured q̂ < 1, and the horizon implied by q̂ tracks the observed k∗"; "two checkpoints spanning two families and a 1B–8B scale ladder") vs. §4.4 ("That estimator is unsound here and we report it as such... The capacity measure above needs no boundaries, which is why we adopted it") and Table 1 (rows: Llasa-1B, 1B; XTTS-v2, 0.4B).
   **Why it matters** — This is a stack of independent statistical-rigour failures around the paper's one operative quantity. First, no numeric value of q̂ appears anywhere in the results — the method used to estimate it is explicitly retracted, and the substitute (capacity gain dNeff/d log k) is a different statistic that is never mathematically related back to q or to N* in Eq. (1), so the abstract's q̂-tracks-k∗ claim has zero supporting numbers in the body. Second, the claimed "1B–8B scale ladder" and "two families" is, in the only table with numbers, two models spanning 0.4B–1B — not 1B–8B, and the §4.1 claim that "the within-family Llasa ladder does not order k∗ monotonically with scale" is asserted about a ladder represented in Table 1 by a single point (Llasa-1B), which cannot establish non-monotonicity. Third, "the two 95% intervals are disjoint for two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints" (§4.3) is a trivial 100%-of-n=2 statement dressed as a finding, and the matched-pair "positive gap in 75% of pairs" is given with no total pair count, no CI, no significance test. Fourth, 59.7% of items are quarantined for transcript/duration disagreement (§3) with no check on whether quarantine rate correlates with proximity to k* — exactly where such a correlation would bias the accuracy curve, since ambiguous transcriptions are plausibly concentrated near the collapse point. Fifth, Lemma 1 predicts a k⁻¹ attention-dilution exponent; the measured exponent is k⁻⁰·²¹ (§4.5) — roughly 5× off in log-log slope — and the entropy slope predicted at 1 is measured at 0.50, roughly 2× off; both are reported as "checkable" confirmations without a goodness-of-fit statistic or any acknowledgment of the magnitude discrepancy. Individually some of these might be forgivable; together they mean the paper's central quantitative bridge between theorem and data is asserted, not shown.
   **What would address it** — Report an actual q̂ per model (even a coarse one, with stated uncertainty) and its correlation/regression against observed k*; either deliver data for all six named checkpoints or rewrite the abstract/Methods to state the true n=2 scope; report total pair counts and a significance test for the 75% figure; analyze whether quarantined items cluster near k*; report R²/goodness-of-fit for the Lemma-1 exponent fits and discuss the 5× discrepancy explicitly rather than treating direction-only agreement as confirmation.

### Minor concerns

- Two literal broken citations in the text: "documented as a stability problem [1, ?]" (§1) and "transcribed with Whisper large-v3 [?]" (§3) — Whisper, the instrument the entire behavioral measurement pipeline depends on, is never actually cited.
- Reference [1] is noted as "Preprint withdrawn 13 Feb 2026 by the authors pending institutional approval" — leaning on a withdrawn preprint to establish that the failure mode is "well documented" is shaky framing.
- Table 1's caption promises a q̂ column ("ˆq is the median per-repetition contraction factor over deep probe layers...") that does not appear among the table's actual columns (k*, accuracy rep/ctl, capacity gain rep/ctl/ratio).
- Fig. 2(b) caption has a raw LaTeX escape leaking into the text: "95\% CI."
- §4.1's phrase "for all two checkpoints" and §4.3's "for two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints" read like un-substituted template variables — worth a proofreading pass regardless of cause, since it undercuts confidence that other numbers were checked carefully.
- Table 2's "faithful vs. hallucinated" split criterion is never defined, and no N per bucket is given.
- No error bars in Table 1 despite "three seeds" per item being stated in Methods.
- XTTS-v2's built-in repetition penalty of 5.0 is disclosed as a confound in Limitations — good practice, but it means one of only two reported models is explicitly a "conservative" (non-neutral) test of the theory, which weakens an already n=2 panel further.

### Belief update

Mild, narrow update: I now find it somewhat more plausible that periodic repetition specifically (not merely length or exposure) produces a qualitatively distinct AR-TTS failure mode, restricted to the two models actually reported. I did not update meaningfully on the paper's specific mechanistic claim — that a contracting per-repetition hidden-state map is the cause — because the one number that would test it (q̂) is never delivered, and a contemporaneous result on repeated-token counting in text LLMs, [2605.09239] ("Repeated-Token Counting Reveals a Dissociation Between Representations and Outputs," 2026), reports the opposite mechanism in an adjacent setting: linear probes (a Lipschitz readout, the exact class Theorem 1(iv) rules out under contraction) decode the correct count near-perfectly at every layer, even at the layer where the wrong output crystallizes — i.e., the information survives, and it's the output policy, not representational capacity, that fails. That's a live, unaddressed competing hypothesis for exactly the phenomenon class this paper is about, and the paper doesn't cite it or rule it out.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the theorem is sound as an implication and the periodicity control is a good idea, but the paper's one empirical bridge from theory to data (a measured q̂ that predicts k*) is explicitly retracted in §4.4 and never replaced with an actual number, while the abstract still asserts it as a finding.
