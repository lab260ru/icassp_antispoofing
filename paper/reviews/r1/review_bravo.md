### Summary

The paper argues that AR-TTS "repetition hallucination" (looping, truncation, miscounting when a phrase repeats k times) is not a sampling artifact but a provable consequence of contraction dynamics in the decoder's hidden state: if the per-repetition update map F contracts (q<1) and if diluted attention over repeated spans makes F approximately state-independent of which copy is being rendered, then no Lipschitz readout — including the stop head — can distinguish repetition counts past a horizon N* that the authors derive in closed form and formalize in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, the authors report a battery of measurements on "two checkpoints spanning two families and a 1B–8B scale ladder" (elsewhere described as Llasa-1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, and Qwen3-TTS-0.6B/1.7B): counting accuracy collapses past a model-specific k*, length-matched non-repetitive controls survive, effective-rank of the generation trajectory saturates under repetition but not under controls, attention dilutes with k, and a fitted contraction factor q̂ is claimed to predict the collapse horizon. The headline claim is that repetition hallucination is "a fixed point, not noise."

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The abstract's core empirical claim — "boundary-state distances decay geometrically with measured q̂ < 1, and the horizon implied by q̂ tracks the observed k*" — is contradicted by the paper's own §4.4, which states that the boundary-distance estimator for q (fitting log‖h_{m+1}−h_m‖ = a + m log q from attention-localized boundaries) "is unsound here," that boundary estimates "collapse onto near-duplicate positions," and that the resulting distances are "dominated by localisation error rather than by state dynamics." The authors explicitly say they replaced this estimator with the effective-rank/capacity measure "because it needs no boundaries" — i.e., the very quantity the abstract advertises as measured is, by the authors' own account, not validly measurable with the method described for it.
   **Where** — Abstract ("boundary-state distances decay geometrically with measured ˆq < 1..."); §3 "State measurement" (the fitting procedure); §4.4 "A note on what we could not measure"; Table 1 caption (defines q̂ as "the median per-repetition contraction factor... measured separately on repeated and control items" but no q̂ column of numbers appears in the table as given).
   **Why it matters** — Assumption 2 (contraction, q<1) is the load-bearing empirical premise of Theorem 1. If the paper cannot in fact report a sound, per-model q̂, then Theorem 1's premise is never verified for any of the tested systems, and the abstract's framing that this was "found" as part of "the predicted signature" is not supported by the evidence presented. This is precisely a case where the headline result does not follow from the data as reported.
   **What would address it** — Either (a) show a validated q̂ per model derived from a method the paper itself certifies as sound (and reconcile it with Table 1, which currently lacks the promised column), explicitly stating how localization error was controlled, or (b) rewrite the abstract/conclusion to not claim geometric decay of boundary-state distances as an empirical finding, and instead present the capacity-saturation result as the (weaker, indirect) stand-in evidence it actually is.

2. **Issue** — The claimed evaluation panel ("two checkpoints spanning two families and a 1B–8B scale ladder," Methods listing Llasa-1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B/1.7B — up to six systems) is never actually shown: every table and figure (Table 1, Table 2, Fig. 1, Fig. 2) reports data for exactly two models, Llasa-1B and XTTS-v2. The text even says so directly ("Figure 1(a) shows counting accuracy against k for all two checkpoints," §4.1; "disjoint for two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints," §4.3), which reads like an edit left over from a larger claimed panel that was never backfilled with results.
   **Where** — Abstract, §3 Models, §4.1, §4.3, Table 1, Table 2.
   **Why it matters** — The scale-independence claim in §4.1 ("the within-family Llasa ladder does not order k* monotonically with scale, so whatever governs the horizon is not capacity") requires k* for Llasa-3B and Llasa-8B, neither of which is reported anywhere. As written, this is a claim about a scale ladder made with zero data points for two of the three rungs of that ladder. Likewise, no Qwen3-TTS results appear despite the model being named in both Methods and Limitations.
   **What would address it** — Either report the full panel's k*, accuracy curves, q̂, and capacity-gain statistics for all named models, or rescope every claim (abstract, §4.1, §4.3, Discussion) to the two models actually analyzed and remove "scale ladder" / "panel" language that implies broader coverage.

3. **Issue** — 59.7% of items are excluded into an unanalyzed "audit bucket" due to disagreement between the Whisper transcript count and the duration-derived count, with no characterization of what that excluded majority looks like or how it would shift the headline numbers.
   **Where** — §3 "Behavioural measurement."
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own logic (§3, "the judge is itself an autoregressive model that collapses repeated material") implies that transcript/duration disagreement is exactly the outcome expected for loops, truncations, and overcounts — i.e., precisely the collapsed cases the theory is trying to explain. If audit-bucket membership correlates with collapse severity, discarding 60% of items is not a neutral quality-control step; it can systematically bias the reported accuracy contrast (58.3% at k≤3 vs. 10.6% at k≥6) in either direction, and the paper gives no sensitivity analysis, inter-method agreement statistic, or manual-audit summary to rule this out.
   **What would address it** — Report outcome distributions for the audited/quarantined items (even coarse, e.g., "N% look like additional loop/truncation cases on manual spot-check"), or a sensitivity bound showing headline accuracy under best-/worst-case allocation of the audit bucket.

4. **Issue** — The claim that "the horizon implied by q̂ tracks the observed k*" is stated as a headline finding but is necessarily built from at most two (model, k*) pairs, a fact the paper concedes in Limitations: "the panel is English-only and covers 2 checkpoints, so the k* regression rests on few points."
   **Where** — Abstract; §4.1; Discussion/Limitations ("k* regression rests on few points").
   **Why it matters** — A "regression" or "tracking" claim with n=2 has no ability to distinguish a genuine relationship from coincidence; any two points define a line. Presenting this as a tracked/predicted relationship in the abstract overstates what two data points can show, and the paper's own limitations paragraph effectively retracts the abstract's framing without the abstract being revised to match.
   **What would address it** — Either obtain enough model instances to fit a meaningful trend with visible scatter and a reported correlation/interval, or downgrade the abstract claim to "consistent with" rather than "tracks."

5. **Issue** — The measured attention-dilution rate (k^-0.21) and entropy growth slope (0.50) deviate substantially — roughly 2–5× — from what Lemma 1 predicts (k^-1 decay, slope-1 entropy growth in log k), and the paper treats this only as directional confirmation ("The direction is the same...") without discussing what the large residual implies for Assumption 1.
   **Where** — §4.5 "Attention dilutes as 1/k."
   **Why it matters** — Assumption 1 (periodic conditioning ⟹ approximately autonomous F) is explicitly grounded in Lemma 1's O(1/k) bound ("Assumption 1 is what Lemma 1 buys"). A measured exponent nearly 5× weaker than predicted (k^-0.21 vs. k^-1) means the O(1/k) residual term the theorem treats as small is, empirically, decaying much more slowly — the paper doesn't quantify whether this residual is still negligible relative to the contraction-driven decay the theorem needs, and doesn't propagate it through the bound despite acknowledging in Limitations that "the residual non-autonomy... we do not propagate through the theorem."
   **What would address it** — Either propagate the measured (not theoretical) dilution exponent through the horizon bound and show the qualitative conclusion survives, or explicitly bound how much the O(δ+1/k) gap could inflate N* and discuss whether that changes the paper's practical recommendations.

6. **Issue** — The submission's own reference list appears to break double-blind anonymity: reference [4], framed in §1 as "this work is the autoregressive-decoder companion to a spectral study... [4]," lists three named authors (Ivan Viakhirev, Kirill Borodin, Grach Mkrtchian) for what reads as the authors' own companion paper. Separately, key related-work citation [1] is annotated as a preprint "withdrawn 13 Feb 2026 by the authors pending institutional approval," and citation [10] contains an internal-repository note ("see literature/gaps.md for how our claim differs") that should not appear in a reviewable manuscript.
   **Where** — References [1], [4], [10].
   **Why it matters** — If [4] is by the same authors, naming them in a supposedly anonymous submission is a policy violation that program chairs, not reviewers, should adjudicate; it also means the paper leans on an unpublished, unreviewable companion work as its main frame for "what the attractor is" (§1, §5) — a form of related-work grounding the reader cannot independently check. Citing a withdrawn preprint [1] as part of the motivating narrative on stability hallucination is similarly a shaky evidentiary foundation for the introduction's framing.
   **What would address it** — Anonymize [4] per venue policy (or flag it for chair review), replace or clearly caveat reliance on the withdrawn preprint [1], and remove internal file references like "literature/gaps.md" from the camera-ready/reviewed text.

7. **Issue** — No artifact/code/data location is given. The Lean development's "no sorry / restricted axiom" audit is asserted ("The check is one command and is part of our release") but no repository, DOI, or supplementary link appears anywhere in the text, and the empirical pipeline (probe-layer selection, duration-calibration procedure per model/template, stimulus set) is described only at a summary level.
   **Where** — §2.3; §3 (Method); throughout.
   **Why it matters** — Both the formal claims (no-sorry, restricted axioms) and the empirical claims (q̂, Neff, k*) are, as presented, unverifiable by a reader — which is a real cost for a paper whose central rhetorical move is "we don't just claim this, we proved/measured it."
   **What would address it** — Add an explicit, resolvable link to the Lean development and analysis code/data (even anonymized), and specify "deep probe layers" and the duration-calibration procedure precisely enough to reproduce Table 1.

### Minor concerns

- Broken/placeholder citations left in the text: "well documented as a stability problem [1, ?]" (§1) and "transcribed with Whisper large-v3 [?]" (§3) — literal `?` marks indicate unresolved `\cite` commands.
- Table 1's caption defines a q̂ statistic that does not appear as a column in the table as transcribed; either the table is malformed or the statistic was dropped.
- Fig. 2 caption contains a leaked LaTeX escape: "95\% CI."
- "chance-adjusted 0.5" (used to define k* in Table 1's caption) is never defined — what is "chance" for a counting task with a continuous/ordinal outcome space?
- The stimulus set explicitly includes "Numbers," "Sentence repetition," and "tongue twisters" strata (§3 Method), but no results are broken out for these anywhere in §4; it's unclear whether they were analyzed at all.
- "Pooled over the panel" language (§4.1, §4.3) implies more than the two models for which data is actually shown — consistent with the Major #2 issue but worth a wording fix throughout.
- The phrase "for all two checkpoints" (§4.1) and "two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints" (§4.3) read as unedited leftovers and should be cleaned up regardless of whether the panel is expanded.
- XTTS-v2's built-in repetition penalty of 5.0 (noted in Limitations) means one of the only two fully-reported models is explicitly a non-neutral test of the theory; this caveat deserves to be surfaced earlier, e.g., next to Table 1, not only in Limitations.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the Lean formalization is a genuine and checkable contribution, but the empirical section's headline claim about q̂ is directly contradicted by the paper's own §4.4, the claimed multi-model scale ladder is not actually shown in any table or figure, and a near-60% unanalyzed data-exclusion bucket plus an n=2 "tracking" claim leave the central empirical argument unsupported as written; these are fixable with more complete reporting and a rescoped abstract, but as it stands the evidence does not yet establish what the abstract asserts.
