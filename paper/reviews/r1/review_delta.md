### Summary

The paper argues that repetition hallucination in autoregressive TTS is not a sampling artefact but a structural consequence of a contracting per-repetition state map: if the decoder's update between successive occurrences of a repeated phrase is (approximately) autonomous and Lipschitz-contracting, then no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* that is derived and machine-checked in Lean 4. Empirically, the authors report a repetition-ladder benchmark with length-matched non-repetitive controls, run on TTS checkpoints, and claim three converging signatures: a finite model-specific collapse point k*, a periodicity-not-length dissociation between repeated and control items, and a saturating effective-rank ("capacity gain") on repeated trajectories relative to controls. The headline claim is that the theoretically implied horizon tracks the empirically observed collapse point.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The multi-model, multi-scale panel described in the Methods is not the panel actually reported in the Results, and conclusions are drawn from data that appear never to have been shown.
   **Where** — Abstract ("two checkpoints spanning two families and a 1B–8B scale ladder"); §3 Models (lists Llasa-1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, and Qwen3-TTS-0.6B/1.7B — six models); §4.1 ("Figure 1(a) shows counting accuracy against k for all two checkpoints"); §4.3 ("two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints"); Limitations ("the panel...covers 2 checkpoints").
   **Why it matters** — Tables 1–2 and Figures 1–2 report exactly two models: Llasa-1B (1B parameters) and XTTS-v2 (0.4B parameters). Neither reaches "8B," so the abstract's "1B–8B scale ladder" claim is unsupported by any shown result. More seriously, §4.1 asserts "the within-family Llasa ladder does not order k* monotonically with scale" — a claim that requires at least the Llasa-3B and Llasa-8B data points, neither of which appears anywhere in the paper. This is a conclusion drawn from data not presented, which is a design/reporting violation independent of whether the conclusion happens to be true.
   **What would address it** — Either report k*, ˆq, accuracy curves and effective-rank statistics for all six described models (and for the full Llasa ladder specifically, to justify the "not monotonic" claim), or rewrite the abstract, §3, and §4.1 to state plainly that the analysis is a 2-model, 2-family study and drop claims that require more points than were collected.

2. **Issue** — The paper's own primary measurement protocol for ˆq is abandoned as unsound, but the headline claim built on it is never actually replaced with a demonstrated result.
   **Where** — §3 State measurement (describes boundary-localization + geometric fit as the method for obtaining ˆq); §4.4 ("That estimator is unsound here... The failure is not incidental"); Table 1 caption ("ˆq is the median per-repetition contraction factor...measured separately on repeated and control items"); Abstract ("the horizon implied by ˆq tracks the observed k*").
   **Why it matters** — Table 1's caption still promises a ˆq column, but the printed table has none — only k*, accuracy, and capacity-gain columns. No numeric ˆq value, and no numeric N* (the theorem's own quantity, Eq. 1), appears anywhere in the paper. The abstract's central empirical claim — that the theoretically-derived horizon tracks the observed collapse point — is therefore asserted but never instantiated with numbers. The substitute metric adopted in §4.3 (effective-rank "capacity gain") is not shown to be quantitatively related to q as defined in the theorem (a Lipschitz constant of F); the substitution is motivated only qualitatively.
   **What would address it** — Either report actual ˆq and N* values (via a corrected estimator) and a real comparison against k*, or explicitly retract the "ˆq tracks k*" claim from the abstract/intro and reframe the contribution as "a qualitatively consistent but unquantified capacity signature," which is a materially weaker claim than currently advertised.

3. **Issue** — 59.7% of behavioural items are placed in an "audit bucket" due to transcript/duration disagreement, and the paper never states how these are resolved before computing the headline accuracy numbers.
   **Where** — §3 Behavioural measurement.
   **Why it matters** — With a majority of the data excluded or otherwise handled by an undisclosed procedure, the reported accuracy figures (58.3% at k≤3, 10.6% at k≥6) cannot be interpreted without knowing what happened to most of the sample. Disagreement between the two counting channels is very plausibly correlated with the very collapse behaviours (loops, truncations) the paper is testing for — both channels are likely to fail together on exactly the degenerate outputs of interest — so non-random exclusion risks biasing the accuracy curve in the direction the theory predicts.
   **What would address it** — Report how the audit bucket is scored (manual adjudication, inter-rater agreement, or exclusion), report accuracy with and without the audit bucket, and show that the disagreement rate is not itself a function of k (which would confirm the confound).

4. **Issue** — The only two models carrying every cross-family claim differ in an uncontrolled decoding-time intervention.
   **Where** — Limitations ("XTTS-v2 ships a repetition penalty of 5.0 on acoustic tokens, which acts directly against the behaviour we measure and makes it a conservative rather than a neutral member of the panel").
   **Why it matters** — Given concern 1, the entire cross-family comparison (k* range 1–4, Table 1, Table 2) rests on exactly these two models. One has an explicit anti-repetition decoding mechanism and one does not, confounding "architecture/family" with "decoding-time mitigation." This directly undercuts the paper's claim that the collapse mechanism is decoding-rule agnostic (§2.2, §5), since the one piece of cross-model evidence available is itself contaminated by a decoding-rule difference.
   **What would address it** — Either disable XTTS-v2's repetition penalty for the collapse-measurement runs (with the standard-penalty condition reported separately as a robustness check), or add a genuinely uncontrolled-decoding model to the cross-family comparison.

5. **Issue** — Table 2 contradicts the paper's own stated directional claim for one of its two models.
   **Where** — §4.6 and Table 2.
   **Why it matters** — The text asserts "the direction is the same one that [the companion] study found... reduced effective rank and hardened spectra" for hallucinated vs. faithful generations. But Table 2 shows Neff dropping from 194.7 (faithful) to 164.0 (hallucinated) for Llasa-1B — consistent with the claim — while for XTTS-v2, Neff *rises* from 49.2 (faithful) to 78.6 (hallucinated), the opposite direction. This is a direct, checkable contradiction between the paper's own reported numbers and its own stated result, not a matter of interpretation.
   **What would address it** — Either correct the claimed direction to acknowledge the model-dependent (non-uniform) pattern, or explain the XTTS-v2 anomaly (e.g., attribute it to the repetition-penalty confound in concern 4) rather than presenting Table 2 as uniformly confirmatory.

6. **Issue** — The capacity-gain / effective-rank proxy (§4.3) is a global, aggregate measure of the whole hidden-state trajectory, not a targeted measure of whether count-specific information survives, and the closest available prior work suggests the opposite of what the paper assumes.
   **Where** — §4.3; absent discussion of alternative explanations.
   **Why it matters** — A model that correctly repeats a phrase produces near-duplicate acoustic content by design, so a shrinking effective rank on repeated trajectories could simply reflect legitimate output redundancy rather than destruction of the low-dimensional count signal the theorem is about — a scalar or log-k-bit count could persist in a small subspace invisible to a whole-state participation-ratio statistic. [2605.09239] reports exactly this dissociation for token counting in LLMs: linear probes on the residual stream decode the count near-perfectly at every layer, including at the layers where the output is already wrong, meaning the failure is at readout/decoding rather than at representation — which is precisely the possibility Theorem 1(iv) is designed to rule out ("no Lipschitz readout... can recover the count"), and precisely the possibility Neff cannot test. This paper does not attempt a linear-probe test for the count itself, so its central causal claim ("the decoder runs out of internal states in which a mistake and a correct continuation could differ," §4.3) is not directly demonstrated, and prior evidence in a closely related setting cuts the other way.
   **What would address it** — Train/evaluate a linear (or otherwise Lipschitz) count-probe on the hidden states directly, at the boundary steps, and show it also loses the ability to separate counts past k*; this would be a far more direct test of Theorem 1(iv) than the aggregate Neff proxy, and would let the paper engage with [2605.09239] rather than leaving the readout-side of its own theorem untested.

7. **Issue** — Cross-model and cross-condition claims are supported by n=2 checkpoints with no reported test statistics.
   **Where** — §4.1 (k* range "1 to 4"), §4.3 (matched-pair "positive gap in 75% of pairs" — pair count not given), Abstract ("the horizon...tracks the observed k*").
   **Why it matters** — With two instrumented checkpoints, "tracks" is not a quantified relationship (no correlation coefficient, no CI, no test), and a 75%-of-pairs statistic without a reported n or a test against the 50% null cannot be assessed for significance. This is stated as a load-bearing empirical result but has no accompanying inferential statistics.
   **What would address it** — Report the pair count and a binomial/permutation test for the matched-pair result; if the k*-vs-horizon "tracking" claim is to survive, it needs either more than two data points or an honest downgrade to "consistent with, but not statistically established by."

8. **Issue** — The nominally anonymous submission contains identifiability leaks.
   **Where** — Reference [4] ("Ivan Viakhirev, Kirill Borodin, and Grach Mkrtchian," cited as "the companion" work to this paper); reference [10]'s note ("see literature/gaps.md for how our claim differs," exposing an internal repository path).
   **Why it matters** — Naming the companion paper's authors directly and pointing reviewers to an internal, non-public file both strongly identify the submitting group, undermining double-blind review.
   **What would address it** — Anonymize the companion-paper citation (as is standard for self-citations under review) and remove or generalize the internal file reference.

9. **Issue** — No stated plan for releasing the stimulus set, generation configs, probe-layer selection, or (crucially, given concern 3) the audit-bucket adjudication procedure.
   **Where** — Absent; only the Lean formalization is explicitly said to be released ("The check is one command and is part of our release," §2.3).
   **Why it matters** — The formal proof's reproducibility is well handled, but the empirical pipeline — on which the paper's applied claims rest — has no stated data/code availability, and given how much of the accuracy result depends on an undocumented reconciliation step (concern 3), this is a significant reproducibility gap on top of the disclosure gap itself.
   **What would address it** — Commit to releasing the 180-item benchmark, generation seeds/configs, and the audit/reconciliation logic (or the raw Whisper/duration counts per item so readers can re-derive it) alongside the Lean artifact.

### Minor concerns

- Broken/placeholder citations in the compiled text: "[1, ?]" (§1) and "Whisper large-v3 [?]" (§3) indicate unresolved `\cite`/`\ref` commands.
- Reference [1] is noted as "Preprint withdrawn 13 Feb 2026 by the authors pending institutional approval," yet is cited to establish that the failure mode is "well documented" — a shaky anchor for the paper's motivating claim.
- Table 1's caption defines k* via "chance-adjusted 0.5" accuracy, but how chance accuracy is computed for a free-response counting task is not defined in §3.
- The "numbers" and "tongue twister" stimulus categories described in §3 are never separately analyzed or reported in §4; unclear whether they behave like word-repetition items or differently.
- Repeated odd phrasings ("all two checkpoints," "two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints") read like leftover template text from a larger originally-planned panel and should be cleaned up regardless of how concern 1 is resolved.
- Ligature/typography artifacts ("diﬀiculty," "eﬀiciency," "Neﬀ" rendering) suggest a PDF-extraction or LaTeX font issue; worth a proofreading pass.
- The gap between "approximately autonomous" (what Lemma 1 empirically supports, with an O(δ+1/k) error term) and "exactly autonomous" (what Assumption 1 and the Lean-verified Theorem 1 require) is acknowledged in Limitations but the error term is never propagated into a finite-sample version of the horizon bound, so the machine-checked guarantee does not, strictly, apply to any real measurement in the paper.

### Verdict

**Reject** — the paper's headline empirical claim (that the theoretically-derived horizon tracks observed collapse) is never actually computed or shown, the multi-model/multi-scale panel promised in the abstract and Methods is not the panel delivered in the Results (with conclusions drawn from unreported data), a majority of the behavioural data is quarantined by an undisclosed procedure, and the submission contains double-blind-breaking self-identification — the Lean formalization itself appears sound but cannot rescue an empirical section with this many unresolved design and reporting gaps.
