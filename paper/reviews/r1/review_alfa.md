## Summary

The paper claims that repetition hallucination in autoregressive TTS (looping, truncation, miscounting) is a structural consequence of contraction dynamics rather than a sampling artifact. Modelling one repetition cycle as a map F on the decoder's hidden state, the authors prove (formalised in Lean 4/mathlib) that if F is a q<1-contraction under periodic text conditioning, boundary states converge geometrically and no L-Lipschitz readout — including the model's stop head — can separate repetition counts past a horizon N* determined by q, an orbit-scale constant C, and the readout's decision margin. A companion lemma shows softmax attention over k identical spans dilutes as O(1/k). Empirically, on a 180-item repetition ladder (word/sentence repetition, number phrases, tongue twisters) scored via Whisper transcription reconciled against duration, the paper reports: (1) a finite, model-specific collapse point k*; (2) a periodicity-vs-length dissociation, with length-matched non-repetitive controls surviving where repeated items collapse; and (3) an effective-rank ("capacity gain") signature showing internal state diversity saturates under repetition but not under controls — offered as indirect support for the contraction premise, since the paper's own attempt to measure q directly from boundary-state distances is reported as methodologically unsound.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's scale- and family-generalization claims are asserted for a six-system panel, but every reported number comes from only two systems.
   **Where** — Abstract ("on two checkpoints spanning two families and a 1B–8B scale ladder"); §3 Models ("the Llasa family at 1B/3B/8B ... plus XTTS-v2 ... and Qwen3-TTS at 0.6B/1.7B"); §4.1 ("the within-family Llasa ladder does not order k∗ monotonically with scale, so whatever governs the horizon is not capacity"); Table 1 and Figs. 1–2 (rows/curves only for Llasa-1B and XTTS-v2); §5 Limitations ("The panel is English-only and covers 2 checkpoints, so the k∗ regression rests on few points").
   **Why it matters** — §4.1 draws a specific negative claim ("whatever governs the horizon is not capacity") from an alleged non-monotonic ordering across Llasa-1B/3B/8B, yet no 3B or 8B datapoint is shown anywhere. Qwen3-TTS, introduced explicitly "for cross-family coverage," never receives a k*, accuracy figure, or ˆq. A claim about what a variable does *not* explain is exactly the kind of claim that most needs the full evidence base, and here it is drawn from n=2 fully-reported systems while §5 itself admits the panel "covers 2 checkpoints" — directly contradicting the breadth implied by the abstract and §3.
   **What would address it** — Report k*, capacity gain, and the periodicity-vs-length contrast for all six named systems with the same statistical treatment as Llasa-1B/XTTS-v2, or rewrite the abstract/§4.1 to state plainly that only two systems were fully evaluated and drop the scale/family-generalization language that isn't backed by shown data.

2. **Issue** — The abstract's headline claim that "boundary-state distances decay geometrically with measured ˆq < 1" is contradicted by §4.4's own account of that measurement.
   **Where** — Abstract; §3 State measurement (defines the boundary-distance fit "log dm = a + m log q, giving ˆq"); §4.4 ("That estimator is unsound here and we report it as such ... boundary estimates collapse onto near-duplicate positions and the resulting distances are dominated by localisation error rather than by state dynamics"); Table 1 caption (describes a ˆq column that does not appear among the table's reported values).
   **Why it matters** — Assumption 2 (q<1) is the load-bearing empirical premise of Theorem 1. The abstract states it was measured and confirmed; §4.4 retracts the only described method for measuring it and substitutes a different statistic (effective-rank "capacity gain") that is not derived from, and does not numerically instantiate, q in equation (1). No ˆq value and no N* computed from a ˆq appear anywhere in the paper as given, so the abstract's third empirical pillar ("the horizon implied by ˆq tracks the observed k∗") has no supporting number, figure, or table.
   **What would address it** — Either show actual ˆq values and the N* computed from them alongside observed k* using a boundary-localization method the authors trust, or rewrite the abstract to state that q was not directly measurable and that the effective-rank result is offered as indirect corroboration, not a measurement of q.

3. **Issue** — A plausible alternative locus for the collapse — acoustic/codec-token repetition attractors, independent of text-side state contraction — is not ruled out.
   **Where** — §5 Limitations ("XTTS-v2 ships a repetition penalty of 5.0 on acoustic tokens, which acts directly against the behaviour we measure"); §3 Models (Llasa decodes over X-codec2 discrete audio tokens).
   **Why it matters** — That one of only two evaluated systems needs an explicit decoding-time penalty specifically on acoustic tokens to suppress this behaviour is evidence that a mechanism in the acoustic-token decoding loop — not the text-attention dilution the theorem is built on — is at least a contributing cause. Repeated words plausibly elicit repeated/near-duplicate codec frames, which is exactly the condition known to trigger loop attractors in codec-LM decoders independent of any text-conditioning contraction. The periodicity-vs-length contrast (§4.2) is consistent with either account, and no experiment separates them.
   **What would address it** — An intervention holding text periodicity fixed while manipulating acoustic-token repetition risk (or vice versa) would let the authors attribute collapse to the text-side mechanism specifically rather than to a confounded combination.

4. **Issue** — A closely analogous result in text-only autoregressive LMs is in tension with the theorem's operative claim that no Lipschitz readout can recover the count past N*, and is not addressed.
   **Where** — Theorem 1(iv); §1 and §5 interpretive claims ("past a computable horizon no Lipschitz function of the state—the stop-token logit included—can recover the count").
   **Why it matters** — [2605.09239] reports that in autoregressive LLMs performing repeated-token counting, linear probes on the residual stream decode the true count with near-perfect accuracy at every layer, including the exact layer where the wrong output crystallizes — i.e., the count is not erased, only unused by the model's output pathway. A linear probe is an L-Lipschitz readout, so this result is in direct tension with a claim that any Lipschitz readout's margin must collapse past N*. The present paper never tests whether a trained linear (Lipschitz, margin-quantified) probe can still recover the count from its own "collapsed" boundary states; it only reports an unsupervised dimensionality statistic (effective rank), which does not test count-decodability. Without that test, "information genuinely erased" (the paper's account) and "information present but unused by the stop head" (the competing account) are observationally indistinguishable from what is shown.
   **What would address it** — Train and report the accuracy/margin of a linear probe predicting count directly from the boundary states used for the effective-rank analysis, across k, and check whether its achievable margin shrinks with k the way equation (1) predicts.

5. **Issue** — 59.7% of stimulus items are excluded from behavioural scoring with no breakdown of how the exclusion rate varies with k or model, and no sensitivity analysis.
   **Where** — §3 Behavioural measurement ("disagreements are quarantined into an audit bucket (59.7% of items)").
   **Why it matters** — Disagreement between transcript-based and duration-based counts is most likely exactly where the paper's central claim lives (high-k looping/truncation, where transcript length and audio duration diverge most). If quarantining disproportionately removes heavily-collapsed high-k items, the reported accuracy floor and the 44.4%/43.9% overcount/undercount split could be biased in either direction, and the reader cannot tell which.
   **What would address it** — Report quarantine rate by (model × k) and report accuracy under at least one alternative treatment of quarantined items (e.g., scored as failures) to bound how much the headline percentages could move.

6. **Issue** — The "not capacity" conclusion rests on a two-model comparison in which one model is admitted, in the same paper, to be non-neutral on that exact axis.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("It is not a simple function of parameter count... whatever governs the horizon is not capacity"); §5 Limitations (XTTS-v2's repetition penalty of 5.0 "acts directly against the behaviour we measure").
   **Why it matters** — k* = 1 (Llasa-1B) vs. k* = 4 (XTTS-v2) is used to argue capacity doesn't govern the horizon, but XTTS-v2's higher k* is confounded by a decoding-time repetition penalty targeting the exact behaviour under study. With the confound acknowledged but not controlled for, the comparison cannot support the stated conclusion.
   **What would address it** — Drop the "not capacity" claim, or rerun the comparison with the repetition penalty disabled/matched across models to isolate the theorized mechanism from this known confound.

## Minor concerns

- Unresolved citation markers: "[1, ?]" (§1) and "Whisper large-v3 [?]" (§3) indicate broken `\cite`/`\ref` targets.
- Reference [10]'s annotation ("see literature/gaps.md for how our claim differs") reads as an internal repository note left in the bibliography rather than reader-facing text, and risks an anonymity leak.
- Reference [1] is annotated as withdrawn ("Preprint withdrawn 13 Feb 2026 by the authors pending institutional approval") yet is used to support "well documented as a stability problem" — a weak anchor for that claim.
- "chance-adjusted 0.5" (Table 1 caption) is never defined in the Methods.
- §4.2: "Models that cannot say very six times in a row will say twelve or sixteen different adverbs without difficulty" appears to be missing quotation marks around the repeated word "very"; as typeset it reads as a grammatical error.
- Awkward phrasing that inadvertently signals small n: "for all two checkpoints" (§4.1), "disjoint for two of the 2 instrumented checkpoints" (§4.3).
- No stated release plan for stimuli, transcripts, probing code, or model configs — only the Lean check is described as "part of our release."
- Percentages (58.3%, 10.6%, 44.4%, etc.) are given to one decimal place without underlying item counts, making precision hard to assess given the stated sample sizes.

## Verdict

**Reject** — the abstract's central empirical claim (a measured ˆq<1 whose horizon tracks k*) is contradicted by the paper's own §4.4 admission that the boundary-based q estimator is unusable, and the generalization claims (scale ladder, cross-family robustness) are asserted for four of six named models that never appear in any reported result.
