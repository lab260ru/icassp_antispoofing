### Summary
The paper asks whether neural TTS models' tendency to miscount repeated words ("stops early, runs on, or locks into a loop") is driven by textual *repetition* per se rather than by input *length*. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" (target word repeated k times vs. a length- and word-count-matched control with distinct fillers) run across six checkpoints from three architectures (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a held-out fourth architecture (CosyVoice 2), the authors report a large repeated-vs-control gap (46.8 points overall, 75.4 at k≥6) that survives decoding changes, penalty sweeps, exclusion-rule ablations, and rescoring with four different speech recognizers. A companion period-ladder experiment shows the deficit scales gradually with periodicity rather than switching on only for exact verbatim repeats. The paper further formalizes and then empirically refutes its own proposed mechanism — a Lean-4-verified "contraction" bound — by estimating the relevant Jacobian singular value (q = 21.0–347.7, never < 1) across five checkpoints, concluding the failure is real, reproducible, and currently mechanistically unexplained.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central "robustness" claim rests on hundreds of correlated specifications with no correction and no formal null model, while the framing implies this is equivalent to statistical validation.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "Across 420 specifications over six analysis axes … the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points, median 70.5, and none reverses its sign … we correct across none: the curve is a robustness envelope over one comparison, not a family of hypotheses."
   **Why it matters** — Declaring 420 specifications "not independent tests" is an assertion, not a demonstration; the paper never reports the correlation structure of the specification curve (e.g., how many of those axes are actually free to vary versus nested/redundant), so a reader cannot tell whether "median 70.5, none reverses sign" reflects genuine robustness or a curve dominated by a handful of independent choices multiplied out combinatorially. Sign-consistency is also a very low bar — a specification curve can be entirely non-null-compatible (per a formal permutation test) while individual cells still show huge variance, and "none reverses sign" says nothing about whether the effect could plausibly arise from chance under the paper's own design. The established methodology for this exact situation — specification-curve / multiverse analysis with a permutation-based joint significance test — is not cited or applied. Recent methodological literature is directly critical of exactly this move: a 2026 analysis of published multiverse analyses argues the approach is frequently used to "manufacture doubt" or "abdicate responsibility" for a single defensible estimate rather than to actually quantify robustness [2607.14623], and a companion methods paper proposes explicit meta-analytic synthesis across multiverse specifications precisely because informal "none reversed sign" summaries are not a substitute for one [2511.17064]. Neither is engaged with here.
   **What would address it** — Report the specification-curve permutation test (shuffle condition labels within the same pipeline and re-run the full curve to get a null distribution of "fraction of specifications with sign X"), or explicitly justify — with the correlation structure shown — why that step is unnecessary. At minimum, state how many of the 420 specifications are mutually independent draws versus deterministic recombinations of a much smaller number of underlying choices.

2. **Issue** — The instrument-validation and several downstream ablations rely on very small audit samples treated as decisive.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth… 0.19 of the true count, n=12 over 6 donors"); Section 3.2 (F5-TTS duration ablation, n=60; CosyVoice 2, n=90; VITS, n=90); Section 5 (rank-1 patch experiment, n=36).
   **Why it matters** — The entire methodological pivot away from Whisper (arguably the paper's most consequential design decision, since it determines which of the two candidate instruments the rest of the paper trusts) is justified by n=12. No confidence interval, per-donor breakdown, or variance estimate is given for that ratio; a single atypical donor or utterance could substantially shift 0.19. Later, precise point claims ("43.3% to 76.7%… equivalence bound 8.3 points" for F5-TTS) are built on n=60, again without a stated CI in the main text.
   **What would address it** — Report a bootstrap or exact binomial CI on the n=12 audit ratio and on the small-n ablations; if the true uncertainty is wide, say so explicitly rather than quoting a single decimal (0.19) as though it were precisely estimated.

3. **Issue** — The mixed-effects/partial-pooling extrapolation to an out-of-panel architecture is presented as a validated prediction despite the authors' own admission that the variance component is nearly unidentifiable.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "three groups barely identify a group-level scale and under the widest prior on it that gap touches zero (−1.6), so we did not rest on the extrapolation: we tested it… CosyVoice 2 … a gap of 64.4 points, inside the predicted interval and nearer its 65.3 mean than an interval that wide entitles us to expect."
   **Why it matters** — A random-effects variance estimated from three architecture groups is a textbook case of an unidentifiable hierarchical parameter; the authors' own sensitivity analysis shows the "prediction" is compatible with a null effect under a plausible prior. A single subsequently-observed data point (one architecture, n=90) cannot statistically "validate" a prediction interval that wide — it is one data point falling inside a wide interval, which has high prior probability regardless of whether the underlying model is correct. Framing this as the interval landing "nearer its mean than … entitles us to expect" over-interprets what is, at best, weak corroborating evidence, and risks reading as post-hoc narrative fitting since CosyVoice 2's data collection date is stated to precede the fit (S16) but the interpretive framing ("entitles us to expect") is doing more rhetorical than statistical work.
   **What would address it** — Present the CosyVoice 2 result as descriptive corroboration, not confirmation of the partial-pooling model; report the posterior predictive check formally (e.g., a Bayesian p-value for the observed gap under the fitted prior-predictive distribution) rather than a qualitative "nearer the mean than expected."

4. **Issue** — Several load-bearing numbers depend on data-dependent choices (the k≥6 threshold, "the strictest of five rules," "we quote the smaller" between cycled/never-cycled control estimates) whose selection process is disclosed but not quantified.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge" and "surviving fraction of 0.547 on the strictest of five rules and 0.69–0.86 on the others."
   **Why it matters** — Disclosing that a threshold was chosen post hoc is good practice, but it does not by itself neutralize the statistical consequence: the headline 75.4-point figure is, by construction, close to a local maximum of gap-vs-threshold. Similarly, quoting "the strictest of five rules" for the p=2 surviving fraction, and "the smaller" of two family/checkpoint aggregations for the never-cycled control comparison, are selection rules that systematically bias reported numbers toward the more dramatic (or more conservative-looking) end, even when well-intentioned. Without showing the full curve of gap vs. threshold (or the full spread across all five rules, not just the "strictest"), a reader cannot judge how sensitive the headline numbers are to this choice.
   **What would address it** — Plot gap magnitude as a continuous function of the threshold k in the main text (not just cite S16/S21), and report the full range across all rules/aggregations actually computed rather than selecting the "strictest" or "smaller" value for the reported headline.

5. **Issue** — The clustering/resampling unit used to construct the reported intervals is never stated in the main text, and the three-seeds-per-item design creates a repeated-measures structure that, if ignored by the bootstrap, would produce anti-conservative intervals.
   **Where** — Throughout Section 3.1 (e.g., "all six interval constructions (S21) exclude zero," "carrier bootstrap P=0.010").
   **Why it matters** — Each item is rendered "at three seeds"; if the ~1558 retained generations are resampled at the generation level rather than the item or checkpoint level, the effective sample size used for interval construction is inflated relative to the true number of independent stimuli, narrowing the reported intervals and making "excludes zero" claims easier to satisfy than they should be. The single explicitly named bootstrap ("carrier bootstrap") suggests the authors are aware of this, but it is only invoked for one specific number (P=0.010 on the shuffle-ordering result), leaving the clustering basis for the other "six interval constructions" unstated in the paper proper.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly, in the main text, the resampling/clustering unit for every reported interval, and confirm all use item- or checkpoint-level (not generation-level) resampling given the repeated-seed design.

6. **Issue** — The paper's numeric claims are currently unverifiable: the code, stimuli, Lean development, and per-item tables are gated behind "public on acceptance."
   **Where** — Section 2, "Code, data, settings."
   **Why it matters** — Given that the paper's rhetorical structure depends heavily on very specific decimal claims (q = 21.0–347.7; gap = 76.4 with six named interval constructions; 420 specifications; a sorry-free Lean 4 proof on mathlib), and that its own stated philosophy is "our claims rest on effect sizes," a reviewer currently has no way to check that build.sh reproduces these numbers from the pinned revisions, or that the Lean proof is in fact sorry-free as claimed. This is a materially higher reproducibility bar to ask readers to trust, precisely because the paper's rhetorical strategy is "trust the numbers, not a p-value."
   **What would address it** — Provide reviewers a private/anonymized link to the repository (many venues explicitly support this) rather than deferring all verification to post-acceptance.

### Minor concerns
- "V ALL-E 2" is rendered with an internal space throughout (abstract, Section 3.2, references) — likely a PDF/ligature extraction artifact, but should be checked in the camera-ready.
- Two of the paper's more load-bearing citations — the companion paper [8] (arXiv:2604.08591, 2026) and the dissociation result attributed to Venkatesh [15] (arXiv:2605.09239, 2026) — are very recent (same-year) preprints that could not be independently located via the arXiv search run for this review; their claims (e.g., "the count … stays decodable late… as Venkatesh found in text models") should be checked at copyediting for correct attribution.
- Section 4 references "an earlier draft" ("so we withdraw the architectural claim an earlier draft made") — this kind of meta-commentary about the paper's own revision history reads oddly in a submitted manuscript and should probably be reworded to state the (withdrawn) claim and its correction without referring to draft history.
- The relative count error statistic (ĉ−k)/k is reported as a single median per cell without an accompanying dispersion measure in the main text (Table 1 gives only medians); an IQR or comparable spread per cell would help readers judge homogeneity within checkpoints.
- Figure 1's caption states period-ladder results run "on the four checkpoints the ladder ran on," but the main text elsewhere reports results as being "monotone in all four checkpoints" without stating in the main text (only implicitly via the figure) which four of the six checkpoints these are — worth naming explicitly for clarity.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the empirical phenomenon and the paper's self-directed skepticism (including refuting its own proposed mechanism) are genuinely valuable, but the statistical apparatus underpinning the "robustness" claims (uncorrected multi-hundred-specification curves, unidentifiable hierarchical variance components treated as validated, small-n instrument audits, and unstated bootstrap clustering) needs to be tightened and made verifiable before the headline numbers can be taken at face value.
