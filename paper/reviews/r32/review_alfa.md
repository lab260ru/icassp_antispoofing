### Summary
The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-documented tendency to lose count on repeated phrases is an artifact of input length (attention span, exposure bias, duration budget) or a distinct effect of repetition/periodicity per se. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" with length-matched controls (repeated word vs. distinct fillers at the same carrier and word count) across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families, plus a separate period ladder and various ablations (penalty sweeps, greedy decoding, exclusion-rule removal, alternate recognisers), the authors report that repeated text is rendered exactly far less often than matched controls (18.2% vs 94.3% at k≥6), that the deficit is graded with period rather than a step function, and that a proposed mechanistic explanation — a Lean-4-verified contraction bound on per-repetition hidden-state dynamics — is empirically false in every decoder measured (q ≫ 1 throughout). They conclude the count survives in hidden state but the readout policy fails to use it, leaving the actual mechanism unresolved.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's headline effect size (75.4-point gap) rests on a threshold ($k\ge 6$) that the authors admit was chosen after inspecting the data, and this post-hoc figure, not the pre-specified whole-ladder figure, dominates the in-text narrative (Table 1, §3.1 prose, most of §3.2's contrasts).
   **Where** — §3.1, final paragraph ("The $k \ge 6$ threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge"); Table 1 caption ("k ≥ 6 under the CTC judge"); abstract clause pairing 46.8 (whole ladder) with 75.4 (post-hoc subset).
   **Why it matters** — A number selected because it maximizes the observed gap, then used as the primary descriptive statistic for most of the paper's subsequent claims (the mixed-effects estimate, the CosyVoice-2 "held-out" comparison, the recogniser-robustness range), inflates the apparent severity of the phenomenon relative to what a pre-specified analysis would show. The authors' own honesty about this ("a threshold set after seeing the data") is commendable, but disclosure in one clause does not neutralize its downstream use as the paper's working number throughout §3.2 and Table 1.
   **What would address it** — Lead consistently with the unconditioned, non-cherry-picked ladder-wide statistic in every table/figure that currently uses the $k\ge6$ number, and clearly flag every subsequent reuse of 75.4/18.2%/94.3% as post-hoc, or better, replicate the $k\ge6$ threshold choice on a held-out set of checkpoints/templates never used to pick it.

2. **Issue** — The title and abstract claim to "isolate" the counting failure, but the paper's own architecture-generality test (AR vs. non-AR) explicitly fails to isolate anything, with contradictory results from a sample of one model per strategy.
   **Where** — Title; abstract ("of two non-autoregressive baselines one is immune, one is not, though at one model per strategy that is suggestive rather than isolating"); §3.2 (VITS shows no dissociation, F5-TTS does, with a post-hoc "what plausibly separates the two" account that is not itself tested).
   **Why it matters** — "Isolating" is true for the repetition-vs-length axis (well-supported by the matched-control design) but false for the AR-vs-NAR axis, which the paper investigates with equal prominence in §3.2's structure. Readers taking the title at face value would over-generalize a claim the authors themselves call merely "suggestive." The post-hoc account of why F5-TTS fails and VITS doesn't ("where the count must live" — per-token vs. total duration) is offered as an explanation but not tested against a third NAR system with intermediate duration modeling, so it remains speculative.
   **What would address it** — Either scope the title/abstract to the axis actually isolated (repetition vs. length) or add at least one more model per NAR duration-modeling strategy before asserting even a "plausible" separator.

3. **Issue** — Several load-bearing calibration and artifact-exclusion claims rest on very small samples, yet are used to license strong conclusions (choice of judge, withdrawal of an architectural claim, "ruling out" the judge artifact).
   **Where** — Whisper ground-truth audit, n=12 over 6 donors; CTC audit, n=24; rank-1 causal-patch experiment, n=36; F5-TTS duration-supply ablation, n=60 with an "equivalence bound of 8.3 points"; VITS/CosyVoice-2 comparisons, n=90.
   **Why it matters** — The Whisper-vs-CTC calibration ratio (0.19 vs 1.00) is the empirical basis for switching judges for the entire rest of the study; with n=12–24, this ratio could be noisy rather than a stable property of the two recognisers. Similarly, the rank-1 patch result ("it is registered but carries nothing") that anchors the discussion's "representations survive, readout fails" narrative rests on n=36. None of these in-text numbers carries a reported confidence interval (unlike the mixed-effects/pooling estimates in §3.1, which do).
   **What would address it** — Report per-item variance or bootstrap CIs for each of these audits in the main text (not deferred to supplement only), and scale the smallest audits (n=12) to match the size used elsewhere in the panel.

4. **Issue** — The abstract states percentages (18.2% vs 94.3%) as if they characterize the ladder generally, but they are in fact conditioned on the same post-hoc $k\ge6$ subset as concern #1, and this conditioning is not restated at the point the numbers are given.
   **Where** — Abstract, second sentence: "It favours the control in all 6 checkpoints, where models render repeated text exactly in 18.2% of generations against 94.3%."
   **Why it matters** — §3.1 states models are "essentially exact" below $k=6$, so a reader who takes the abstract's 18.2%/94.3% as ladder-wide (rather than $k\ge6$-only, confirmed by matching against Table 1's identical figures) would substantially overestimate how often the deficit actually bites across the full range of $k$ tested (1 through 32).
   **What would address it** — Explicitly tag every summary statistic in the abstract with its conditioning subset, and give the ladder-wide (unconditioned) exact-match rate alongside the $k\ge6$ figure for direct comparison.

5. **Issue** — The paper's own strongest threat to its causal story — that repeated-word sentences may simply be rarer in training data, independent of periodicity — is acknowledged but only partially addressed.
   **Where** — §5: "Repetition is not separated from training-set frequency, though the obvious form of it predicts the wrong arm: under an independent LM the control is the less probable text in 87% of pairs... Text likelihood is not corpus frequency: weakened, not closed."
   **Why it matters** — If the actual driver were training-corpus repeat-frequency (a plausible confound the authors name but cannot rule out), the "repetition, not length" framing would still be broadly correct in spirit but the mechanistic story ("periodicity taxes the decoder") would be displaced by a distributional-frequency story, which has different implications for how tractable a fix would be (more data vs. an architectural limit). The independent-LM check only rules out one operationalization (sentence-level likelihood), not the more directly relevant one (sub-string/n-gram repeat frequency in training corpora).
   **What would address it** — Estimate n-gram or exact-repeat frequency for the ladder's target words/templates in a public proxy corpus (e.g., a large web-text corpus reasonably representative of TTS training text) rather than relying solely on an independent LM's sentence-level probability.

6. **Issue** — After empirically refuting the paper's own proposed mechanism (the Lean-4-verified contraction bound: $q$ ranges 21.0–347.7, never below 1, expansive on both repeated and control text, with a dose-response pointing the *wrong* direction), the paper still foregrounds "formal verification" as a headline contribution and index term.
   **Where** — Abstract ("A Lean-4-verified bound says... We measure that premise... and reject it... it never described any decoder we measured"); §4 conclusion ("We keep the bound because it motivated the manipulation... but it explains nothing in Section 3"); Index Terms line.
   **Why it matters** — The mechanistic content actually delivered is a negative result (a hypothesis ruled out), which is legitimate and useful, but keeping the Lean-4 machinery as a marquee element risks the reader inferring the paper explains the counting failure mechanistically when it explicitly does not. This is the clearest instance in the paper of framing outrunning what the evidence supports.
   **What would address it** — Reframe the formal-verification component explicitly as a falsified hypothesis test in the abstract's contribution list (rather than listing it alongside the positive findings with parallel grammatical weight), and consider whether "formal verification" belongs among the index terms given the model it verifies is shown not to apply to any measured decoder.

### Minor concerns
- The 420-specification robustness curve (§3.1) explicitly declines any multiple-comparison correction ("we correct across none"); the reasoning given (robustness envelope, not a family of hypotheses) is plausible but stated tersely and would benefit from one added sentence distinguishing "establishing existence" from "characterizing magnitude" claims.
- Almost every quantitative claim in the main text cites a supplement section (S1–S29) not included in the reviewed manuscript; as submitted, a large fraction of the evidentiary chain is unverifiable from the text alone.
- Figure 1(b) caption reports n=1539 with "no interval," inconsistent with the interval-reporting standard used elsewhere in the paper (e.g., the six interval constructions in §3.1).
- Typographic/formatting: "V ALL-E 2" is inconsistently spaced throughout (likely an extraction artifact); "R2" appears where "R²" is presumably intended.
- The introduction's illustrative example ("six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six") is stylistically colorful but adds no evidentiary content; consider a more neutral example or move it to a footnote.
- Self-citation [8] ("companion to a spectral study of hallucination... [8]") is used to justify scope decisions but the relationship between the two papers' claims is not explained beyond "companion."

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the controlled length-vs-repetition design and the honest refutation of the authors' own mechanistic hypothesis are real strengths, but the headline numbers are post-hoc-thresholded and presented with more generality than the paper's own hedges (architecture axis "suggestive, not isolating"; frequency confound "weakened, not closed") support, and several load-bearing side claims rest on very small, uncertainty-free samples.
