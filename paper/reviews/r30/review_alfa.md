STANCE: Adversarial (assume the headline result is false; what would falsify it?)

### Summary
The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-documented tendency to lose count on repeated phrases is a repetition-specific failure or simply a symptom of longer/harder inputs. Using a parametric "repetition ladder" (a target word repeated k times vs. a length- and word-count-matched control in which the repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers), the authors measure count accuracy across six checkpoints spanning three TTS architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a held-out fourth architecture (CosyVoice 2). They report a large, robust repeated-vs-control gap (46.8 points overall, 75.4 at k≥6) that survives penalty sweeps, greedy decoding, exclusion-rule ablations, and rescoring by four different recognisers (ruling out judge artifacts), and that a Lean-4-verified "contraction" mechanism they proposed to explain it is empirically false (measured contraction factor q is always ≫1). They conclude the deficit tracks repetition (partially isolable from mere periodicity) rather than length, but leave open whether the count is erased from internal state or merely unreadable by the decoding policy.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The control condition does not isolate "repetition" from "token distinctiveness/discriminability"; it isolates repetition-of-an-identical-token from a set of k *distinct* tokens, so the observed gap could equally be a discriminability confound rather than evidence about counting identical repeated events per se.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Method: Stimuli"), where the control is defined as "the repeated token replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight"; carried through into every headline result in Section 3.1.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's central claim ("the limit tracks repetition, not length") rests entirely on this one contrast. If what actually drives the failure is that k acoustically/lexically indistinguishable events are harder for the alignment/stop mechanism to individuate — as opposed to k occurrences of the *same* word specifically — then "repetition" is the wrong causal label, and the mechanistic story built on it (periodicity ladder, contraction bound, state-probing) is aimed at the wrong target. The one attempt to rule this out (an independent LM finding the control text less probable in 87% of pairs, S8) only tests a *frequency* account, not a *discriminability* account, and the authors themselves concede this is "weakened, not closed."
   **What would address it** — A control that keeps the token identical across repetitions but varies something orthogonal to identity (e.g., inserted prosodic/positional markup, or distinct-but-confusable near-homophone fillers) versus a control with maximally distinctive fillers, to separate "same token" from "individually discriminable token" as the operative variable.

2. **Issue** — The "six checkpoints, three architectures, all positive" robustness claim substantially overstates independent replication, since within-family scale variants (Llasa 1B/3B/8B; Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) share backbone, tokenizer, and training pipeline and are not independent draws from "architecture space."
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("by family the gap is 75.4... by checkpoint 76.7... positive in all 6"), and the partial-pooling extrapolation to CosyVoice 2.
   **Why it matters** — The paper itself flags this ("we model the clustering rather than count checkpoints") and then still leans on "positive in all 6" phrasing throughout the abstract and results as a generalization signal. With only three genuinely independent families, the posterior predictive interval for an out-of-panel architecture is reported as touching zero under the widest prior (−1.6) — i.e., the authors' own analysis shows the extrapolation is only barely non-null before they add the CosyVoice 2 test. The subsequent retraction of an "architectural claim an earlier draft made" once F5-TTS was tested is itself evidence that generalizations from n≈3 families were premature.
   **What would address it** — Report and foreground the family-level (n=3) statistics as the primary robustness claim, not the checkpoint-level (n=6) statistics, and be explicit in the abstract/discussion that architectural generalization rests on n=3 independently trained systems plus two out-of-panel spot checks.

3. **Issue** — The claim that VITS shows "no dissociation" (and thus rules out a judge-side CTC blank-collapse artifact) is confounded by a floor/ceiling effect: VITS's absolute exact-match rate is low on *both* arms (65.6% repeated vs. 58.9% control), unlike every other checkpoint's control arm, which sits at 87–100%.
   **Where** — Section 3.2 ("VITS shows no dissociation through the same recogniser on the same strings... a gap of -6.7").
   **Why it matters** — A near-null gap when both arms are already unreliable is not equivalent evidence to a near-null gap when the control arm is near-ceiling; a system that fails ~35–40% of the time on *unrepeated* controls may simply lack the precision to express a repetition-specific deficit at all, rather than being architecturally immune to it. This weakens VITS's role as the paper's key counter-example against the blank-collapse confound.
   **What would address it** — Report VITS's overall error profile (e.g., WER on control items) alongside the exact-match numbers, and ideally test a second non-autoregressive/duration-explicit system with a higher control-arm ceiling to replicate the "no dissociation" pattern under conditions where a real gap could actually be detected.

4. **Issue** — The measured contraction constant q, offered as the decisive refutation of the paper's own mechanistic hypothesis, is computed under teacher forcing, which is off-policy relative to the free-running autoregressive generation in which the failure is actually observed.
   **Where** — Section 4 ("Measuring q... teacher-forcing the generated tokens so the map is well defined").
   **Why it matters** — The paper is careful to flag this ("Teacher-forcing removes the token channel, so these are lower bounds") but then states the refutation ("no map is a contraction... q < 1 in no item") with the same confidence as the rest of the paper's causal claims. A mechanism about failures compounding during autonomous generation cannot be conclusively falsified by a quantity measured with the generation process's own error-correcting signal (ground-truth tokens) fed back in at every step; this measurement gap goes in the direction of making contraction look *less* likely than it would under free-running decoding, so the "reject" verdict is directionally biased by the method used to test it, not just conservatively bounded.
   **What would address it** — Report q under free-running (non-teacher-forced) decoding as well, even as a noisier estimate, and present both figures with explicit discussion of which regime each supports conclusions about.

5. **Issue** — The paper's central open causal question (is the count erased from state, or present but unreadable by the policy?) is addressed with a single, underpowered, blunt intervention that the authors themselves say could not be made informative.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("The rival we could not decide"): rank-1 patch transplant, n=36, tested on a handful of checkpoints, "the write matches a sampler re-roll on all 6 readouts... registered but carries nothing."
   **Why it matters** — This is presented as a load-bearing part of the Discussion's narrative arc (contraction refuted → is it erasure or misreading? → inconclusive but suggestive) but a rank-1 patch at n=36 is a very low-power test of a causal claim about a high-dimensional decoder policy; "6–20% of a full transfer" and a null result matching a random re-roll baseline is consistent with the intervention simply being too weak to move the readout at all, not with the count being causally inert. The paper's own honesty about this ("we could not build one") is appropriate, but the Discussion section still uses the result to argue "it is registered but carries nothing," which asserts more than a null result at this power can support.
   **What would address it** — Either scale the intervention (higher-rank patches, multi-layer patches, activation steering across the full duration span) before drawing any directional conclusion, or reframe the paragraph as purely negative (inconclusive) rather than supporting a substantive claim about the readout.

6. **Issue** — No blinding protocol is described for the one human-verifiable check in the pipeline (the 165-clip stratified sample "so a judgement can be checked by ear"), despite the paper's entire Section 2 argument being that automated judges can be biased toward manufacturing the effect.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Code, data, settings"), absent elsewhere.
   **Why it matters** — If a human reviewer checking transcription/scoring decisions knows which clip is a repeated-condition item versus a control, or which checkpoint produced it, the same confirmation-bias risk the paper takes pains to rule out for Whisper applies to this manual QA step, and it is never addressed.
   **What would address it** — State whether the spot-check was performed blind to condition/model, and if not, report inter-rater or repeat-rater reliability under blinding for a subsample.

### Minor concerns
- The abstract and Results both quote many precision-heavy numbers (e.g., "76.4," "65.3 [5.9, 93.3]," "q = 21.0–347.7") without a single consolidated table of sample sizes per analysis; n ranges from 36 to 2052 across sub-claims with no stated power justification for the smaller ones.
- The period ladder (Fig. 1b) "ran on" only four of the six checkpoints (S16 presumably explains why, but the main text doesn't state the reason inline).
- "420 specifications... median 70.5, and none reverses its sign" is a strong robustness claim, but the specification-curve axes and their construction are deferred entirely to supplement (S11); the main text gives no sense of what "exclusions, k threshold... aggregation" concretely vary.
- The Spanish generalization claim ("Nor is the contrast an English artefact") is undercut two sentences later by "the Spanish arm needs a better judge (S27)" in the Discussion — this should be flagged as a limitation nearer the claim itself rather than only in the limitations paragraph.
- Self-citation [8] to a companion paper with an arXiv ID dated after several other 2025/2026 references is unusual to verify independently; authors should ensure the ID is stable before camera-ready.
- The Lean-4 formal verification is described as "free of sorry and with an axiom audit" but the axiom set itself is never summarized in the main text, only referenced as S1–S2 — for a claim this load-bearing to the paper's narrative, at least a one-line description of what axioms were used would help readers assess trust in the "machine-checked" framing.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the central empirical finding (repetition-specific deficit, robust across judges/decoding settings) is well-supported and the paper is unusually transparent about its own limitations, but the control's discriminability confound, the over-stated cross-architecture independence, the VITS floor-effect issue, and the underpowered causal-locus experiment each need to be addressed or explicitly bounded before the paper's causal language ("tracks repetition, not length") is fully earned.
