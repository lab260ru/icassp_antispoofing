### Summary
The paper asks why autoregressive TTS models miscount phrase repetitions in the input text, proves (and Lean-formalizes) a conditional theorem stating that if per-repetition decoder dynamics are approximately autonomous and contractive, no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* that shrinks with contraction strength, and reports a companion attention-dilution lemma showing softmax attention over k identical spans flattens as Θ(1/k). It then tests the qualitative signature of this theory across six TTS checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) on a 180-item repetition ladder, judged by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically to avoid an autoregressive judge's own repetition bias. The headline empirical results are: a one-directional undercount that tracks periodicity rather than length (median error −8.3% at k≥6 for repeated items vs 0.0% for length-matched controls; 18.2% vs 94.3% exact-match), state-space effective-rank growth at 0.46 of the control rate, proportional (non-saturating) error up to k=32, and — in a post-hoc extension to k=128 on 4 of 6 checkpoints — an eventual saturation of the repeated-item count at a fitted scale roughly 3.5× lower than for controls.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's own most careful statistical instinct (treat the checkpoint, not the individual generation, as the unit of replication to avoid pseudo-replication) is applied only to some analyses and abandoned for the headline numbers quoted in the abstract and §4.1/§4.3.
   **Where** — Abstract ("median count error of −8.3% at k ≥ 6... exact in 18.2%... against 94.3%"); §4.1 ("median error of -8.3% [−12.5, −6.2] (n = 457)"); §4.3 ("94.3% for controls against 18.2%..."); contrast with §4.2/§4.4, which explicitly say "we take the checkpoint as the unit of replication, reducing each to its own repeated-minus-control difference" precisely because "the claim is about models, not about our corpus."
   **Why it matters** — Table 1 shows the six checkpoints' count error ranges from −16.7% to 0.0%, and their capacity-gain ratios range from 0.21 to 0.80 — the between-checkpoint variance is large relative to the within-checkpoint spread. Pooling all generations as if independent (as in the n=457 CI) manufactures a much narrower interval than the data's actual clustering structure supports, because repeated draws from the same checkpoint/template/seed are correlated. This makes the abstract's crisp point estimate ("−8.3%") look far more precise than the checkpoint-level analysis (which itself only reaches p=0.031, the exact floor at n=6, for one metric and a non-significant p=0.062 for another) actually warrants.
   **What would address it** — Recompute all headline statistics (not just the ones in §4.2/§4.4) with checkpoint as the clustering unit — e.g., a mixed-effects model with checkpoint as a random effect, or cluster-robust confidence intervals — and report those, not the pooled item-level CI, in the abstract and §4.1/§4.3.

2. **Issue** — No correction for multiple comparisons is applied or even discussed, despite the paper reporting a large number of statistical tests, intervals, and correlations across the ladder, extension, state-measurement, and dilution analyses.
   **Where** — Throughout §4: RSS model-comparison (485.3 vs 2.6), two bootstrap CIs for K̂ (rep and ctl), two signed-rank tests (p=0.031, p=0.062), the capacity-gain test (p=0.031), the correlation r=0.59 across "23 cells," the perturbation-probe test (p=0.12), and the XTTS repetition-penalty ablation (S7) — none of these p-values or intervals are adjusted for the family of tests being run.
   **Why it matters** — With this many derived quantities, some fraction of "significant" or "suggestive" findings are expected under noise alone even if the true effect is smaller or absent for some checkpoints/metrics. The paper leans on the discreteness of the n=6 signed-rank test to explain away one non-significant result (p=0.062, "misses even its own floor") but doesn't apply the same scrutiny to the several p<0.05 results elsewhere, which risks selectively contextualizing only the results that don't support the theory.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly which comparisons are confirmatory vs. exploratory, and apply a correction (Holm/Bonferroni/FDR) across the confirmatory family, or at minimum report all p-values together in one table so a reader can judge the multiplicity.

3. **Issue** — Several analytic choices that materially shape the headline narrative appear to have been made after seeing the data, with no pre-registration or held-out replication to guard against a garden-of-forking-paths pattern.
   **Where** — The choice of the k≥6 threshold for "pooled" statistics (§4.1: "Below k=6 every model is essentially exact"); the decision to extend the ladder to k∈{48,64,96,128} "post hoc" after the original ladder showed proportional, non-saturating error (§4.2, explicitly labeled "post hoc"); the choice of the specific saturating functional form ĉ(k)=K̂(1−e^(−k/K̂)) fit only after the saturating pattern was observed.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably transparent about the post-hoc nature of the extension, and it pre-states falsification criteria in §5 ("a control with its twin's deficit; a model with the deficit whose capacity gain matches its control..."), which is good practice. But the k≥6 cutoff and the curve-fit functional form are not among the pre-stated falsifiable criteria, and both were plausibly chosen to fit the observed break point and observed saturation shape. This inflates the apparent strength of the "3.5-fold" and "proportional-then-saturating" findings relative to what an independent, pre-registered replication would show.
   **What would address it** — Report results for a small grid of alternative cutoffs (k≥4, k≥8) and alternative saturating functional forms (e.g., power-law, logistic) to show the qualitative conclusion is robust to these post-hoc choices, or explicitly flag them as exploratory and reserve confirmatory language for the pre-registered falsification tests only.

4. **Issue** — The headline "3.5-fold" saturation-scale reduction, prominently stated in the abstract without qualification, is derived from only 4 of the 6 checkpoints, and within those 4 the pattern is not consistent: disjoint bootstrap intervals for 2 (Llasa-1B, Llasa-8B), and a reversal for one (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B, which "shows no repeated-side saturation... and inverts the ratio").
   **Where** — Abstract ("a post-hoc extension to k = 128 on 4 checkpoints finds one, repetition lowering the fitted saturation scale 3.5-fold"); §4.2 ("K̂rep = 30 [24, 46] against K̂ctl = 105 [94, 114] pooled... The panel is not unanimous, and the exception is informative").
   **Why it matters** — A 3.5-fold headline number pooled over 4 heterogeneous checkpoints, one of which points the opposite direction, is not the same claim as "repetition lowers the counting horizon 3.5-fold" stated as a general finding in the abstract. Readers skimming the abstract will not learn that this number rests on a 4-checkpoint, non-unanimous, post-hoc analysis.
   **What would address it** — Qualify the abstract claim explicitly (sample size, non-unanimity), or report the per-checkpoint K̂ ratios individually rather than only the pooled estimate, so the reader can see the actual spread (30/105 pooled vs. whatever Qwen3-TTS-1.7B's own ratio is).
   **What would address it** — Restate the finding conditionally in the abstract (e.g., "in 3 of 4 extended checkpoints") and give the pooling method (fixed vs. random effects across checkpoints) used to combine the four K̂ estimates.

5. **Issue** — The central quantity that the formal theorem is actually about — the contraction factor q, and hence the theorem's own N* — is never measured, so the quantitative empirical work (curve-fitting K̂ with an unrelated descriptive functional form) does not actually test Theorem 1's quantitative content, only a qualitative "does the count eventually saturate" pattern.
   **Where** — §5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... The premise remains unestablished"); §4.2 ("K̂ is a saturation scale read off counts, not the theorem's N*, a function of the unmeasured q").
   **Why it matters** — This is a genuine and well-flagged limitation by the authors, but it means the paper's rigor (Lean-checked theorem with an explicit, falsifiable formula for N*) and its empirical rigor (curve-fitting a logistic-like saturation form to raw counts) are not actually connected by any measurement — the theorem predicts a mechanism-derived horizon, the data show a descriptively-fit one, and no evidence in the paper links the fitted K̂ to the theorem's Lipschitz constant, orbit scale, or margin. The "3.5-fold" number is therefore a fact about a curve fit, not a validated instance of N*.
   **What would address it** — Either report the failed q-estimation attempts' actual numeric results (not just "found no difference... p=0.12") so a reader can judge how close the design came, or reframe the abstract/conclusion to state unambiguously that the quantitative fit (K̂) is a separate descriptive exercise from the theorem's N*, not a confirmation of it.

### Minor concerns
- §4.4: "rendering very sixteen times really does produce monotonous audio" — garbled phrasing, likely a dropped word (e.g., "the word 'sixteen' times").
- The correlation r=0.59 [0.35, 0.75] "over 23 cells" (S12) doesn't state how the 23 cells were defined (model×k combinations?) or whether the CI accounts for non-independence across k within the same model — same clustering concern as Major #1, worth a footnote at minimum.
- It is not stated whether the signed-rank tests (p=0.031, p=0.062) are one- or two-sided; given the paper frames the deficit as "one-directional" a priori, this should be stated explicitly rather than left implicit.
- Reference [1] is cited as a foundational alignment-analysis source but is noted as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a sentence on why a withdrawn preprint is still safe to cite for that analysis.
- Table 1's "capacity gain" columns (rep., ctl., ratio) are not clearly cross-referenced to the dNeff/d log k definition given in §4.4's text; a table note would help.
- The RSS comparison (485.3 vs 2.6) in §4.1 is reported without any measure of uncertainty (CI, p-value, or number of data points fit) — as stated it's just a bald numeric contrast.
- Equation (1)'s log base is unspecified (immaterial to the ratio, but worth a one-word note for clarity).
- "Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB) are available on request" undercuts the otherwise strong reproducibility framing ("Both transcript sets are released" / full code released) — "on request" is not the same as archived/DOI'd public release.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal-verification contribution and the qualitative periodicity-vs-length dissociation are valuable and mostly well-supported, but the quantitative headline claims (−8.3%, 18.2%/94.3%, the 3.5-fold saturation reduction) need to be recomputed and re-stated at the checkpoint-clustered level with multiple-comparison discipline and honest hedging about the post-hoc, 4-of-6-checkpoint basis of the extension result before the paper's abstract-level claims are adequately supported by its own evidence.
