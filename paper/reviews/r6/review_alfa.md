### Summary

The paper argues that repetition-induced hallucination in autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech (TTS) models — looping, truncating, or miscounting repeated phrases — follows from a simple mechanism: softmax attention "dilutes" across repeated spans (Lemma 1), making the decoder's per-repetition state update approximately autonomous, and if that update map is a contraction (Assumption 2), a theorem proved and machine-checked in Lean 4 (Theorem 1) shows that no Lipschitz readout, including the model's own stop head, can distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon N*. Empirically, the authors test six checkpoints across three model families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) on a 180-item parametric "repetition ladder," scored with a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the autoregressive bias of a Whisper-style judge. They report a median count error of -8.3% at k≥6, exact-match rates of 18.2% for repeated items versus 94.3% for length-matched non-repetitive controls, slower growth of hidden-state effective rank under repetition (0.46× the control rate), and — after a post-hoc extension of the ladder to k=128 on 4 of 6 checkpoints — a 3.5-fold reduction in a fitted saturation scale for repeated versus control text.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem and its framing are pitched as general claims about "autoregressive text-to-speech models," but the empirical population that is supposed to instantiate the theorem's premises is a narrow, homogeneous slice: six checkpoints, all English-only, all softmax cross-attention codec-language models, tested on a curated synthetic carrier-sentence ladder rather than naturalistic text.
   **Where** — Abstract ("Autoregressive text-to-speech models loop, truncate, and lose count..."); §1 Introduction; §3 Models; §5 Discussion, "Limits" paragraph ("an English-only panel of codec-language models with softmax text attention").
   **Why it matters** — Part (iv) of Theorem 1 is advertised as decoding-rule-agnostic ("No property of the decoding rule enters, so the bound covers greedy, sampled and beam decoding alike"), which invites readers to treat the result as broadly architecture-general. But nothing in the paper tests whether the dilution-contraction mechanism, or its empirical signatures (proportional undercount, slower effective-rank growth), survive under: non-Latin scripts or tonal/morphologically reduplicative languages (where "k copies of a phrase" is not a free experimental variable but a property of the lexicon itself); non-softmax attention or non-transformer AR decoders (e.g., SSM-backboned TTS); or, for the one zero-shot voice-cloning model in the panel (XTTS-v2), different reference speakers/voices. The paper's own "Limits" paragraph concedes exactly this scope restriction, but the abstract and title do not carry the same qualification, so the headline claims read more general than the evidence supports.
   **What would address it** — Either scope the title/abstract explicitly to "English, softmax-attention codec-LM TTS" or add at least one architecturally distinct AR TTS system and one non-English/non-Latin-script language to the panel; report whether the effect is sensitive to reference-speaker choice for the zero-shot model.

2. **Issue** — The paper's most quotable number (3.5-fold reduction in saturation scale) comes from a post-hoc extension of the ladder, run on only 4 of 6 checkpoints, one of which (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows no repeated-side saturation at all and "inverts the ratio," yet the figure appears in the abstract without these qualifiers.
   **Where** — Abstract; §4.2.
   **Why it matters** — The text is candid that the extension to k∈{48,64,96,128} happened only "So we extended it, post hoc" after the original, larger ladder (k≤32, all 6 checkpoints) failed to show the predicted horizon at all — count error stayed proportional, "not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts." Running a new, more extreme condition after a null result, on a shrunken and non-unanimous subset, and then reporting the resulting effect size prominently in the abstract is a textbook researcher-degrees-of-freedom pattern, even though the authors themselves flag the risk and call 3.5 "an upper bound." As presented, a reader skimming the abstract would not learn that a third of the panel contradicts the pattern.
   **What would address it** — Qualify the 3.5-fold number wherever it is emphasized (abstract included) with "n=4/6 checkpoints, post-hoc, one checkpoint dissenting"; ideally validate on an independently generated extended-ladder stimulus set or additional seeds before treating it as confirmatory.

3. **Issue** — Assumption 2 (contraction, q<1), the load-bearing premise of the entire theorem, is never empirically confirmed; the one direct attempt to measure it produced a null result.
   **Where** — §5 Discussion ("two attempts to measure q failed... The premise remains unestablished").
   **Why it matters** — A perturbation probe designed to detect differential local contraction between repeated items and controls found no significant difference (7 of 18 pairs, p=0.12), and its own linearity control failed. Given this, the capacity-gain / effective-rank results (§4.4) are correlational surrogates for a mechanism that was directly tested and not found, not confirmatory evidence of contraction. The paper's title, "A Formally Verified Attractor Theory," and its abstract framing risk being read as "we established an attractor mechanism," when what is actually shown is "we proved a conditional theorem and found behavior consistent with, but not diagnostic of, its premise."
   **What would address it** — Tone down title/abstract language to reflect the conditional, unconfirmed status of Assumption 2; invest in a better q-estimator (e.g., a Jacobian-spectral-radius estimate at the relevant layers) rather than relying solely on a perturbation probe that already failed its own linearity check.

4. **Issue** — Cross-checkpoint statistical claims rest on n=6, which the paper acknowledges gives a mechanically fixed "exact floor" p-value, and no correction is discussed across the many statistics reported.
   **Where** — §4.2 ("mean 76.7 points [68.4, 84.4], signed-rank p=0.031, the exact floor at n=6"; "so p=0.062: on that statistic the panel misses even its own floor"); §4.4 (p=0.031, SD 16.0 across a fourfold range).
   **Why it matters** — With six checkpoints as the unit of replication, a signed-rank test cannot produce anything below its floor value regardless of true effect size, so "significance" here is an artifact of sample size, not evidence of a precisely estimated or robust effect. The paper is transparent about this, which is to its credit, but then still uses the p-values rhetorically to support general claims about "the mechanism." Combined with the many other contrasts computed across §4.1–4.4 (median error, exact-match rate, capacity-gain ratio, attention-flatness correlation r=0.59, probe p=0.12, penalty-sweep direction), there is no discussion of multiplicity, and the overall garden of reported statistics makes it hard to know how many non-significant or inconvenient results were also computed and not foregrounded.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly frame n=6 results as pilot-scale; expand the panel before leaning on hypothesis tests for the paper's central claims; consider a single pre-specified composite statistic rather than a large family of individually flagged p-values.

5. **Issue** — The CTC judge, adopted specifically to avoid the AR-decoder bias of Whisper, is itself acknowledged to be biased in the same direction as the headline effect, and specifically worse at high k — exactly the regime carrying the paper's most novel claims.
   **Where** — §3 Behavioural measurement; §5 "Limits" ("a judge whose blank-collapse merges adjacent word copies, so high-k magnitudes err our way"); §4.2 ("The judge's bias points the same way as the effect... making 3.5 an upper bound").
   **Why it matters** — The audit reported (ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth vs. 0.19 for Whisper) is presumably pooled and not broken out as a function of k, yet the paper's own text concedes that CTC blank-collapse increasingly merges adjacent repeated words as k grows — precisely where the k=128 extension and the 3.5-fold saturation-gap claim live. An unquantified upper-bound caveat leaves the reader unable to distinguish a true 3.5× effect from an artifact-inflated one that might be much smaller.
   **What would address it** — Report the CTC judge's blank-collapse error rate as an explicit function of k (not just pooled at k≥4) on the concatenative ground-truth audio, and propagate a bias-corrected saturation-scale estimate with its own uncertainty interval.

6. **Issue** — The two largest raw artifacts underlying the paper's second empirical pillar (audio, activations) are not archived but only "available on request."
   **Where** — §3 "Code, data, settings."
   **Why it matters** — Code, the Lean development, stimuli, and transcripts are released on GitHub, which is good practice, but the 2.5GB of audio and 25GB of activations behind the effective-rank/capacity-gain analysis are gated informally. The claim that these are "regenerable from the released seeds" is only as good as the stability of the exact library/checkpoint/hardware environment, which is not pinned down in the text, and "on request" access cannot be independently verified by reviewers or future readers.
   **What would address it** — Deposit the audio and a representative sample of activations in a persistent public archive (e.g., Zenodo, an HF dataset) with a DOI, and pin exact library/checkpoint versions and hardware/seed conditions needed for regeneration.

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is cited for its "alignment analysis" but is annotated "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — worth a sentence on why a withdrawn preprint is still treated as citable evidence.
- Reference [14] ("Clustered attractor manifolds...", arXiv:2608.08922, 2026) is a very recent, presumably unreviewed preprint; leaning on it in the related-dynamics discussion (§5) without noting its review status is a small transparency gap.
- δ (logit spread bound) is used informally in the text right after Assumption 1 ("O(δ + 1/k)") before being formally introduced in Lemma 1 — reorder or forward-reference for clarity.
- Table 1's "ratio" column is not explicitly glossed in the caption as rep./ctl.
- Figure 1(c)/(d) axis quantities ("d_eff/d log k", the value "50" on panel (d)'s y-axis) lack explicit units/scale explanation in the caption or body text.
- No ethics/consent statement regarding the reference/voice data used for the zero-shot cloning model (XTTS-v2); given the panel includes a voice-cloning system, a brief note on speaker consent for any reference audio used would be appropriate.
- The opening example ("six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six") is a stylistic choice that reads informally for an ICASSP submission; not a substantive issue but worth a copyedit pass for register consistency with the rest of the technical prose.
- Ref [7] is a companion paper by the same author team — not improper, but the paper should make explicit (beyond the passing mention in §1) that this is a self-citation establishing continuity with prior work, to avoid any appearance of independent corroboration.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the Lean-verified theorem and the periodicity-vs-length control (§4.3) are genuine, well-designed contributions, but the paper's title/abstract generalize well beyond its actual evidentiary base (six English-only, architecturally homogeneous checkpoints; an unconfirmed core contraction assumption; a post-hoc, non-unanimous headline effect measured by a judge whose known bias points the same direction as the claimed result), and these need to be scoped down or substantiated before the claims as stated are publishable.
