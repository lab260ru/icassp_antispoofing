### Summary

The paper models one "lap" of an autoregressive TTS decoder around a repeated phrase as an autonomous state map $F$ and proves (in Lean 4, machine-checked) that if $F$ is a contraction ($q<1$), then any Lipschitz readout of the decoder's state — including its own stop-token logit — loses the ability to distinguish repetition counts past a horizon $N^*$ that depends on the contraction rate, orbit scale, and readout margin. Empirically, six TTS checkpoints from three families are run on a repetition ladder ($k\in\{1,...,32\}$, later extended post hoc to $k=128$ on four checkpoints) and scored with a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the autoregressive bias of Whisper. The headline results are: (1) a one-directional undercount that grows with $k$ but does not saturate within $k\le32$ (contradicting the theorem's own prediction of a finite horizon until the ladder is extended); (2) a sharp contrast between repeated stimuli (18.2% exact) and length-matched non-repeating controls (94.3% exact), taken as evidence the failure tracks periodicity rather than length; (3) slower growth of trajectory effective rank under repetition (0.46× the control rate); and (4) at $k=128$, a fitted saturation scale roughly 3.5× lower for repeated vs. control stimuli. The authors explicitly flag that the theorem's premise (contraction, $q<1$) is never measured and that two independent attempts to estimate it were inconclusive.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem-confirming result (a finite counting horizon) only appears after a post-hoc, results-contingent extension of the experimental design, which is a textbook HARKing pattern rather than a pre-registered test.
   **Where** — Section 4.1 ("the deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts... the horizon is not inside this ladder") followed by Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc, to $k\in\{48,64,96,128\}$ on the 4 checkpoints whose decoders admit utterances that long").
   **Why it matters** — The original, apparently pre-specified ladder ($k$ up to 32, 180 items) falsified the theorem's central qualitative prediction. Rather than reporting that as a negative/boundary result, the authors extended the design specifically until a saturation curve appeared, on a *subset* of checkpoints selected post hoc by which ones "admit" long enough utterances. There is no pre-registration, and the discussion section's own list of falsification criteria ("counts still tracking $k$ past the ladder... The last was live until we ran it") makes explicit that the extension was run *because* the original test was on the verge of falsifying the theory. A design that extends sampling until the predicted effect appears cannot, by construction, fail to eventually find "a" saturation-shaped curve in noisy count data — it doesn't discriminate the theorem from a null model with any horizon-free explanation for eventual utterance-length ceilings (which the authors themselves note exist independently, since controls saturate too).
   **What would address it** — Report the $k\le32$ null result as the primary finding and treat the $k=128$ extension as an explicitly labeled, non-confirmatory follow-up (which the authors partly do rhetorically, but the abstract and Section 4.2 headline still present it as confirming the theorem). Better: pre-register the extended ladder's stopping rule and the checkpoint-selection criterion before running it, or run the full $k=128$ ladder on all six checkpoints so the "4 of 6" is not itself a selection artifact.

2. **Issue** — The paper's own dose-response check of the mechanistic story (flatter attention → worse counting, per Lemma 1 + Theorem 1) comes out *reversed*, and the study design (purely observational/correlational) cannot distinguish "dilution causes miscounting" from "a decoder that has already failed produces peaked attention."
   **Where** — Section 4.4: "within a (model, k) cell, items with flatter attention are counted better, not worse ($r=0.59$...). That reverses the lemma's direction... peaked attention plausibly marks a decoder already stalled, which this design cannot separate from a cause."
   **Why it matters** — Attention dilution (Lemma 1) is the causal engine that is supposed to make $F$ approximately autonomous and hence a candidate contraction. If the one place the authors can test the mechanism's directional prediction shows the opposite sign, the paper's causal story (dilution → autonomous map → contraction → count loss) has no supporting internal evidence at the item level — only aggregate correlations at the checkpoint level, which are consistent with many other causal orderings. No intervention (e.g., artificially flattening or peaking attention and observing counting accuracy) is attempted anywhere in the paper, so the design remains purely correlational for the mechanism it foregrounds.
   **What would address it** — An interventional design: directly manipulate attention entropy (e.g., via attention-logit smoothing/temperature) at inference time and measure whether counting accuracy moves in the predicted direction. Absent that, the claim should be downgraded from "the mechanism is dilution-driven contraction" to "dilution and contraction are compatible companions of an unexplained counting failure," and the abstract/title's causal framing ("Attractor Theory of Repetition Hallucination") should be qualified accordingly.

3. **Issue** — The statistical unit of replication ($n=6$ checkpoints) is treated as six independent samples for signed-rank tests, but the checkpoints are clustered within only three families (Llasa contributes 3 of 6, Qwen3-TTS contributes 2 of 6), so the effective number of independent architectural replicates is closer to 3–4, not 6.
   **Where** — Section 4.2: "we take the checkpoint as the unit of replication... signed-rank p = 0.031, the exact floor at n = 6"; Section 4.4: "mean 24.7 [13.7, 36.5], p = 0.031."
   **Why it matters** — With $n=6$, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test's minimum achievable two-sided p-value is already at the reported floor (0.03125), so the significance claim is maximally fragile — a single sign flip in one comparison changes the conclusion entirely. Worse, three of those six "independent" data points are same-architecture scale variants (Llasa 1B/3B/8B) sharing training data, tokenizer, and codec, so they are not exchangeable draws for a between-architecture claim. Pseudoreplication of this kind inflates the apparent evidentiary weight of a result that is already sitting at the smallest possible p-value the design can produce.
   **What would address it** — Either restrict the family-level test to one representative checkpoint per family (giving a properly independent $n=3$, and honestly reporting that this cannot reach significance), or use a mixed/hierarchical model with family as a random effect and report the family-level and checkpoint-level variance components separately, rather than a flat signed-rank test over 6 non-independent units.

4. **Issue** — The "periodicity, not length" control (Fig. 1b, Section 4.3) is confounded above $k=8$: the filler pool used for controls is only 8 items and is *cycled*, meaning controls at $k=16,24,32$ are themselves periodic (period-8), not aperiodic — a fact the Method section states directly but the Results/Discussion do not fully reckon with when they later say "collapse-by-length predicts that a 32-word control and a 32-word repetition fail alike."
   **Where** — Section 3 ("same carrier and word count, the repeated token replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k=8. The contrast tested is thus period-8 repetition at most against period-1 (S10)."); Section 4.3; Section 5.
   **Why it matters** — The claim "the limit tracks periodicity, not length" is only demonstrated as a period-1 vs. period-8 dichotomy, not as a genuine periodic/aperiodic contrast, and no intermediate periodicities (period-2, period-4) are tested to establish a dose-response curve in periodicity itself (only in $k$, which conflates length and repeat count for the treatment arm but not, cleanly, for the control arm at high $k$). This weakens the strong claim that periodicity per se, rather than "some coarse enough periodicity," is what the decoder cannot survive, and it is never flagged as a limitation outside a parenthetical pointer to a supplement (S10) not included in the reviewed text.
   **What would address it** — Add a periodicity-graded control condition (e.g., pools of size 2, 4, 8, 16) to show the horizon degrades monotonically with pool size, which would be a much stronger test of "periodicity, not length" than a single period-1-vs-period-8 comparison.

5. **Issue** — The theorem's load-bearing premise (Assumption 2: $F$ is a $q<1$ contraction) is never established for any of the six checkpoints; both independent attempts to estimate $q$ are reported as failed/null, yet the paper's title and framing ("Attractor Theory," "Counting Collapse") assert the mechanism as if demonstrated.
   **Where** — Section 5: "two attempts to measure q failed — a boundary-distance estimator needs a monotone read-head these decoders lack, and a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated items and their controls (7 of 18 pairs, p = 0.12), its own linearity control failing (S5). The premise remains unestablished."
   **Why it matters** — This is the crux of an adversarial read: if $q\ge1$ (no contraction) for the tested decoders, Theorem 1 is vacuously true but empirically irrelevant, and the observed undercounting must have some other cause entirely (e.g., a learned heuristic, training-data length statistics, decoder-side sampling artifacts) that merely correlates with the state-space diagnostics the paper reports (effective rank, exactly-correct rate). The paper's own honesty about this is commendable, but the abstract and title do not carry the same hedge — a reader skimming only those will come away believing the contraction mechanism was shown, not merely rendered "not yet falsified with the instruments tried."
   **What would address it** — Either develop and validate an estimator for $q$ that does not require a monotone read-head (the paper identifies this as the blocker but proposes no alternative), or retitle/reframe the contribution as "a formally verified conditional theorem plus behavioral evidence consistent with, but not confirmatory of, its premise," with the causal language in the abstract downgraded to match.

6. **Issue** — The CTC judge is validated against "concatenative audio with known ground truth" — audio very likely constructed by splicing repeated recorded tokens — which may not resemble the acoustic character of genuinely model-generated repeated speech (coarticulation, prosodic drift, degenerate loop artifacts) at the high-$k$ regime where the headline saturation claim lives.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth... put through the same audit: ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper.").
   **Why it matters** — A CTC recognizer with no language-model prior is the right choice in principle to avoid Whisper's autoregressive bias, but its accuracy is only demonstrated on synthetic, spliced ground truth, not on the actual model outputs being scored in Section 4. If genuine TTS repetition failures produce acoustically blurred or merged syllables near the collapse regime (plausible exactly where the paper's strongest claims are made, $k\ge48$), a CTC judge with "no language model, output length governed by the acoustics" could systematically mis-segment in ways the concatenative benchmark never exercises. The paper does flag one related caveat ("a judge whose blank-collapse merges adjacent word copies, so high-k magnitudes err our way") but does not report a direct spot-check of judge accuracy on actual model-generated audio.
   **What would address it** — Report inter-annotator agreement (human transcription on a stratified sample of actual model outputs, especially at $k\ge48$) against the CTC judge's counts, not only against the concatenative synthetic benchmark.

7. **Issue** — Several key statistics in the extension (Section 4.2) rest on very small per-cell samples after post-hoc exclusions, undermining the precision implied by the reported confidence intervals.
   **Where** — Section 4.2: "Of 240 generations 20 hit our budget and 10 were degenerate, leaving 21–26 per cell."
   **Why it matters** — A two-parameter saturating-exponential fit ($\hat K(1-e^{-k/\hat K})$) across four $k$ values (48/64/96/128) with ~21–26 items per cell per condition is a small-$n$ curve fit; the reported $\hat K_{\text{rep}}=30\ [24,46]$ vs. $\hat K_{\text{ctl}}=105\ [94,114]$ contrast (the paper's central "3.5-fold" number) is exactly the kind of estimate that is highly sensitive to a handful of items in the tails, particularly given that truncated/degenerate items were excluded in a way the authors admit is directionally conservative but do not show is quantitatively small.
   **What would address it** — Report the fit with the excluded items imputed via a censored-regression model (since the paper itself explains why exclusion is conservative, a bounded sensitivity analysis is a natural and cheap check) rather than simple exclusion, and show per-checkpoint (not only pooled) curve fits with per-checkpoint CIs so the reader can see how much the pooled 3.5× figure is driven by the two checkpoints (Llasa-1B, Llasa-8B) explicitly called out as the only ones with disjoint intervals.

8. **Issue** — Full audio and activation data — the direct evidentiary basis for the effective-rank/capacity-gain claims in Section 4.4 and Table 1 — are not released, only "available on request" and claimed "regenerable from the released seeds."
   **Where** — Section 3, "Code, data, settings."
   **Why it matters** — "Regenerable from released seeds" presumes bit-for-bit (or at least statistically identical) determinism of GPU-based autoregressive sampling across hardware/driver/library versions, which is frequently not guaranteed in practice (nondeterministic CUDA kernels, library version drift). Since the state-measurement results (effective rank, capacity gain — one of the paper's four headline contributions) depend on activations that are not directly shared, independent verification of Section 4.4 specifically is currently blocked behind a request process and an unverified reproducibility assumption.
   **What would address it** — Release at minimum a stratified sample of activations/audio (e.g., one seed per checkpoint × k-value cell) directly, and report whether re-generation from seeds was actually verified to reproduce the reported effective-rank numbers exactly or only approximately.

### Minor concerns

- Abstract states the horizon as an inequality, "$N^* \le \log(\mu/2LC)/\log q$," while Equation (1) *defines* $N^*$ as exactly that quantity ("$m \le \log(\mu/(2LC))/\log q =: N^*$"); the abstract's "$\le$" and the missing parenthesis around $2LC$ are inconsistent with the body and should be reconciled.
- Reference [1] is cited for "its alignment analysis" but flagged as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026," with no note on why it was withdrawn or whether the withdrawal bears on the cited claim — citing a withdrawn preprint without comment is a citation-hygiene issue.
- The "closest prior result" discussion in Section 5 leans on the authors' own unpublished companion preprint [7] (same author list, arXiv:2604.08591) to establish the framing ("attractor regime descriptively... here the attractor is what the theorem is about") — this is not disclosed as a companion/self-citation beyond the passing phrase "companion to a spectral study," which readers could easily miss as a conflict-of-interest-adjacent framing dependency.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" column and the prose figure "0.46 of the control rate" (Section 4.4) are not obviously reconcilable at a glance — the table's per-checkpoint ratios range from 0.21 to 0.80, a fourfold spread the text acknowledges (SD 16.0) but Table 1's caption does not state whether the pooled 0.46 is a mean, median, or pooled-slope estimate.
- Section 4.2's two significance statements for what is described as "the same underlying phenomenon" (exactly-correct rate favors control in 6/6, $p=0.031$; median-error gap positive in 5/6, $p=0.062$) are presented side-by-side without correction for the multiplicity of outcome measures tested (median error, exact-match rate, capacity gain, correlation r, degenerate rate all reported without a stated family-wise correction).
- Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is described as "the exception" and reinterpreted post hoc via a new logical framing ("capacity loss is necessary, not sufficient") rather than against a pre-specified decision rule for how anomalous checkpoints would be classified.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the Lean-verified theorem is a genuine and rare contribution to this literature, and the paper is unusually candid about its own gaps, but the empirical chain from "theorem" to "counting collapse in real TTS decoders" currently rests on a post-hoc-extended, underpowered, partly confounded, and (for its own proposed mechanism) sign-reversing set of experiments, with the contraction premise explicitly unmeasured.
