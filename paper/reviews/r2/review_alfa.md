STANCE: Skeptical-but-fair

### Summary
The paper asks whether repetition-induced counting failure in autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech models (looping, truncation, miscounting) is a structural consequence of decoder dynamics rather than a sampling artifact. It models one repetition as an approximately autonomous state map F on the decoder's hidden state, proves in Lean 4 that if F is a q-Lipschitz contraction (q<1) then no L-Lipschitz readout can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* set by q, orbit scale, and readout margin (Theorem 1), and supports this with an attention-dilution lemma showing softmax attention over k repeated spans flattens as O(1/k). Empirically, across checkpoints from the Llasa, XTTS-v2, and Qwen3-TTS families on a 180-item repetition ladder, counting accuracy collapses at a model-specific k*, length-matched non-repetitive controls survive much longer (11.1% vs. 48.9% at k≥6), and the effective rank of hidden-state trajectories grows more slowly under repetition than under the controls (capacity-gain ratio 0.57), which the authors read as the direct observable signature of the theorem's premise.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The number of models/checkpoints actually studied is reported inconsistently across the paper, and the headline population (five checkpoints, three families) does not match the checkpoints that generate the headline statistics.
   **Where** — Abstract ("across five checkpoints from three families"); §3 Methods ("five checkpoints: the Llasa family at 1B/3B/8B … plus XTTS-v2 … and Qwen3-TTS at 0.6B/1.7B" — this lists six, not five); Table 1 (five rows: Llasa-1B, Llasa-3B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B, Qwen3-TTS-1.7B — no Llasa-8B); Fig. 1(a) legend (six series, including "xtts2norp" but not Llasa-8B); Fig. 1(c) legend (seven series, now including Llasa-8B); §4.3 ("disjoint 95% intervals for six of the 7 instrumented checkpoints"); §4.2 ("Across 6 models k∗ differs…").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's central external-validity claim is that collapse "tracks periodicity rather than length" *across the tested population*, but a reader cannot tell what that population is from one place to the next. Most importantly, Llasa-8B — the largest, presumably most capable checkpoint in the within-architecture scale ladder that the paper uses to argue "scale buys a longer horizon but a bounded one" — never appears in Table 1 or in the primary accuracy figure (Fig. 1a/b), only in the effective-rank figure (Fig. 1c). If Llasa-8B behaves differently on the accuracy metric (e.g., a much larger k*, or a k* that no longer looks power-limited), silently omitting it from the accuracy table/figure would make the "universal collapse" claim look more general than the data support. The 0.57 headline capacity-gain ratio in the abstract also does not equal the mean of the five per-model ratios in Table 1 (mean ≈0.53), implying it is computed over a different, larger set of checkpoints than the one whose breakdown is shown — so the headline number is not traceable to any table in the paper.
   **What would address it** — Report one consistent census of instrumented checkpoints, used identically in every figure/table, and give Llasa-8B's counting-accuracy and k* numbers explicitly (in Table 1 and Fig. 1a/b) rather than only its effective-rank curve. State explicitly which checkpoint is the one whose bootstrap interval is *not* disjoint in §4.3, and discuss whether it is an outlier in capability, family, or scale.

2. **Issue** — The tested model population is a narrow slice of the AR-TTS space (all softmax-cross-attention, codec-language-model architectures), yet the abstract's claims and the Discussion's reasoning about mitigations are stated for AR-TTS/AR-decoders in general.
   **Where** — Abstract ("Autoregressive text-to-speech models loop, truncate, and lose count…"); §3 Methods (model list); §5 Discussion ("Existing mitigations act late: attention supervision, alignment priors, contrastive decoding [1,3]. Under this account they help only insofar as they raise q or enlarge the readout margin…").
   **Why it matters** — Lemma 1 is a statement specifically about softmax attention; its applicability to AR-TTS systems built around hard/monotonic alignment (e.g., RALL-E [3], CosyVoice 2 [4], ELLA-V [2]) — precisely the systems the Discussion invokes as "mitigations" whose effect the theorem supposedly explains — is asserted, not tested. None of those alignment-supervised systems, nor any non-Transformer AR decoder (RNN/SSM-based TTS), is in the panel. The claim that decoding-rule and alignment-level mitigations "help only insofar as they raise q" is therefore a prediction about an untested population, dressed as an explanation of results the paper does show only for the one mitigation it does test (XTTS-v2's repetition-penalty ablation, a decoding-time heuristic, not an alignment-supervision method).
   **What would address it** — Either soften the generality of the abstract/Discussion claims to "codec-language-model TTS decoders with softmax cross-attention" (the actual tested class), or add at least one alignment-supervised or non-softmax AR-TTS system to the ladder to test whether raising q via alignment supervision actually shifts k* as predicted.

3. **Issue** — The stimulus/model coverage for language, voice/speaker conditioning, and tokenization is essentially uncontrolled for, beyond a one-line acknowledgment.
   **Where** — §5 Discussion, "Remaining limits: the panel is English-only; text attention is unavailable where text and acoustic embeddings are additively fused (Qwen3-TTS)…"; §3 Methods does not report how many reference speakers/voices were used for the (implicitly zero-shot) XTTS-v2/Llasa generations.
   **Why it matters** — Lemma 1's mechanism operates on attention over *text tokens*; how a repeated word tokenizes (BPE segmentation, morphology, script) will differ substantially across languages, so the periodicity-vs-length dissociation demonstrated here for English carrier sentences is not obviously portable to, e.g., agglutinative or logographic languages, where "the same word repeated k times" may not correspond to k repetitions of the same token span. Similarly, if a single (or few) reference speaker(s) were used for zero-shot cloning models, the reported accuracies could be entangled with that voice's speaking rate/prosody rather than reflecting a general property of the architecture. Neither is discussed enough to know how much of the finding is architecture-general versus stimulus/voice-specific.
   **What would address it** — Report the number and identity of reference speakers/voices used per model, and, even briefly, replicate the periodicity-vs-length contrast in one additional language/script to show the effect is not an artifact of English tokenization.

4. **Issue** — Assumption 2 (the decoder's per-repetition map F is a genuine contraction, q<1), which is the load-bearing premise of Theorem 1, is never directly verified in any of the tested models; the paper's own attempt to estimate q is reported as unsound.
   **Where** — §4.4 "Two things we could not establish" ("We first tried to estimate q directly… That estimator is unsound here…"); §4.3 substitutes effective-rank saturation as an indirect proxy.
   **Why it matters** — The Conclusion states the theorem is "proved in Lean, measured in real decoders," but what is measured is a downstream, qualitatively-consistent signature (effective rank grows more slowly under repetition), not the contraction property itself. Effective-rank saturation is also consistent with several non-contracting alternatives (e.g., a periodic/limit-cycle orbit, or attention dilution alone driving observationally similar saturation without a genuine Banach-style contraction to a fixed point). Because Assumption 2 is explicitly flagged in §2 as "an empirical claim… where it fails, the theorem is silent," the paper's own logic implies that without verifying q<1 for the tested checkpoints, one cannot conclude Theorem 1 (rather than some other mechanism) is what explains the observed collapse — only that the results are compatible with it.
   **What would address it** — Either propose and validate an alternative q-estimator that survives the boundary-localization confound identified in §4.4, or explicitly retitle the empirical contribution as consistent with, rather than a direct measurement of, the contraction premise.

5. **Issue** — Headline accuracy contrasts are reported as bare percentages pooled across models/conditions with no confidence intervals, significance tests, or per-cell sample sizes, despite a small stimulus set.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("accuracy is 71.3% at k≤3 and 11.1% at k≥6… 57.0% of generations run long… 34.3% stop short"); §4.2 ("11.1% vs. 48.9% for controls"); §4.2 probability comparison ("89% of the 54 matched pairs").
   **Why it matters** — With 180 stimulus items spread across ≥4 stimulus types, 10 k-values, repeated/control conditions, and up to 7 checkpoints × 3 seeds, several reported cells (e.g., "k≥6" pooled per model) likely rest on single-digit item counts. Bootstrap intervals are reported only for the capacity-gain statistic (Fig. 1d), not for the accuracy contrasts that carry the paper's sharpest claim ("periodicity, not length"). Without uncertainty quantification on the 11.1%/48.9% contrast (and the many secondary "alternative-explanation-ruled-out" comparisons in §4.2, none corrected for multiplicity), a reader cannot judge whether the gap is robust or driven by a few templates/models.
   **What would address it** — Report per-model and pooled confidence intervals (bootstrapped over items/templates, as already done for capacity gain) for every headline accuracy contrast, and state item counts per (model, k, condition) cell.

6. **Issue** — Reference list quality issues undermine verifiability of the paper's evidentiary base for its framing claims.
   **Where** — References [1], [5], [9], [11], [13].
   **Why it matters** — Ref. [11] is corrupted in the bibliography (author list present, title/venue rendered as `,” .`); I confirmed via arXiv search that it is almost certainly Barbero et al., "Transformers need glasses! Information over-squashing in language tasks" [2406.04267], which proves a representational-collapse result for decoder-only Transformers closely related to this paper's Theorem 1 — the missing title obscures exactly the paper a reader most needs to compare against for novelty. Ref. [1], used in the Introduction to establish that "the behaviour is well documented as a stability problem," is flagged by the authors themselves as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026," which weakens its evidentiary weight for that framing claim even though the withdrawal is disclosed transparently. Refs. [5], [9], [13] are all dated 2026 (two of them the authors' own companion/self-citations), which I could partly verify: [9] (Venkatesh, "Repeated-token counting reveals a dissociation between representations and outputs," arXiv:2605.09239) exists and matches the description in §4.4/§5, but its finding is about a text LM, not TTS — the paper's contrastive use of it in §4.4 ("far from the near-perfect decodability reported for text LMs [9]") compares across modality and probing methodology (a ridge probe on trajectory summaries vs. a linear probe directly on the residual stream), a confound not discussed.
   **What would address it** — Fix the corrupted reference [11] and explicitly compare Theorem 1 against Barbero et al.'s representational-collapse result rather than only the depth-indexed dynamical-systems papers [12,13]; note the modality/probe-methodology confound when citing [9] as a contrastive baseline.

### Minor concerns
- §5 characterizes refs [12,13] and [11] as indexing "convergence by depth or an abstract temperature," but the Barbero et al. abstract found via search describes a representational-collapse result tied to input-sequence properties in decoder-only Transformers, not purely depth — the characterization may understate its similarity to this paper's contribution.
- The self-citation to the companion ASR paper [5] (arXiv:2604.08591) is used to corroborate the "spectral signature" claim in §4.3; since it is a very recent, evidently unpublished companion work by the same authors, its corroborating role is somewhat circular and should be flagged as such rather than presented as independent confirmation.
- Table 1's "acc.k≥6(%)" column headers are cramped/ambiguous typographically (rep./ctl./ratio spread across sub-columns without clear alignment in the text extraction) — worth double-checking in the camera-ready render.
- §4.2 states "the harder-to-predict text is the one models render correctly" — this is a slightly confusing inversion of phrasing (controls are harder to predict by an LM yet rendered *more* correctly by the TTS model); consider rewording for clarity.
- The phrase "no property of the decoding rule enters" (§2) is a strong claim about greedy/sampled/beam decoding equivalence that is asserted from the theorem but never empirically tested across decoding strategies in §3–4 (only one decoding configuration per model appears to be used).

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal result and the periodicity-vs-length control are genuinely valuable, but the inconsistent and partly untraceable model census (Llasa-8B silently absent from the headline figure/table, "five" vs. six vs. seven checkpoints), the unverified contraction premise, and the absence of uncertainty quantification on the headline accuracy contrasts need to be resolved before the paper's population-level claims can be assessed as stated.
