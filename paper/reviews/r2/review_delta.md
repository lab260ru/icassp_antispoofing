### Summary

The paper argues that repetition failures in autoregressive TTS (looping, truncation, miscounting) are a structural consequence of contractive per-repetition dynamics in the decoder's hidden state, rather than a sampling or exposure-bias artifact. It models one repetition boundary as a state map $F$, proves (and formalizes in Lean 4/mathlib) that if $F$ is $q$-contractive then no $L$-Lipschitz readout can separate repetition counts beyond a horizon $N^* = \log(\mu/2LC)/\log q$, and pairs this with an attention-dilution lemma showing attention over $k$ identical spans flattens toward uniform. Empirically, across five (per the abstract) TTS checkpoints from three model families, counting accuracy collapses at a model-specific $k^*$ while length-matched non-repetitive controls survive much longer (11.1% vs. 48.9% accuracy at $k\ge6$), and the effective rank of the generation trajectory grows more slowly under repetition than under the control condition (capacity-gain ratio 0.57), which the authors read as the theorem's own predicted consequence made observable.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's headline theoretical contribution (a formally proved representational-collapse bound for autoregressive decoders under repeated conditioning) substantially overlaps with an existing, more architecturally grounded collapse theorem for decoder-only Transformers, and the paper engages that prior work in only one sentence.
   **Where** — §5 "Relation to prior results" (citing [10, 11]); reference list entry [11], which is bibliographically corrupted (empty title/venue, truncated author list: "Federico Barbero, Andrea Banino, Steven Kaptur-owski, et al., ,'' .").
   **Why it matters** — [11] resolves to Barbero, Banino, Kapturowski et al., *"Transformers need glasses! Information over-squashing in language tasks"* [2406.04267], which proves — for decoder-only Transformers specifically, the exact architecture family studied here — that distinct input sequences can collapse to arbitrarily close final-token representations, and derives this from the architecture itself (bounded weights, LayerNorm, softmax saturation) rather than positing it as an unverified black-box assumption. That paper's central empirical demonstrations are tasks that require distinguishing repeated or near-duplicate tokens (counting, copying-style retrieval) — the same phenomenon class this submission studies in TTS. The Discussion dismisses the overlap with a single unworked sentence ("those results index convergence by depth or an abstract temperature; ours indexes it by generation step under periodic conditioning") without a side-by-side comparison of the two collapse mechanisms, and without considering whether Barbero et al.'s architecturally-grounded result could be used to *derive* rather than merely assume Assumption 2 (see Concern 2). Combined with the authors' own companion paper [5], which "identified an attractor regime descriptively," the genuinely new content here narrows to a Lean formalization plus a TTS-specific empirical ladder — a reasonable but much narrower contribution than the framing in the abstract/introduction ("we show this is not a sampling accident but a structural limit") suggests. The corrupted citation for [11] also means the one paper closest to this submission's core claim is currently unidentifiable to a reader working from the bibliography alone.
   **What would address it** — Add a worked comparison between Theorem 1 and Barbero et al.'s collapse theorem (same hypotheses? does one imply the other for standard Transformer decoders?), state explicitly whether Assumption 2 could be derived rather than assumed via their signal-propagation analysis, and repair the [11] bibliography entry.

2. **Issue** — The theorem's load-bearing premise, Assumption 2 (that $F$ contracts with $q<1$), is explicitly reported as unverifiable with the paper's own methods, yet the abstract and conclusion state the result in confirmatory rather than conditional language.
   **Where** — §4.4 "Two things we could not establish"; Abstract ("we prove that if $F$ contracts... boundary states converge geometrically... The decoder does not merely err... it runs out of states"); §2 Assumption 2.
   **Why it matters** — The direct estimator for $q$ is reported as "unsound," and the ridge probe for the count decodes only coarsely (median $R^2=0.47$) with degradation, not erasure, on repeated text ("this is degradation, not erasure, and a stronger probe could change the picture," per the authors' own admission). What is actually shown is that effective rank saturates and attention flattens — both consistent with contraction, but also consistent with a non-fixed-point alternative the theorem does not cover: a low-dimensional limit *cycle* rather than convergence to a unique fixed point. This alternative is at least as plausible given that "looping" (57.0% of failures at $k\ge8$) is reported as a dominant failure mode — looping is exactly what a stable periodic orbit, not a contracting map converging to one point, would produce. The paper does not rule this out, yet the prose ("runs out of states in which the count could be represented") asserts the contraction mechanism as established fact.
   **What would address it** — Either find an estimator for $q$ that survives the boundary-localization problem described (e.g., subspace tracking rather than boundary-distance differencing), or temper the causal language throughout to state clearly that the results are consistent with, but do not establish, strict contraction, and explicitly address the limit-cycle alternative given that looping is the majority failure mode.

3. **Issue** — The number of models/checkpoints underlying the paper's statistics is stated inconsistently across the text, and the headline "0.57" capacity-gain ratio is not traceable to any stated aggregation rule.
   **Where** — Abstract ("five checkpoints from three families"); §3 Models (enumerates Llasa 1B/3B/8B + XTTS-v2 + Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B — six checkpoints, despite being introduced as "five"); Table 1 (5 rows, omitting Llasa-8B); §4.2 ("Across 6 models"); §4.3 ("six of the 7 instrumented checkpoints"); §4.4 ("seven of 7 variants"); Fig. 1(a) legend (6 entries, no Llasa-8B) vs. Fig. 1(c) legend (7 entries, including "xtts2norp").
   **Why it matters** — A reader cannot determine the actual sample behind any given number. This directly undercuts the paper's own transparency claim that "paper/build.sh regenerates every number in this paper from the released tables." Separately, the abstract's "0.57" figure does not equal the mean of the five per-model ratios in Table 1 (0.42, 0.28, 0.60, 0.57, 0.78 → mean ≈ 0.53); it happens to match only the Qwen3-TTS-0.6B row, with no stated pooling or weighting rule.
   **What would address it** — State the exact N for every reported statistic, reconcile the "five checkpoints" claim with the six-to-seven entities actually used across figures and results (including whether "xtts2norp" counts as a checkpoint), and specify exactly how "0.57" is computed from Table 1's per-model values.

4. **Issue** — The correctness judge (Whisper large-v3) is itself an autoregressive decoder, and its differential reliability on repeated versus non-repeated audio is not measured, despite this being exactly the failure class under study.
   **Where** — §3 "Behavioural measurement."
   **Why it matters** — The paper's sharpest empirical contrast (11.1% vs. 48.9% at $k\ge6$) is entirely mediated by Whisper's transcription, which the authors themselves note "de-duplicates repeated material, so a transcript count alone understates loops." The duration cross-check compensates for undercounting but not for other ASR failure modes specific to periodic audio, and no independent ASR system or human-transcribed subsample is used to bound how much of the repeated-vs-control gap originates in the judge rather than the generator.
   **What would address it** — Report inter-rater/inter-ASR agreement split by condition (repeated vs. control), and quantify Whisper's own error rate on ground-truth periodic audio to bound the measurement-instrument contribution to the reported gap.

5. **Issue** — No correction for multiple comparisons, and per-cell sample sizes are not disclosed for most reported percentages.
   **Where** — Throughout §4 (e.g., 71.3% vs. 11.1%, 57.0%/34.3% split, "89% of the 54 matched pairs," "78% of pairs," "seven of 7 variants"); Table 1; Fig. 1.
   **Why it matters** — With 180 base stimuli spread across a 10-point $k$-ladder, three units (word/number/sentence), repeated/control pairing, and 3 seeds per model, several headline percentages plausibly rest on small per-cell n that is never stated. Only the effective-rank capacity-gain statistic receives bootstrap intervals; the accuracy and NLL comparisons do not.
   **What would address it** — Report per-cell n for each headline statistic, add interval/test statistics for the accuracy and NLL comparisons, and state how (or whether) multiple comparisons across models, $k$-values, and conditions are accounted for.

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is cited to motivate the "stability problem" framing but is flagged "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" with no discussion of why — leaning on a withdrawn preprint for foundational framing deserves at least a sentence of justification.
- Fig. 1(a) omits Llasa-8B entirely while Fig. 1(c) includes it — no explanation is given for why the largest Llasa checkpoint is dropped from the main accuracy-collapse panel.
- The Lemma 1 symbol $\delta$ (attention-logit spread bound) is reused in §4.3 for a measured quantity ("$\delta \le 0.06$ nats") without confirming it is the same $\delta$ under the same conditions the lemma assumes — worth a clarifying sentence.
- The "per-model, per-template duration model... fitted on the low-$k$ regime" is not validated out-of-sample; if evaluated on overlapping $k$ ranges this could inflate apparent agreement in the conjunctive correctness criterion.
- The digit-repetition example ("six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six") conflates place-value repetition with lexical/phrase repetition without discussing whether Assumption 1 (a single autonomous map $F$) is equally well-motivated for digit conditioning.
- "xtts2norp" (the no-repetition-penalty ablation) appears in some figure legends but not in Table 1, making its role in the paper's aggregate statistics inconsistent.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the empirical periodicity-vs-length contrast is a genuinely informative design, but the theorem's core premise is admittedly untested by the paper's own account, the novelty of the formal collapse result is not adequately weighed against closely related prior art (Barbero et al., 2406.04267), and internal inconsistencies in model counts and a plausible measurement-instrument confound need to be resolved before the headline claims are safe to publish as stated.
