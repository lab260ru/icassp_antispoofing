# Review by charlie

### Summary
The paper argues that repetition-induced failures in autoregressive TTS decoders (early stopping, looping, miscounting) are not a sampling artifact but a structural consequence of a formal mechanism: when a prompt repeats a phrase, softmax attention over the repeated spans dilutes toward uniform (Lemma 1), which makes the decoder's per-repetition state update approximately autonomous; if that autonomous map is a contraction (Assumption 2), a Banach-style argument (Theorem 1) shows any Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts only up to a computable horizon N*. Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, across six TTS checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) scored by a CTC recognizer (chosen because Whisper's own autoregressive decoder is shown to be repetition-biased), repeated-phrase items show a median relative count error of -8.3% at k≥6 while length-matched non-repeating controls are exact at every k up to 32, and the effective rank of the generation trajectory grows at roughly half the control's rate under repetition — offered as the theorem's directly observable consequence. One of six checkpoints (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows no deficit, which the authors interpret as the theorem's premise (contraction) simply not holding for that model.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central novelty claims rest on a "closest prior result" whose citation is broken, and omit a closely related contemporaneous theoretical framework that makes essentially the same "structural, not accidental" argument for a different autoregressive-execution setting.
   **Where** — §5 Discussion ("The closest prior result is representational collapse [9]...Collapse-by-length predicts...Collapse-by-periodicity predicts..."); Reference list entry [9] ("Federico Barbero, Andrea Banino, Steven Kapturowski, et al., ,” .”); Introduction/Contributions ("to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact").
   **Why it matters** — [9] is almost certainly Barbero, Banino, Kapturowski et al., "Transformers need glasses! Information over-squashing in language tasks" [2406.04267], which already proves a representational-collapse phenomenon for decoder-only Transformers — that certain distinct input sequences become indistinguishable in the last-token representation used for next-token prediction. That is conceptually very close to what this paper's Theorem 1 delivers for TTS decoder states, yet the reference-list entry is empty (no title, no venue), so no reader can independently check the "collapse-by-length vs. collapse-by-periodicity" distinction the Discussion leans on to differentiate the two mechanisms. Separately, [2602.06413] ("Intrinsic Stability Limits of Autoregressive Reasoning: Structural Consequences for Long-Horizon Execution") argues, for LLM reasoning rather than TTS, that autoregressive breakdown over long horizons is intrinsic/structural rather than attributable to task complexity — the same rhetorical move this paper makes in its opening paragraph ("We argue that a large part of this behaviour has a simpler and more fundamental cause, and that the cause can be stated as a theorem"). It is not cited or discussed anywhere. Together, a malformed citation to the closest acknowledged precedent and a missing citation to an unacknowledged parallel precedent weaken the paper's claim to have identified a novel, TTS-specific mechanism rather than having re-derived a more general phenomenon in a new domain.
   **What would address it** — Repair the [9] reference with its actual title/venue and spell out precisely how over-squashing (information bottleneck through the residual stream as sequence length grows) differs mechanistically from attention-dilution-driven contraction (a periodicity-indexed argument); add a paragraph situating the paper against [2602.06413], clarifying what is specific to codec-language TTS versus what is a re-application of a general autoregressive-collapse argument.

2. **Issue** — The empirical evidence offered for Lemma 1 / Assumption 1 (attention dilution) is not available for a third of the panel, yet is generalized as panel-wide confirmation.
   **Where** — §4.3 ("Attention over the repeated spans is within a few percent of uniform, which is what Assumption 1 needs") versus §5 Limits ("text attention is unreachable where text and acoustic embeddings are additively fused (Qwen3-TTS)").
   **Why it matters** — If text attention cannot be measured for the Qwen3-TTS checkpoints, the entropy-slope and δ≤0.06-nats evidence in §4.3 cannot have been computed for them, so the sentence presenting it as validating Assumption 1 overstates its coverage. This matters concretely because Qwen3-TTS-0.6B is one of the "5 of 6" models reported as showing the predicted deficit (-8.3%); the paper never proposes what diluting mechanism (if not softmax attention dilution) makes its per-repetition update approximately autonomous. The causal story is underdetermined for exactly the models where it is hardest to check.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly scope the §4.3 attention-entropy results to the checkpoints where text attention is actually measurable, and either identify/test an alternative dilution mechanism for the additively-fused models or soften the "Assumption 1 holds across the panel" framing.

3. **Issue** — The theory is structured so that both outcomes (deficit present or absent) confirm it, because the contracting-map assumption it hinges on (Assumption 2, q) is never independently measured.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("The theorem is silent about such a model...we report it as a distinct regime rather than as a failed prediction"); §4.4 (direct estimation of q "needs a monotone text read-head" and fails); §5.
   **Why it matters** — With q unmeasured in every model, "the effect is present" (Assumption 2 holds) and "the theorem is silent" (Assumption 2 fails) are two labels applied post hoc to the same behavioral outcome, not independent checks. This is compounded by an unexplained non-monotonicity within the one true scale ladder in the panel: Llasa-8B (the largest Llasa checkpoint) has the worst deficit (-25.0%), worse than both smaller Llasa checkpoints, which sits awkwardly beside the Qwen3-TTS story where the larger checkpoint is the one without a deficit. The paper does not attempt to reconcile these two opposite scale trends under its own framework.
   **What would address it** — Provide an independently motivated proxy for q that does not derive from the count-error outcome itself (even an indirect one), or explicitly flag the circularity and reframe "5 of 6 models confirm" as a post hoc classification rather than a prediction the data independently corroborated. Discuss the Llasa within-family non-monotonicity directly.

4. **Issue** — The CTC recognizer adopted to replace the (demonstrably biased) Whisper judge is never itself validated against ground truth, despite the paper doing exactly that validation for the instrument it replaces.
   **Where** — §3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong."
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably rigorous about auditing Whisper (concatenative audio with known ground truth, counted/true ratio 0.27–0.65 at k≥4) and uses that audit to justify discarding it. But CTC decoding has its own structural repeat-collapsing behavior (consecutive identical labels merge unless separated by a blank), which is exactly the kind of periodicity-sensitive artifact the paper is trying to rule out in its judge. "No decoder, no language model" is true but doesn't establish the instrument is unbiased with respect to repeated acoustic material — that requires the same ground-truth audit the paper ran on Whisper, which is not reported for the CTC model. The paper's entire headline number (-8.3% median error) depends on this unaudited instrument.
   **What would address it** — Run the same concatenative-audio ground-truth audit on the CTC recognizer across the k ladder, for both repeated and length-matched control items, and report its counted/true ratio alongside Whisper's.

5. **Issue** — Two specific numerical claims in the results cannot be verified from the reported table/CI as printed.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("median error of -8.3% [−12.5, −8.3]"); Table 1 caption ("k∗ is the largest k with counting accuracy above 0.5") versus Table 1's actual columns (Par., count err., capacity gain — no k* column appears); §4.2 ("the collapse point differs between the two families by 1.7 repetitions while the token count there differs by 1.50×").
   **Why it matters** — A bootstrap interval whose upper bound equals the point estimate itself ([−12.5, −8.3] for a median of −8.3%) is either a typo, a one-sided interval reported without saying so, or a genuinely unusual bootstrap result that needs explanation — as printed it's ambiguous. More substantively, the specific figures used to argue against the "acoustic frame count" alternative explanation in §4.2 (1.7 repetitions, 1.5× tokens) rely on a k* quantity the table promises but does not display, so this comparison — one of the paper's stated sharpest tests — is not independently checkable from what's given.
   **What would address it** — Clarify or correct the CI notation; add the k* column to Table 1 (or a supplementary table breaking down k* per model) so the family-level horizon comparison in §4.2 can be checked against reported numbers.

### Minor concerns
- Lemma 1's stated bound, "e^{-δ/k} ≤ softmax(z)_i ≤ e^{δ/k}," is inconsistent with the subsequent difference formula "(e^δ − e^{-δ})/k" (which implies the intended bounds are e^{-δ}/k and e^{δ}/k, not exponents scaled by 1/k). This looks like a PDF/text-extraction artifact rather than a real error, but the camera-ready should confirm the typesetting is correct.
- Reference [1] is marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" yet is cited without qualification in the opening paragraph as documentation that repetition instability "is well documented as a stability problem"; a withdrawn preprint used as supporting evidence should be flagged or replaced.
- §4.2's naturalness-alternative check ("scored under a causal LM outside the panel, the control is the less probable member in 87% of the 54 matched pairs") doesn't name the LM or the scoring metric (perplexity? log-likelihood per token?) — needed for reproducibility since this result is used to rule out an alternative explanation.
- The Neff (effective rank) statistic in §3/§4.3 is introduced without specifying probe-layer selection, window length, or how per-item trajectories are pooled into the reported dNeff/d log k slopes.
- "the collapse point differs between the two families by 1.7 repetitions" (§4.2) is locally ambiguous — "families" elsewhere in the paper denotes model families (Llasa/XTTS/Qwen3-TTS), but here appears to mean stimulus classes (word- vs. sentence-repetition).
- The released repository name (github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing) doesn't match the paper's stated topic; worth confirming it's the intended, final link before publication.
- No statement on funding, conflicts of interest, or IP/ethics review, though this is likely a non-issue for a synthetic-audio study of this kind.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal artifact and the periodicity-vs-length control are genuine contributions, but the broken/missing related-work engagement, an unaudited replacement measurement instrument, coverage gaps in the attention-dilution evidence, and an unfalsifiable-by-construction treatment of the one dissenting model need to be fixed before the paper's novelty and causal claims can be taken at face value.
