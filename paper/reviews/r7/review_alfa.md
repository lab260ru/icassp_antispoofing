### Summary

The paper claims that autoregressive (AR) TTS models miscount repeated phrases because of a mechanism, not just a training artifact: if the per-repetition update of the decoder's hidden state is approximately autonomous (attention dilutes across repeated spans) and contracting, then no Lipschitz readout — including the stop head — can distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon. This conditional theorem and its supporting attention-dilution lemma are machine-checked in Lean 4. Empirically, six AR checkpoints across three families are run on a repetition ladder (k up to 32, later extended to 128 on four checkpoints) and scored by a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the AR bias of Whisper; models undercount repeated material (median error −8.3% at k≥6, 18.2% exact) but not length-matched distinct-word controls (94.3% exact), and a non-AR baseline (VITS) shows no such dissociation. The authors are explicit that the theorem's premise (contraction) was never directly measured — two attempts failed — and that a dose-response prediction of the dilution lemma came out sign-reversed, leaving the paper's contribution as a proved implication plus a periodicity-specific empirical deficit and horizon of the "predicted shape."

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's sharpest evidence for "periodicity, not length" is contaminated by a periodic control over most of the k-range where the effect is reported.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Stimuli": fillers "cycled above k = 8"); Section 4.3 (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match contrast, and the explicit admission "above k = 8 the control's fillers cycle a pool of eight, making that contrast period-1 against period-8").
   **Why it matters** — The dissociation is reported "at every k up to 32," but the deficit itself is negligible below k=6 and grows through k=12, 16, 24, 32 — precisely the range where, by the authors' own disclosure, the control is no longer non-repetitive but period-8. The only ranges where the authors confirm the effect survives with a genuinely non-cycled control are k≤8 (54.2% vs 77.9%, a far smaller gap than 18.2% vs 94.3%) and k≥48 in the separate extension ladder (a 146-word, never-cycled pool). No uncycled-control data is reported for k=12–32, which is exactly the interval driving the headline 94.3%/18.2% contrast. An adversarial reading is therefore available: the "periodicity vs. length" story may really be a "high-periodicity vs. lower-periodicity" story, and the paper has not ruled this out in the range that matters most.
   **What would address it** — Apply the extension ladder's uncycled ≥32-word filler pool (already built) to the original k=12–32 items, and report the exact-match / error contrast split by cycled vs. uncycled control at each k, rather than a single pooled figure.

2. **Issue** — The k=128 extension that produces the theorem's signature result (saturation, ˆK_rep 3.5-fold lower than ˆK_ctl) was added only after the pre-specified k≤32 ladder falsified the theorem's central prediction, with no pre-registration of the revised design.
   **Where** — Section 4.1 ("Within k ≤ 32 that deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts"); Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc, to k ∈ {48, 64, 96, 128} on the 4 checkpoints whose decoders admit utterances that long").
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook HARKing pattern: design a test, get a null/disconfirming result for the theorem's most distinctive prediction, then extend the design specifically to look for the missing signal, on a post-hoc-selected subset of models. The authors' own honesty about this sequence is commendable, but it does not remove the risk that the saturation curve fitted post hoc (ˆK a free parameter, on n=21–26 generations per cell after excluding truncated/degenerate items) is shaped by the same look-elsewhere process that motivated running it. Reporting it as "the horizon" rather than as an exploratory, unreplicated follow-up overstates its evidential weight.
   **What would address it** — Treat the k=128 result explicitly as exploratory; ideally replicate it as a pre-registered confirmatory test on an independent stimulus set (new carrier sentences/words) and, if possible, the two checkpoints excluded from the extension, before using the 3.5-fold figure as a headline number.

3. **Issue** — The decisive test against the "any synthesiser mishandles long repeated text" rival architecture relies on a single non-AR model (VITS) with no stated seed replication, unlike the 3-seed AR panel.
   **Where** — Section 4.3, paragraph beginning "A fourth rival is architectural..."; the paper itself flags "VITS is smaller than the panel, so only the within-model dissociation is compared."
   **Why it matters** — The claim that Assumption 1 has "no recurrence to apply to" in non-AR synthesis, and that this is why VITS shows "no dissociation" (65.6% vs 58.9%, −6.7 points), is used to settle a structural boundary condition of the theory. A single architecture, run with an unspecified number of seeds, is a thin basis for a "the effect is AR-specific" conclusion — VITS could differ from the AR panel for reasons unrelated to autoregression (duration modelling, training data, vocoder artifacts). This is exactly the kind of claim that needs a second, architecturally distinct non-AR system (e.g., a flow-matching or diffusion-based TTS) to be convincing.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one more non-AR baseline with the same seed count as the AR panel, and report per-seed variance for VITS so the −6.7-point gap can be judged against noise.

4. **Issue** — The circularity check for the capacity-gain result ("restricting to correctly rendered items") uses two subsamples with a massive, structurally different survival imbalance (94.3% of controls vs. 18.2% of repeated items), which the authors themselves flag as "not like-for-like," yet the resulting adjusted ratio (0.52) is still reported as supporting evidence.
   **Where** — Section 4.4, paragraph on "the obvious objection is circularity."
   **Why it matters** — If only 18.2% of repeated-item generations survive the "correctly rendered" filter, that subsample is almost certainly the easiest, least-repetitive-feeling cases for the repeated condition, while the control subsample (94.3% surviving) is close to the full distribution. Comparing effective-rank growth between two such differently-selected samples does not isolate the causal factor (repetition per se) from a selection effect (which items happened to be rendered correctly), so the 0.50–0.52 "corrected" ratios cannot be interpreted as cleanly as the text implies.
   **What would address it** — Use matched or stratified comparison (e.g., propensity-matching on realized output length/diversity) rather than a simple correctness filter, or explicitly bound how much of the residual gap could be attributed to the selection asymmetry.

5. **Issue** — The unit of analysis shifts between reported statistics without a consistent rule, risking pseudoreplication in the headline numbers.
   **Where** — Section 4.1 (median error −8.3% [−12.5,−6.2], n = 457, apparently pooling items/seeds/models) vs. Section 4.2 ("we take the checkpoint as the unit of replication, reducing each to its own repeated-minus-control difference," n = 6, then n = 3 by family).
   **Why it matters** — Pooling hundreds of generations from 6 checkpoints × 3 seeds into a single n = 457 confidence interval treats correlated, non-independent observations (multiple items from the same model/seed) as independent, which understates the true uncertainty of the −8.3% figure quoted in the abstract and Section 4.1 — even though the authors later demonstrate, in the very next subsection, that they know this matters (by switching to checkpoint- and family-level units for the exact-rate comparison). The paper does not explain why the headline number in 4.1 was not computed the same, more conservative way.
   **What would address it** — Report the abstract/Section 4.1 headline statistic using a model that accounts for clustering (e.g., mixed-effects with model/seed as random effects, or the same checkpoint-level aggregation used later), and state explicitly which unit of analysis backs each reported CI.

6. **Issue** — Tests designed to probe (and potentially falsify) the theorem's own premises are small and underpowered, yet their null results are reported as substantive "disconfirmation" without any power analysis.
   **Where** — Section 4.4 (dose-response: r = 0.59 in "23 cells," disconfirming Lemma 1's prediction); Section 5 ("a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction... 7 of 18 pairs, p = 0.12").
   **Why it matters** — An n = 18-pair test with p = 0.12 is entirely consistent with a real but modest difference in local contraction between repeated and control items going undetected for lack of power; treating it as evidence that Assumption 2 does not differentially hold is an absence-of-evidence/evidence-of-absence conflation. This matters more than usual here because the theorem's premise (contraction) is never positively established anywhere in the paper — this underpowered null is one of only two attempts, and it is being used to help calibrate how much weight to put on the theorem at all.
   **What would address it** — Report the minimum detectable effect size for the n=18 perturbation probe, and/or increase the sample before treating the p=0.12 result as informative rather than merely inconclusive.

7. **Issue** — Numerous claims the reader cannot verify are deferred to a supplement not included in the manuscript text under review (S4, S5, S7, S8, S10, S11, S12).
   **Where** — Throughout (e.g., δ ≤ 1.23 nats "S4"; perturbation-probe linearity control "S5"; repetition-penalty ablation "S7"; rival explanations "improbability and an acoustic attractor" "S8"; stimulus/exclusion details "S10"; significance-testing floor "S11"; dose-response measures "S12").
   **Why it matters** — Several of the concerns above (stimulus construction, exclusion criteria, the ablation's model coverage, the exact multiplicity-correction procedure) hinge on details that are gestured at but not shown. A reviewer following the "experimental design" lens cannot fully evaluate blinding, exclusion rules, or pre-specification without seeing S10 and S11 in particular.
   **What would address it** — Since this review only had the main text, this is flagged as a gap in the evaluated materials; the supplement should be made available alongside the main paper for review, and the main text should summarize (not just cite) the exclusion and stopping rules central to concerns 1–2 above.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the formal contribution and the paper's unusual candor about disconfirming results are genuine strengths, but the headline "periodicity, not length" contrast is confounded by a periodic control over most of its own reported k-range, the theorem-saving k=128 result follows a HARKing pattern with a single non-AR baseline standing in for architecture-generality, and several supporting statistics mix units of analysis or lean on underpowered null tests — all fixable with re-analysis of data the authors appear to already have, but not yet fixed.
