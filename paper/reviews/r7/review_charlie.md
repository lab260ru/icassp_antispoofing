# Review

## Summary
The paper argues that autoregressive TTS decoders undercount repeated phrases because softmax attention "dilutes" over near-identical repeated spans (Lemma 1) and, if the resulting per-repetition state update contracts (Assumption 2), no Lipschitz readout — including the stop head — can recover the repetition count past a bound N* (Theorem 1), both machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six checkpoints across three model families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B), scored by a CTC recognizer chosen because Whisper is itself shown to be repetition-biased, undercount repeated material (median −8.3% at k≥6) while length-matched non-repeating controls stay near-exact; a non-autoregressive baseline (VITS) shows no such dissociation. A post-hoc extension to k=128 finds saturation consistent with the theorem's shape, but two direct attempts to measure the contraction factor q failed, and a dose-response test of the dilution mechanism came out sign-reversed. The authors present this candidly as "what is proved" (an implication) versus "what is measured" (a periodicity-specific deficit and a horizon of the predicted shape), not a confirmation of the theorem's premise.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — A load-bearing Discussion claim ("a probe finds the count degraded, not erased") is reported with zero inline methodology, sample size, or supplementary pointer, unlike every neighboring claim in the same paragraph.
   **Where** — Section 5, Discussion, the sentence "Consistent with that, the state budget stays positive while growing at 0.46 of the control rate, and a probe finds the count degraded, not erased (0.97 against 1.11)." The adjacent sentences in the same paragraph and the preceding one both carry `(S5)` citations; this one carries none, in either the given text or the source file it comes from (`discussion_body.tex`).
   **Why it matters** — This number is doing real argumentative work: it is the paper's evidence that the theorem's "erasure" prediction is only partially borne out (degradation, not erasure), which is central to how the paper frames its own honesty about disconfirmation. A reader of the 4-page paper cannot tell what was probed (which layer, which readout class, how "retention" is defined, on how many items) or judge whether 0.97 vs 1.11 is a meaningful gap or noise. Cross-checking the released supplementary source shows this is a leave-one-template-out ridge probe of log₂k over early/late trajectory thirds (n≈54 items/model, panel median over the six checkpoints) — a substantive, checkable procedure that simply isn't cited from the point where it's used.
   **What would address it** — Add the missing `(S#)` pointer and state n and the retention definition in one clause at first use, matching the disclosure standard already applied to every other quantitative claim in the paper.

2. **Issue** — Two of the paper's central robustness analyses silently narrow from "the panel" (6 checkpoints, 3 families) to a subset, and the main text does not disclose this.
   **Where** — Section 4.4, the dose-response test ("items with flatter attention are counted better, not worse, r = 0.59 ..., 23 cells, all 3 measures agreeing") and the circularity check ("covarying out realised output diversity leaves the ratio at 0.50").
   **Why it matters** — The released per-model implementation notes state plainly that Qwen3-TTS's architecture fuses text and codec embeddings before self-attention, so "there is no column of the self-attention matrix that corresponds to 'the text'" — Qwen3-TTS is therefore structurally absent from every attention-based measurement, including the dose-response test that is the paper's chief piece of evidence *against* Lemma 1's item-level role. Separately, the covariate-adjusted capacity-gain regression (the paper's rebuttal to the "it's just circular monotony" objection) is only estimable for the Llasa family, because XTTS-v2 and Qwen3-TTS don't persist per-token identities needed for the type/token-ratio and repeat-rate covariates — so the "ratio at 0.50" defense covers half the families in the panel, not all three. Both facts change how much these checks actually generalize the paper's mechanism story across "every checkpoint and every family," a phrase used elsewhere in the paper.
   **What would address it** — State the analysis-specific coverage inline next to each number (e.g., "23 cells, 4 checkpoints, Qwen3-TTS architecturally excluded"; "adjusted ratio 0.50, Llasa only, n=3"), and discuss whether the excluded families are expected to differ.

3. **Issue** — Universal-quantifier language in the headline claim is not supported by a 6-point, non-significance-tested panel that contains an explicit dissenting case.
   **Where** — Abstract ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family") and Section 4.3.
   **Why it matters** — Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows 0.0% median count error at k≥6 (Table 1), no saturation at k≤128, and an *inverted* repeated/control ratio in the extension ladder (Section 4.2) — i.e., on the paper's own numbers this checkpoint does not show the phenomenon the abstract says holds "in every checkpoint." The paper itself acknowledges the panel is underpowered for significance testing at n=6 (the exact signed-rank floor at n=6 is 0.031, uncorrectable below 0.05) and reduces to "the effective replicate count is nearer 3" at the family level. Combining an admittedly-underpowered design with "every checkpoint and every family" phrasing in the abstract overstates what the evidence licenses; a skeptical reader could reasonably read the whole panel as "5 of 6 checkpoints, weighted toward the Llasa family, show the effect," which is a materially weaker claim.
   **What would address it** — Soften the universal claim to match the reported effect-size language used correctly elsewhere in the results (e.g., "positive in 5 of 6 checkpoints, all 3 families") and keep it consistent between the abstract and the body.

4. **Issue** — The judge-validity claim that licenses replacing Whisper with a CTC recognizer is built on a small, synthetic, single-voice ground-truth set that the main text does not describe at all.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Behavioural measurement": "Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth, its counted/true ratio falls to 0.27–0.65 for k ≥ 4 ... ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper."
   **Why it matters** — This single audit is the entire justification for the paper's most consequential methodological pivot (every downstream number in the paper depends on trusting the CTC judge over Whisper). The main text gives no n, no description of what "known ground truth" audio is, and no acknowledgment of any construction artifact. The released supplementary material shows the ground truth is six individually-verified k=1 clips (one per carrier template, all from a single XTTS-v2 voice) concatenated with inserted 0.25s silences between repeats — a small, synthetic, single-speaker, artificially-segmented test. A perfect 1.00 ratio on audio with explicit silence gaps between repeats is a much easier segmentation problem for a CTC recognizer than the continuous, natural-cadence repeated speech being scored throughout the rest of the paper; the audit's near-perfect score does not by itself establish that the CTC judge is unbiased on the harder, gap-free real generations. (The VITS same-judge control in Section 4.3 offers a partial, indirect defense against this — but it doesn't substitute for describing the audit's actual construction.)
   **What would address it** — State n, voice count, and the silence-gap construction inline (or explicitly flag it as a known limitation), and ideally extend the audit to gap-free concatenations or genuinely independent human-recorded ground truth before leaning on "ratio 1.00" as strongly as the paper does.

5. **Issue** — Reproduction cost and friction are materially understated relative to what the released repository shows.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Code, data, settings": "Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB) are available on request and regenerable from the released seeds."
   **Why it matters** — "On request" is not an archival guarantee (mail lapses, personal drives get replaced); the released README further shows these artifacts live at a single non-institutional local mount path, not a versioned public store (e.g., Zenodo, HF datasets). Separately, the README states that regenerating results requires **four mutually incompatible conda environments**, each pinned for compatibility reasons the README itself calls out as unresolvable in one environment — a substantial reproduction barrier that is not mentioned anywhere in the submitted paper text, only in the code repository.
   **What would address it** — Deposit audio/activations (or a representative subset sufficient to re-run the judge audit and Table 1) in an archival, DOI-bearing location, and add one sentence to the paper noting the multi-environment build requirement so reviewers/readers can budget for it.

6. **Issue** — The entire stimulus set is English-only, despite testing a model marketed as "massively multilingual" (XTTS-v2) and framing the mechanism as a property of softmax attention that should be language-agnostic.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Stimuli" (carrier sentences, number phrases, tongue twisters — all English); Section 3, "Models" (XTTS-v2 cited via [9], "massively multilingual zero-shot").
   **Why it matters** — If attention dilution over repeated spans (Lemma 1) is the real driver, the dissociation should be roughly language-invariant, and testing that would be a cheap, sharp corroborating (or falsifying) check — exactly the kind of test the paper's own falsification criteria in Section 5 gesture toward but do not include. Its absence leaves open whether the periodicity/length dissociation is an artifact of English morphology/tokenization (e.g., subword segmentation of repeated English number-words) rather than a general attention/decoder property, which matters directly for how far the "no property of the decoding rule enters" claim in Section 2 can be trusted to generalize.
   **What would address it** — Add even one non-English language ladder (ideally on XTTS-v2, which already supports it) as a generalization check, or explicitly scope the claims to English in the abstract/limitations.

## Minor concerns
- Citation [1] is marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" and is nonetheless cited for its alignment analysis with no comment on why it was withdrawn or whether that affects reliance on it — either replace it or add a one-clause caveat.
- The main text's sampling-hyperparameters sentence (Section 3) lists decoding parameters for XTTS-v2 and Llasa but omits Qwen3-TTS's entirely, even though "the penalty is the one parameter we vary" implies all baselines should be stated for the ablation to be interpretable.
- The `(S#)` cross-references in the main text (e.g., `(S10)`, `(S11)`) are typed as literal strings rather than compiled `\ref` pointers in the released supplementary source; the project's own revision history shows supplementary sections have been renumbered more than once. Currently consistent (checked), but structurally fragile — a future reorder could silently desync a main-text citation from its target with no compile error.
- Fig. 1(b)'s k-axis for the extension ladder is captioned around powers of two (2^6, 2^7) even though the added points (48, 96) are not powers of two; worth double-checking the camera-ready tick labeling isn't implicitly misleading readers into thinking the ladder is log2-uniform there.
- Self-citation [7] (the "companion" spectral-hallucination paper) is dated in the same 2026 cycle as this submission and is leaned on for framing ("identified an attractor regime descriptively"); its own review/publication status isn't stated, which matters when a submission cites a concurrent, seemingly unpublished sibling work as established motivation.
- Section 4.2's line "the same checkpoint that shows no deficit at k ≤ 32, a conditional theorem behaving conditionally" reframes a disconfirming data point (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) with language that reads more exculpatory than the numbers alone support (see Major #3); consider stating the exception with the same neutral weight given to the confirmatory cases.
- No conflict-of-interest or ethics statement is present in the given text; likely a venue-formatting omission rather than an oversight, but worth confirming it's included in the camera-ready per ICASSP requirements.

## Verdict
**Major revision** — the theorem, its Lean formalization, and the honest reporting of negative results (failed q measurement, sign-reversed dose-response test) are a genuine and unusually candid contribution, but as currently written several headline and Discussion claims (Major #1–#4) present subset-scope or thinly-documented results with panel-wide or unqualified framing that the released supplementary material itself does not support at that scope, and these are all concretely fixable with inline disclosure rather than new experiments.
