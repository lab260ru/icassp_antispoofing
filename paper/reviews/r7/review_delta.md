### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech decoders miscount repeated words or phrases, and proposes a mechanistic account: if softmax attention over k repeated spans dilutes to weight Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1) and the resulting per-repetition state update F contracts (Assumption 2), then no Lipschitz readout of decoder state — including the stop-token logit — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib with a reported zero-`sorry`, three-standard-axiom closure. Empirically, six checkpoints across three families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B), scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically because Whisper's own AR decoder is shown to be repetition-biased, undercount repeated material (median error −8.3% at k≥6, exact-match 18.2%) while length-matched non-repeating controls are essentially exact (94.3%) — a dissociation the paper argues tracks periodicity rather than length, and which vanishes in a non-AR baseline (VITS). Within k≤32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating (contrary to the theorem's predicted horizon); a post-hoc extension to k=128 on 4 of the 6 checkpoints finds saturation, with the repeated-side fitted horizon 3.5× lower than controls, except in one checkpoint that shows no deficit at any scale. The paper is unusually candid that the theorem's premise (contraction) was never directly confirmed — two attempts to measure q failed, and the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction reversed sign — and frames its contribution as a validated implication plus a suggestively-shaped but mechanistically unconfirmed measurement.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The artifacts that ground every behavioural number in the paper (generated audio and hidden-state activations) are not actually released; they are "available on request" and said to be regenerable from released seeds.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Code, data, settings" ("Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB) are available on request and regenerable from the released seeds").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's entire evidentiary chain runs through an ASR judge's transcript of generated audio, and Section 3 spends a full paragraph justifying why the CTC judge is trustworthy where Whisper is not. That argument can only be independently checked by listening to specific renders and comparing them against specific transcripts. With the audio itself gated behind an off-repo request (and, per the project's own repository layout, symlinked from a private host path rather than packaged for distribution), a reader cannot spot-check a single "exact" vs. "off-by-one" vs. "degenerate" classification without first reconstructing four mutually-incompatible conda environments and downloading ~74 GB of checkpoints to regenerate it themselves. That is not independent verification, it is independent replication of the whole pipeline — a much higher bar, and one most reviewers/readers will not clear.
   **What would address it** — Release a stratified, citable sample (e.g., 50–100 clips spanning k, model, and correct/incorrect/degenerate outcomes) directly with the paper via a persistent archive (Zenodo, HF Datasets), not "on request," so that the judge's calibration claims in Section 3 are checkable without a full re-run.

2. **Issue** — The causal chain linking the theorem to the data is unconfirmed at every intermediate step, yet the title and abstract present the "Lean-verified bound" and the "measured counting horizon" as a single coordinated finding.
   **Where** — Title; Abstract; Section 4.4 (dose-response reversal, r=0.59 in the wrong direction); Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... its linearity control failing too").
   **Why it matters** — Lemma 1 (attention dilution) is the paper's proposed bridge from "periodic text" to "F is approximately autonomous," and Assumption 2 (contraction) is what turns that into a finite horizon. Section 4.4 reports that the dilution lemma's own dose-response prediction is falsified in the observed direction (flatter attention correlates with *better* counting, not worse), and Section 5 reports that both direct attempts to estimate q failed outright. That leaves zero confirmed links between the proved implication and the measured deficit: what remains is a periodicity-specific undercount plus a horizon-shaped saturation curve, which is *consistent with* the theorem but does not confirm it, since the paper itself declines to derive the k=128 saturation scale from any measured q ("not one derived from a measured q, locating these decoders just below the collapse regime rather than confirming the bound," Section 5). The paper's body text is careful about this distinction, but the title's "AND" and the abstract's back-to-back listing of the theorem and the measurement invite the conflation the authors elsewhere warn against.
   **What would address it** — Either (a) retitle/re-lead the abstract to make explicit that the measured horizon is offered as *consistent with*, not *derived from*, the proved bound, or (b) invest in a third, better-motivated attempt to measure q (the paper only reports two failed estimators) before claiming the two halves of the title as a joint result.

3. **Issue** — The post-hoc k=128 extension, which is the only place the paper's saturating-horizon claim is observed at all, is run on "the 4 checkpoints whose decoders admit utterances that long," without stating which 4 or the selection criterion.
   **Where** — Section 4.2, absent detail; Table 1 reports full-panel k≤32 statistics for all 6 but the k=128 numbers appear only piecemeal in prose (Llasa-1B and Llasa-8B named as having disjoint intervals; Qwen3-TTS-1.7B named as the exception).
   **Why it matters** — A reader cannot determine whether the two excluded checkpoints were dropped for a reason independent of the effect under study (e.g., a fixed context-length ceiling) or for a reason entangled with it (e.g., a model that saturates earliest also exhausts its budget soonest and so never reaches k=128 at all). Section 4.2 already reports that 20/240 extension-ladder generations "hit our budget" and were excluded as conservative (censored downward) — which shows the paper is alert to budget-censoring within the kept checkpoints, but says nothing about whether budget considerations also drove which checkpoints were kept.
   **What would address it** — Name the 4 checkpoints and the quantitative admission criterion in the main text or a table, and report (or bound) how the 2 excluded checkpoints behaved at whatever k they could reach, so the selection can be shown not to correlate with the horizon effect.

4. **Issue** — The panel's headline "median count error of −8.3%" (Abstract, Section 4.1) is an item-pooled statistic (n=457) that disagrees with the checkpoint-level median computable from the paper's own Table 1.
   **Where** — Abstract; Section 4.1; Table 1 ("count err. k≥6 (%) rep." column: −12.5, −8.3, −16.7, −12.5, −12.5, 0.0 for the six checkpoints).
   **Why it matters** — The median of those six per-checkpoint values is −12.5%, not −8.3% (the value coincides only with the single Llasa-3B row). Section 4.2 explicitly argues that "the checkpoint [is] the unit of replication" for exactly this kind of aggregation ("Because the claim is about models, not about our corpus, we take the checkpoint as the unit of replication, reducing each to its own repeated-minus-control difference"), which is the right instinct — but that principle is applied to the exact-match statistic in 4.2 while the headline error statistic in 4.1 and the Abstract instead pools across items/seeds, implicitly weighting checkpoints by however many items/seeds survived exclusion for each. The two aggregation choices give materially different pictures of central tendency (a 50%-relative gap) and the paper does not flag or reconcile the discrepancy.
   **What would address it** — Report both the item-pooled and checkpoint-level statistics side by side wherever a headline number is quoted, and use the checkpoint-level one (already argued to be the principled choice in 4.2) as the number that leads the Abstract.

5. **Issue** — Section 3 discloses generation hyperparameters for XTTS-v2 and Llasa but not for Qwen3-TTS.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Code, data, settings": "Sampling uses shipped defaults (XTTS-v2 T=0.75, top-p 0.85, top-k 50, penalty 5.0; Llasa T=0.8, top-p 1.0); the penalty is the one parameter we vary."
   **Why it matters** — Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is the single most load-bearing exception in the paper — the checkpoint with no k≤32 deficit and no k=128 saturation, cited repeatedly (Sections 4.2, 4.4, 5) as evidence the theorem is "conditional... behaving conditionally" rather than false. Without its decoding temperature/top-p/top-k on record, a reader cannot rule out that its null result reflects unusually conservative (near-deterministic) sampling defaults for that family rather than a genuine architectural or scale-driven exception — a confound the paper is well-positioned to rule out (it already varies penalty as a targeted ablation elsewhere) but does not address for this specific model.
   **What would address it** — State Qwen3-TTS's sampling parameters alongside the other two families, and note whether they differ systematically in effective stochasticity.

6. **Issue** — The one experiment that directly tests the theorem's claim of decoding-rule indifference (part iv: "No property of the decoding rule enters, so the bound covers greedy, sampled and beam decoding alike") — a repetition-penalty sweep — is run on a single checkpoint out of six.
   **Where** — Section 5, "Implications and limits": "a fourfold sweep of XTTS-v2's repetition penalty moves it the wrong way (S7)"; Section 3 lists "further XTTS-v2 runs at altered repetition penalty as ablations" and notes they are "neither... pooled into the panel."
   **Why it matters** — This is a genuinely interesting disconfirming-leaning result (penalty moving the count the "wrong way") that bears directly on whether standard AR-decoding mitigations act on the mechanism the paper proposes, but with n=1 checkpoint it cannot support the panel-wide claims the Discussion draws from it, and it is not clear whether XTTS-v2 was chosen for this ablation because it is representative or because of practical convenience.
   **What would address it** — Extend the penalty sweep to at least one checkpoint per family (as done for the main behavioural measurement) before using it to support a general claim about decoding-rule indifference.

### Minor concerns

- Table 1's "capacity gain ... measured at deep layers" (caption) does not say which layers, how many, or how they were selected; this is a load-bearing measurement (Section 4.4's central quantitative claim) reported with less specificity than the count-error metric.
- The stated panel-wide "factor 0.46" capacity-gain ratio (Section 4.4) is not the simple mean of Table 1's six per-checkpoint ratios (0.32, 0.21, 0.33, 0.69, 0.60, 0.80 → mean ≈0.49); if 0.46 is a ratio-of-means or otherwise weighted figure, say so.
- Reference [1] is marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" yet is still used to frame the introductory motivation ("cited for its alignment analysis"); a withdrawn preprint is a fragile foundation for framing even if only its analysis, not its conclusions, is being used — a brief note on why the withdrawal doesn't affect the cited analysis would help.
- The Discussion's "closest prior result" comparison (Section 5, vs. [12]) and its treatment of a "competing account" (vs. [18]) each cite a single paper; for a claim as central as "to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact" (Section 1), the related-work coverage is thin and would benefit from a more systematic search rather than one representative citation per adjacent subfield.
- Section 4.4: "rendering very sixteen times really does produce monotonous audio" appears to be a typo (likely "the word sixteen times").
- Figure 1(a)/(b) axes are given in what appear to be powers of two (2⁰...2⁵, 2⁶...2⁷) without an explicit log-scale label in the reproduced caption text.
- The claim that Whisper's counted/true ratio is "0.27–0.65 for k≥4" (Section 3) is demonstrated on "concatenative audio with known ground truth" — i.e., synthetic splicing, not genuine model output — which is a reasonable calibration design but should be named as such rather than left implicit, since it is a different distribution from the panel's actual generations.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core periodicity-vs-length dissociation and the formal artifact appear solid, but the paper needs full artifact release or a checkable sample, disclosure of the extension-ladder's checkpoint-selection criterion and Qwen3-TTS's hyperparameters, and reconciliation of its two conflicting "median error" statistics before its reproducibility and claim-calibration meet publication standard.
