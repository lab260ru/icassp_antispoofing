# Review — Reviewer bravo

### Summary

The paper makes three linked claims: (1) a Lean-verified theorem that *if* an AR TTS decoder's per-repetition state update is a contraction (Assumption 2, never confirmed), then no Lipschitz readout can distinguish repetition counts beyond a computable horizon; (2) an empirical finding that six checkpoints across three model families undercount repeated phrases (median −8.3% relative error, 18.2% exact) while nearly perfectly rendering length-matched non-periodic controls (94.3% exact) — periodicity, not length, predicts the failure; and (3) a post-hoc extension to k=128 that finds the count does eventually saturate, at a 3.5-fold lower scale for repeated vs. control text. The paper is unusually candid about what it hasn't shown: both direct attempts to measure the theorem's key premise (the contraction factor q) failed, and the dose-response test of the companion attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed. That honesty is a genuine strength and makes this a harder paper to red-team than most. My main reservations are that the title/abstract's framing ("Lean-verified bound *and* a measured counting horizon") invites a stronger reading of mechanistic confirmation than the body delivers, and that the paper's cleanest architectural claim — that recurrence, not general capability, explains the periodicity dissociation — rests on a single, old, and (on the paper's own numbers) much weaker non-AR baseline.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The title and abstract foreground the Lean-verified theorem and a "measured counting horizon" as if they corroborate each other, when the body explicitly says they don't. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Title; Abstract ("The theorem and a companion attention-dilution lemma are machine-checked..."); contrast with §5: "Extending to k=128 found saturation of the predicted kind but not one derived from a measured q, locating these decoders just below the collapse regime rather than confirming the bound."
   **Why it matters** — Lean verification certifies an algebraic consequence of Assumptions 1–2; it says nothing about whether real decoders satisfy those assumptions. A reader who stops at the abstract could easily walk away thinking the horizon N* was measured and matches theory, when what was measured is a generic curve-fit saturation whose scale is not tied to any estimated q.
   **What would address it** — State plainly, in the abstract itself (not just the last sentence, which does gesture at this but reads as a hedge rather than a headline), that formal verification covers only the implication, and that the empirical "horizon" is a fitted saturation scale, not a validation of N*.

2. **Issue** — The paper's own dose-response test of Lemma 1 (attention dilution) is sign-reversed: flatter attention correlates with *better* counting (r=0.59 [0.35, 0.75]), not worse. `[Evidence — distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — §4.4, last paragraph; also referenced in §5.
   **Why it matters** — This isn't merely "unconfirmed" — it's evidence against the specific causal chain the paper builds (dilution → approximately autonomous map → contraction → miscount). The paper calls this "disconfirming," which is honest, but the introduction and theorem section keep using dilution as the load-bearing mechanism. If peaked attention is symptom, not cause (their own alternative: "peaked attention instead follows from a stalled decoder"), the theoretical story motivating Assumption 1 needs to be presented as considerably weaker than the confident derivation in §2 suggests.
   **What would address it** — Either find the mediating variable that does show the predicted dose-response, or reframe Lemma 1 as a plausible-but-unconfirmed mechanism throughout, not just in the discussion's closing paragraph.

3. **Issue** — The headline 3.5-fold saturation-scale reduction (K̂_rep=30 vs K̂_ctl=105) is a single pooled fit across only 4 checkpoints, one of which individually shows the *opposite* pattern. `[Evidence — statistical rigour / pooling]`
   **Where** — §4.2 ("K̂_rep=30 [24,46] against K̂_ctl=105 [94,114]... Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation within k≤128 and inverts the ratio").
   **Why it matters** — A pooled point estimate from n=4, with one discordant case, is fragile; it isn't clear the "3.5-fold" number describes any actual checkpoint rather than being an artefact of how the pooled curve-fit averages over heterogeneous behaviour. This is compounded by a genuinely tiny per-cell N (21–26 generations after exclusions) feeding a nonlinear least-squares fit.
   **What would address it** — Report the per-checkpoint K̂_rep/K̂_ctl fits alongside the pooled one (the way Table 1 already does for count error), so readers can see how much of the 3.5× is carried by 3 checkpoints vs. diluted by the fourth.

4. **Issue** — The paper motivates itself by citing several published AR-decoder repetition fixes (attention guidance [1], alignment-guided reordering [2], CoT prompting [3], streaming alignment [4]) and predicts in the Discussion that effective mitigations should raise q or the readout margin — but the only mitigation actually tested is a repetition-penalty sweep on one checkpoint, and it moves the wrong way. `[Evidence — baselines, missing mitigation test]`
   **Where** — §1 ("fixes proposed at the level of attention supervision or decoding rules [3,4]"); §5 ("Mitigations act late... a fourfold sweep of XTTS-v2's repetition penalty moves it the wrong way (S7)").
   **Why it matters** — Repetition penalty is a sampling-time logit adjustment, not the training/alignment-level fix the cited literature actually proposes. Testing only the weakest, least on-theory intervention, on a single model, and reporting a negative result without further investigation, leaves the paper's most actionable claim ("this predicts what should and shouldn't help") essentially untested against any real competing method.
   **What would address it** — Run at least one of the cited alignment-supervision or CoT-style fixes on the ladder. This would double as an indirect test of Assumption 2, sidestepping the fact that direct q-measurement failed twice.

5. **Issue** — VITS is the sole non-autoregressive comparator, and on the paper's own numbers it is a substantially weaker model overall, not just a differently-architected one, which confounds the paper's central "architecture explains the dissociation" claim. `[Evidence — baselines]` **(primary axis)**
   **Where** — §4.3, "A fourth rival is architectural": "It shows no dissociation: 65.6% of repeated items exact against 58.9% of controls... VITS is smaller than the panel."
   **Why it matters** — VITS (Kim et al., 2021) is an older, much smaller, single non-scaled model whose *control*-item accuracy (58.9%) is dramatically below the AR panel's control accuracy (94.3%). That gap means VITS is failing broadly, not just failing to show the specific periodicity/length dissociation — a low, possibly floor-effected accuracy on both arms can produce a small gap (−6.7 points) for reasons that have nothing to do with the absence of recurrence (e.g., VITS may simply not encode long conditioning text reliably at all, repeated or not). The paper explicitly frames this result as "leaving Assumption 1 no recurrence to apply to," i.e., as an architectural explanation — but a single weak, dated model can't cleanly isolate "no recurrence" from "lower overall capability" as the explanation for "no dissociation." This is the paper's most novelty-relevant empirical claim (it's presented as ruling out the "any synthesiser mishandles long repeated text" rival, §4.3) and it hangs on the weakest baseline in the paper.
   **What would address it** — Add a second, more competitive non-AR TTS system closer in quality/scale to the AR panel (a modern flow-matching or diffusion-based synthesiser, ideally one built on a comparable codec/tokenizer to keep the comparison as apples-to-apples as an architectural contrast requires). Absent that, the VITS result should be presented as suggestive, not as "settling" the architectural rival, and the control-accuracy gap between VITS and the AR panel should be discussed as a confound rather than left as an aside ("VITS is smaller than the panel, so only the within-model dissociation is compared" doesn't fully address the floor-effect concern raised above).

### Minor concerns

- **Cherry-picking check** produced nothing to flag — the paper presents no qualitative audio examples at all (hand-picked or otherwise); it's fully quantitative. That's defensible, but it also means a reader can't sanity-check by ear what a "collapsed" vs. "exact" rendering actually sounds like from the paper itself.
- **Alternative explanation not addressed**: a learned anti-repetition bias from text/audio pretraining (distinct from geometric state-forgetting, and distinct from the "improbability" and "acoustic attractor" rivals named in S8) — i.e., models may simply have learned to down-weight literal verbatim repeats, à la neural text degeneration [5,6], independent of any contraction dynamics. The repetition-penalty ablation touches this only obliquely (it's a decoding-time knob, not a claim about the trained distribution).
- Table 1's per-checkpoint capacity-gain ratio column averages to ≈0.49 across the six models, marginally different from the "factor 0.46" quoted in §4.4 — likely a ratio-of-means vs. mean-of-ratios artefact, worth a one-line clarification.
- The binary "exact match" metric is acknowledged to be noisy at the boundary ("flips on ±1 of slop," §3) yet is still the headline contrast in the abstract (18.2% vs. 94.3%); a slack-tolerant version (e.g., ±1) reported alongside would show whether the gap survives reasonable tolerance.
- Reference [1] is cited despite being marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026," with no note on why, or whether the withdrawal bears on the "alignment analysis" borrowed from it.
- Reference [7] is a self-citation to a contemporaneous companion paper by the same authors (arXiv:2604.08591) that a reader has limited independent means to evaluate at review time; leaning on it for the "attractor regime" framing in the intro is fine but worth flagging as unverified-by-a-third-party context.
- The GitHub repo given for code/data is named `lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing`, which doesn't match this paper's topic — likely a shared/repurposed repo, but worth fixing for reader navigation.
- Audio (2.5GB) and activations (25GB) are "available on request" rather than hosted — a soft reproducibility gap for the state-measurement claims in §4.4, which are otherwise central to the paper's evidence.
- No language is stated for the stimuli ladder; given multilingual checkpoints (XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS) are in the panel, it's unclear whether "across three families" claims hold beyond English.
- Figure 1(a)'s y-axis as transcribed ("100 50 0 count error (%)") is hard to parse for sign/direction from the text alone; worth double-checking the rendered figure has an unambiguous zero-line and signed axis.

### Belief update

The periodicity-vs-length dissociation (§4.3) is a genuinely well-designed distinguishing test, and it's the strongest thing in the paper — it moved me toward crediting "AR TTS repetition undercounting is periodicity-specific, not a generic length effect" as more likely true than I'd have guessed before reading. I did *not* update much on the dynamical-systems/contraction story as the actual mechanism: the two direct attempts to measure the premise failed, and the mechanistic sub-prediction (dilution dose-response) was disconfirmed in direction. The Lean formalization, while a nice piece of craft, doesn't move that needle either — it de-risks the algebra, not the physics. Net effect: modest upward update on the phenomenon, negligible update on the proposed explanation for it.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the paper's most novelty-bearing empirical claim (architecture, not general capability, explains the dissociation) rests on a single outdated, floor-effected non-AR baseline, and until a second, competitive non-AR comparator is added, the "architectural" framing in §4.3 and the title's implicit claim to have located a real dynamical mechanism outrun what the evidence, by the authors' own honest accounting, supports.
