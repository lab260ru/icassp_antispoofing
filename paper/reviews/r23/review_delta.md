### Summary

The paper makes three linked claims: (1) TTS repetition-counting failure tracks *periodicity*, not sequence length — shown by pairing each k-fold repeated stimulus with a length- and vocabulary-matched non-periodic control, across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families, with the effect surviving decoding-rule and exclusion-rule sweeps; (2) the standard evaluation instrument (Whisper) is itself compromised by the phenomenon under study, so a CTC recognizer is substituted and validated; and (3) a Lean-4-verified contraction bound that the authors themselves propose as the mechanism is measured directly and rejected — the boundary-to-boundary Jacobian is expansive (q > 1) in every checkpoint, on both the repeated and control arms. The steelmanned version of this paper is genuinely strong: the length-vs-periodicity control is a real distinguishing test, not just a consistency check, the authors pre-register the post-hoc-ness of their own k≥6 threshold and run a 420-specification robustness curve, and the mechanism section is a rare example of a group building, then honestly discarding, its own hypothesis. Pressed harder, though, the paper's title promises to *locate* the failure and only half-delivers: it locates the triggering *condition* (periodicity) and rules out one candidate *mechanism* (contraction), but never localizes anything about *why* periodicity does this — no ablation isolates whether the effect is driven by literal repeated-token/embedding identity versus a more abstract periodic structure, and the one obvious test available from the authors' own toolkit (effective rank, borrowed from their companion Whisper paper) is computed but never actually reported against the periodicity manipulation in the main text. Reproducibility is also weaker than the density of statistics suggests: virtually every number in the four-page body is footnoted to one of 28 supplementary sections not provided here.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The abstract's closing claim ("it never described these decoders at all") generalizes beyond what was actually measured, and cites at least one number not traceable in the body text. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Abstract ("q = 21.0–347.7... so it never described these decoders at all") vs. Section 4 ("Across five checkpoints and two families, q runs 21.0–347.7"); also abstract's "46.8 points over the whole ladder from k=1," which I could not locate restated or derived anywhere in Section 3.
   **Why it matters** — The panel is six checkpoints across three families; the mechanism-refutation claim was tested on five checkpoints across two families (XTTS-v2's family appears absent from Section 4 entirely). "These decoders" in the abstract reads as the whole panel. A reader taking the abstract at face value would believe the contraction premise was tested and falsified for every architecture in the study, when at least one wasn't tested at all.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly in the abstract which checkpoints/families were and weren't tested for q, and either trace every abstract number to a body sentence or drop it from the abstract.

2. **Issue** — The headline generalization claim ("posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel is 65.3 [5.9, 93.3], P=0.996") is a hierarchical-model extrapolation from only three groups, and the paper's own sensitivity check shows this conclusion is not robust to prior choice. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — Section 3.1, the mixed-effects/partial-pooling paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — With n=3 architecture families, a group-level variance term is barely identified; the paper admits as much when it reports that under "the widest prior on the group-level scales it touches zero (−1.6, P=0.956)." That's not a robustness check that passed, it's a demonstration that the headline P=0.996 is an artifact of the chosen prior, not of the data. Presenting the favorable-prior number in the main flow and the unfavorable one as a parenthetical undersells how fragile this generalization claim is.
   **What would address it** — Report the prior-sensitivity result with equal prominence, or drop the extrapolation-beyond-panel claim until a fourth *held-out* architecture is tested prospectively (which the paper does do next with CosyVoice 2 — that follow-up check is the actually convincing evidence here, not the Bayesian extrapolation that preceded it).

3. **Issue** — Almost every quantitative claim in the main text is qualified by a pointer into unincluded supplementary material (S1–S28), making the four-page body largely unauditable on its own. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — Throughout; e.g., exclusion criteria (S18), the Lean development (S1–S2), per-condition rates (S10), the specification curve (S11), four of the paper's five sections cite an S-number for the number that actually grounds the claim.
   **Why it matters** — A reviewer (or reader) working from the manuscript alone cannot check the exclusion rule that dropped 342/2052 generations "in one template whose fillers the judge never transcribes," cannot see the causal-patch protocol beyond a sentence, and cannot verify the Lean proof is free of a smuggled assumption. The paper is written as though every claim is self-contained and precisely quantified, but the actual evidentiary weight sits in a document not being reviewed.
   **What would address it** — For a venue-length submission this may be unavoidable, but at minimum the abstract/intro should flag that verification requires the supplement, and the most load-bearing numbers (exclusion criteria, Lean axiom audit) should get a sentence of self-contained justification rather than a bare S-number.

4. **Issue** — The causal-patching result that the paper leans on to argue "the count is not erased" only succeeds on 3 of 4 tested checkpoints; the fourth ("cannot tell") is a genuine non-result that gets folded into confident framing. `[Evidence — alternative explanations / distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — Section 5, "The rival the evidence favours," and the Llasa-8B "cannot tell" sentence.
   **Why it matters** — A rank-1 patch moving the count "not at all" on 3 checkpoints is consistent with (a) the representation-survives-but-readout-fails story the authors favor, and equally consistent with (b) the patch simply not carrying enough information to matter on any of the four checkpoints, with Llasa-8B's failure mode (same-k and cross-k donors move the count equally) being the tell that the patch isn't isolating what they think it is even on the "successful" checkpoints. The paper doesn't clearly rule out (b).
   **What would address it** — A positive control showing the same rank-1 patch protocol *can* move some other decodable quantity (e.g., word identity, not count) on Llasa-8B, to demonstrate the patch mechanism itself works there and the null is about count specifically, not about the patch being inert on that checkpoint.

5. **Issue** — The paper isolates *when* periodicity breaks counting but never ablates *what about* periodicity does it — specifically, whether the effect is driven by literal repeated-token/embedding identity recurring in the input stream, versus a more abstract periodic pattern, and whether the representational-collapse account the paper cites as "closest" related work is actually consistent with their own effective-rank measurements. `[Evidence — missing ablations]`
   **Where** — Section 4 end ("Neither that nor the rate of effective-rank gain distinguishes a horizon from any saturating process") and Section 5 ("Closest is representational collapse [14]... which our control separates from periodicity").
   **Why it matters** — The Method section states effective rank is computed for every instrumented generation ("Effective rank is the exponentiated entropy of the normalised singular-value spectrum"), and the companion paper by the same authors [2604.08591] builds an entire "Spectral Sensitivity Theorem" around exactly this quantity, predicting a phase transition into a rank-1 "attractor regime." That is the single most natural covariate to report against the periodicity manipulation — does effective rank collapse faster on periodic than control inputs, and does the collapse rate track the exact-match deficit the way q was shown not to? The paper computes the ingredient, cites the directly relevant hypothesis (Barbero et al. [2406.04267]), and then reports only a dismissive one-clause verdict with no numbers in the main text. Separately, the periodic/control contrast confounds "periodic structure" with "the repeated token is literally the same embedding at every occurrence" — a trivial KV-cache/attention-shortcut explanation that a rank-1-collapse account and a genuinely abstract-periodicity account would predict differently, and the paper never perturbs the repeated tokens' embeddings (e.g., near-duplicate but non-identical fillers at graded similarity) to separate them.
   **What would address it** — Report effective-rank trajectories (periodic vs. control, by checkpoint) with the same rigor given to q in Section 4, and add a graded-similarity filler condition between "identical token" and "distinct token" to test whether the deficit is continuous in embedding similarity or a step function at exact identity.

### Minor concerns

- The abstract sentence mixing "94.3% for their cycled controls" with "67.6 points... with never-cycled control fillers, 75.4 with the cycled pool" is genuinely hard to parse on first read — two control conditions are introduced in one clause without disambiguation.
- Figure 1(b) caption ("hollow markers past the rule are the p = k arm, matched to neither in vocabulary nor length") is not decipherable without having already read deep into Section 3.1 — the figure fails the "interpretable from caption alone" bar.
- The abstract gives the Lean-4-verified bound prominent, almost marketing-grade billing ("A Lean-4-verified bound says...") for a result the paper itself later says "explains nothing in Section 3" — the framing weight and the actual evidentiary weight are mismatched.
- Reference [7] (companion paper, same authors) proposes a rank-1 "attractor regime" collapse theory for a related architecture; this paper's own q-measurements find the opposite (expansive, non-contracting maps) in a different architecture family, and the tension/consistency between the group's two theories is never discussed.
- The intro's motivating example ("six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six") implies number-phrase stimuli are central, and the Method section confirms "number-phrase families" exist in the stimulus set, but no number-phrase result appears anywhere in Section 3.
- Heavy jargon and constant S-number interruption (CTC blank-collapse, RSS, "sub-stack," dozens of inline confidence intervals) leave little room to orient a reader who works in ASR/TTS but not in this group's specific ladder-methodology vocabulary — several sentences read as written for reviewers who already know the supplement.

### Belief update

I did update, moderately, on the proposition "TTS repetition-counting failures are specifically about periodic structure rather than raw sequence length" — Figure 1's paired-control design is a real distinguishing test, not just a consistency check, and it replicates across three architecture families plus a prospectively-tested fourth (CosyVoice 2), which is unusually convincing for a single paper. I also updated (modestly, since it was somewhat their own strawman to begin with) against the specific contraction-bound account of the failure — a clean negative result, honestly reported. I did not update on any positive mechanistic account of *why* periodicity causes the failure: the paper's own causal evidence is 3-of-4 successful with one explicit non-result, and the most obvious mechanistic test available from their own instrumentation (effective rank vs. periodicity) is computed but not shown.

### Verdict

**Minor revision** — the title promises to "locate" the failure, but after refuting the one mechanism the authors propose, the paper never runs the ablation that would actually locate it (effective-rank collapse vs. periodicity, or embedding-identity vs. abstract-periodicity), leaving the mechanistic half of the paper's own stated contribution unaddressed.
