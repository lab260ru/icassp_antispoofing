### Summary
The paper asks whether a well-known TTS failure — mis-rendering the count of a repeated word or phrase — is driven by input *length* or by *periodicity* per se. Using a "repetition ladder" of 180 templated stimuli (a target word repeated k times vs. a length- and word-count-matched control with distinct fillers), the authors measure exact-count accuracy across six checkpoints spanning three(-plus-one) TTS architectures, with a CTC recognizer chosen over Whisper as the scoring instrument after showing Whisper undercounts periodic ground truth. The headline finding is a large, consistent accuracy gap between repeated and control conditions (18.2% vs 94.3% exact-match at k≥6) that survives decoding-rule sweeps, exclusion-rule ablations, and re-scoring with four different recognizers, and that a separate "period ladder" shows tracks periodicity rather than verbatim-token adjacency. The authors then propose and formally verify (in Lean 4) a contraction-based mechanism for the failure, measure the relevant Lipschitz constant empirically, and find it is never a contraction (q ≫ 1 everywhere) — refuting their own candidate mechanism — before turning to causal state-patching evidence suggesting the count representation survives internally but the output policy fails to use it.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The empirical test of the paper's own theoretical mechanism uses teacher-forced Jacobians, which by the authors' own admission "removes the token channel," so it may not measure the actual autonomous state map the Lean-verified theorem is about.
   **Where** — Section 4, "Measuring q": "Teacher-forcing removes the token channel, so these are lower bounds."
   **Why it matters** — The theorem concerns a per-repetition boundary-to-boundary map that presumably includes the causal feedback of previously emitted tokens re-entering the model. If token feedback is exactly the channel through which contraction (if any) would occur — e.g., because repeated tokens produce increasingly confident, low-entropy embeddings that damp the residual stream — then teacher-forcing could systematically eliminate contraction that exists in free-running generation. Calling the measured q's "lower bounds" asserts a directional relationship (q_free-run ≥ q_teacher-forced) without justification; the paper never argues why removing the token channel could only inflate, never deflate, the estimated contraction factor. If that directional assumption is wrong, the entire refutation in Section 4 — one of the paper's three headline contributions — could be measuring the wrong dynamical system.
   **What would address it** — Report q under actual free-running (non-teacher-forced) rollouts, or provide an explicit argument/bound for why teacher-forced q strictly dominates the free-running value in this architecture class.

2. **Issue** — Periodicity and sequence compressibility/predictability are not dissociated by the design; the period ladder covaries period with filler-pool diversity, so "periodicity" could equally be redescribed as "low local entropy triggers a redundancy-suppression response," a mechanism much closer to the text-degeneration/repetition-penalty literature the paper cites (Holtzman et al., Welleck et al.) than to a distinct counting failure.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Stimuli"), Section 3.1 ("Periodicity, or verbatim identity?"), Fig. 1(b).
   **Why it matters** — The paper's central rhetorical move is to rule out "length" as the driver and install "periodicity" in its place. But an equally consistent alternative — that TTS decoders apply an implicit anti-repetition/novelty-seeking bias that scales with how compressible the recent context is — is not excluded by any condition in the ladder, because every manipulation that increases period also increases local vocabulary entropy. The paper does rule out an *explicit* repetition-penalty account (penalty-off Llasa still shows the effect), which is good, but that only excludes one specific implementation of a redundancy-suppression mechanism, not the general class. A genuinely adversarial reading of the p=2 result ("half the deficit remains where no token is adjacent to itself") is consistent with a compressibility account as much as a periodicity-specific one, since an alternating AB…AB sequence is still far more predictable/compressible than an 8-word non-repeating control.
   **What would address it** — A condition that holds local n-gram entropy/compressibility constant while varying strict periodicity (or vice versa) — e.g., a "shuffled-period" control that repeats the same multiset of tokens as the periodic condition but in non-periodic order, matching entropy while breaking periodicity.

3. **Issue** — The judge-validation claim rests on a form of circularity and lacks an independent human-reliability study: the CTC recognizer is "validated" against concatenative audio with synthesized ground truth and cross-checked against other ASR models, but no formal human-transcription agreement statistic is reported for the actual TTS output being scored.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Behavioural measurement..."), Section 3.2 ("Nor is it the recogniser...").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own logic is that ASR judges can "manufacture" or "under-state" the effect they are meant to detect (this is exactly why Whisper was disqualified). Having established that principle, the remaining validation strategy — audit on concatenative ground truth (n=12–24) plus agreement across multiple *automatic* recognizers — never breaks out of the family of instruments whose shared biases the paper itself worries about. The "stratified 165-clip sample... so a judgement can be checked by ear" is offered as a spot-checking resource, not as a conducted study with a reported human–machine agreement rate. Given that repetitive, degenerate-sounding audio is exactly the regime where ASR systems (all trained on natural, non-repetitive corpora) are least trustworthy, this gap is not incidental — it sits at the crux of the measurement.
   **What would address it** — Report an actual human-listener count-agreement statistic (e.g., Cohen's κ or exact-match rate) on a sample of real model outputs, not just synthesized ground truth.

4. **Issue** — The paper's most novel mechanistic claim (Section 5: the count representation survives in state but the readout/policy fails to use it) is supported by causal patching on only 3 of 4 tested checkpoints, deliberately excluding the one checkpoint (Llasa-8B) whose result was inconclusive.
   **Where** — Section 5, "The rival the evidence favours": "The fourth, Llasa-8B, returns cannot tell, not no... so that arm measures foreign state."
   **Why it matters** — n=36 total across 3 architectures for a zero-effect causal-patch result is already a small evidentiary base for a strong "count is present but ignored" claim. Excluding the largest, and by Table 1 among the worst-performing, checkpoint precisely because its patch didn't behave as predicted (same-k donor moves the count as much as cross-k) removes the one data point most likely to stress-test the "state preserves count" hypothesis at scale, rather than incorporating it as a partial disconfirmation or boundary condition.
   **What would address it** — Either diagnose and fix the "foreign state" problem for Llasa-8B and rerun the patch, or explicitly frame the mechanistic claim as scale-limited (n=3, small models only) rather than presenting it in the Discussion as though established across "three checkpoints across two families."

5. **Issue** — The primary k≥6 threshold used for the headline 18.2%/94.3% contrast is explicitly admitted to have been chosen after inspecting where the two arms diverge, i.e., post hoc, with no pre-registered primary analysis.
   **Where** — Section 3.2: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge... Two exploratory analyses, dated in S16, are reported in text alone."
   **Why it matters** — The authors partially mitigate this with a 420-specification robustness curve (34.4–86.4 points, sign never reverses) and by disclaiming p-value-based inference, which is commendable transparency. But the abstract still leads with the k≥6 number as if it were a pre-specified primary contrast, and "not correcting across any of them" is an acceptable stance only if the paper is read purely as effect-size estimation, not as the confirmatory test its framing ("a companion... [contributing] a controlled manipulation") implies. A reader skimming only the abstract has no way to know the threshold was selected from the same data used to report it.
   **What would address it** — State the post-hoc nature of k≥6 directly in the abstract (not just in the body), and designate one specification as pre-registered/primary going forward (e.g., using the CosyVoice2 addition, which does appear to have been genuinely predicted in advance — commit 6f34945 — as the template for how the rest of the panel should have been analyzed).

6. **Issue** — The paper reports the central "gap" effect size under many similar but distinct numeric guises (67.6, 69.6, 75.4, 76.1, 76.4, 76.7, 65.1–76.7, 61.7, 72.2, 75.0, 46.8, and others) spread across cycled/never-cycled fillers, by-checkpoint/by-family aggregation, and exclusion variants, without ever anchoring one canonical headline number and definition.
   **Where** — Abstract; Section 3.1; Section 3.2 throughout.
   **Why it matters** — This is more than a stylistic quibble under an experimental-design lens: with only 3–4 true architecture "clusters" underlying every hierarchical/family-level estimate, minor changes in which subset of numbers is quoted (family vs. checkpoint, cycled vs. never-cycled) shift the apparent effect size by up to ~9 points, and the reader cannot easily tell which of these is the paper's actual claim versus a sensitivity check. Combined with concern 5, this makes it hard to falsify or replicate a single specific quantitative claim.
   **What would address it** — A single clearly labeled primary effect size (with the rest explicitly marked as robustness checks in a table), and consistent terminology distinguishing "by-checkpoint," "by-family," and "cycled/never-cycled" gaps.

### Minor concerns
- Section 2/3 depend heavily on ~28 supplement sections (S1–S28) for details essential to assessing several claims (e.g., the causal-patch procedure, full exclusion-rule definitions, the exact filler-cycling scheme) — reasonable given ICASSP's page limit, but it means the main text alone is insufficient to fully verify the paper's own robustness claims.
- The filler-pool construction ("a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k=8"; "Rungs p=2, 4, 8 share one 8-word pool") is described tersely enough that the exact stimulus construction is hard to reconstruct without the supplement or code.
- Table 1's two metrics can diverge sharply (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B: 0.0% median-error gap vs. a 60-point exact-match gap) with only a brief in-text gloss; a short worked explanation of why central-tendency and exact-match metrics disagree would help readers.
- The repository name ("icassp_antispoofing") is noted as unrelated to the study's topic ("name predates this study") — a distracting aside in the main text that could be moved to an acknowledgment/footnote.
- The companion paper [7] is cited with a 2026 arXiv date; readers/venues enforcing anonymity or dual-submission rules may need this flagged explicitly.
- The abstract omits that one of the four checkpoints tested in the causal-patch experiment (Llasa-8B) returned an inconclusive result, which is relevant context for how strongly to read the "state survives, policy fails" claim.
- CosyVoice2's confirmatory result (n=90) is reported as agreeing with a pre-registered predictive interval but is never formally pooled into the main panel's hierarchical estimates, leaving two parallel, not-quite-reconciled reporting tracks.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the controlled manipulation and self-directed falsification of the paper's own mechanistic hypothesis are genuine strengths, but the teacher-forcing mismatch with the theorem's premise, the unvalidated-by-humans judge, the thin and partially excluded causal-patch evidence, and the post-hoc primary threshold each need to be directly addressed before the headline "periodicity, not length" and "state survives, policy fails" claims can be taken at face value.
