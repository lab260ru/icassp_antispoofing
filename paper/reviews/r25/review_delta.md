### Summary

The paper's central claim is that TTS repetition-counting failure tracks **periodicity**, not sequence length: a length-matched-control design (Fig. 1a) and an orthogonal period-only ladder (Fig. 1b) both show the deficit dissociating from length, surviving decoding-rule sweeps, exclusion-rule removal, and four different ASR judges. Its second claim is that Whisper, the field's default judge, is disqualified for measuring this specific phenomenon because its own autoregressive decoder shares the failure mode. Its third claim is negative: the authors' own proposed mechanism — a Lean-4-verified theorem that a contracting per-repetition state map bounds any readout's counting horizon — is measured directly and refuted (q is always > 1). The empirical core (periodicity ≠ length) is unusually well pressure-tested for a 6-page ICASSP paper, and the willingness to formalize, measure, and then discard the authors' own mechanism is genuinely uncommon scientific hygiene. But I went looking for where the "controlled, pre-specified" framing the paper wears is actually load-bearing versus decorative, and — helped by direct access to the exact repository the paper cites as its evidentiary backing — found that the paper's single most consequential mechanistic result (the causal rank-1-patch "positive control" in the Discussion) rests on a detection statistic that was changed *after* seeing pilot data, on sample sizes small enough that the same experiment already flipped its verdict once when the sample was doubled, and that the artifact tag the paper cites for reproducibility does not currently exist on the repository it names. None of this is fatal to the periodicity-vs-length finding, but it directly undercuts the paper's strongest epistemic selling point — that its claims are pre-committed rather than shaped by what the data showed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The period-ladder's headline "half the deficit survives at p=2" claim is reported off "the strictest of five [scoring] rules," with no stated reason that rule is primary rather than post-hoc-selected for producing the roundest number. `[Evidence — post-hoc threshold selection]`
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "Periodicity, or verbatim identity?" paragraph: "a surviving fraction of 0.547 on the strictest of five rules and 0.69–0.86 on the recount rules, where we rest the claim."
   **Why it matters** — The five rules span 0.547–0.86, a factor of >1.5×. The value that gets promoted to the abstract ("half the deficit survives") is the smallest one, and it is suspiciously close to exactly 0.5 (0.508 pooled by family). A reader has no way to tell from the text whether "strictest" was the pre-specified primary rule or the rule chosen because it produced the cleanest headline number.
   **What would address it** — State, with a timestamp or commit reference (the paper does this successfully elsewhere, e.g. the CosyVoice2 prediction), which of the five rules was designated primary *before* the period ladder was scored, and report all five with equal prominence rather than relegating four of them to a supplementary aside.

2. **Issue** — The judge-validation audits that license the entire pivot from Whisper to a CTC recognizer are themselves tiny (n=12 for Whisper, n=24 for CTC, both against concatenative audio rather than natural speech). `[Evidence — statistical rigor]`
   **Where** — Section 2, "Behavioural measurement" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — This is not a peripheral check; it is the justification for discarding the field's default instrument (Whisper) and reporting all headline numbers through a different one. A 12-trial and a 24-trial audit is a thin foundation for a methodological pivot this consequential, especially since concatenative audio may not stress the CTC recognizer's own known failure mode (blank-collapse) the same way natural repeated speech does.
   **What would address it** — Report the audit at panel scale (hundreds of trials, ideally spanning the actual stimuli rather than concatenative substitutes) with confidence intervals, not point estimates.

3. **Issue** — The paper tests only one non-autoregressive model per duration-modeling "strategy" (VITS for per-token duration, F5-TTS for total-duration estimation) and draws a mechanistic distinction ("what plausibly separates the two is where the count must live") from an n=1-per-arm comparison. `[Evidence — baselines/ablations]`
   **Where** — Section 3.2, VITS/F5-TTS paragraphs; the authors themselves flag this in Discussion ("The duration story rests on one model per strategy").
   **Why it matters** — A single confounded pair (different architectures, different training data, different everything except the duration-modeling strategy) cannot isolate duration-modeling as the causal variable; VITS and F5-TTS differ on far more axes than that one. The self-admission in the limitations section is honest but doesn't fix the inferential gap — the "what plausibly separates" sentence in Section 3.2 still reads as a mechanistic claim, not a hedged one.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the Section 3.2 language to match the Discussion's hedging, or add a second NAR model per strategy (e.g., a second flow-matching TTS system) before asserting the duration-modeling distinction as an explanation.

4. **Issue** — The paper leans on two same-lab, same-quarter companion preprints ([7], a spectral-hallucination ASR paper by the identical three authors, and [13], Venkatesh's dissociation paper) to establish load-bearing background claims ("the count is not erased… as Venkatesh found in text models"), and I could not independently verify either via arXiv search due to repeated service errors. `[Novelty — attribution / unverifiable prior art]`
   **Where** — Section 5 ("Related work, and what the control decides"), citing [7] and [13].
   **Why it matters** — [13] is cited as prior confirmation that counts "survive in state while readouts fail" — a claim this paper's own causal experiment (concern #6 below) could not actually confirm for TTS. If [13] is itself a very recent, unreplicated result, the paper is building on a single unvalidated data point rather than an established finding, while presenting it with the confidence of settled prior art.
   **What would address it** — Cite [13]'s own validation status (single study? replicated?) rather than treating "as Venkatesh found" as settled ground; this is easy to check and the authors are evidently in a position to know, given the apparent lab overlap.

5. **Issue** — The reproducibility artifact the paper points readers to does not currently exist. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — Section 2, "Code, data, settings": "public at tag v1.3-isolating of https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing."
   **Why it matters** — I have direct access to this exact repository. `git tag -l` and `git ls-remote --tags origin` both confirm the newest tag actually present, locally and on the remote, is `v1.2-locating` — `v1.3-isolating` has not been cut or pushed. A reader following the paper's own citation today gets nothing. This is not a hypothetical concern about *whether* reproducibility artifacts exist somewhere; it is a directly verified, current gap between what the paper asserts and what is retrievable.
   **What would address it** — Trivial to fix (run the release script before submission), but it should not have been asserted as done in a submitted paper text before it was actually true.

6. **Issue** — The Discussion's causal "positive control" (rank-1 patch on the count coordinate) — the paper's one attempt to adjudicate whether the count is erased or just unread — rests on a detection statistic that was changed *after* seeing 35–41 pilot rows, and on sample sizes small enough that the identical experiment's verdict already flipped once when n was doubled; the number currently printed for the checkpoint most directly named by a reviewer objection (Llasa-8B, "never detectable and bounds only 36%") still rests on the un-doubled, smaller sample. `[Evidence — pre-registration integrity / post-hoc-shaped hypothesis]`
   **Where** — Section 5, "The rival we could not decide" paragraph (main text); corroborated by the paper's own repository history, which is directly inspectable given the repo is cited as the paper's evidentiary record. The repository's own change log states, of this exact experiment: "The positive control is NOT fully pre-registered: four operationalisations were repaired after 35-41 pilot rows, and one of them mattered — the detection statistic was a median word-edit distance whose noise-floor value is exactly zero, so 'at least twice the floor' would have marked every cell detectable by construction, which is precisely the answer that would have rescued the causal claim." Separately, a later entry in the same log reports that at n=9 the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B write "reached 1.98 times the sampler-re-roll floor against a pre-committed bar of 2.0 — a knife-edge," and that doubling the sample (n=9→18, n=18→36) moved it to 4.01×, reversing the reported verdict; the Llasa-8B number quoted in the paper's Discussion is explicitly flagged in the same entry as resting on "the exact configuration where Qwen's numbers just moved most," with a re-verification pass described as still running at the time of that commit.
   **Why it matters** — This is the single experiment the paper uses to gesture at the live mechanistic question ("erased from the state, or the policy reading it fails") after ruling out contraction. A reader of the main text sees a clean number ("bounds only 36%") with no indication that (a) the scoring rule for "is this registered at all" was tuned after a pilot run specifically because the original rule would have mechanically rescued the causal claim, or (b) the exact same experimental configuration already produced a false "marginal detection" result for a sibling checkpoint that reversed on more data, and the reported checkpoint hasn't yet received the same correction. This is exactly the failure mode this axis is built to catch: a result that looks pre-registered in the prose ("the detection statistic was… fixed in advance," per the supplement) is, on the one occasion I could check against source-of-truth, shown to have been repaired mid-course in the direction that salvaged the finding.
   **What would address it** — State plainly in the main text (not buried in a supplement the ICASSP reader will not open) that the positive-control statistic was revised after piloting, disclose the small n and the already-observed instability at that n, and either hold the Llasa-8B number until it has received the same doubled-sample treatment that reversed Qwen's, or report it explicitly as provisional.

### Minor concerns

- The abstract packs roughly fifteen distinct numeric claims into one paragraph (67.6, 75.4, 46.8, 10.4/47.9/73.6/93.1, 61.7, 65.1–76.7, 0.19, 21.0–347.7…) with no signalling of which is the headline effect versus a robustness-check side number — it reads as an attempt to overwhelm rather than to communicate, and a skimming reader (which is most readers of an abstract) cannot extract "the" result from it.
- "Name predates this study" (Section 2, repo-name parenthetical) is a strange thing to need to tell a reviewer and suggests the repository has been repurposed across projects — worth a sentence of context rather than a parenthetical aside.
- Fig. 1 caption density is high (two sub-panels, multiple checkpoints, "hollow markers are the p = k arm, matched to neither in vocabulary nor length") — the caveat about the hollow markers not being matched is important enough that it should be flagged in-figure (e.g., a distinct marker legend entry), not just in prose.
- Table 1's "Par." column gives parameter counts without specifying whether these are decoder-only or full-pipeline (codec + LM) parameter counts, which matters for the scale claims made about q growing with Llasa scale in Section 4.
- The stratified 165-clip human-checkable sample (Section 2) is a genuinely good anti-cherry-picking practice and deserves more prominence than a single clause gets it — this is exactly the kind of check ("are random examples shown anywhere?") this framework asks for, and the paper does it, which should be stated more directly rather than buried in the availability sentence.
- "S9, S27" and similar pointers accumulate rapidly in Section 3; for a paper this dense with supplementary cross-references, an index of what each S-number contains (even a one-line table) would help a reader triage which claims are checkable from the main text alone.

### Belief update

I came in expecting a typical "our intervention fixes X" TTS paper and instead found the periodicity-vs-length dissociation itself reasonably compelling — the length-matched control and the independent period-only ladder are two different designs converging on the same qualitative conclusion, and the specification-curve / robustness-envelope reporting (420 forking choices, gap never reversing sign) is better practice than I expect from this venue. I updated some credence toward "TTS repetition failure is a periodicity-specific phenomenon, not a length/attention-span artifact" — modestly, because the underlying mechanism is left genuinely unresolved (contraction refuted, and the one attempt to adjudicate the remaining hypothesis is exactly the part I found compromised). I did *not* update toward the paper's implicit self-framing as maximally pre-registered and self-correcting; direct inspection of the cited repository shows that framing survives selectively — true for the CosyVoice2 extrapolation prediction (the 65.3-point posterior-predictive figure and CosyVoice2 test genuinely do appear pre-committed in the repository's own history, which I verified and which deserves credit), false for the causal positive-control experiment discussed above. A paper's claim to have pre-registered its riskiest test should be checked case by case, not taken as a blanket property of the project.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is well-supported and worth publishing, but the causal positive-control result in Discussion (concern #6) needs either removal, explicit provisional labeling, or completion of the re-verification the authors' own process already flagged as necessary, before the paper can claim what it currently implies about pre-registration integrity.
