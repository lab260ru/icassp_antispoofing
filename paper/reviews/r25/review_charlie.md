### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-known tendency to lose count on repeated phrases is caused by input *length* or by input *periodicity*, and disentangles the two with a "repetition ladder" of 180 stimuli in which each repeated-word item is paired with a length- and carrier-matched control whose repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints from three architecture families (Llasa, Qwen3-TTS, XTTS-v2), plus non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a formally verified (Lean 4) contraction-map hypothesis for the mechanism, the paper reports: (1) a large, robust exact-count gap between repeated and control items (18.2% vs 94.3% at k≥6) that survives decoding, exclusion, and judge-choice robustness checks; (2) a graded period ladder showing the deficit tracks periodicity rather than verbatim identity or length; (3) that the standard evaluation instrument (Whisper) is itself biased toward manufacturing the effect, motivating a switch to a CTC recognizer; and (4) that the Lean-verified "contracting state map" mechanism the authors proposed is empirically false (measured Lipschitz constants q always ≫ 1), leaving the actual failure mechanism (readout vs. representation) undetermined.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central novelty claim — a "controlled manipulation isolating periodicity from length" — is asserted without situating it against the transformer counting/state-tracking literature it already cites, several of which plausibly study exactly this length-vs-periodicity confound in the text domain, and against Venkatesh [13], whose finding the paper's own Discussion says anticipates its central dissociation result ("It is not erased: it stays decodable late… as Venkatesh [13] found in text models").
   - **Where** — Introduction ("We contribute, in order of what the evidence supports: a controlled manipulation isolating periodicity from length…") and Section 5, "Related work, and what the control decides," citing [13–20].
   - **Why it matters** — If length-matched, non-periodic controls (or equivalent period-vs-length disentanglement) already exist in the LLM counting-expressivity literature the paper cites for [15–20], or if Venkatesh [13] already demonstrates the representation/readout dissociation the current paper spends Section 5 failing to resolve, then the paper's claimed primary contribution is largely a domain transfer (text → speech) of an existing paradigm, not a novel experimental isolation. The paper never tells the reader which is true, so the reader cannot calibrate the novelty claim in the abstract and introduction.
   - **What would address it** — Explicitly state whether any cited counting/expressivity paper used a length-matched, non-periodic control design, and whether Venkatesh's causal manipulation of the readout (which this paper says it "could not build," Section 5) was achieved in the text setting; if so, explain concretely what prevents porting that intervention to a TTS decoder, rather than presenting the open question as a fresh discovery.

2. **Issue** — Under an adversarial reading, the "periodicity, not length" headline is not fully isolated from a *predictability/entropy* confound: replacing the repeated token with distinct fillers changes not only periodicity but also local n-gram predictability (each filler is a one-off, lower-frequency continuation, whereas the repeated token is maximally predictable given itself). The Discussion partially concedes an adjacent version of this ("Periodicity is not separated from training-set frequency… under an independent LM the control is the less probable text in 87% of pairs… weakened, not closed").
   - **Where** — Section 3.1, "Periodicity, or verbatim identity?"; Section 5, "What the design does not separate, and other limits."
   - **Why it matters** — A model that struggles specifically because verbatim self-repetition triggers a distinct dynamic (e.g., copy/induction-style attention collapse) is a different — and arguably more interesting — story than "periodicity" as an abstract structural property, and the two are not distinguished by the current design. The authors' own admission that this alternative is "weakened, not closed" means a plausible falsifier of the headline claim survives in the paper as written.
   - **What would address it** — An entropy- or perplexity-matched control (not just word-count matched) at each period level, so periodic structure can vary while local predictability is held fixed, would let the paper actually rule out — rather than merely "weaken" — the predictability account.

3. **Issue** — The paper explicitly runs "420 specifications" as a robustness/specification-curve check and states "we correct across none of them," treating "none reverses sign" as sufficient evidence, without any joint/omnibus test of the kind specification-curve methodology typically requires to make that claim statistically meaningful rather than descriptive.
   - **Where** — Section 3.1: "Across 420 specifications spanning every forking choice we made… the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points, median 70.5, and none reverses its sign… our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values (S11)."
   - **Why it matters** — With 420 highly correlated, non-independent specifications drawn from the same underlying data, "none reverses sign" is a much weaker claim than it sounds; without a permutation-based joint test (the standard companion to specification-curve analysis), the claim reduces to "the point estimate stayed positive across many re-slicings of the same effect," which is expected even for effects of moderate reliability.
   - **What would address it** — Report a joint significance test across the specification curve (e.g., a permutation test on the median or aggregate effect against a null distribution of relabeled/shuffled repeated-vs-control assignments), or explicitly downgrade the framing from "robustness" to "descriptive sensitivity."

4. **Issue** — The Lean-4 formal verification is advertised prominently (abstract, index terms: "formal verification") but its substantive payoff for the paper's contribution is negative and, by the authors' own account, empty ("a verified conditional with a measured, rejected antecedent… explains nothing in Section 3"), while no detail is given on the size, scope, or fidelity of the Lean translation from informal premises (attention-weight spread, contraction) to the checked statement.
   - **Where** — Abstract; Index Terms; Section 4.
   - **Why it matters** — Presenting a refuted, formally verified mechanism as a headline-level contribution risks over-crediting the paper's rigor relative to what it actually establishes; readers cannot audit whether "free of sorry and with an axiom audit" corresponds to a non-trivial theorem or a generic contraction-mapping restatement, since the Lean source itself is only claimed to be available, not shown or summarized in the text.
   - **What would address it** — Either reframe the Lean bound in the abstract/index terms as an explicitly negative/ruled-out mechanism rather than a co-equal contribution, or include the formal theorem statement (not just a pointer to a repository) so the translation from informal to formal claims can be checked in the paper itself.

5. **Issue** — Several of the paper's most consequential confirmatory tests rest on small samples used to validate extrapolation or causal claims: the CosyVoice2 out-of-panel test (n=90) is used to "confirm" a Bayesian partial-pooling extrapolation the authors themselves flag as barely identified ("three groups barely identify a group-level scale… under the widest prior… that gap touches zero"), and the rank-1 causal patch used to adjudicate "the rival we could not decide" runs on n=18 for the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B carrier-direction control.
   - **Where** — Section 3.1, CosyVoice2 paragraph; Section 5, rank-1 patch paragraph (n=18); Section 3.2, F5-TTS duration-control experiment (n=60).
   - **Why it matters** — A single additional architecture (CosyVoice2) is not independent replication at the scale needed to certify a family-level generalization the authors admit their own 3-family panel cannot support alone; treating it as resolving the extrapolation risk overstates what one more data point can show.
   - **What would address it** — Add at least one more out-of-panel architecture before claiming the extrapolation is validated, or soften "checkable rather than asserted" language to reflect that this is suggestive, not confirmatory, evidence.

6. **Issue** — Heavy reliance, for a load-bearing methodological premise (Whisper's autoregressive decoder biasing the judge), on a same-author companion paper that is itself only an arXiv preprint.
   - **Where** — Introduction: "The work is a companion to a spectral study of hallucination in encoder–decoder ASR [7]"; reference [7] Viakhirev, Borodin, Mkrtchian, arXiv:2604.08591, 2026.
   - **Why it matters** — The judge-methodology contribution (Section 2/3.2) is one of the paper's three claimed contributions, and its theoretical grounding partly leans on an unreviewed, same-team companion work; this is not disqualifying but creates a circularity risk that an independent reviewer cannot yet check.
   - **What would address it** — Summarize the companion paper's relevant finding in enough self-contained detail that this paper's judge argument does not depend on taking [7] on faith, or note its review status.

### Minor concerns

- Units are used inconsistently between "points" (percentage-point differences) and "%" (raw percentages) throughout the abstract and Section 3, which is easy to misread on a first pass (e.g., "a gap of 67.6 points" immediately followed by "94.3% for their cycled controls").
- Table 1's Qwen3-TTS-1.7B row (0.0 median count-error gap but a 60-point exact-match gap) is explained in prose but not flagged in the table itself; a footnote there would help readers not skim past it.
- The GitHub repo hosting the code/data is named "icassp_antispoofing," acknowledged as a naming mismatch ("name predates this study") — harmless but worth fixing before camera-ready to avoid reader confusion about scope.
- Reference [7] carries a 2026 arXiv ID (2604.08591); worth double-checking at camera-ready that the ID and venue status are final.
- The prose density (many numbers per sentence, parenthetical confidence intervals nested inside already-dense clauses) is very high even for ICASSP's page limits; several Section 3–4 sentences pack 3–4 distinct statistics without clear signposting of which claim each number supports.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core empirical effect (a large, multiply-robust periodicity-tracking deficit) is credible and well-stress-tested, but the paper's novelty framing needs to be reconciled with the text-domain counting/dissociation literature it already cites, the predictability/entropy confound needs an added control rather than a textual concession, and the specification-curve and small-n extrapolation claims need firmer statistical backing before the stated conclusions can be taken at face value.
