### Summary
The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-known tendency to mis-render repeated phrases is driven by input length or by periodicity per se. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" (a target word repeated k times, each paired with a length- and word-count-matched control in which the repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers), the authors score six checkpoints from three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2) plus two non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) with a CTC recogniser chosen over Whisper (which the authors argue is itself biased by the phenomenon under study). The headline result is a large, robust gap between exact-count accuracy on repeated text (18.2%) versus length-matched controls (94.3%) at k≥6, which a follow-up "period ladder" shows tracks periodicity (not verbatim identity or length) directly, surviving decoding, penalty, exclusion-rule, and recogniser-choice manipulations. A second component formalizes a candidate mechanism (state-contraction bounding a Lipschitz count readout) as a Lean-4-verified theorem, measures its premise empirically, and reports that the premise is false in every decoder tested — so the theorem, while valid, does not explain the behavior.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central robustness argument (420 specifications, "we correct across none of them") substitutes a "sign never reverses" criterion for any formal joint or permutation-based test, so the reported robustness is descriptive, not statistical.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "Across 420 specifications spanning every forking choice we made… the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points, median 70.5, and none reverses its sign… we correct across none of them: the curve is a robustness envelope over one comparison, not a family of hypotheses, and our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values (S11)."
   **Why it matters** — Specification-curve analysis (in its standard published form) derives inferential weight from comparing the *observed* specification-curve statistic against a null distribution generated by permuting the repeated/control labels (or an equivalent randomization scheme), not merely from the qualitative observation that the sign never flips across 420 non-independent, highly correlated re-analyses of the same underlying trials. Without that null comparison, "median 70.5, never reverses sign" is consistent with a scenario where most of the variance across specifications is driven by shared nuisance factors (the judge, the exclusion rule, the k≥6 cut) rather than by independent corroboration of a periodicity effect. The authors' own framing ("effect sizes, not p-values") is a reasonable philosophical stance but doesn't substitute for the specific inferential machinery specification-curve analysis is supposed to supply.
   **What would address it** — Report a permutation/randomization-inference test on the specification curve (e.g., shuffle the repeated-vs-control label within matched pairs, recompute the full 420-specification curve, and locate the observed median/range in that null distribution), which is the standard way this literature establishes that "it never reverses sign" is not itself an artifact of correlated specifications.

2. **Issue** — The generalization machinery (mixed-effects model, partial pooling, out-of-panel posterior prediction) is applied with only 3 architecture-family clusters, and the paper's own numbers show the resulting estimate is fragile to prior choice.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "A mixed-effects linear probability model with architecture as a random effect and checkpoint nested inside it puts the gap at 76.4 points… Partial pooling puts the posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel at 65.3 [5.9, 93.3], but three groups barely identify a group-level scale and under the widest prior on it that gap touches zero (−1.6)."
   **Why it matters** — With n=3 clusters, a random-effects variance component is essentially unidentifiable; the [5.9, 93.3] interval and the admitted zero-touching under a wider prior are symptomatic of exactly that. The paper still leads with a single point estimate (76.4 / 65.3) in the abstract-adjacent prose, which risks readers treating a barely-identified extrapolation as comparably solid to the well-powered within-panel comparison. The CosyVoice 2 check is a welcome single confirmatory data point but is n=1 at the family level — it cannot resolve whether the partial-pooling machinery is well-calibrated in general, only that this one prediction happened to land inside a very wide interval.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the out-of-panel/posterior-predictive framing until more independent architecture families are available, or report the full prior-sensitivity curve (not just two endpoint priors) so readers can see how much of the "65.3-point" estimate is prior-driven versus data-driven.

3. **Issue** — The judge-validation samples that the entire measurement pipeline is calibrated against are extremely small, and no uncertainty is attached to the calibration ratios that are then treated as fixed facts throughout the paper.
   **Where** — Section 2: "it transcribes the repeated word exactly once in every trial that returns anything (0.19 of the true count, n=12 over 6 donors)… ratio 1.00 (n=24, mean 0.996)"; also "in Spanish Whisper again returns nothing at the two highest k, scoring 0.19… (mean 0.93, S9, S27)."
   **Why it matters** — A point ratio of exactly 1.00 from n=24 (mean 0.996) and 0.19 from n=12 are being used, without any confidence interval, to license discarding Whisper as a judge and to certify the CTC recogniser as ground-truth-accurate for the entire 2052-generation panel. At these sample sizes, the true CTC accuracy could plausibly be anywhere from, say, 90% to 100%, which matters because the paper's later robustness claim ("re-scoring with four recognisers... leaves the gap at 65.1–76.7 points") depends on CTC-family judges all being independently well-calibrated, not just correlated with each other.
   **What would address it** — Report Wilson/bootstrap CIs on both audit ratios, and substantially expand the concatenative-audio validation set (this is cheap to synthesize) before using a bare point estimate to justify swapping the primary evaluation instrument.

4. **Issue** — The primary paired comparison is reported only as medians with a degenerate control interval, obscuring the actual distribution of the paired effect.
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "repeated items are rendered with a median count error of -12.5% per checkpoint against 0.0% [0.0, 0.0] for controls (n=525)."
   **Why it matters** — A [0.0, 0.0] interval on a continuous-looking ratio metric ((ĉ−k)/k) is a strong claim of essentially zero dispersion in control performance; it is left unclear whether this interval is a bootstrap CI over items, over checkpoints, or over generations, and if the metric is this floor-clipped for controls, the same metric may be a poor discriminator for the more interesting question of *how much* controls vary. Readers cannot tell from the reported summary whether "control is at the floor" reflects a genuinely deterministic effect or a metric with limited dynamic range at the low end (e.g., rounding/clipping).
   **What would address it** — Report the full paired-difference distribution (IQR, or a swarm/violin summary) rather than a point median plus a degenerate interval, and state explicitly what unit the bootstrap resamples (item, checkpoint, or generation).

5. **Issue** — The k≥6 threshold that anchors Table 1 and most in-text headline numbers is explicitly admitted to be chosen after inspecting where the two curves diverge, and two supporting analyses are described as "reported in text alone," i.e., without the accompanying data the rest of the paper otherwise provides.
   **Where** — End of Section 3.1: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge, which is why the abstract gives the unconditioned figure too (46.8 points from k = 1) and the specification curve varies it. Two exploratory analyses, dated in S16, are reported in text alone (S8, S10, S17)."
   **Why it matters** — This is a disclosed instance of data-dependent threshold selection (garden-of-forking-paths), and while the transparency is commendable, the practical effect is that Table 1 and most narrative numbers use the post hoc, effect-maximizing cut (67.6–76.7-point gaps) rather than the pre-specifiable whole-ladder number (46.8 points from k=1) that the authors themselves flag as the honest unconditioned estimate. Combined with two analyses that are asserted in prose without accompanying tables/figures in the visible manuscript, a reader cannot independently verify a nontrivial share of the paper's claims.
   **What would address it** — Lead the abstract and Table 1 with the k=1 (unconditioned) numbers as primary, present k≥6 explicitly as a secondary/exploratory breakdown, and include the S8/S10/S17 analyses as data (table or figure) rather than prose-only assertions.

### Minor concerns
- The manuscript's extremely dense, clause-stacked prose style (e.g., single sentences carrying 4–5 distinct numeric claims) makes it unusually hard to audit specific numbers against their described procedure without the supplement (S1–S28), none of which is included in the text provided for review.
- Table 1 shows Qwen3-TTS-0.6B controls at exactly 100.0% exact-match — worth double-checking this isn't a rounding artifact given n is presumably small per cell.
- The "strictest of five rules" is chosen as the number to "rest the claim" on for the p=2 survival fraction (0.547), while "recount rules" give a higher 0.69–0.86 range; the rationale for treating the strictest rule as the load-bearing one (rather than, say, an average) isn't argued, only asserted.
- Figure 1(b) caption states n=1539 but doesn't break down how many items fall in each period condition, making it hard to assess per-cell power for the "monotone in all four checkpoints" claim.
- Terminology switches between "points" (percentage-point gaps), plain percentages, ratios, and q-values/R² without a consolidated glossary, which increases the risk of misreading given how compressed the prose already is.
- References [7] and [13] are very recent (2026) self- or closely-related preprints underpinning key interpretive claims (the "companion" spectral hallucination study, and the "it stays decodable late" claim attributed to Venkatesh); given their recency, readers have essentially no independent replication to lean on for those specific claims.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the length-matched control design and multi-recogniser/multi-decoding robustness sweeps are a genuine methodological strength, but the paper's inferential apparatus (uncorrected 420-specification "robustness," a 3-cluster mixed-effects extrapolation whose own reported interval touches zero, and single-digit-to-low-20s judge-validation samples anchoring the whole measurement pipeline) needs to be either formalized with proper null/permutation tests and uncertainty quantification or explicitly downgraded from headline to exploratory status before the claims can be taken at face value.
