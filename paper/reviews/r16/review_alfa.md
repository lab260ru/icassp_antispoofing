LENS: Experimental design (controls, confounds, blinding, pre-registration)
STANCE: Steelman-then-press

*(Steelman first: the paper's strongest version is that it is unusually honest about its own negative results — two failed attempts to measure the contraction factor, a sign-reversed dose-response test, disjoint checkpoint intervals, and a probe that confirms the theorem in only one of two checkpoints are all reported rather than buried, and the length-matched non-adjacent-word control is a genuinely sharp, well-executed design choice that isolates periodicity from length. That transparency is real credit. The critique below presses on where the experimental design nonetheless falls short of supporting the claims built on top of it.)*

### Summary
The paper models one repetition cycle in an autoregressive TTS decoder as a state map, proves in Lean 4 that if this map is a contraction (q<1) then no Lipschitz readout can recover repetition counts past a computable horizon N\*, and then tests the resulting behavioral prediction — periodicity, not length, should break counting — on a 180-item repetition ladder (k ≤ 32, later extended post hoc to k ≤ 128) across six checkpoints from three TTS architectures, scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically to avoid the autoregressive bias of Whisper. The headline results are a median count error of −12.5% per checkpoint at k ≥ 6 versus 0.0% for length-matched distinct-word controls (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match), a proportional (non-saturating) deficit within k ≤ 32 that only saturates once the ladder is extended to k = 128, and a linear probe that recovers the count from decoder states below the fitted horizon but not above it — in one of two checkpoints tested. The paper explicitly does not establish the theorem's premise (contraction), reporting two failed attempts to measure it, and finds that a non-autoregressive baseline (F5-TTS) shows the same dissociation as the AR models, leading the authors to withdraw the architectural framing.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The two analyses that rescue the theorem's central falsifiable prediction (a saturating horizon) are both post-hoc, multiple-look extensions of a protocol that had already failed once, and the decisive confirmatory test runs on only two checkpoints with a 1-of-2 hit rate.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 (the k=128 extension, explicitly labeled "post hoc" and dated only by commit log via S16); Section 5 / S12 (the post-horizon linear-probe test); Abstract ("survives one direct test... in 1 of the 2 checkpoints we could run it on").
   **Why it matters** — The k ≤ 32 ladder found no horizon and fit far better as proportional (RSS 2.6 vs 485.3). The authors then extended to k = 128, chose a saturation functional form, and only then found the predicted horizon. A commit-log audit trail records *when* a decision was made, not whether the decision to keep extending the ladder, which functional form to fit, and which two checkpoints to run the confirmatory probe on were made independent of already knowing the shorter ladder had failed. That is exactly the researcher-degrees-of-freedom pattern pre-registration exists to close off, and it is layered on top of a probe test whose "1 of 2" result the paper itself elsewhere reads in the opposite direction ("Llasa-8B the reverse") — a sample too small to distinguish the theorem's prediction from chance, yet the abstract frames it as the prediction "surviving."
   **What would address it** — Freeze the extension protocol and saturation model before running it (or hold out unseen checkpoints/seeds for it); run the post-horizon probe on more than two checkpoints before claiming it as confirmatory evidence, or explicitly downgrade the language to reflect n=2.

2. **Issue** — Assumption 1's justification (an approximately autonomous per-repetition map F) rests entirely on attention-content dilution (Lemma 1), but says nothing about whether positional information surviving in a transformer's keys/values could keep the true update position-dependent even when content attention is diluted — and the one direct test of the mechanism (dose-response) came out inverted.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("This is what the lemma buys"); Section 4.4 (dose-response test, r=0.59 in the wrong direction).
   **Why it matters** — If position-dependence survives dilution, then the two failed attempts to measure q and the sign-reversed dose-response result are not incidental measurement difficulties, they are what you would expect if attention dilution is not actually the operative mechanism behind the observed undercounting. The paper reports the reversal but pivots past it ("whatever selects which generations fail is not the degree of dilution") rather than revisiting whether Assumption 1 is the right model of what's happening at all.
   **What would address it** — A causal test of state autonomy independent of the attention profile — e.g., activation-patch decoder states at matched dilution but different absolute position m and check whether readouts are actually indistinguishable, as Assumption 1 requires.

3. **Issue** — The "six checkpoints" used as the unit of replication are not six independent draws: three are scale variants of one Llasa/Llama backbone and two are scale variants of one Qwen3-TTS backbone, leaving only three architecturally independent points, yet several headline statistics are computed and reported as if n=6.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("The checkpoint, not the generation, is the unit of replication"; "positive in 5 of 6"; "mean 76.7 [68.4, 84.4] points"); Table 1; Section 2 Models.
   **Why it matters** — Scale variants within one training pipeline share code, data, and tokenizer, so their errors are correlated, not exchangeable; pooling them as six units overstates the independent evidence and makes the effect look more consistent than three architecturally distinct systems actually demonstrate. The paper does report the honest by-family number (75.4 points [71.1, 78.9], effectively n=3) but leads with the n=6 framing in the abstract and in 4.2.
   **What would address it** — Lead with the by-architecture-family statistic (n=3) as the primary claim and present the checkpoint-level breakdown explicitly as non-independent; a mixed-effects model with architecture as a random effect would be more defensible than flat checkpoint pooling.

4. **Issue** — Load-bearing statistical claims (bootstrap CI construction and clustering correction, the signed-rank test, the filler-pool confound check, exclusion-rule sensitivity) are deferred to supplementary sections (S5, S8, S10–S22) not included in the reviewed text.
   **Where** — Throughout; e.g., "whose interval ignores clustering and is narrower for it" (4.1), "with every rule off it is 61.7 points against 76.1 (S18)" (4.2).
   **Why it matters** — A reviewer cannot check the resampling unit for any reported CI, whether the same exclusion and model-fitting choices were applied uniformly across the k≤32 and k≤128 analyses, or whether any correction was applied across the many comparisons swept across S11–S22 (repetition-penalty sweep, filler-pool re-generation, decoding-rule check, duration-injection test). The paper asserts robustness to these choices without showing the derivation.
   **What would address it** — Make the supplementary material available with the submission, and state explicitly in the main text which analyses were fixed before the k=128/post-horizon-probe extensions versus exploratory.

5. **Issue** — The non-autoregressive baseline F5-TTS reproduces the same repetition-specific dissociation the theorem was built to explain, directly outside the theorem's stated scope (a contracting per-repetition map in an AR decoder), and the rescue account (a single global duration estimate) is introduced only after this falsifying case appears.
   **Where** — Section 4.3, "A third rival is a decoding rule" and "What plausibly separates them" paragraphs.
   **Why it matters** — The authors candidly "withdraw the architectural claim," but this leaves unresolved whether contraction dynamics contribute to the panel-level effect at all, or whether a generic duration/position-budget failure (which the F5-TTS duration-injection test only partially supports — relief below k=12, none at or above it) could produce the entire observed pattern in both AR and non-AR systems without invoking Theorem 1's mechanism.
   **What would address it** — A pre-specified, falsifiable signature that distinguishes contraction-driven collapse from generic length/duration-budget failure, tested symmetrically on the AR checkpoints (e.g., does forcing the correct token budget relieve the AR deficit the way partial duration injection does for F5-TTS below k=12?).

6. **Issue** — External validity is narrow and undiscussed: stimuli are synthetic carrier sentences and number phrases in English only, tested against six open-weight checkpoints from three families, with no naturalistic long-form or dialogue text and no widely deployed closed commercial TTS systems.
   **Where** — Section 3 (Stimuli, Models); absent from Discussion.
   **Why it matters** — The claim that "the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and family" is scoped to a curated, single-language benchmark; whether the effect and its periodicity-vs-length signature hold for naturalistic repetition (e.g., repeated dialogue turns, poetic refrains) or for production systems with different training objectives (e.g., RLHF'd for dialogue) is not established or flagged as a limitation.
   **What would address it** — Either add a naturalistic-text or multilingual condition, or add an explicit scope-of-claim limitation to the Discussion.

### Minor concerns
- The horizon formula is parenthesized differently in the Abstract (`N∗ ≤ log(µ/2LC)/log q`) than in the Theorem statement (`log(µ/(2LC))/log q`); harmless once read carefully but invites misreading of operator precedence.
- Table 1 reports median count error and capacity gain per checkpoint but not the per-checkpoint exact-match rates that Section 4.3's headline 94.3%/18.2% figures and the "disjoint intervals for Llasa-1B and Llasa-8B" / "60.0-point exact-rate gap" claims rely on — these numbers should be tabulated in the main text, not only narrated.
- The number of unique carrier templates and reference speakers used to build the 180-item ladder, and whether the same reference clip is reused across items for zero-shot models (XTTS-v2, F5-TTS), is not stated.
- Table 1's "factor 0.46 of the control's" capacity-gain claim doesn't match a simple mean of the six per-checkpoint ratios (≈0.49); likely a pooled or weighted computation, but the method should be stated.
- Abstract clause "the obvious autoregressive judge being biased against repetition" is compressed to the point of ambiguity on first read.
- Reference [7], the "companion" paper this work leans on for the attractor framing, carries a 2026 arXiv date with no venue/status noted; worth clarifying since the present paper's motivation partly depends on it.
- The relationship to [2605.09239] (cited as [13]) — which independently argues that in text LLMs the *representation* survives while only the *output policy* fails at counting — is discussed only briefly ("does not separate this from the rival... R² 0.48"); given how closely it parallels this paper's own probe-vs-output dissociation, a more direct comparison of findings would strengthen the Discussion.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal artifact and the length-matched control design are genuinely valuable contributions, but the paper's confirmatory evidence for its own headline theorem (the k=128 extension and the n=2 post-horizon probe) is built on post-hoc, uncorrected multiple looks and a sample too small to support the "survives one direct test" framing, while the F5-TTS anomaly leaves the theorem's explanatory scope unresolved rather than merely narrowed.
