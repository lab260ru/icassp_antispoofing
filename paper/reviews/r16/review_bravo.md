# Review by bravo

## Summary

The paper proposes a formal, mechanism-level explanation for why autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated phrases: modelling one repetition-boundary-to-boundary update as an autonomous map $F$ on decoder state, it proves (Lean 4/mathlib, no `sorry`) that if $F$ contracts with factor $q<1$, no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can separate repetition counts past a horizon $N^* = \log(\mu/2LC)/\log q$. The paper is explicit that it never establishes the theorem's premise: two attempts to measure $q$ failed, and a companion "attention dilution" lemma's dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed. Empirically, across six checkpoints (three architecture families), scored by a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid an autoregressive judge's own repetition bias, repeated-word items show a median count error of −12.5% at $k\ge6$ against near-perfect length-matched distinct-word controls (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match) — establishing a periodicity-specific, not length-specific, failure. Within $k\le32$ the deficit is proportional rather than saturating (no horizon visible), but a post-hoc extension to $k=128$ on 4/6 checkpoints finds saturation with a 2.7–4.3-fold lower scale for repeated vs. control text, and a post-horizon linear probe recovers count from decoder states in 1 of 2 checkpoints tested. The authors frame the result as a genuine dissociation between a real phenomenon (counting collapse) and an unconfirmed mechanism (contraction), explicitly titling the horizon "one it does not yet explain."

## Major concerns

The paper deserves credit, on the reproducibility axis, for going further than most conference-length papers: it reports an axiom audit of the Lean proof, timestamps analysis decisions against a commit log to distinguish pre-registered from post-hoc analyses (Sec. 4.2, "S16 dates every analysis decision against the commit log"), ships a `build.sh` that claims to regenerate every reported number from pinned revisions, releases both judges' full transcript sets, and provides a stratified 165-clip human-auditable sample. That infrastructure is the strongest form of the paper's implicit argument: "trust the numbers because everything needed to regenerate them is public." Taken at face value, this is exactly the standard a formally-flavored empirical claim should be held to. Pressing on that standard, however, the manuscript itself — the object actually under review — does not meet it: the text is not a self-contained record of what was done, and several of the artifacts it says exist are described only by label (S5, S8, S10–S22) with no content given here.

1. **Issue** — Nearly every quantitative claim in Results is sourced to an unseen appendix section rather than stated in the reviewable text, so no number in Sections 4–5 can actually be audited from this manuscript, and the many different sample sizes reported across analyses are never reconciled against each other.
   **Where** — Throughout Sec. 4–5 (e.g., "(S10)", "(S11)", "(S12)", "(S13)", "(S15)", "(S16)", "(S17)", "(S18)", "(S19)", "(S20)", "(S22)"); Sec. 3's exclusion cascade (2052 → −342 → −132 → −19 → 1559) versus later per-analysis $n$'s of 457, 525, 90, 30/60.
   **Why it matters** — A reader cannot tell, from the paper alone, whether $n=457$ (pooled median, Sec. 4.1), $n=525$ (control median, Sec. 4.3), $n=90$ (VITS, Sec. 4.3), or $n=30$/$60$ (F5-TTS duration test, Sec. 4.3) are consistent subsets of the 1559 retained generations or independently-drawn ones, which materially affects how the effect sizes should be read (e.g., whether disjoint subsets could be cherry-picked, even inadvertently, by the exclusion rules described only qualitatively).
   **What would address it** — A single accounting table (even in an appendix) mapping every reported $n$ to the exclusion rule and stimulus subset that produced it, cited by the same S-numbers already used in text.

2. **Issue** — Two of the paper's central derived metrics — "effective rank" (used for the "capacity gain" result, Sec. 4.4, Fig. 1c, Table 1) and the linear probe used for the post-horizon test (Sec. 4.4/5) — are never defined in-text: no formula for effective rank, no stated layer indices (Table 1 says only "deep layers" across six architectures of different depths), and no probe architecture, train/test split, or regularization.
   **Where** — "State measurement" (Sec. 3); Sec. 4.4; Table 1 header ("capacity gain is measured at deep layers"); Sec. 5.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's second load-bearing empirical result (capacity gain ratio 0.46, positive in all 6 checkpoints) and its most direct test of the theorem's prediction (post-horizon MAE of 0.50 vs. 0.63 vs. 1.12 vs. 0.43) are both unreproducible from the text even if the released activations were downloaded, because the reader does not know what was computed on them.
   **What would address it** — State the effective-rank definition (e.g., participation ratio vs. a numerical-rank threshold and its cutoff) and the probe's architecture/CV protocol explicitly, plus the specific layer index chosen per model and the rule used to choose it.

3. **Issue** — The validation of the CTC judge against Whisper (ratio 1.00 vs. 0.19 on "periodic ground truth") depends on an unspecified "concatenative audio with known ground truth" construction and an unspecified CTC decoding configuration.
   **Where** — "Behavioural measurement" (Sec. 3).
   **Why it matters** — Both details are material to the paper's central methodological move (replacing Whisper with a CTC judge specifically because AR judges share the failure mode under study). CTC models are not immune to their own decoding-time artifacts (e.g., blank-collapse under greedy decoding on highly periodic acoustic input), and greedy vs. beam-with-LM decoding would change exactly the quantity this audit is meant to certify.
   **What would address it** — One sentence stating how the concatenative ground-truth set was built (source utterances, splicing method, how "known ground truth" count was assigned) and the exact CTC decoding configuration used throughout.

4. **Issue** — The abstract and Sec. 4.2 state the periodicity-not-length result holds "in every checkpoint and family," while the same section reports $n=6$ is "too small to test," that the signed-rank test floor at $n=6$ is $p=0.031$, and that "no multiplicity correction clears 0.05" (S11).
   **Where** — Abstract; Sec. 4.2.
   **Why it matters** — The rhetorical certainty of "in every checkpoint and family" is not licensed by six units of replication, three of which are scale variants of the same architecture (Sec. 3 itself notes "six checkpoints but only three designs"). The paper is honest about this in the fine print but does not adjust the headline language to match, which risks the claim being read as more statistically established than the authors' own numbers support.
   **What would address it** — Soften abstract/Sec. 4.2 framing to "consistent across all six checkpoints tested (three families; underpowered for significance)" rather than implying a general law, and consider whether additional checkpoints/architecture families are feasible before the venue's final version, since the released pipeline should make adding checkpoints comparatively cheap.

5. **Issue** — The Lean development's guarantees (no `sorry`, standard axioms only) certify internal logical consistency of whatever was formalized, not that the formalized statements faithfully correspond to the informal Theorem 1 / Lemma 1 as stated in Sec. 2 (e.g., that the Lean "Lipschitz readout" is defined over the same space and constants as the $\mathbb{R}^d$ hidden-state readouts actually probed in Sec. 4.4).
   **Where** — "Formal verification" paragraph (Sec. 2); Sec. 5's discussion of the linear probe as "test[ing] the theorem."
   **Why it matters** — This is the exact failure mode the Lean-formalization community has begun naming explicitly: a proof can pass the type checker while not expressing the intended mathematics ("semantic hallucination" in formalized proofs [2604.16347]). Nothing in the paper reports an informal-to-formal correspondence check (e.g., a table mapping each hypothesis/conclusion of Theorem 1 to its Lean identifier) or an independent read of the Lean files by someone other than the theorem's author, and the axiom audit — which only checks *how* the proof was built, not *what* it says — cannot detect this class of error.
   **What would address it** — Include an explicit correspondence table (informal statement ↔ Lean statement, with the metric/space/constants named on both sides) and note whether the Lean development was reviewed by a second person independent of drafting it.

6. **Issue** — Several of the paper's "direct tests" of the theorem's own distinctive predictions are run at $n=1$ or $n=2$ and then discussed as though informative rather than underpowered: the contraction factor $q$ was attempted twice and failed both times (Sec. 5); the post-horizon linear probe was run on only 2 checkpoints, "confirmed" in 1; the $k=128$ extension itself covers only 4/6 checkpoints at 21–26 generations per cell after exclusions.
   **Where** — Sec. 4.2; Sec. 5 ("Its conclusion survives one direct test... in 1 of the 2 checkpoints we could run it on"; "Llasa-1B is what the theorem predicts and the rival does not, Llasa-8B the reverse").
   **Why it matters** — At $n=2$, "confirmed in 1" is not distinguishable from a coin flip, yet Sec. 5 treats the split as substantively diagnostic of which checkpoints obey the theorem, rather than flagging it as inconclusive by construction. This matters more than it would for a purely empirical paper, because it is the theorem's own quantitative signature ($N^*$, not just qualitative saturation) that is being tested this way.
   **What would address it** — Either run the probe on more checkpoints before claiming a directional split is meaningful, or explicitly downgrade the language to "inconclusive at $n=2$; presented for transparency, not as evidence either way."

## Minor concerns

- Figure 1's axis tick labels are garbled/ambiguous in the provided text (e.g., "22 25" for the $k$ axis, "26 27" for rendered count) — likely a text-extraction artifact of subscripts/superscripts, but as presented the figure is not interpretable from the text alone.
- Table 1's "ratio" column (capacity gain rep./ctrl.) is never given as an explicit formula in the caption or text; it appears to be rep./ctrl. by inspection but this should be stated.
- The paper is not anonymized (author names, affiliations, and a specific identifiable GitHub organization appear in the header and Sec. 3) — worth confirming this matches the venue's review policy if ICASSP double-blind review applies.
- "elementary machine-checked proof" (Sec. 1 contributions) is authorial framing about difficulty, not something the Lean type-checker itself certifies; consider rephrasing to avoid implying the tooling validated "elementary."
- The companion paper supplying interpretive context for the attractor framing (ref. [7], arXiv:2604.08591) is itself a very recent, evidently unreviewed preprint by the same authors; since Sec. 1 leans on it ("here the attractor is what the theorem is about"), it would help to note explicitly that this foundation is not yet independently vetted.
- The repeated-median values at $k=48$ and $k=128$ are reported as identically "25 and 25" (Sec. 4.2) and used to argue "tracking stops"; with only 21–26 generations per cell, two equal small-sample medians could plausibly reflect a coincidental floor rather than genuine saturation — a sensitivity check (e.g., mean instead of median, or per-checkpoint values rather than the pooled figure) would strengthen this specific claim.
- Abstract sentence "judged by a CTC recogniser, the obvious autoregressive judge being biased against repetition" is a dangling apposition that reads ambiguously on first pass (unclear until Sec. 3 that "the obvious ... judge" refers to Whisper, not the CTC recognizer itself).

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the formal artifact and empirical dissociation are genuine contributions and the paper is unusually candid about its own null/failed results, but the manuscript withholds the operational definitions, judge-validation details, and informal-to-formal correspondence needed to independently verify its own headline numbers, and its abstract-level certainty outruns the $n=6$, uncorrected-significance evidence it presents.
