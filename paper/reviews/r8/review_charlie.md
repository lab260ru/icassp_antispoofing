### Summary

The paper argues that autoregressive TTS models undercount repeated phrases because softmax attention over near-identical repeated spans dilutes to an approximately state-independent, per-repetition update map; if that map is a contraction, a Lean-4/mathlib-verified theorem shows any Lipschitz readout (including the stop head) loses the ability to distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon N*. Six checkpoints across three AR-TTS families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) are evaluated on a 180-item repetition ladder (k = 1–32, extended post hoc to k = 128 on four checkpoints) scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically because the more natural Whisper judge is shown to be itself repetition-biased. The headline result is a periodicity-specific, non-saturating (within k ≤ 32) undercount — median relative error −8.3% at k ≥ 6, exact-match 18.2% vs. 94.3% for length-matched distinct-word controls — that disappears in a non-autoregressive baseline (VITS) and only saturates once the ladder is extended to k = 128, while the two direct attempts to measure the theorem's contraction factor q, and a dose-response test of the attention-dilution lemma, both failed or came out sign-reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The strongest rival explanation for the paper's "sharpest test" (periodicity-specific deficit) is a purely mechanical one — decoding-time repetition penalty — and the paper's own collected ablation data to rule it out is never reported.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Models"/"Code, data, settings": "further XTTS-v2 runs at altered repetition penalty as ablations; neither is pooled into the panel," and "The penalty is the one parameter we vary"); no corresponding result appears in Section 4 or 5, and no supplementary tag (S-something) is cited for it as is done for every other stated result.
   **Why it matters** — Two of the three families ship with an explicit anti-repetition decoding heuristic (XTTS-v2 penalty 5.0 — unusually aggressive relative to typical values of 1.0–1.3; Qwen3-TTS penalty 1.05). A repetition penalty mechanically discourages re-emitting recently used tokens regardless of whether the repetition is user-requested or degenerate looping. Applied to a stimulus that is *by construction* a repeated token and to a control that is *by construction* not, this heuristic alone would generate exactly the periodicity-specific, length-invariant undercount the paper attributes to hidden-state contraction — with no contraction map, no dilution, and no fixed point required. The paper explicitly frames its own headline contrast ("periodicity, not length") as the decisive test distinguishing its theory from rivals, but the one experiment that could rule out the most obvious rival (rerun the repetition condition with the penalty neutralized, for all three families, not just XTTS-v2) was apparently run and not reported. The theorem's claim that "no property of the decoding rule enters" is a statement about the map F and readout g given a hidden-state trajectory; it says nothing about whether an explicit logit-level penalty is doing the work observed in the data.
   **What would address it** — Report the XTTS-v2 neutral-penalty ablation's count-error and exact-match numbers in the main results, and run the analogous ablation for Qwen3-TTS (penalty 1.05 → 1.0) and ideally a synthetic penalty sweep for Llasa. If the periodicity-specific deficit and the k=128 saturation persist with penalty removed, the confound is defused; if it shrinks substantially, the paper's causal story needs revision.

2. **Issue** — Every direct empirical test of the theorem's actual mechanism (Assumption 2's contraction factor q, and Lemma 1's dilution → miscounting dose-response) failed or inverted, so what remains supported is a phenomenological curve-shape match, not the proposed causal chain.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... its linearity control failing too (S5)"); Section 4.4 ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately (δ ≤ 1.23 nats)... its dose-response prediction fails outright... r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]... We record it as disconfirming").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's rhetorical structure leans on the Lean proof's rigor to lend weight to the empirical section, but the proof only certifies an implication (periodic conditioning + contraction ⟹ horizon). Assumption 1 itself is stated as an approximation ("the same to within O(δ + 1/k)"), yet Theorem 1 as formalized requires the exact autonomous map sm+1 = F(sm); no perturbed/robust version propagating the O(δ + 1/k) slack through the fixed-point bound is given or verified. Combined with the failed q-measurement and the sign-reversed dilution dose-response (flatter attention predicts *better* counting, the opposite of Lemma 1's implication), there is no empirical link — direct or indirect — connecting the formally verified mechanism to the observed deficit. What is actually demonstrated is: models undercount periodic content, proportionally to log(k) in a way that resembles a saturating curve at large k. That pattern is consistent with the theorem but equally consistent with several undifferentiated alternatives (including concern #1), and the paper's own falsification attempts undercut the specific mechanism rather than merely leaving it "unconfirmed."
   **What would address it** — Either drop or substantially soften the mechanistic framing in the abstract/title relative to the "horizon of the predicted shape" language, or invest in a working q-estimator (the authors note a boundary-distance estimator needs a monotone read-head "these decoders lack" — this is worth stating as a concrete limitation of the formal apparatus's applicability, not just a failed attempt).

3. **Issue** — The k = 128 extension and its saturating-exponential fit are explicitly post hoc, run on a non-random, non-preregistered subset of checkpoints, with a functional form selected after the k ≤ 32 proportional fit failed to show a horizon.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc, to k ∈ {48, 64, 96, 128} on the 4 checkpoints that admit utterances that long"); the ˆK fit is introduced only after "Within k ≤ 32 that deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts" (Section 4.1).
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook multiple-degrees-of-freedom setup: which checkpoints to extend (chosen by which ones "admit" long utterances, i.e., XTTS-v2 is excluded by its own token ceiling — a selection that happens to remove the family with the largest repetition-penalty confound from concern #1), which functional form to fit (proportional first, then saturating-exponential once the first failed), and which sub-ladder cutoffs to report (k ≤ 8 vs. k ≥ 48 "never-cycled" comparison) were all chosen after seeing intermediate results. The paper is commendably candid that the panel is "too small for significance testing" and that "no multiplicity correction clears 0.05" — but that candor is offered alongside a fairly strong causal narrative ("repetition lowering the fitted saturation scale 3.5-fold") built from exactly the kind of post-hoc, uncorrected comparisons the authors themselves flag as statistically unsupported.
   **What would address it** — Either preregister (even informally, e.g., in a versioned document before running k=128) the extension protocol and fit family, or explicitly downgrade the k=128 result to "exploratory" throughout rather than centering it in the abstract's causal claim ("a post-hoc extension... finds one").

4. **Issue** — The circularity control for the capacity-gain analysis (Section 4.4) compares populations with wildly different survivorship (94.3% of controls retained vs. 18.2% of repeated items), which is itself a confound the paper names but does not resolve.
   **Where** — Section 4.4: "restricting to correctly rendered items leaves the ratio at 0.52, on a subset that is admittedly not like-for-like (94.3% of controls survive that filter against 18.2%)."
   **Why it matters** — The whole point of this robustness check is to show the capacity-gain gap isn't an artifact of monotonous audio driving down effective rank. But comparing effective-rank trajectories on the "correctly rendered" 18.2% of repeated items against the "correctly rendered" 94.3% of controls compares two samples selected by entirely different mechanisms — the surviving repeated items may be systematically easier (e.g., smaller effective k, particular templates), which could itself shrink or inflate the apparent capacity-gain ratio independent of any genuine representational difference. The authors' own parenthetical concedes this but the reported 0.52 figure is still used as reassurance in the text.
   **What would address it** — Match on template/effective-k when comparing the "correct-only" subsets, or report the capacity-gain ratio stratified by k so readers can see whether the 0.50–0.52 robustness numbers hold within matched strata rather than pooled across a heavily unbalanced filter.

5. **Issue** — The non-autoregressive control condition, which anchors the paper's architectural claim, is a single model (VITS) at n = 1, run at only three seeds.
   **Where** — Section 4.3 ("We ran the same ladder through VITS... VITS is smaller than the panel, so only the within-model dissociation is compared").
   **Why it matters** — The claim that the deficit "vanishes in a non-autoregressive synthesiser run on the same ladder" (Abstract) generalizes from exactly one architecture, trained on its own data with its own duration predictor and flow vocoder. A single model cannot separate "AR vs. non-AR" as a class property from "VITS-specific" properties (training corpus, model capacity — VITS is not parameter-matched to any panel member, duration-predictor design, etc.). The within-model repeated-vs-control gap (−6.7 points) is a legitimate and useful negative control, but treating it as evidence that the effect is general to a decoding paradigm rather than idiosyncratic to VITS overstates what an n = 1 comparison can support.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one more non-AR TTS architecture (e.g., a diffusion-based or flow-matching TTS system) to the ladder before generalizing to "non-autoregressive synthesiser" as a class.

### Minor concerns

- The "0.46 of the control gain" figure in Section 4.4 doesn't obviously match a simple mean of the per-checkpoint ratios reported in Table 1 (mean ≈ 0.49); it's unclear whether 0.46 is a ratio-of-means, a weighted mean, or something else — state the exact statistic.
- Reference [1] is cited despite being "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026"; even though it's used only for background framing, citing a withdrawn preprint without further caveat in the main text (only in the bibliography annotation) is unusual practice.
- The paper doesn't specify how many of the 180 ladder items fall into each stimulus category (word-repetition, sentence-repetition, tongue twisters, number phrases) or how these are distributed across the 10 values of k, which would help readers judge whether the pooled n = 457 in Section 4.1 is dominated by any one stimulus type.
- Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB) are "available on request" rather than hosted persistently (e.g., Zenodo/HF datasets) — weaker reproducibility guarantee than the code/transcripts, which are released outright.
- Related-work coverage of TTS-specific attention-robustness fixes could be broadened; e.g., "Rhythm-controllable Attention with High Robustness for Long Sentence Speech Synthesis" [2306.02593] addresses exactly the skip/repeat/collapse robustness failure mode via attention-level fixes and isn't discussed alongside [3, 4].
- The abstract's "median count error of -8.3%" and the body's "-12.5% per checkpoint" figures for the same regime (k ≥ 6) are both given without a clear top-line statement of which is the paper's preferred summary statistic for citation purposes.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core periodicity-specific deficit and its dissociation from a non-AR baseline are a real and interesting empirical pattern, but the paper's own uncontrolled repetition-penalty confound (concern 1) directly threatens its central "periodicity, not length" claim and its ablation data to address this exists but is unreported, which alone should block acceptance until resolved.
