### Summary

The paper makes three claims: (1) a Lean-verified theorem that *if* an AR decoder's per-repetition update contracts, *then* no Lipschitz readout can recover repetition counts past a computable horizon; (2) an empirical claim that six TTS checkpoints across three families undercount repeated phrases proportionally to periodicity, not length, distinguished by matched controls and a non-AR baseline; (3) a post-hoc extension to k=128 that finds something horizon-shaped in 2–3 of 6 checkpoints. The authors are unusually candid that the theorem's premise (contraction, Assumption 2) was never established — two direct measurement attempts failed, and the companion dilution lemma's dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed. That candour is a real strength and should be credited. But candour about what wasn't shown doesn't fix what the abstract implies was shown: the headline "94.3% vs 18.2%" contrast is inflated by a design artifact the body itself flags and then downgrades to a much smaller gap; the k=128 "horizon" is admitted post-hoc and fit with a function that isn't the theorem's N*; and the single experiment built to discriminate the paper's actual causal mechanism (state erasure) from the leading rival (output-policy failure with intact representations) reports numbers that don't obviously favor their side and directly collide with the closest published result on an analogous task. My prior going in is that bold impossibility-theorem-plus-empirics packages overclaim the link between the two halves, and this paper does not clear that prior.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The abstract's sharpest statistic (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match) is inflated by a control condition that is itself periodic above k=8. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Abstract ("against 94.3% for length-matched distinct-word controls"); §4.3, where the caveat and the corrected numbers appear.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own text admits: "above k = 8 the control's fillers cycle a pool of eight, making that contrast period-1 against period-8," and when restricted to genuinely non-periodic controls (k ≤ 8, or the extension ladder's 146-word pool), the effect shrinks substantially — "54.2% against 77.9% exact at k ≤ 8, median error -0.62 against -0.12 at k ≥ 48." A reader who stops at the abstract (which is most readers) takes away a ~76-point gap; the properly controlled version is closer to a ~24-point gap, and even that comes from a different k-range than the headline. That's not a rounding difference — it's roughly a threefold shrinkage in the effect once the confound is removed, and it's buried in body text as "a caveat," not surfaced where the claim is made.
   **What would address it** — State the corrected, non-periodic-control-only numbers in the abstract, or drop the period-8-confounded comparison from the headline statistic entirely.

2. **Issue** — The k=128 "horizon" is an admittedly post-hoc search, fit with a function that is explicitly *not* the theorem's N*, and confirmed in at most half the checkpoints tested. `[Evidence — pre-registration]`
   **Where** — §4.2; abstract ("a post-hoc extension to k = 128 on 4 of them finds one... though not in the one checkpoint that shows no deficit either").
   **Why it matters** — The paper is explicit that the extension was run "post hoc," after k ≤ 32 showed no horizon — this is textbook post-hoc range-selection, disclosed but not mitigated by disclosure. Worse, the fitted quantity, ĉ(k) = K̂(1−e^{−k/K̂}), is stated to be "a saturation scale read off counts, not the theorem's N∗, a function of the unmeasured q" — so even where it "works," it doesn't test the theorem's actual prediction, only a qualitatively similar shape chosen after the fact. Of the 4 checkpoints tested, disjoint CIs are reported for only 2 (Llasa-1B, Llasa-8B); Qwen3-TTS-1.7B explicitly shows no saturation gap and inverts it; the fourth (Qwen3-TTS-0.6B) gets no individual characterization at all — it appears only inside the pooled K̂rep=30 vs K̂ctl=105 estimate. A pooled statistic driven by 2 of 4 checkpoints, with one explicit non-replication and one unreported, is thin support for "finds one."
   **What would address it** — Report the individual K̂ fit (with CI) for Qwen3-TTS-0.6B explicitly, and either derive K̂ from the theorem's parameters (µ, L, C, q) or drop the framing that this experiment tests Theorem 1's N* rather than a generic saturating-curve heuristic.

3. **Issue** — Several claims that would let a reader assess rigor are routed entirely into an unseen supplement, and some headline numbers in the main text carry no defined units. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — Scattered: exclusion criteria (S10), significance-floor calculation (S11), dose-response measure definitions (S12), δ bound (S4), q-measurement failure detail (S5), rival-hypothesis tests (S8); §5 ("a probe finds the count degraded, not erased (0.97 against 1.11)").
   **Why it matters** — A competent reader of the manuscript as given cannot verify item-exclusion logic, the exact-signed-rank calculation, or what "0.97 against 1.11" even measures (accuracy? R²? bits?) without the supplement. For a 4-page ICASSP paper this is structurally common, but it means several of the paper's most load-bearing numbers (the ones propping up the honesty narrative — "we tried and failed to measure q," "the dose-response reversed") are asserted, not shown, in the text under review.
   **What would address it** — Define units for every reported statistic inline, even briefly ("0.97 vs 1.11 nats of mutual information" or whatever it is), and pull the exclusion criteria and significance-floor arithmetic into the main text given how much weight they carry.

4. **Issue** — No experiment isolates the proposed contraction/dilution mechanism from the rival mechanism the introduction itself names: repetition-induced degenerate sampling dynamics, well documented in text LMs. `[Evidence — ablations]`
   **Where** — §1 ("the speech analogue of neural text degeneration, whose remedies are likewise sampling- or objective-level [5, 6]"); §4, where "audio-level degeneracy is detected separately and reported as its own rate" rather than tested as a competing explanation for the *proportional*, non-degenerate undercount.
   **Why it matters** — The paper distinguishes its account from length-collapse [12] and from an acoustic attractor and improbability account (S8), which is good practice. But it never runs the ablation implied by its own framing in §1: is the proportional undercount at k ≤ 32 better explained by degenerate-sampling dynamics (a known, sampling-level phenomenon, not requiring any claim about state contraction) than by the contraction story? Varying the repetition penalty is done only for XTTS-v2 as an "ablation" and is not connected back to this comparison.
   **What would address it** — Run the repetition-penalty (or temperature) sweep across all families, not just XTTS-v2, and report whether the periodicity-specific deficit tracks known degenerate-sampling levers in the way the neural-text-degeneration account would predict, versus tracking something penalty-sampling can't touch (as the contraction account would predict).

5. **Issue** — The one experiment structurally capable of distinguishing "the count is erased from the state" (this paper's causal claim) from "the count is intact but the output policy fails to use it" (the leading rival, cited as [18]) does not clearly support the paper's side, and is in direct tension with the closest published result on an analogous task. `[Evidence — distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — §5, Discussion: "A competing account holds that in text LMs the count survives in the residual stream, only the output policy failing [18]; our probes favour representational degradation, and deployed mitigations act too late" and "a probe finds the count degraded, not erased (0.97 against 1.11)."
   **Why it matters** — This is Theorem 1's actual mechanism under test: contraction is supposed to make *every* Lipschitz readout — "the stop-token logit included" — unable to separate counts past N*, which should manifest as probe accuracy collapsing at deep layers/large k, not as mild degradation. The number reported, 0.97 against 1.11 (units undefined — see Concern 3), reads as a modest gap, not erasure. That's a problem on its own terms. It's a bigger problem against the literature: Venkatesh [2605.09239], on repeated-token counting in text LLMs — the directly comparable setting — reports via linear probes on the residual stream that "the correct count [is decoded] with near-perfect accuracy at every post-embedding layer and they do so even at the exact layers where the wrong answer crystallizes in the output," i.e. representations stay intact while only the output policy fails — the opposite signature from what this paper's mechanism requires and claims to have found. The paper devotes one clause to this tension and asserts its own probe "favours representational degradation" without giving the reader the layer-by-layer, k-swept comparison that would actually let the two hypotheses be told apart. As reported, this is consistent with either story, and the one external data point that speaks to it favors the account the paper argues against.
   **What would address it** — Run the Venkatesh-style analysis directly: probe accuracy for the true count at every layer, swept across k, plotted against the output-error curve. If accuracy stays flat while output error rises (as in [2605.09239]), the paper's central mechanism is wrong for this modality too, and that should be reported as such rather than folded into "the count degraded, not erased."

### Minor concerns

- No qualitative transcript examples appear anywhere in the main text — nothing to accuse of cherry-picking, but also nothing a reader can sanity-check by eye; at least one worked example per condition would help.
- The Whisper-vs-CTC judge audit (§3) is a genuine methodological strength — flagged here for balance, not as a criticism.
- Table 1's "capacity gain dNeff/d log k" column and Figure 1(c)'s y-axis are never given units or a worked formula for Neff beyond "effective rank of the trajectory" in Methods.
- The RSS comparison in §4.1 (485.3 vs 2.6) is reported with no uncertainty (no bootstrap CI, no AIC/BIC, no p-value) despite driving a fairly strong claim ("the count tracks the request, so no horizon lies inside this ladder").
- Treating "Llasa" (1B/3B/8B, same backbone/tokenizer, differing only in scale) as one independent "family" for the n≈3-replicate analysis is reasonable but still leaves the family-level test very underpowered; say so plainly rather than only at the item/checkpoint level.
- Ref [1] is cited as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" with no comment on whether its withdrawal bears on the alignment-analysis claim being borrowed from it.
- Ref [15] (arXiv:2608.08922, 2026) is essentially contemporaneous with the paper itself; I could not independently verify it via search within budget — flagging as unverified, not wrong.
- "a conditional theorem behaving conditionally" (§4.2) is a cute line that doesn't add information beyond what the data already say — minor style nit, not representative of the paper as a whole, which is otherwise precise.
- Figure 1(b)'s axis labels as extracted ("2⁶ 2⁷", "2⁵ 2⁶ 2⁷") suggest log-scale ticks that may not have rendered cleanly; worth double-checking the camera-ready figure.

### Belief update

I moved a little on "current AR TTS checkpoints undercount long runs of a repeated phrase specifically because of periodicity, not just length" — the effect survives in the properly non-periodic-control subset (54.2% vs 77.9%, -0.62 vs -0.12), just at roughly a third the size the abstract implies. I did not move on "this is caused by a contracting per-repetition state map" — the paper itself couldn't measure the premise, and its own dilution lemma's dose-response prediction reversed sign. On the specific question the paper spends its Discussion section on — whether the failure is representational (state erased) or purely a policy/output-head problem (state intact, per Venkatesh) — I did not update toward the paper's preferred account, and if anything lean slightly toward the rival given the one directly relevant published result points the other way and the paper's own numbers don't clearly discriminate. The Lean formalization is a nice artifact and plausibly a first for this subfield, but since its premise is never empirically pinned down, it doesn't by itself move my belief about what's happening inside these decoders.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the strongest concern is that the paper's one experiment designed to test its actual causal mechanism (state erasure vs. intact-representation/policy failure) doesn't clearly support its own side and conflicts with the closest published analogue [2605.09239]; until that's resolved with a layer-by-layer, k-swept probe comparison, the theorem and the empirics remain two separately-defensible pieces that the paper presents as one connected result.
