### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive (AR) TTS models' well-documented tendency to mis-render repeated phrases ("say a word twelve times") reflects a generic length-driven degradation or a periodicity-specific failure predicted by a fixed-point argument: if the per-repetition update on the decoder's hidden state contracts, no Lipschitz readout (including the stop head) can distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon. This implication is proved and machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six checkpoints across three AR families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) are run on a 180-item repetition ladder (k up to 32, extended post hoc to 128 on four checkpoints) and scored with a CTC recognizer chosen because the more obvious Whisper judge is itself shown to be repetition-biased. The headline result is a periodicity-specific undercount (median relative error −8.3% pooled, −12.5% per-checkpoint at k≥6, exact-match 18.2%) that is essentially absent in length-matched, non-repeated controls (94.3% exact) and absent in a non-autoregressive baseline (VITS), while the theorem's core premise (state-map contraction) is explicitly not verified — two direct attempts to measure it failed, and a dose-response test of the companion attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — Decoding-time repetition penalty, which differs sharply and is uncontrolled across the three model families, is a ready non-theoretical explanation for the entire periodicity-vs-length dissociation, and the one ablation designed to isolate it is never reported.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Sampling uses shipped defaults (XTTS-v2 T=0.75/0.85/50/penalty 5.0; Llasa T=0.8, top-p 1.0; Qwen3-TTS T=0.9/1.0/50/1.05) ... The penalty is the one parameter we vary"); results of that variation are absent from Section 4.
   **Why it matters** — A logit-level repetition penalty is, by construction, a mechanism that suppresses tokens because they repeat, not because of sequence length. It targets exactly the "periodicity, not length" signature the paper treats as the theorem's diagnostic signature (Section 4.3), with zero need for any hidden-state contraction. XTTS-v2 ships with a comparatively strong penalty (5.0) against Llasa's apparently unmodified sampler and Qwen3-TTS's mild 1.05, so cross-family "generality" claims (Table 1, "positive in all 6 checkpoints") confound architecture with an explicit anti-repetition decoding heuristic. The paper says it ran the exact ablation that would settle this ("further XTTS-v2 runs at altered repetition penalty") but never reports its outcome — the single most direct falsification test of the paper's own claim is conducted and withheld.
   **What would address it** — Report the penalty-ablation results explicitly: does the count-error/exact-match gap shrink or vanish as penalty → 1.0 (or → 0) at fixed temperature? If the gap survives penalty ablation, that materially strengthens the internal-state account; if it doesn't, the theorem's premise may be irrelevant to what was actually measured. Ideally, re-run the full panel (or at least Llasa vs. XTTS-v2) at matched, penalty-neutral decoding settings.

2. **Issue** — The k=128 extension, which supplies the paper's only direct evidence for a horizon of "the predicted shape," was explicitly decided *after* the k≤32 data failed to show the predicted saturation, with no pre-registration of the extended design, its checkpoint subset, or its exclusion rules.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc, to k ∈ {48,64,96,128} on the 4 checkpoints that admit utterances that long"); Section 5 ("Extending to k=128 found saturation of the predicted kind but not one derived from a measured q").
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook HARKing pattern: a null result at the pre-specified design scope (k≤32) triggers a new, unregistered probe at a different scope, whose positive result is then reported as confirmatory of "a horizon of the predicted shape." The subset selection (4 of 6 checkpoints, chosen because they "admit" long utterances — XTTS-v2 excluded by its own token ceiling) and per-cell exclusions (budget-truncated and "degenerate" generations dropped, leaving 21–26 of ~60 per cell) are all decided with the k≤32 results already in hand. Worse, one of the four extended checkpoints (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows *no* saturation and an *inverted* ratio — disconfirming evidence that is reframed as "a conditional theorem behaving conditionally" rather than counted against the horizon claim.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the k=128 result to a clearly labeled exploratory finding not counted toward the theorem's confirmation, or pre-register a follow-up extension (fixed k grid, fixed checkpoint set decided before seeing k≤32 data, fixed exclusion rule) and replicate. At minimum, report the extension result with the same skepticism applied to the dilution dose-response reversal, rather than folding a 1-of-4 failure into "predicted shape."

3. **Issue** — The paper's own stated "sharpest test" (periodicity vs. length, 94.3% vs. 18.2% exact) is built substantially on a confounded control: above k=8 the control itself repeats a pool of only eight fillers, i.e., it has periodicity 8, not "no periodicity."
   **Where** — Section 4.3 ("above k=8 the control's fillers cycle a pool of eight, making that contrast period-1 against period-8... Where controls carry no repetition at all — k≤8... it survives: 54.2% against 77.9% exact at k≤8").
   **Why it matters** — The headline contrast (94.3% vs. 18.2%) is pooled across all k, including the k>8 region where the control is not a clean length-matched non-periodic baseline. When the confound is removed (k≤8 subset), the exact-match gap collapses from ~76 points to ~24 points (54.2 vs. 77.9) — under a third of the advertised effect. A design billed as the paper's decisive test of periodicity-vs-length should not itself contain periodicity in the "distinct word" arm for most of its range.
   **What would address it** — Either draw a non-cycling filler pool large enough to cover k up to 32 (and 128), or restrict all headline periodicity-vs-length statistics to the k≤8, non-cycled regime and report the k>8 numbers as a separately labeled, confound-acknowledged extension rather than blending both into one headline percentage.

4. **Issue** — The CTC judge's validity is established on concatenative (spliced) ground-truth audio, not on genuine model-generated repeated speech, and no human-transcribed reference sample is reported for the actual test items.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Behavioural measurement" paragraph ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth... ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper").
   **Why it matters** — Concatenative repeats splice together clean, independently-recorded segments; AR-model-generated repeated speech under a stalling or looping decoder can have distinctive prosodic degradation (monotone pitch, timing drift, near-duplicate frames flagged separately as "audio-level degeneracy"). A CTC recognizer's transcription accuracy on that specific artifact profile is exactly what needs auditing, and it hasn't been — the audit validates the judge on a different acoustic distribution than the one it is asked to score. This leaves open a residual possibility that part of the periodicity-specific undercount is a scoring artifact rather than a generation artifact, a possibility the VITS control only partially addresses (it shares the judge but not the AR decoder's specific degenerate-audio profile).
   **What would address it** — Report inter-rater agreement (or at least a human-transcribed spot-check) between the CTC judge and human transcribers on a sample of actual model-generated repeated-word audio, stratified by k, not just on concatenative ground truth.

5. **Issue** — The theorem's load-bearing premise (Assumption 2, contraction) has no positive empirical support: two independent attempts to measure the contraction factor q failed outright, and the companion dilution lemma's dose-response prediction is sign-reversed.
   **Where** — Section 4.4 ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately... its dose-response prediction fails outright: within a (model, k) cell, items with flatter attention are counted better, not worse (r = 0.59...)"); Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated items and their controls (7 of 18 pairs, p=0.12)").
   **Why it matters** — This is the crux for an adversarial reading. The theorem licenses the paper's causal narrative only if contraction actually occurs; absent that, the observed behavioral dissociation is consistent with the theorem but not evidence *for* the specific mechanism the theorem formalizes — it is equally consistent with an entirely different mechanism (e.g., learned repetition/stop heuristics, training-data scarcity for long repeated spans, the decoding-time penalty confound in concern 1). The dose-response reversal is especially damaging: it is the one place the paper made a directional, falsifiable prediction from the mechanism and got the opposite sign. The paper is candid about this, but the framing ("What is proved is an implication; what is measured is a periodicity-specific deficit and a horizon of the predicted shape") risks readers crediting the theorem with explanatory work the data do not support.
   **What would address it** — Reframe the theorem explicitly as a candidate explanation among several (the paper gestures at this with its refutation criteria in Section 5 but doesn't apply the same rigor to its own preferred account), and prioritize experiments that could actually estimate q under a monotone-readout design, rather than reporting only the two failed attempts as an aside.

6. **Issue** — All "consistent across every checkpoint and every family" language rests on an n=6 (effectively n=3-family) sample with acknowledged insufficient power for significance testing, no correction for the many comparisons made (count error, exact-match, capacity gain, dose-response, three k-regimes), and no mixed-effects modeling to account for within-checkpoint/within-family correlation in the pooled item-level statistics.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("At either level the panel is too small for significance testing — the exact signed-rank floor at n=6 is 0.031 and no multiplicity correction clears 0.05 (S11) — so we rest on effect sizes and their consistency"); Abstract ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family").
   **Why it matters** — The authors are commendably transparent that they cannot claim statistical significance, but the prose throughout (abstract, Section 4.1, 4.3) nonetheless uses categorical, universal-quantifier language ("every checkpoint," "every model," "vanishes... in every checkpoint and every family") that reads as far stronger than an n=3-family effect-size observation supports. Given concern 1 (uncontrolled penalty confound) and concern 2 (post-hoc extension), the actual independent evidence base for universality is thin.
   **What would address it** — Either scale the panel (more checkpoints per family, more families) to support the claimed generality, or consistently hedge the prose to effect-size language ("consistent in direction across the checkpoints tested") rather than "every."

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is cited for background despite being marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — leaning on a withdrawn preprint's alignment analysis, even for framing, is worth flagging since its content can no longer be independently checked in its original venue.
- The companion self-citation [7] ("arXiv:2604.08591") and refs [15], [18] are very recent (2026) preprints that could not be corroborated via search beyond the paper's own description; claims of consistency with [7]'s "attractor regime" framing are not independently verifiable from this text.
- Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB) are "available on request" rather than hosted, which weakens independent verification relative to the otherwise strong reproducibility posture (Lean proof, code, stimuli, transcripts on GitHub).
- No sample sizes or confidence intervals are given for the Whisper/CTC judge audit ratios (0.27–0.65, 1.00, 0.19), making it hard to judge how precisely judge validity was established.
- "the count stays proportional rather than saturating" (Section 4.1) and the later "not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts" reads as a mid-paper concession that could be flagged more prominently in the abstract rather than folded into the same sentence as the eventual (post-hoc) rescue.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" (0.21–0.80) is fourfold variable across checkpoints (SD 16.0 on mean 24.7) yet described as "general in direction, not magnitude" — a fair characterization, but the huge spread deserves at least a candidate explanation (e.g., correlation with the penalty confound in concern 1) rather than being left unexplained.
- VITS is described as showing "no dissociation" despite a nonzero −6.7-point repeated-vs-control gap in the same direction as the AR panel; "no dissociation" slightly overstates a result that is smaller, not absent.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the formal result and the overall research question are of real interest, but the headline empirical claim rests on an unreported penalty-ablation confound, a post-hoc/non-pre-registered range extension, and a "sharpest test" whose own confound (control periodicity) shrinks the effect by roughly two-thirds when corrected, any one of which needs resolving before the claimed periodicity-specific mechanism can be credited over simpler decoding-level explanations.
