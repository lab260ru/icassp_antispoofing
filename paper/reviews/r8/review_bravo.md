### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech (TTS) decoders lose count when a text prompt repeats a phrase many times, and proposes a mechanistic account: if softmax attention over k repeated spans dilutes to Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1) and the resulting per-repetition state update is a q-contractive map (Assumption 2), then no Lipschitz readout — including the stop-token logit — can separate repetition counts beyond a horizon N* that is computable from the contraction factor, orbit scale, and decision margin (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six AR checkpoints across three model families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) are evaluated on a 180-item repetition ladder scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically to avoid the AR bias of Whisper; repeated items show a median count error of roughly -8 to -12% at k≥6 and only 18.2% exact-match, against ~0% error and 94.3% exact-match for length-matched non-repetitive controls, a dissociation absent in a non-AR baseline (VITS). Within k≤32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating (no horizon visible), but a post-hoc extension to k=128 on four checkpoints finds saturation of the predicted shape (with one checkpoint an explicit exception). The paper explicitly reports that two independent attempts to measure the theorem's contraction premise (Assumption 2) failed, and that a dose-response test of Lemma 1 came out sign-reversed — leaving the theorem's implication proved but its premise unconfirmed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The empirical "fingerprint" that stands in for the unconfirmed theorem is built almost entirely on n=6 (self-admitted effective n≈3) comparisons, a post-hoc, apparently pattern-seeking extension of the stimulus ladder, and reports intervals/statistics (bootstrap CIs, curve-fit RSS) whose validity at this sample size is not established.
   **Where** — §4.1–4.2 (ladder extension to k=128), §4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc, to k ∈ {48,64,96,128}"), Table 1, and the "mean 76.7 points [68.4, 84.4]" / capacity-gain bootstrap CIs in §4.2/§4.4.
   **Why it matters** — Steelmanned, the argument is that a multi-part signature — proportional-then-saturating undercount, periodicity-specificity, absence in a non-AR baseline, and consistency across three architecturally distinct families — is distinctive enough that only the periodic-contraction account explains all of it jointly, and the paper is unusually careful to mark what is proved versus measured. But the statistical infrastructure under that signature is thin. (a) The k=128 extension was run *after* the k≤32 ladder failed to show the theorem's predicted saturation ("not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts"); the paper is honest that this was post hoc, but a stopping rule chosen to keep extending exactly until the predicted pattern emerges is a textbook optional-stopping/garden-of-forking-paths problem, and no correction (e.g., for the family-wise error inflated by the earlier failed test at k≤32, the later successful one at k=128, plus the ~10 further secondary analyses — dose-response r, covariate-adjusted capacity-gain ratios 0.50/0.52, RSS comparison, period-1-vs-period-8 caveat check) is applied anywhere in the paper except the one place (§4.2) where the authors themselves note "no multiplicity correction clears 0.05." (b) Bootstrap confidence intervals computed over 6 checkpoints (§4.2, capacity-gain panel) are not statistically well-founded — with n=6 the resampling distribution is coarse and the reported intervals likely understate true uncertainty; this should be flagged as approximate at best, not treated as an interval estimate on par with the item-level bootstraps (n=457, n=525) used elsewhere. (c) The headline per-checkpoint count-error statistic in Table 1 is suspiciously quantized: four of six checkpoints (Llasa-1B, Llasa-3B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B) report an *identical* -12.5% median error despite different architectures, tokenizers, and sampling settings. A median that lands on the exact same rational value (consistent with an off-by-one count out of 8) across four unrelated models is much more likely to be a discretization artifact of a coarse, integer-valued error metric computed over a small number of surviving items per cell than four independent architectures converging on one continuous effect size. If so, the "consistency across families" that the abstract leans on ("in every checkpoint and every family") is partly a resolution artifact rather than substantive agreement, and this should be checked and disclosed.
   **What would address it** — Report the per-checkpoint item counts feeding each median explicitly, and show whether the -12.5% recurrence survives a continuity-corrected or interpolated error statistic. Pre-register (or at minimum flag as exploratory with a multiplicity-adjusted α) the decision to extend the ladder past k=32. Replace or supplement the n=6 bootstrap CIs with an exact permutation/sign-based interval appropriate to that sample size, and consider a sample-efficient evaluation design (cf. small-sample reliability estimation for saturated benchmarks, e.g. [2605.11209]) rather than ad hoc bootstrap over so few units.

2. **Issue** — The theorem's central mechanism (Assumption 2, contraction) is never established, and the two attempts to measure it are themselves underpowered, yet the paper's contributions list and title still assert a "quantitative impossibility result" for these decoders.
   **Where** — §2 (Assumption 2), §5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... a perturbation probe found no difference... 7 of 18 pairs, p=0.12, its linearity control failing too").
   **Why it matters** — A perturbation probe over only 18 pairs testing for a subtle local-contraction difference is very low power; p=0.12 with n=18 is uninformative about whether the effect is absent versus merely undetected, and the paper does not report an a priori power calculation, an effect size the probe was powered to detect, or why n=18 was chosen. Without the premise established, "quantitative impossibility result for repetition counting in AR decoders" (Introduction, Contributions) overstates what has been shown for these specific decoders — the theorem is validated only as a conditional mathematical statement, and the abstract's own more careful "an implication... a periodicity-specific deficit and a horizon of the predicted shape" is a better description than the contributions paragraph.
   **What would address it** — Either scale up the contraction probe with a documented power analysis, or soften the contributions language throughout the paper (not only in the abstract/discussion) to consistently reflect that Assumption 2 remains unverified for every checkpoint tested.

3. **Issue** — Extensive reliance on supplementary sections (S4, S5, S8, S10, S11, S12) for details necessary to evaluate central claims (the attention-dilution measurement, the dose-response design, the rival-hypothesis tests, the exclusion criteria, and the multiplicity discussion), none of which are reproduced in the reviewed text.
   **Where** — §3 ("S10" cited three times), §4.2 ("S10", "S11"), §4.3 ("S8"), §4.4 ("S12"), §5 ("S5").
   **Why it matters** — Several of the paper's load-bearing statistical judgments (the exact sign-rank floor and multiplicity claim in §4.2, the dose-response correlation methodology in §4.4, the exclusion/truncation criteria in §3) are asserted but not shown in the material under review, making it impossible to verify whether the analyses were specified before seeing the data, how many alternative operationalizations were tried, or whether exclusion criteria were applied uniformly.
   **What would address it** — Either the supplement should be reviewed alongside the main text, or the paper should inline the minimum statistical detail (test used, exact exclusion counts, correction method attempted) needed to evaluate each claim without cross-referencing an unseen document.

4. **Issue** — The panel size and design leave essentially no room to distinguish "capacity loss is necessary but not sufficient" (the paper's own framing for the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B exception) from "the effect is inconsistent and partially confounded by decoding stochasticity."
   **Where** — §4.2 ("Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation... and inverts the ratio"), §3 (sampling settings note "Qwen3-TTS is the most stochastic family").
   **Why it matters** — The paper itself flags that Qwen3-TTS uses the most stochastic sampling settings of the three families (T=0.9/1.0, top-p 50, penalty 1.05) and separately that Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is the one checkpoint without a deficit. With only one family member showing this exception, and that family already singled out as an outlier on sampling stochasticity, the data cannot adjudicate between the theorem-consistent explanation offered ("capacity loss is necessary and not sufficient") and a simpler confound (different, more diverse decoding statistics reducing the AR contraction effect via exposure to a wider effective state distribution). Both explanations fit one data point equally well.
   **What would address it** — Run Qwen3-TTS-1.7B (and ideally the 0.6B sibling) at matched, less stochastic decoding settings to test whether the null result persists, isolating architecture from sampling temperature as the source of the exception.

### Minor concerns

- The number "18.2%" appears twice with different meanings: the pooled exact-match rate for repeated items (§4.3, Abstract) and, coincidentally, Llasa-1B's rep. capacity-gain percentage in Table 1. This invites misreading; consider using non-overlapping values or explicit labeling in text near Table 1.
- Reference [1] is cited for "its alignment analysis" but is annotated "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" with no stated reason for withdrawal; leaning on a withdrawn preprint to motivate the problem statement without noting why it was withdrawn is a citation-hygiene gap.
- No explicit statement addresses conflicts of interest or funding, and reference [7] is a self-citation to the same author group's companion paper — reasonable given the stated "companion" relationship, but worth a one-line disclosure.
- Audio (2.5 GB) and activations (25 GB) are "available on request" rather than hosted, which is weaker than the code/stimuli/transcripts already released on GitHub; for a reproducibility-forward paper this is an inconsistency worth resolving (e.g., a persistent-identifier archive).
- "the median error gap is positive in 5 of 6, zero in Qwen3-TTS-1.7B" (§4.2) and "capacity gain... positive in all 6" (§4.4) are both true but juxtaposed loosely across sections; a single summary table cross-tabulating which of the paper's several dissociation criteria each checkpoint satisfies would make the exception-handling easier to audit.
- The claim "to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact" (Introduction) is plausible but unverifiable from the given text alone; a brief search turned up no contradicting prior art, but the claim would be stronger with an explicit comparison to the expressivity/formal-verification literature already cited ([16, 17]) explaining why those do not anticipate this result.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the theorem and its Lean formalization are a genuine contribution and the paper is unusually candid about what remains unproven, but the empirical case resting on it (n≈3–6 effective replicates, a post-hoc extended ladder, unexplained CI methodology at very small n, and a plausible quantization artifact in the headline effect size) needs tighter statistical treatment and either stronger data or more consistently hedged claims before the "counting collapse" account can be presented as more than a conditional, partially-supported hypothesis.
