### Summary
The paper asks whether repetition-induced counting failures in autoregressive TTS decoders (looping, truncation, early stopping) have a common formal cause rather than being a grab-bag of exposure-bias or alignment artifacts. It models one repetition boundary as a state map F on the decoder's hidden state, proves (Lean 4 + mathlib, "sorry"-free) that if F is a q-contraction (q<1) then no L-Lipschitz readout can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* = log(µ/2LC)/log q, and pairs this with an attention-dilution lemma showing softmax weight over k repeated spans flattens as O(1/k). Empirically, six TTS checkpoints across three families are scored with a CTC recognizer (chosen over Whisper because Whisper's autoregressive decoder is itself shown to be biased against counting periodic content) on a repetition ladder up to k=32, later extended to k=128. The headline results are: a one-directional undercount (median -8.3% at k≥6) that stays roughly proportional to k rather than saturating within k≤32; a sharp contrast between repeated items (18.2% exact) and length-matched non-adjacent controls (94.3% exact), taken as evidence the failure tracks periodicity rather than length; slower growth of trajectory effective rank under repetition (0.46× the control rate); and, on the extended ladder, a fitted saturation horizon of N*_rep=23 versus N*_ctl=94 repetitions, offered as the empirical signature of Theorem 1's predicted horizon — while the paper explicitly reports that two independent attempts to directly measure the contraction factor q failed to find one.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's headline "the theorem's consequence" claim conflates the theorem's analytically defined horizon N* (Eq. 1, a function of the measured contraction rate q, Lipschitz constant L, orbit scale C, and readout margin µ) with a same-named but structurally unrelated curve-fit parameter from an exponential saturation model ĉ(k)=N*(1−e^{−k/N*}) fit to raw counts in Section 4.2.
   **Where** — Abstract ("extending to k = 128 locates it, at N∗ rep = 23 … — repetition lowers the counting horizon 4.1-fold"); Section 4.2 ("That is the theorem's consequence, just past where our original ladder stopped"); Section 5 confirms the premise (Assumption 2, contraction) "remains unestablished" and that the finite-difference probe for q found "no difference in local contraction between repeated items and their controls (7 of 18 pairs in the predicted direction, p = 0.12)."
   **Why it matters** — These are two different statistical objects: one is a theoretical quantity derived from model-internal dynamics that was never measured on these checkpoints; the other is a free parameter of a phenomenological curve fit to output counts, chosen post hoc because a "plateau" shape looked qualitatively consistent with what a horizon should look like. Sharing the symbol N* invites readers (and the Abstract) to treat curve-fit agreement with a saturating functional form as confirmation of the theorem, when no quantitative link between the fitted N* and the theorem's q/L/C/µ was ever established — the paper's own p=0.12, n=18 null result on q is the direct evidence that this link is unverified. This is the paper's central empirical claim, and it rests on an unsupported identification between a fit parameter and a theoretical bound.
   **What would address it** — Rename the empirical fit parameter (e.g., k̂½ or N_fit) to avoid notational identity with the theorem's N*; explicitly report the range of q, L, C, µ that would be required to reproduce the fitted N*_rep=23 under Eq. 1, and check whether that implied q is plausible given whatever contraction evidence exists (even indirectly, e.g., via the effective-rank decay rate); or drop the "that is the theorem's consequence" framing in favor of "consistent with, but not derived from, the theorem."

2. **Issue** — The choice of pooling unit changes the reported headline effect size by nearly 2×, and this sensitivity is not surfaced anywhere.
   **Where** — Abstract/Section 4.1: "Pooled at k ≥ 6, repeated items are rendered with a median error of -8.3%"; Table 1 per-checkpoint values are -12.5, -8.3, -16.7, -12.5, -12.5, 0.0.
   **Why it matters** — The unweighted median of the six per-checkpoint values in Table 1 is -12.5% (the two middle values, both -12.5), a clearly larger effect than the item-level-pooled -8.3% quoted in the Abstract. Item-level pooling (n=457) implicitly weights checkpoints by however many generations/items each contributed, so the headline number is sensitive to an unreported allocation of items across models rather than reflecting a robust central tendency across the checkpoint population the paper claims to be sampling ("six checkpoints spanning three families"). Readers cannot tell from the text whether -8.3% is representative of "the typical model" or is pulled toward whichever model happened to have more generations.
   **What would address it** — Report both the item-pooled and the checkpoint-level (unweighted) median with CIs, or use a mixed-effects model with checkpoint as a random effect so the aggregate estimate is not silently a function of per-checkpoint sample size.

3. **Issue** — Throughout Results, the paper's evidentiary standard for "the effect holds" is a series of small, differently-denominated dichotomized fractions of checkpoints (5/6, 4/6, 2/2, "disjoint in 5 of 6," "positive gap in 80% of pairs," "4 of 6 checkpoints show it"), reported without a common significance test, without correction for the many endpoints tested, and without reconciling why the numerator changes across ostensibly related sub-claims.
   **Where** — Introduction ("4 of 6 checkpoints show it"); Section 4.1 ("in 5 of 6 checkpoints (S10)"); Section 4.2 ("disjoint in 2 of 2 checkpoints… The effect separates from its own control in 4 of 6 checkpoints"); Section 4.4 ("disjoint 95% intervals for 5 of 6 panel checkpoints; the matched-pair form… gives a positive gap in 80% of pairs… gain stays positive in 5 of 6 checkpoints").
   **Why it matters** — With only six checkpoints (three families, several of which are scale variants of the same architecture and hence not independent samples), "k of 6" statements have almost no statistical power to distinguish a real, consistent effect from noise, yet they are used repeatedly as the paper's de facto significance test. The numerator drifts across sections (5/6, then 4/6, then 5/6 again) for claims that read as closely related aspects of "the effect," with no accounting of whether the same checkpoint (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B, explicitly flagged as a null case) is the one failing each time or different ones — this matters directly for whether "capacity loss is necessary but not sufficient" (the paper's own gloss on Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) is a principled distinction or a rationalization of an inconvenient data point. No correction for multiple comparisons is applied across the roughly ten distinct quantitative contrasts run in Section 4.
   **What would address it** — Replace ad hoc fraction-counting with a single pre-specified mixed-effects or hierarchical model (checkpoint as random effect, condition — repeated vs. control — as fixed effect) that yields one effect size and CI per outcome, report which specific checkpoints deviate and why in a supplementary table, and apply a multiple-comparison correction (or explicitly justify why none is needed) across the family of tests in Section 4.

4. **Issue** — The paper's most quotable number (N*_rep=23 vs N*_ctl=94, a "4.1-fold" reduction) is derived from only 2 of the 6 checkpoints and, per the authors' own admission, "few items per cell," yet appears in the Abstract with no power caveat.
   **Where** — Abstract; Section 4.2 ("the extension covers 2 checkpoints with few items per cell… a pure horizon predicts a plateau whereas the repeated median declines (27 →15)"); Section 5 restates the same limitation.
   **Why it matters** — A reader encountering only the Abstract would take "4.1-fold" as the paper's confirmed, well-powered finding; only in the body, and more candidly in Discussion, is it downgraded to "an upper bound" obtained from a fit whose own shape (declining rather than plateauing median) is acknowledged to be inconsistent with the theorem's plateau prediction. This is a genuine power problem (n=2 checkpoints for the central quantitative claim of the paper) dressed as a headline result.
   **What would address it** — State the checkpoint count and per-cell item count for the extended ladder directly in the Abstract, alongside the CI already reported; consider withholding a precise fold-change figure until the ladder is extended across more checkpoints, and lead instead with the qualitative (proportional→leveling) finding, which the n=2 data do support more robustly than a specific ratio does.

5. **Issue** — Confidence intervals are reported throughout (median error [-12.5,-6.2], N* fits [16,39]/[71,111]) without stating the resampling unit, except for the capacity-gain bootstrap, which is explicitly "over templates."
   **Where** — Section 4.1 ("median error of -8.3% [−12.5, −6.2] (n = 457)"); Section 4.2 (N* CIs); contrast with Section 4.4, which specifies "bootstrapped over templates."
   **Why it matters** — Repeated measurements from the same template, same checkpoint, or same seed are correlated; resampling at the item level rather than the template or checkpoint level would understate true uncertainty and produce artificially narrow, "disjoint" intervals — exactly the intervals the paper leans on as its evidentiary bar (e.g., "disjoint in 5 of 6 checkpoints" in Section 4.4). Since the paper's own significance argument is built from interval disjointness rather than formal hypothesis tests, the resampling unit is not a cosmetic detail — it determines whether the central "disjoint CI" claims are trustworthy.
   **What would address it** — State the resampling unit for every reported CI, and use the same (appropriately clustered) resampling scheme throughout rather than only for the capacity-gain statistic.

6. **Issue** — Generations that hit the token budget are excluded "throughout" (Method, S10), but this exclusion is least controlled precisely in the k=128 extension where output-length saturation is the phenomenon being measured, and the paper reports no per-condition exclusion counts.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Generations stopped by our token budget rather than the model… are excluded throughout (S10)"); Section 4.2 (the k=48–128 saturation finding).
   **Why it matters** — At high k, a decoder that "should" keep repeating for longer is more likely to be cut off by the token budget, and if repeated-condition items hit that budget more often than length-matched controls (plausible, since loop/degenerate continuations run long), excluding those items removes exactly the tail of the distribution that would distinguish "genuine internal saturation" from "we truncated before the model was done." Since the reported N*_rep/N*_ctl gap is the paper's flagship number, an unreported, condition-asymmetric exclusion process at this specific k range is a serious potential confound that the paper does not rule out.
   **What would address it** — Report exclusion counts and rates separately for repeated vs. control arms at each extended-ladder k value, and show the saturation fit is robust to raising the token budget (or to modeling exclusion explicitly, e.g., as right-censoring in the ĉ(k) fit rather than by discarding truncated items).

### Minor concerns
- Notation: N* denotes both the theorem's analytic bound (Eq. 1) and the Section 4.2 curve-fit parameter; even setting aside Major Concern 1's substantive issue, using distinct symbols would reduce reader confusion on its own.
- Table 1's "ratio" column (capacity gain rep./ctl.) isn't explicitly defined as such in the table or caption — it's only inferable from body text ("a factor 0.46 of the control gain").
- Fig. 1(a) legend includes "xtts2rp2/rp3/rp8" (the repetition-penalty ablation arms) plotted alongside the main six checkpoints, which sits awkwardly next to the Methods statement that these ablations are "reported separately and never pooled" — if they're drawn on the same axes as the pooled comparison, the caption should clarify they are visually distinct and excluded from the pooled statistics.
- Reference [1] is cited as support ("cited for its alignment analysis") despite being marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" in the bibliography; a one-line note on why it was withdrawn (methodological retraction vs. venue issue) would help readers weigh how much to trust the alignment analysis being cited.
- The released code repository is named "icassp_antispoofing" (github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing), which does not match this paper's subject (TTS repetition-counting collapse) — worth double-checking the link/README actually points reviewers to the right artifact.
- Prose is extremely compressed/aphoristic throughout ("Worse in absolute WER, right for this purpose"; "a probe finds the count degraded rather than erased (0.97 against 1.11)" with no units given for these two numbers in-line) — several results-bearing sentences would benefit from stating units/baselines inline rather than relying on the reader to infer them from context.
- Section 4.4's "0.97 against 1.11" (Discussion) is never defined in the main text — unclear what statistic or units these refer to.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal (Lean-verified) theorem and lemma appear sound and are a genuine, clearly-scoped contribution, but the empirical section's headline numbers (especially the 4.1-fold horizon claim) are built on underpowered comparisons, an unexplained conflation between a theoretical and a curve-fit quantity sharing the same symbol, uncorrected multiple dichotomized-fraction comparisons, and an unquantified truncation confound at exactly the k range the central claim depends on.
