### Summary
This paper argues that repetition-induced hallucination in autoregressive TTS (undercounting, looping, truncation on repeated phrases) has a common dynamical-systems cause: when text repeats a phrase k times, softmax attention over the repeated spans flattens (Lemma 1, "attention dilution"), the decoder's per-repetition state update becomes approximately autonomous, and if that update contracts (Assumption 2), any Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — loses the ability to distinguish repetition counts beyond a computable horizon N* (Theorem 1). Both results are formalized end-to-end in Lean 4/mathlib with a clean axiom audit. Empirically, across six checkpoints from three TTS families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B), scored with a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the known repetition-bias of an autoregressive judge (Whisper), repeated stimuli are undercounted by a median -8.3% at k≥6 and are exact only 18.2% of the time versus 94.3% for length-matched non-repetitive controls, and internal-state effective rank grows more slowly under repetition (0.46× the control rate). Within the original ladder (k≤32) the deficit scales proportionally with k rather than saturating — not the theorem's signature — but an extension to k≤128 on two checkpoints finds a leveling-off, with N*rep ≈ 23 versus N*ctl ≈ 94, which the authors read as locating (not confirming) the predicted horizon, while explicitly noting that the theorem's contraction premise itself remains empirically unverified.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The empirical panel is too narrow, and partly non-applicable, to support the paper's general framing of "an attractor theory of repetition hallucination in autoregressive TTS."
   **Where** — Section 3 (Models); Section 4.4, last paragraph ("Qwen3-TTS sums text and acoustic embeddings per position, so the lemma cannot be checked there"); Table 1 (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B: 0.0% count error, i.e., no deficit); Section 5 ("Limits: the panel is English-only codec-language models with softmax attention over text").
   **Why it matters** — The theorem is stated for any Lipschitz readout over any q-contracting state map, but the evidence base is six checkpoints, all English, all sharing a broadly similar codec-LM design. Lemma 1 — the mechanistic story that motivates the whole causal chain — is admitted to be *unverifiable* for the Qwen3-TTS family (1/3 of the panel by family, 2/6 checkpoints) because it doesn't attend to text the same way. And where the behavioral signature *can* be checked, one of six checkpoints (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) shows literally zero deficit, matching its control exactly. A theory whose core mechanism is inapplicable to a third of its own panel, and whose predicted effect is absent in one of six models, is being generalized to "autoregressive TTS" on unusually thin cross-architecture footing.
   **What would address it** — Either restrict all title/abstract/discussion claims explicitly to "softmax-attention, text-conditioned, English AR codec-LM TTS" (which the paper does state as a limitation only in the discussion, not in the abstract or title), or add checkpoints/architectures for which the lemma is checkable, plus at least one non-English or non-transformer AR TTS system as a generalization probe.

2. **Issue** — Assumption 2 (contraction, q<1), the load-bearing premise of Theorem 1, is reported as empirically unestablished, yet the abstract's language ("extending to k=128 locates it") reads as confirmatory of the mechanism rather than of a qualitatively similar-looking curve.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("A finite-difference perturbation probe then found no difference in local contraction between repeated items and their controls (7 of 18 pairs in the predicted direction, p = 0.12), with its own linearity control failing... The premise remains unestablished."); Abstract.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's honesty in the discussion is commendable, but it creates a gap between what is asserted up front (a "theorem" whose "consequence" is "located" in the data) and what is actually shown (a leveling-off curve and a periodicity/length dissociation that are *consistent with*, but do not *establish*, a contracting dynamical system). Alternative, non-dynamical explanations — e.g., a learned n-gram/self-repetition stopping heuristic, or generic exposure-bias drift unrelated to contraction — could produce the same qualitative signature (proportional-then-saturating undercount, periodicity>length sensitivity) without a q<1 fixed point existing at all.
   **What would address it** — Reframe abstract/title to make explicit that the mechanism (contraction) is a hypothesis motivated but not confirmed by the data; discuss concretely why the two rival mechanisms addressed in S8 are less plausible than contraction, since that section is not reproduced in the main text and the reviewer cannot verify its reasoning.

3. **Issue** — The extension-ladder result that is presented as "the theorem's consequence" — a finite horizon N* — rests on only 2 of 6 checkpoints with "few items per cell," and its own fitted curve doesn't actually plateau (the repeated median declines from 27 at k=48 to 15 at k=128).
   **Where** — Section 4.2.
   **Why it matters** — A pure bounded-distinguishability account predicts convergence to a plateau, not decline past the horizon; the observed decline is at least as consistent with an unrelated degeneration mode (e.g., outright truncation or loop-collapse dominating at extreme k) taking over once the ladder is pushed far past training-length norms. The paper acknowledges this ("keep this short of clean confirmation") but still uses the fitted N* values (23 vs 94, "4.1-fold") as the paper's central quantitative deliverable, despite n of 2 model instances.
   **What would address it** — Expand the extension panel beyond 2 checkpoints before quoting a specific fold-change; report the audio-degeneracy rate alongside count at k=48–128 (the paper says degeneracy is tracked separately in Section 3 but doesn't show it for the extension) to test whether the decline is a distinct failure mode rather than horizon-crossing.

4. **Issue** — Generalizability claims are repeatedly expressed as vote-counts across checkpoints ("disjoint in 5 of 6," "separates in 4 of 6," "disjoint in 2 of 2") rather than as effect estimates that treat checkpoint as the unit of replication, while pooled CIs (e.g., -8.3% [−12.5, −6.2], n=457) are computed at the generation level.
   **Where** — Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.4; Table 1.
   **Why it matters** — With only 6 (or 2) independent model checkpoints, the effective N for any claim about "TTS decoders in general" is 6 (or 2), not hundreds of generations. Vote-counting across such a small number of units is a weak, imprecision-discarding form of aggregation, and pooled CIs that don't account for checkpoint-level clustering will understate the true uncertainty about the population of architectures the paper wants to generalize to.
   **What would address it** — Report a mixed/hierarchical model with checkpoint as a random effect, or present per-checkpoint effect sizes with individual CIs next to the pooled number so a reader can judge heterogeneity directly (Table 1 partially does this for point estimates but not for the headline CI-bearing claims in the text).

5. **Issue** — The practical mitigation test (repetition-penalty sweep) is run on a single architecture only, yet the discussion draws a general conclusion about mitigations.
   **Where** — Section 5, "Implications and limits" ("sweeping XTTS-v2's repetition penalty... over {2,3,5,8}... No setting both preserves quality and removes the deficit").
   **Why it matters** — Concluding that "existing mitigations act late... helping only insofar as they raise q or enlarge the readout margin" from an n=1-architecture, 4-point sweep is a strong generalization from a narrow test, especially since the paper elsewhere stresses that its own theory's applicability varies markedly by family (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no deficit at all).
   **What would address it** — Repeat the repetition-penalty (or analogous) sweep on at least one Llasa checkpoint and one Qwen3-TTS checkpoint before generalizing the mitigation conclusion across the panel.

6. **Issue** — Large primary data artifacts (2.5 GB audio, 25 GB activations) are "available on request" rather than deposited in an open, citable archive.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Code, data, settings").
   **Why it matters** — This is inconsistent with the otherwise strong reproducibility posture (public Lean proofs, code, transcripts, an axiom-audit script) and is exactly the artifact an independent group would need to re-derive the effective-rank/capacity-gain results underlying Section 4.4 and Table 1's capacity-gain column.
   **What would address it** — Deposit on a persistent archive (Zenodo/OSF/HF Hub) with a DOI, or state why public hosting isn't feasible.

### Minor concerns
- "Qwen3-TTS at 0.6B/1.7B, three seeds each" (Section 3) is ambiguous about whether "three seeds" multiplies the effective sample for those two checkpoints or refers to something else (e.g., stimulus regeneration); the per-checkpoint n underlying pooled statistics is never made explicit in the main text.
- Reference [1] (cited in the Introduction to motivate the "stability problem" framing) is annotated "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — leaning on a withdrawn preprint, even just "for its alignment analysis," is worth a stronger caveat or a replacement citation.
- Multiple central claims depend on supplementary sections (S4, S5, S8, S9, S10) not included in the reviewed text (e.g., "ruled out by their own predictions (S8)," "0.19 for Whisper... (S9)"); as presented, these claims cannot be independently checked.
- Figure 1's x-axis tick labels ("21 23 25") are presumably meant to render as 2¹, 2³, 2⁵ (log₂ scale) — confirm this renders correctly in the camera-ready PDF, as the extracted text is ambiguous.
- The phrase "ablations of a panel member" (Section 3) is confusing on first read — clarify that it means "a member of the model panel" (i.e., XTTS-v2), not a human reviewer/author.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" column and the in-text "0.46" and "0.50"/"0.52" (covariate-adjusted) figures are given without explicit CIs in the main text, though CIs are mentioned for other statistics — inconsistent precision reporting.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the Lean-formalized theorem is a genuine and well-executed contribution, but the paper's own admissions (contraction premise unverified, lemma inapplicable to a third of the panel, horizon evidence resting on 2 checkpoints with a non-plateauing curve, one checkpoint showing no effect at all) mean the empirical generalization claimed in the abstract and title outruns what six narrowly-selected, mostly-English, single-attention-style checkpoints can support.
