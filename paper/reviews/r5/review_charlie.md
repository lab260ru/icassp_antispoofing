### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive TTS decoders lose count when a text prompt repeats a phrase many times, and proposes a mechanistic account: if softmax attention over k identical spans dilutes to near-uniform (Lemma 1, attention dilution) and the per-repetition hidden-state update is a contraction (Assumption 2), then a Banach-style argument (Theorem 1, machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib) implies a finite horizon N* beyond which no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts. The theorem is explicitly conditional: Assumption 2 is stated as an empirical claim the authors do not prove for transformers. Empirically, across six TTS checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) scored with a CTC recognizer (chosen over Whisper because Whisper's own autoregressive decoder is shown to be periodicity-biased), repeated-word/sentence stimuli are undercounted (median -8.3% at k≥6) while length-matched non-repeating controls are essentially exact (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match). Within the original k≤32 ladder the deficit is proportional rather than saturating, which the authors say contradicts Theorem 1(iv)'s prediction of a hard horizon; extending to k∈{48,64,96,128} on a subset of checkpoints yields a fitted saturation with N*_rep=23 vs N*_ctl=94, an ostensible 4.1× reduction in counting horizon under repetition, alongside a slower growth of trajectory effective rank (capacity gain) under repetition (0.46× the control rate). The authors are candid that two independent attempts to directly measure the contraction factor q failed, leaving the theorem's central premise empirically unconfirmed.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's headline quantitative claim (N*_rep=23 vs N*_ctl=94, "4.1-fold") is derived from an ad hoc follow-up experiment covering only 2 of the 6 panel checkpoints, with no stated criterion for which checkpoints were extended or why, and with the paper itself noting "few items per cell."
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("the extension covers 2 checkpoints with few items per cell"); Abstract ("N*rep = 23 repetitions against 94… repetition lowers the counting horizon 4.1-fold").
   **Why it matters** — This is the single most-quoted number in the abstract, yet it is not shown to generalize across the panel used everywhere else in the paper. A 4× effect estimated from 2 units, with no sample-size or per-cell-n disclosure, cannot support the confident abstract framing ("locates it"); Table 1 already shows large model-to-model heterogeneity in the related capacity-gain ratio (0.21–0.80), so there is no reason to assume the 2 extended checkpoints are representative.
   **What would address it** — Extend all 6 checkpoints (or explicitly justify and pre-specify the subsampling rule), report per-checkpoint N* with CIs, and downgrade the abstract's phrasing to match the actual evidentiary weight (2/6, exploratory).

2. **Issue** — The extension ladder appears to have been designed only after the original k≤32 ladder failed to show Theorem 1's predicted saturation, using a fitted functional form, ĉ(k)=N*(1−e^{−k/N*}), that is not derived anywhere from the theorem itself.
   **Where** — Section 4.1 ("Within k ≤ 32, though, the deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts") followed immediately by Section 4.2 ("So we extended it… Tracking stops").
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook adaptive/optional-stopping pattern: run the planned test, get a null/contradictory result, then design a new test until the predicted qualitative pattern appears, and report the second test as if it evaluates the same hypothesis with equal confirmatory weight. The exponential-saturation form is a free-parameter curve fit chosen for narrative convenience, not a shape derived from Theorem 1(iv) (which bounds the horizon, but does not specify the transient shape of ĉ(k) below it). The Discussion partially concedes this ("what is shown is a horizon of the predicted kind, not one derived from a measured q"), but that caveat does not appear in the Abstract or Section 4.2's framing.
   **What would address it** — Pre-register (or at minimum explicitly flag as post-hoc/exploratory) the extension experiment; justify the exponential fit against at least one alternative saturating form (e.g., logistic, power-law-with-cap) and report which best fits the data; keep the hedged framing consistent between Abstract and Discussion.

3. **Issue** — The "length-matched, distinct-word" control is not periodicity-free once k exceeds 8: fillers are drawn from a per-template pool of only eight and cycled, so a k=32 control repeats each filler four times, and the k=128 extension-ladder control repeats each filler sixteen times.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("replaced by fillers from a per-template pool of eight, cycled above k=8… period-8 repetition at most against period-1"); Section 4.2's extension ladder (k up to 128) and Section 4.3's "periodicity, not length" claim (Figure 1b, up to k=32).
   **Why it matters** — The paper's sharpest claimed result (Section 4.3, and implicitly the entire extension-ladder contrast) is that the deficit tracks periodicity and not length, evidenced by controls staying near zero error. But at exactly the k-range where the most dramatic numbers are reported (k=16…128), the "control" itself contains embedded period-8 repetition, which by the paper's own Lemma 1/Theorem 1 logic should itself induce some (weaker) attractor dynamics. A genuinely periodicity-free control would need an effectively unbounded, non-repeating filler vocabulary at high k. As it stands, the control-vs-repeated gap is a period-1-vs-period-8 comparison dressed as a periodicity-vs-no-periodicity comparison, which is a materially weaker test than claimed.
   **What would address it** — Rerun the control condition with a filler vocabulary large enough to avoid any repetition up to the maximum k tested (128), or explicitly report and control for the control's own period-8 structure (e.g., an intermediate period-8 condition to show the effect scales with period as Theorem 1 would predict).

4. **Issue** — Assumption 2 (contraction, q<1), the load-bearing premise of the whole theory, is never empirically confirmed; both direct attempts to measure it are reported as failing.
   **Where** — Section 5, Discussion ("two attempts to measure q failed… A finite-difference perturbation probe then found no difference in local contraction between repeated items and their controls (7 of 18 pairs in the predicted direction, p = 0.12), with its own linearity control failing").
   **Why it matters** — Without a validated q<1, the paper has not established that the mechanism it names in the title ("attractor theory") is actually operating in any of the six checkpoints; it has only shown a directionally consistent behavioral and representational pattern (undercounting, slower rank growth) that is compatible with the attractor account but does not distinguish it from several competing mechanisms (e.g., simple length/output-budget saturation, learned stopping heuristics keyed to n-gram repetition counts, or the "count survives in the residual stream but the output policy fails" account the authors themselves cite as a rival explanation [17] without resolving it). A non-significant, underpowered (n=18 pairs) contraction probe is not evidence for the premise; it is an unresolved test.
   **What would address it** — Either report a properly powered direct estimate of q (e.g., via a designed intervention with a text read-head that does not require monotonic attention advance), or consistently frame every downstream result as "consistent with, but not diagnostic of, an attractor mechanism" rather than mixing that hedge (present only in Discussion) with confirmatory language in the Abstract and Results.

5. **Issue** — The analysis meant to rule out the "circularity" objection (that low state-rank on repeated text is just a stimulus artifact of monotonous output) restricts to "correctly rendered items," but the two conditions have wildly different retention rates (18.2% of repeated trials vs 94.3% of control trials survive this filter).
   **Where** — Section 4.4 ("restricting to correctly rendered items leaves it at 0.52").
   **Why it matters** — Comparing a heavily curated ~1-in-5 subsample of the repeated arm against a near-complete (~19-in-20) subsample of the control arm reintroduces exactly the kind of selection confound the analysis is supposed to preclude: the surviving repeated-arm trials are a highly non-random, presumably "easier"/more-model-favorable subset, while the control-arm subsample is close to the full population. A stable ratio (0.52) under such asymmetric selection does not demonstrate that circularity is ruled out; it demonstrates the ratio is robust to one particular (confounded) restriction.
   **What would address it** — Match selection rates across arms (e.g., matched subsampling of controls to the same 18.2% retention, or report the correctly-rendered comparison stratified by matched output-diversity bins rather than raw correctness) before claiming the circularity objection is addressed.

6. **Issue** — Exclusion rules (token-budget-truncated generations, one template the judge cannot transcribe) are applied "throughout" but exclusion counts/rates per condition (repeated vs. control, per checkpoint, per k) are never reported in the main text — only deferred to a supplement (S10) not included here.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Behavioural measurement" ("Generations stopped by our token budget rather than the model… are excluded throughout (S10)").
   **Why it matters** — If runaway repetition loops are more likely than controls to hit the token budget (plausible, since a decoder that loses count on repeated content is the population most likely to loop or run on), then exactly the most severe collapse cases would be censored from the reported repeated-arm statistics, biasing the measured deficit downward (i.e., making the true effect larger than reported) or introducing an unknown, uncharacterized skew. Differential attrition between arms is a standard threat to internal validity that isn't addressed anywhere in the visible text.
   **What would address it** — Report exclusion counts/rates by condition and checkpoint in the main paper (not just the supplement), and show the main results are robust to including budget-truncated generations with some principled count-error floor/ceiling treatment.

7. **Issue** — Pooled summary statistics (median -8.3% at k≥6; 0.46 capacity-gain ratio) are reported without correction for multiple comparisons across the many contrasts run (6 checkpoints × multiple k values × several outcome measures), and they average over substantial cross-model heterogeneity that Table 1 itself documents (count error ranging 0.0% to -16.7%; capacity-gain ratio ranging 0.21–0.80).
   **Where** — Section 4.1 ("Pooled at k ≥ 6… median error of -8.3%"); Table 1.
   **Why it matters** — A single pooled figure implies a roughly homogeneous phenomenon across the panel, but the per-model breakdown shows one checkpoint (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) with literally no count deficit and a nearly 4× spread in capacity-gain ratio; the pooled statistic obscures this and risks over-generalizing a pattern that is strong in some architectures and absent in others. Combined with the many separately-tested contrasts (periodicity-vs-length, capacity gain, exact-match rate, repetition-penalty sweep), no correction or pre-specified hierarchy of tests is described.
   **What would address it** — Report model-stratified statistics as primary and the pooled statistic as secondary/descriptive only; state explicitly which comparisons were planned a priori vs. exploratory, and apply (or justify not applying) a multiple-comparison correction.

### Minor concerns

- "Ablations of a panel member" (Section 3) is ambiguous — clarify whether this means a member of the model panel (i.e., the XTTS-v2 checkpoint) or a specific co-author's individually run sweep; as written it could be misread as a conflict-of-interest disclosure.
- The control-error CI reported as exactly "0.0% [0.0, 0.0]" over n=525 (Section 4.3) is an unusual zero-width bootstrap interval; clarify the estimator (median of a distribution with a large point mass at zero can produce this, but it should be stated explicitly rather than left to infer).
- Table 1's Llasa-1B capacity-gain (rep.) value, 18.2, is numerically identical to the paper's separately reported overall exact-match rate of 18.2% (Abstract, Section 4.3); almost certainly coincidental, but worth the authors double-checking no cross-referencing error occurred.
- Reference [1] is cited as support for the framing of repetition/stability failures despite being noted as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — using a withdrawn preprint as a citation warrants a sentence explaining why its alignment analysis remains reliable despite withdrawal.
- The companion paper [7] (self-citation, same three authors, arXiv:2604.08591) is used to frame the "attractor regime" precedent; since it is itself a recent, not-obviously-peer-reviewed preprint by the same team, its claims should be treated as background rather than independent corroboration.
- Figure 1's axis tick labels render as bare numerals ("21 23 25", "26 27") in the extracted text, apparently lost exponent formatting (presumably 2¹, 2³, 2⁵); worth confirming the camera-ready figure renders correctly.
- Qwen3-TTS checkpoints are noted as untestable for Lemma 1 ("the lemma cannot be checked there") because of summed text/acoustic embeddings, yet they remain in pooled statistics elsewhere without a corresponding caveat at each point of use.
- The repetition-penalty mitigation sweep (Section 5) is run on a single checkpoint (XTTS-v2) but the conclusion ("No setting both preserves quality and removes the deficit") is stated as if general to "existing mitigations."

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the formal (Lean-verified) contribution and the qualitative periodicity-vs-length contrast are genuine and mostly well-supported, but the paper's headline quantitative claims (the 4.1× horizon reduction, N*_rep=23) rest on an underpowered, apparently post-hoc 2-checkpoint follow-up with confounded controls and an unvalidated core mechanistic assumption, and these limitations are inconsistently disclosed between the Abstract/Results and the more hedged Discussion.
