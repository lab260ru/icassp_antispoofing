### Summary

The paper makes three linked claims: (1) a Lean-4-verified theorem that a contracting per-repetition state map imposes a hard horizon on any Lipschitz readout's ability to count repetitions; (2) an empirical finding, replicated across six checkpoints in three families, that AR TTS systems undercount long runs of an exactly-repeated phrase, and that this deficit tracks *periodicity* rather than *length* (94.3% exact on length-matched distinct-word controls vs. 18.2% on repeated twins); (3) a post-hoc extension of the count ladder to k=128 that finds a saturating horizon of the shape the theorem predicts in some but not all checkpoints. The paper is unusually candid that the theorem's premise (contraction, q<1) was never established — two measurement attempts failed and the companion dilution lemma's dose-response prediction came out sign-reversed — and it says so in its own title. That honesty is a real point in its favor. My stance is sharp-but-balanced: the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuinely well-controlled finding I believe more after reading this than before; the formal-verification apparatus, by the authors' own account, is currently disconnected from that finding, and — my focal axis — the paper's reproducibility story does not hold up when checked: the GitHub URL it repeatedly points to as the place to verify every number, transcript, and "free of sorry" claim returns a 404 to an anonymous fetch as of this review.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The one non-AR system that *does* show the deficit (F5-TTS) has no autoregressive per-token state map for Theorem 1 to apply to, which is a bigger problem for the mechanism than the paper's framing suggests. `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — §4.3, "The last rival is architectural."
   **Why it matters** — Theorem 1 is stated for a map $s_{m+1}=F(s_m)$ realized *at repetition boundaries during autoregressive decoding*. F5-TTS estimates one total duration and denoises the whole utterance in parallel — there is no boundary-to-boundary autoregressive map in the sense the theorem defines. If a system without that mechanism nonetheless reproduces the periodicity-specific signature (25.6% vs 85.6% exact, growing with k "as the panel's does"), that is evidence the *phenomenon* is not specific to the mechanism the theorem formalizes, not merely evidence that "absence of an AR decoder confers no immunity." The paper's own §5 falsification list names "an AR decoder behaving like the non-AR baseline" as a refuter, but the actual near-miss is the mirror image — a non-AR decoder behaving like the AR failure group — and the text never addresses why that doesn't count against the same mechanism.
   **What would address it** — Either state explicitly why F5-TTS's failure is compatible with a *different* instantiation of contraction (e.g., in its denoising trajectory rather than its token-by-token state), with independent evidence for that claim, or downgrade the theorem from "the explanation" to "one candidate explanation, contradicted by at least one system that shows the target phenomenon by a different route."

2. **Issue** — The k=128 extension's headline "3.5-fold" horizon number is fit over 4 checkpoints, but the text only reports individual saturation verdicts for 2 of them (Llasa-1B, Llasa-8B: disjoint CIs; Qwen3-TTS-1.7B: no saturation); Qwen3-TTS-0.6B's individual result is never stated. `[Evidence — missing breakdown / post-hoc range]`
   **Where** — §4.2.
   **Why it matters** — This is exactly the kind of number a skeptical reader most wants to audit, because the whole horizon claim rests on 4 checkpoints and one of the four is already an admitted null. Without knowing which way Qwen3-TTS-0.6B goes, a reader can't tell whether the pooled $\hat K_{rep}=30$ vs $\hat K_{ctl}=105$ is being carried by 3 of 4 checkpoints or 2 of 4. The range itself (k=48..128) was also chosen post-hoc after the k≤32 ladder came back proportional rather than saturating — disclosed honestly (S16), but it means the fitted saturation scale is a single post-hoc curve-fit on a small, non-preregistered range, explicitly *not* the theorem's $N^*$.
   **What would address it** — Report Qwen3-TTS-0.6B's individual saturation status and CI alongside the other three in the main text, not only in the pooled fit.

3. **Issue** — The abstract and title foreground a Lean-verified theorem whose premise is not merely "unestablished" but has one directly falsified sub-prediction. `[Narrative — theory/empirics decoupling]`
   **Where** — Abstract; §4.4, "its dose-response prediction fails outright... $r=0.59$" (flatter attention → *better* counting, the opposite sign).
   **Why it matters** — "Unestablished" (can't confirm) and "falsified" (confirmed wrong) are different epistemic states, and the paper's own language blurs them together under "does not yet explain." The dilution lemma's dose-response test isn't a null result — it's a sign-reversal on a mechanistic prediction the theory makes. A reader skimming the abstract for the takeaway ("a Lean-verified bound... models render repeated text with a median count error...") could easily walk away crediting the theorem with explanatory work that the paper's own §4.4/§5 disclaim.
   **What would address it** — State plainly, once, near the top: "one of the two formally verified components makes a prediction that is empirically falsified in the direction opposite to that predicted," rather than leaving that fact to be assembled from §4.4 and §5 separately.

4. **Issue** — A plain distributional/OOD explanation for the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is never raised. `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — absent (nearest candidate section: §4.3, which addresses length, penalty, judge bias, and architecture, but not training-data typicality).
   **Why it matters** — TTS training corpora are overwhelmingly natural prose; exact k-fold literal repetition of a phrase is a much rarer surface pattern than "many distinct words of similar total length." A model could underperform on repeated-phrase inputs simply because they are further out-of-distribution than length-matched distinct-word controls, with no contraction dynamics required — pure exposure-frequency mismatch. This is a live rival to "periodicity is causally special because of state-map dynamics," and it predicts the same qualitative signature (periodic >> non-periodic controls) that the paper treats as diagnostic of its mechanism.
   **What would address it** — At minimum, name this as a considered-and-set-aside rival, ideally with some proxy for training-distribution typicality (e.g., LM perplexity of the repeated vs. control carrier text) shown not to track the effect.

5. **Issue** — Six checkpoints, no significance testing, but claim language ("in every checkpoint and every family," "general in direction") reads more confident than the acknowledged n=6 supports. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — Abstract; §4.2 ("The panel is too small for significance testing at either level... so we rest on effect sizes and their consistency").
   **Why it matters** — The authors are explicit and correct that n=6 (3 families) can't clear even an uncorrected p=0.05 by sign test, and I credit them for saying so rather than p-hacking a test that doesn't apply. But the abstract's "in every checkpoint and every family" is doing more rhetorical work than 6 units of replication should license, especially once one of those six (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) is a documented, unexplained exception on two of the paper's central measures (k≤32 deficit and k≤128 saturation).
   **What would address it** — Hedge the abstract's universal-quantifier language to match the effect-size framing used correctly in §4.2, e.g. "in 5 of 6 checkpoints, with one unexplained exception," rather than "every."

6. **Issue** — The repository the paper repeatedly cites as where every number, transcript, and formal-verification claim can be checked is not publicly reachable. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — §3, "Code, data, settings" ("Lean development, stimuli, code, per-item tables and both transcript sets: https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing").
   **Why it matters** — I fetched that exact URL on 2026-08-12 (the review date) and got HTTP 404 from an unauthenticated request; a control fetch of an unrelated public repository on GitHub succeeded (HTTP 200) in the same session, so this isn't a general network failure, and the GitHub org itself (`lab260ru`) resolves fine — the specific repo just isn't visible to an anonymous reader. That means none of the paper's most checkable claims are currently checkable by an actual reader: not the "no sorry, axiom-audited against propext/Classical.choice/Quot.sound" claim that is load-bearing for the entire "first Lean-verified hallucination result" novelty pitch, not the per-item tables that would resolve concern #2 above, not the 165-clip stratified audio sample the paper says exists "so a judgement can be checked by ear," not the two released transcript sets the judge-validation argument in §3 depends on. A competent grad student handed only the paper text plus this link cannot replicate the headline figure, or even confirm the formal artifact compiles, today. Separately, the paper states heavy artifacts (audio, activations) are meant to "regenerate from the released seeds," but nothing in the text discusses whether TTS sampling with fixed seeds is bit-reproducible across GPU/driver/library versions — a real gap for stochastic autoregressive decoding, where cuDNN/kernel nondeterminism routinely breaks seed-exact replication even when a paper reports seeds.
   **What would address it** — Make the repository actually public before the link is printed in a paper (or clearly mark it as "will be made public on acceptance," which the current text does not do), and add one sentence on what determinism guarantee, if any, "regenerate from the released seeds" carries across hardware/software stacks.

### Belief update

I moved my credence up meaningfully on a narrow, well-supported proposition: *AR (and at least one non-AR) TTS systems miscount long runs of an exactly-repeated phrase in a way that specifically tracks periodicity rather than utterance length* — the twin-control design in §4.3, replicated with a de-cycled filler pool to rule out the period-8 confound, is the paper's strongest piece of evidence and I believe it. I did not update, or updated only slightly, on the theorem's actual causal story (contraction of a per-repetition state map) — by the authors' own report the premise is unmeasured, one of its two formalized predictions is sign-reversed, and the strongest architectural test available (F5-TTS) shows the target phenomenon in a system the theorem's mechanism doesn't obviously apply to. The Lean artifact is a genuine, unusual piece of scholarly effort, but as things stand it functions as a validated-in-isolation mathematical object bolted onto an empirical finding that doesn't currently depend on it.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the strongest single problem is reproducibility: the paper's central novelty claim ("the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact... free of sorry") and its most granular empirical claims are currently unverifiable because the linked repository 404s to an anonymous reader as of this review (2026-08-12), and until code/data are actually public, this is a paper asking to be trusted rather than checked.
