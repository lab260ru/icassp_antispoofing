### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) TTS models miscount repeated phrases, proves (and Lean-formalizes) a conditional theorem that a contracting per-repetition state map bounds how many repetitions any Lipschitz readout can distinguish, and then runs a repetition-count benchmark across six TTS checkpoints from three families, scored by a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the AR bias of Whisper. The headline empirical result is a periodicity-specific undercount (median relative count error −8.3% at k≥6, 18.2% exact vs. 94.3% for length-matched non-repetitive controls) that is not architecture-exclusive (one of two non-AR baselines shows it, one does not) and that only becomes saturating, in line with the theorem's shape, once the ladder is extended post hoc to k=128. Critically, the paper reports that its two direct attempts to measure the theorem's contraction premise (Assumption 2) failed, and that a dose-response test of its companion attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed — so what is empirically supported is a periodicity effect and a horizon of roughly the predicted shape, not the mechanism the theorem specifies.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The theorem's signature prediction (a saturating horizon) was falsified by the pre-specified ladder and only "recovered" by a post-hoc extension whose stopping rule, checkpoint subset, and fit family were all chosen after the disconfirming result was in hand.
   **Where** — Section 4.1 ("Within k ≤ 32 that deficit is proportional, not saturating — not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts"), Section 4.2 ("So we extended it, post hoc … to k ∈ {48,64,96,128} on the 4 checkpoints admitting utterances that long").
   **Why it matters** — This is a textbook garden-of-forking-paths design: the initial, honestly-reported disconfirmation (RSS 2.6 for a proportional fit vs. 485.3 for a constant, i.e., no evidence of a horizon at all) is exactly the kind of negative result a pre-registered protocol would have to accept. Instead, the authors extended the design, selected the 4 (of 6) checkpoints that happened to support longer generation, and fit a new curve family (ĉ(k) = K̂(1 − e^{−k/K̂})) that was not specified before k=32 data came in. Nothing rules out that a different post-hoc extension (different k values, different fit family, different checkpoint subset) would have produced a different verdict. The paper's own transparency about the post-hoc timing (S16) documents when the decision was made but does not neutralize the statistical risk it created.
   **What would address it** — Pre-register the extended protocol (k-grid, fit family, inclusion criteria) on a held-out set of checkpoints not used to motivate the extension, or replicate the k=128 saturation finding on new checkpoints that were not part of the k≤32 analysis that triggered it.

2. **Issue** — The abstract's universal claim ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and every family") is contradicted, by the paper's own numbers, for Qwen3-TTS-1.7B on the primary metric.
   **Where** — Abstract; Table 1 (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B: count err. 0.0% rep. vs. 0.0% ctl.); Section 4.2 ("the median-error gap is positive in 5 of 6, zero in Qwen3-TTS-1.7B … Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation within k ≤ 128, inverts the ratio, and is also the checkpoint with no deficit at k ≤ 32").
   **Why it matters** — This checkpoint is not a marginal outlier; on the headline count-error metric it shows literally zero effect, at every rung tested. The authors even list "a model whose capacity gain matches its control" as one of their own pre-stated falsification conditions (Section 5) — Qwen3-TTS-1.7B's capacity-gain ratio of 0.80 is the closest any checkpoint comes to that condition, yet it is folded into "an unexplained exception" rather than treated as a candidate falsification. An adversarial reading: one full checkpoint in one of three families does not show the phenomenon by the metric the abstract leads with, which undermines the "every checkpoint and every family" framing used to sell external validity.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the universality claim to reflect the 5-of-6 pattern explicitly in the abstract, or run the diagnostic experiments needed to determine whether Qwen3-TTS-1.7B genuinely lacks the contracting dynamic (which would be a real falsification of Assumption 2 for that architecture) rather than reporting it as unexplained.

3. **Issue** — The "architectural rival is not excluded" conclusion rests on exactly two non-AR systems, the second of which (F5-TTS) was evidently brought in only after the first (VITS) produced a null result, with a mediating hypothesis constructed after seeing both outcomes.
   **Where** — Section 4.3 ("The last rival is architectural … VITS … shows no dissociation … F5-TTS … does show it … What plausibly separates them is where the count must live — a hypothesis formed after seeing them, not a result").
   **Why it matters** — With n=2 non-AR architectures and a freely-constructed post-hoc mediator (explicit vs. implicit duration modeling), the design cannot distinguish "the phenomenon is duration-representation-general" from "F5-TTS happens to show a superficially similar pattern for unrelated reasons." A single additional counterexample in either direction (a third explicit-duration NAR model, or a second implicit-duration one) would flip the conclusion. This is the same forking-paths structure as concern 1, applied to architecture instead of horizon.
   **What would address it** — Test at least one more model on each side of the proposed explicit/implicit-duration split before asserting the mediating hypothesis has any evidential weight; at minimum, label it explicitly as untested post-hoc speculation in the abstract/discussion rather than embedding it in the results narrative.

4. **Issue** — Generation hyperparameters (temperature, top-p, repetition penalty) differ across families and are not held constant in the main panel, confounding cross-checkpoint comparisons of effect magnitude.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Sampling uses shipped defaults (XTTS-v2 T=0.75/0.85/50/penalty 5.0; Llasa T=0.8, top-p 1.0; Qwen3-TTS T=0.9/1.0/50/1.05) … The penalty is the one parameter we vary").
   **Why it matters** — Table 1's fourfold spread in capacity-gain ratio (0.21–0.80) and count-error range (−8.3% to −16.7%) is reported as if it reflects architecture/scale differences, but each family also carries a different sampling regime. The repetition-penalty sweep (Section 4.3) addresses only the penalty, and only on 2 of 6 checkpoints; temperature and top-p are never varied or matched. The paper pre-empts one specific instance of this threat (arguing Qwen3-TTS's high stochasticity means its null "is not an artifact of conservative decoding") but this argument only covers the direction of one confound for one checkpoint, not the general design gap.
   **What would address it** — Add a matched-hyperparameter condition (same T/top-p/penalty) across at least one checkpoint per family, or explicitly caveat that magnitude comparisons across families are confounded by decoding settings even though the periodicity-vs-length dissociation (within-model, matched-carrier controls) is not.

5. **Issue** — The most parsimonious rival explanation — that k-fold verbatim repetition is simply out-of-distribution relative to TTS training corpora, so the "collapse" reflects a learned frequency prior rather than state-space contraction — is never tested or even listed among the rivals the paper works through.
   **Where** — Absent. Section 5 enumerates and addresses exposure bias/monotonic-alignment framings (via citation), a decoding-rule rival (repetition penalty, Section 4.3), an architectural rival (Section 4.3), and a representations-vs-output-policy rival ([13], Section 5), but not a corpus-frequency/distributional-generalization rival.
   **Why it matters** — Read speech and audiobook corpora essentially never contain a phrase repeated verbatim 6+ times; such prompts are severely out-of-distribution. A model that has simply never seen "render 12 repeats" would produce something that looks exactly like this paper's data: length-matched non-repetitive controls unaffected, error accumulating with the (unfamiliar) repeat count, and a leveling-off once the model runs into whatever count it treats as maximally plausible — all without requiring any particular claim about fixed-point contraction in the hidden state. Since Assumption 2 was never independently validated (see concern 6), this rival is not ruled out by anything in the paper, and it would explain the data at least as well as the theorem does.
   **What would address it** — A dedicated control: fine-tune (or few-shot condition) a checkpoint on synthetic data containing high-k verbatim repeats and test whether the deficit shrinks once such inputs are in-distribution; or measure the corpus frequency of k-fold repeated spans in the models' training data and show the collapse curve is not simply the mirror image of that frequency distribution.

6. **Issue** — The two attempts to validate Assumption 2 (the contraction premise) are reported as inconclusive, but one is a null result from an 18-pair sample with no power analysis, and the paper still uses "predicted shape" framing for the k=128 saturation in the abstract.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items (7 of 18 pairs, p=0.12), its linearity control failing too"); Abstract ("a horizon of the predicted shape").
   **Why it matters** — A non-significant result from 18 pairs (p=0.12) is weak evidence for "no difference," not evidence that the contraction premise doesn't matter; no effect size, minimum detectable effect, or power calculation is given to show the test could have detected the contraction gap the theorem requires. Absent a validated q, "predicted shape" is a generic saturating-curve shape consistent with many other bounded processes (token budget, KV-cache/positional degradation, or the frequency-prior account in concern 5) — the theorem's contribution to explaining the k=128 data is therefore closer to "consistent with" than "predicted by."
   **What would address it** — Report the power/minimum-detectable-effect of the 18-pair perturbation probe; if underpowered, say so explicitly rather than letting the abstract's "predicted shape" stand as if the mechanism were corroborated.

7. **Issue** — Pseudoreplication risk in pooled statistics: the 3 Llasa checkpoints share a backbone family/tokenizer, items recur across 3 seeds and across checkpoints, yet several headline intervals (e.g., "−8.3% [−12.5, −6.2]" pooling n=457 items, "76.7 points [68.4, 84.4]" across n=6 checkpoints) are reported without discussing the non-independence this induces.
   **Where** — Section 4.1 (pooled count-error CI, n=457); Section 4.2 (checkpoint-level and family-level effect-size CIs).
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably explicit that "the checkpoint is the unit of replication" and that n=6 is too small for significance testing (correctly flagging the signed-rank floor of 0.031 and the lack of multiplicity correction, S11). But the item-pooled CI (n=457) and the family-clustering step (6 checkpoints → 3 families) both involve nested, non-independent sampling units (same carrier templates reused across seeds/checkpoints; 3 Llasa checkpoints are scale variants of one architecture/tokenizer), which is not accounted for in the reported confidence intervals. This risks understating uncertainty exactly where the paper is trying to be most careful about not overclaiming significance.
   **What would address it** — Use a mixed-effects or cluster-bootstrap procedure that treats template, seed, and checkpoint-within-family as nested random effects, or at minimum state explicitly what the resampling unit was for each reported interval.

### Minor concerns

- Lemma 1 as printed states `e^{-δ/k} ≤ softmax(z)_i ≤ e^{δ/k}`; the standard bound for logits within δ of each other is `e^{-δ}/k ≤ softmax(z)_i ≤ e^{δ}/k` (δ dividing the whole term, not the exponent). This may be a PDF-extraction artifact, but as transcribed it changes the asymptotics substantially (the printed form does not vanish as k→∞) and should be checked against the camera-ready PDF.
- The CTC judge's calibration ("ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth against 0.19 for Whisper") is validated on concatenative audio with known ground truth, not on model-generated (potentially degenerate/looping) audio — the acoustic distribution being scored in the actual experiment. A brief note on whether this calibration transfers to genuinely degenerate generations would strengthen the judge-validity argument.
- Reference [1] is cited as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026; cited for its alignment analysis" — leaning on a withdrawn preprint for motivating background, even while disclosing the withdrawal, is worth flagging; a withdrawn paper's claims should ideally not anchor framing without an alternative citation for the same point.
- Self-citation [7] (same three authors, companion ASR paper) is reasonably used, but the framing ("identified an attractor regime descriptively; here the attractor is what the theorem is about") is close to citing one's own unpublished/unreviewed claim as established fact; worth a caveat that [7] is itself a preprint.
- Exclusion accounting is incomplete in the main text: "generations stopped by our token budget… and one template whose fillers the judge never transcribes, are excluded throughout (S10)" gives no per-cell exclusion counts in the visible text, making it impossible to check how much data attrition affected any specific comparison (the k=128 extension does report exclusion counts — 20 hit budget, 10 degenerate — but the main k≤32 panel does not appear to).
- Figure 1's axis labels are garbled in the extracted text ("22 25 k 100 0 count error"), likely a PDF-extraction artifact rather than a paper defect, but should be double-checked in camera-ready rendering.
- arXiv:2605.09239 (Venkatesh, "Repeated-Token Counting Reveals a Dissociation Between Representations and Outputs") is confirmed as a real, closely-matching citation for reference [13]; the paper's engagement with it in Section 5 is appropriately cautious and does not overclaim victory over the rival account — worth noting as a point in the paper's favor.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuine, carefully controlled empirical contrast, but the paper's chain from theorem to horizon relies on post-hoc curve-fitting after an initial disconfirmation, an unaddressed corpus-frequency rival, an unvalidated contraction premise, and rival-elimination tests (architecture, output-policy) run at n=1–2 with post-hoc mediating hypotheses — all of which need tightening before the Lean-verified theorem can be said to explain, rather than merely be compatible with, the measured effect.
