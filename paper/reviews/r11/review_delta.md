### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech (TTS) models' well-known failure to render repeated phrases correctly ("say the word six times") has a mechanistic, provable cause rather than being just an engineering artifact of exposure bias or attention drift. The authors model one repetition boundary as an approximately autonomous state map F and prove (Theorem 1, machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib) that if F contracts with factor q<1, no Lipschitz readout of the decoder state — including its own stop-token logit — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a horizon N* that depends on q, the orbit scale, and the readout margin. Empirically, across six AR TTS checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B) scored with a CTC recognizer (chosen because Whisper's own AR decoder is shown to share the bias under study), repeated-phrase items are undercounted (median error −8.3% at k≥6, exact-match 18.2%) while length-matched non-repetitive controls are essentially exact (94.3%). The deficit looks proportional rather than saturating up to k=32, but a post-hoc extension to k=128 on 4 checkpoints finds saturation, with the fitted "capacity" for repeated content 3.5× lower than for controls in most checkpoints (one checkpoint, Qwen3-TTS-1.7B, shows neither the k≤32 deficit nor the k≤128 saturation). Two non-AR baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) disagree — VITS shows no dissociation, F5-TTS does — leading the authors to withdraw their tentative architectural interpretation in favor of a "where the count must live" hypothesis. Critically, the authors report that direct attempts to measure the theorem's own premise (the contraction factor q, and a dose-response prediction of the attention-dilution lemma) failed or came out sign-reversed, so what is empirically supported is an implication and a periodicity-specific deficit of the theorem's predicted shape, not a validated mechanism.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The primary ladder experiment (k≤32), which carries the paper's headline numbers (−8.3% pooled error, n=457/525, 18.2% vs 94.3% exact-match), reports no exclusion accounting anywhere in the main text, unlike the k=128 extension, which is fully accounted (20/240 budget-truncated, 10/240 degenerate, 21–26 surviving per cell).
   **Where** — Section 4.1/4.3 ("Generations stopped by our token budget... are excluded throughout (S10)"; the extension's accounting appears only in §4.2).
   **Why it matters** — Without knowing how many of the primary-ladder generations were excluded (and whether exclusion rates differ between repeated and control items — a plausible confound, since repeated items are the ones most likely to run on or truncate), the reader cannot tell whether the 76-point exact-match gap is partly an artifact of asymmetric exclusion rather than of counting failure per se. The paper explicitly flags this exact risk for the extension data ("dropping truncated items is conservative, their counts being censored downward") but never performs or reports the equivalent accounting for the much larger, headline-bearing primary ladder.
   **What would address it** — Report per-model, per-k raw generation counts, exclusion counts by reason (budget, degenerate, filler-word template), and exclusion rates split by repeated vs. control condition for k≤32, with a sensitivity check treating excluded items as censored-downward (as they do for k=128).

2. **Issue** — The two non-AR baselines carrying the paper's central "architecture is not the cause" pivot (VITS null, F5-TTS positive) are reported with bare percentages and no sample sizes, seed counts, or methodological parity checks against the AR panel.
   **Where** — Section 4.3, penultimate two paragraphs ("VITS... shows no dissociation (65.6%... against 58.9%...)"; "F5-TTS... does show it: 25.6% against 85.6%").
   **Why it matters** — F5-TTS is the single data point that rescues the paper's causal story after the architectural claim is withdrawn (its presence is used to argue the deficit tracks "where the count must live," not decoder autoregressivity). A one-model, sample-size-unstated result carrying that much interpretive weight is exactly the kind of claim that should be reproducible in detail: how many ladder items, how many seeds, whether the CTC-judge validity audit (done for the AR panel and for VITS's blank-collapse concern in S13) was repeated for F5-TTS, and whether the same truncation/exclusion rules applied. None of this is stated.
   **What would address it** — Report n, seeds, and exclusion accounting for both non-AR baselines at parity with the AR panel; extend the CTC-judge audit explicitly to F5-TTS output (the paper audits VITS via S13 but does not mention doing so for F5-TTS).

3. **Issue** — The theorem's own premise (Assumption 2, contraction) is tested only via two methods described in one sentence each, with no in-text detail sufficient to distinguish a genuine null from an underpowered one.
   **Where** — Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... a perturbation probe found no difference in local contraction between repeated and control items (7 of 18 pairs, p=0.12), its linearity control failing too (S5)").
   **Why it matters** — An 18-pair test with p=0.12 is not evidence of no effect; it is consistent with a real but modest contraction difference that this design lacks power to detect. Because "its linearity control failing too" is mentioned only in passing, a reader cannot judge whether the entire probe is diagnostic. This matters acutely for a paper whose title advertises "a Lean-verified bound": the bound's applicability to real models rests entirely on Assumption 2, and the one attempt at a dose-response test (Lemma 1's premise) came out sign-reversed (flatter attention correlates with *better* counting, r=0.59 [0.35,0.75]) — the opposite of what the dilution mechanism predicts.
   **What would address it** — Report the perturbation-probe protocol (what "local contraction" estimator, what perturbation magnitude, what monotone-read-head requirement it needed and why these decoders lack it) and an a priori power calculation or a post hoc effect-size confidence interval for the 7/18 result, so "failed to detect" is not conflated with "detected absence."

4. **Issue** — Hyperparameter disclosure is incomplete and scattered rather than centralized in Methods.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Sampling uses shipped defaults (XTTS-v2 T=0.75/0.85/50/penalty 5.0; Llasa T=0.8, top-p 1.0; Qwen3-TTS T=0.9/1.0/50/1.05)") vs. Section 4.3, where the reader first learns "the three Llasa checkpoints pass no penalty."
   **Why it matters** — Repetition penalty is one of the paper's own tested rival explanations (§4.3), so its value (or absence) per model is a load-bearing methodological fact, not incidental detail; burying "Llasa has no penalty" three pages after the Methods section risks readers assuming the Llasa T/top-p tuple is the complete decoding configuration. Separately, the "token budget" that triggers exclusion throughout the paper is never given a numeric value anywhere in the provided text, despite gating an unknown fraction of both the primary and extension datasets.
   **What would address it** — A single Methods table listing, per checkpoint: temperature, top-p/top-k, repetition penalty (or "none"), token/step budget, and seed count, with the budget value stated explicitly.

5. **Issue** (claim-vs-evidence, from the reproducibility lens: the one linking number is admitted absent) — The k=128 "horizon" is fit as K̂ from raw counts, which the paper itself states is *not* N*, "a function of the unmeasured q" — so the empirical saturation and the Lean-verified theorem are never actually connected by a shared, measured quantity.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("ˆK a saturation scale read off counts, not the theorem's N∗, a function of the unmeasured q").
   **Why it matters** — Adversarially: if q was never measured, an alternative account — that repeated-phrase rendering hits a generic utterance-length/token-budget ceiling sooner than distinct-word rendering for reasons unrelated to state-contraction (e.g., codec/duration-model saturation, degenerate-loop detection interacting with the token budget) — is not excluded by this data, and is arguably favored by the one checkpoint (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) that shows neither the k≤32 deficit nor the k≤128 saturation while also being flagged as the *most stochastically sampled* checkpoint in the panel (§3). The paper is candid that this checkpoint is "unexplained," but an adversarial reading treats it as a candidate falsifier of the contraction account rather than a curiosity, since Assumption 2 was never independently verified for that checkpoint (or any checkpoint).
   **What would address it** — Either measure q directly for at least the checkpoints used in the k=128 extension (even a coarse, model-specific estimator) and report the predicted N* alongside the fitted K̂ for comparison, or reframe the abstract/title claims to state explicitly that the observed horizon is consistent with, but not derived from, the verified bound.

6. **Issue** — No version pin (Lean toolchain / mathlib commit) is given for the formal-verification artifact.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Formal verification" paragraph, and the Code/data paragraph in Section 3.
   **Why it matters** — mathlib is a continuously evolving library; "contains no sorry" and "depends only on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound" are claims about a specific commit of both the proof and its dependency. Without a pinned Lean/mathlib version (or a CI badge/hash referenced in-text), the "machine-checked" claim is only verifiable at the moment of writing and may silently break as mathlib evolves, undermining the durability of the paper's most distinctive contribution claim ("to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact").
   **What would address it** — State the Lean toolchain version and mathlib commit hash (or tag) used, and note whether CI reproduces the axiom audit on each push.

7. **Issue** — The released repository is named `icassp_antispoofing`, which has no apparent relation to TTS repetition/counting collapse.
   **Where** — Section 3, Code/data paragraph ("https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing").
   **Why it matters** — This is a concrete, checkable discrepancy: either the link is wrong, the repo was repurposed from an unrelated project without renaming, or the artifact bundles unrelated content — any of which a reviewer should be able to resolve by visiting the link, but which as stated in the paper raises reasonable doubt about whether the linked artifact is the one actually described (Lean development, stimuli, per-item tables, both transcript sets, `build.sh`, the 165-clip stratified audio sample).
   **What would address it** — Rename the repository to match the paper, or add a clarifying note explaining the mismatch (e.g., a shared monorepo with the companion ASR paper [7]).

### Minor concerns

- Figure 1 axis labels as rendered in the provided text ("22 25" on the x-axis of panel (a), "26 27" on panel (b)) appear to be garbled superscripts (likely log-scale ticks, e.g. 2², 2⁵); if this is a extraction artifact it should be checked, but if it reflects the actual camera-ready figure, the axes are unreadable.
- Reference [1] is cited despite being "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026" — disclosed transparently, but leaning on a withdrawn preprint's alignment analysis in the Introduction's motivating narrative is worth flagging even with the caveat attached.
- Supplementary sections (S5, S8, S10–S16) are referenced roughly a dozen times for essential methodological detail (exclusion rules, statistical tests, filler-word pool construction, degeneracy audit, multiplicity correction) but the main text never explicitly names or scopes a "Supplementary Material" — a reader working from the main text alone cannot assess several of the paper's central robustness claims.
- The sequence of post-hoc analyses (k=128 extension, penalty sweep, re-generated non-cycled control pool) is dated against a commit log (S16) for transparency, which is commendable, but the *decision to run* the k=128 extension was itself presumably triggered by the k≤32 result being "not what Theorem 1(iv) predicts" — this is disclosed candidly but is still a form of data-dependent stopping that the commit-log dating cannot fully neutralize.
- My arXiv search for prior formal-verification treatments of generative-model hallucination did not surface speech-specific precedents (only general NN verification work, e.g. [1711.00455], [1812.06161]), which is weakly consistent with the "first ... formal artifact" novelty claim, but the search was not exhaustive enough to be treated as confirmation.
- The self-citations to the companion ASR paper [2604.08591] and to [2605.09239] (Venkatesh) were confirmed to exist as described via arXiv lookup, which is reassuring but underscores point 3/5 above: the current paper's own negative test against [13]'s "representations survive, only output policy fails" alternative (the linear-probe analysis in Discussion) is reported with only a single R² figure (median R² 0.48) and no per-checkpoint breakdown, sample size, or probe-training detail.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the theoretical contribution and the periodicity-vs-length dissociation are well-motivated and unusually honestly reported, but the paper as written does not give enough sample-size, exclusion, hyperparameter, and version-pinning detail for its most load-bearing empirical claims (the primary ladder's exact numbers, the F5-TTS/VITS pivot, and the never-measured contraction premise) to be independently verified or reproduced.
