STANCE: Skeptical-but-fair

### Summary
The paper asks whether TTS models' well-documented tendency to lose count on repeated phrases ("say X six times") is really about repetition per se, or a confound with input length/attention-span limits. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" with length-matched non-repeating controls, tested across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2) plus two non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a fourth family (CosyVoice2) introduced only in the results, the authors report a large and robust repeated-vs-control gap (46.8–75.4 points depending on k-threshold) that survives penalty sweeps, greedy decoding, exclusion-rule ablations, and rescoring with four different ASR recognisers. They also formally state (and Lean-4-verify) a contraction-based mechanistic account of why counting should fail, then measure the relevant Jacobian and report the contraction premise is false in every decoder tested (q ≫ 1 throughout), so the verified bound "explains nothing" empirically.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's own mechanistic account (attention flattens across repeated spans → the boundary-to-boundary map becomes an autonomous, potentially contracting operator) is a special case of the transformer token-uniformity/rank-collapse phenomenon established by Dong, Cordonnier & Loukas ("Attention is Not All You Need: Pure Attention Loses Rank Doubly Exponentially with Depth," [2103.03404]), which is not cited anywhere in the reference list, even though the closely related and less foundational Barbero et al. "Transformers need glasses" over-squashing paper [15] is engaged with directly in Section 5.
   **Where** — Section 4 (the Lean-4 contraction lemma and its motivation); Section 5 ("Related work, and what the control decides").
   **Why it matters** — Rank/token-uniformity collapse is the standard explanation in the literature for exactly the near-uniform-attention regime the paper's lemma assumes as its antecedent. Without engaging this prior art, the paper cannot situate what is actually new in its Lean-verified bound: is it a novel corollary, a re-derivation with formal machinery attached, or does it in fact contradict established rank-collapse theory (which predicts uniformity emerges *with depth*, not with repetition count, and predicts a *drift toward* uniformity rather than the bounded-δ regime the paper measures and rejects, δ ≤ 1.23 nats)? The novelty claim of the formal-verification contribution is unassessable without this comparison.
   **What would address it** — Cite and explicitly contrast the rank-collapse literature (at minimum [2103.03404]) against the paper's contraction lemma, and state precisely what is claimed as new: the Lean formalization, the specific per-repetition-boundary framing, or the empirical refutation.

2. **Issue** — The introduction frames the repetition/length confound as an open, previously "confounded" question, but does not cite or discuss any prior TTS robustness/stress-test literature that already probes model behavior on repeated words, tongue twisters, or long/degenerate input strings — despite the stimulus set itself including a "tongue-twister" family, which is a recognized TTS stress-testing tradition.
   **Where** — Section 1 ("Reports of this behaviour confound two things..."); Section 2 (stimuli description mentions "tongue-twister and number-phrase families" with no citation).
   **Why it matters** — If a length-matched controlled comparison for repeated text already exists somewhere in the TTS robustness literature, the paper's leading novelty claim ("a controlled manipulation isolating repetition from length") is weakened or needs qualification. If it genuinely does not exist, the paper should say so explicitly rather than let the absence of citation stand as implicit claim of primacy — a "no prior work does this, to our knowledge" statement is missing.
   **What would address it** — Add an explicit related-work paragraph on TTS text-robustness/stress-test benchmarks and state the novelty claim relative to it, or add the disclaimer if no such work was found.

3. **Issue** — One of the paper's three listed contributions — "a judge methodology for repetitive speech, the obvious judge shown disqualified" — substantially overlaps with the authors' own companion paper on Whisper hallucination dynamics [8], which is cited only once, in passing, at the end of the introduction, not engaged with in the judge-methodology discussion in Section 2 where the overlap is most relevant.
   **Where** — Section 1 (contributions list); Section 2 ("Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong").
   **Why it matters** — Claiming disqualification of Whisper-as-judge as a fresh contribution, while relying for its plausibility on an unreviewed, same-authorship preprint that is not otherwise integrated into the argument, risks double-counting a single research thread as two separable contributions. It also means a key premise of this paper's methodology rests on a citation that has not itself been independently vetted.
   **What would address it** — Either integrate [8]'s findings explicitly as the basis for the judge critique here (making the dependency transparent) and drop the double-counted contribution framing, or make the novel, TTS-counting-specific part of the judge audit (the concatenative-audio ground-truth calibration, n=12/n=24) the sole claimed contribution.

4. **Issue** — Section 5's "rival we could not decide" (is the count erased from state, or does state survive while readout fails?) is presented as an open question the paper investigates, but the cited answer — Venkatesh [2605.09239] — already resolves this exact question for text-only transformers ("Linear probes... decode the correct count with near-perfect accuracy... even at the exact layers where the wrong answer crystallizes in the output"), matching almost verbatim the paper's own late-layer decodability finding (R² 0.48).
   **Where** — Section 5, paragraph "The rival we could not decide."
   **Why it matters** — The novel contribution here is the modality transfer (does this text-model finding replicate in TTS codec-token decoders?), not the open-endedness of the question itself, which was already closed by prior work. The current framing ("we could not decide," "it is not erased") risks reading as an independent discovery rather than a replication/extension, especially since the rank-1 patch experiment that follows (which fails to move the count) is offered as if resolving new territory when it is, at best, a TTS-specific probe of a phenomenon already characterized in text models.
   **What would address it** — Reframe explicitly as a cross-modal replication of Venkatesh's dissociation, with the patch experiment positioned as testing whether the same dissociation is causally load-bearing in the TTS setting — a genuinely new question — rather than as re-litigating whether the dissociation exists at all.

5. **Issue** — CosyVoice2 is introduced as a fourth data point ("CosyVoice 2, whose alignment supervision makes it the design most likely to be immune...") with a full quantitative result (n=90, 28.9% vs 93.3%) in Section 3.1, but is never listed among the panel models in the Methods section ("The panel is six checkpoints but only three designs... Llasa..., Qwen3-TTS..., plus XTTS-v2. VITS and F5-TTS run as non-autoregressive baselines").
   **Where** — Section 2 (Models) vs Section 3.1 (Results, CosyVoice2 paragraph).
   **Why it matters** — Checkpoint version/size, seeds, sampling settings, and whether CosyVoice2 was held out deliberately (the paper implies this — "a fit committed before the checkpoint's first waveform existed") or added post hoc are not disclosed in the Methods description the reader is pointed to. Given the paper's own emphasis on pre-registration discipline elsewhere (e.g., the k≥6 threshold caveat), the absence of CosyVoice2 from the stated panel composition is an inconsistency that undercuts the paper's careful accounting of "6 checkpoints, 3 families."
   **What would address it** — Add CosyVoice2 explicitly to the Methods/Models description with its checkpoint identity and settings, and state clearly whether/when it was held out as an ex-ante extrapolation test versus added afterward.

### Minor concerns
- "VALL-E 2" is rendered with a stray internal space ("V ALL-E 2") in multiple places (abstract, Section 1, reference [5] entry) — looks like a typesetting/kerning artifact, not a content error, but should be fixed.
- Heavy reliance on supplementary sections (S1–S28) not included in the provided text (e.g., S5, S8, S12, S13, S14, S15, S16, S18, S20, S22, S26, S27, S28 are all cited for load-bearing claims) makes several central assertions — the validation gates for the Jacobian estimator (S5), the specification-curve robustness envelope (S11), the counting probe's leave-one-template-out fit (S6) — unverifiable from the main text alone.
- The repo name "icassp_antispoofing" is flagged by the authors themselves as predating the study; harmless but potentially confusing for readers trying to locate the artifact.
- Table 1's "count err." column reports 0.0% for Qwen3-TTS-1.7B's repeated arm while the "exactly right" column shows only 38.9% for the same row — worth a sentence clarifying that a zero median error is compatible with a large non-exact-match rate (the paper does discuss this elsewhere, e.g. "including the one whose median-error gap is zero," but Table 1 itself has no note pointing there).
- "q = 21.0–347.7" and similar ranges throughout would benefit from stating which specific checkpoints/conditions produced the extremes, since the discussion later cites specific per-model q values (Llasa 38/119/348, Qwen-1.7B 24.7) that are not obviously the same range as "21.0–347.7" reported earlier — a brief reconciling note would help.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the empirical core (repetition-vs-length isolation and the honest, well-instrumented refutation of the paper's own mechanistic hypothesis) is unusually rigorous and self-critical, but the novelty attribution around the Lean-verified contraction argument, the judge-methodology contribution, and the "rival we could not decide" framing need to be corrected against missing prior art (notably [2103.03404] and the already-cited [14]), and the CosyVoice2 panel-membership inconsistency needs to be resolved before the contribution claims can be taken at face value.
