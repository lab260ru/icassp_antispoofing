### Summary
The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-known tendency to miscount repeated phrases is a structural, provable consequence of decoder dynamics rather than a decoding-level accident. It models one repetition boundary as a state map on the decoder's hidden state and proves (machine-checked in Lean 4) that if this map is a contraction, any Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — loses the ability to distinguish repetition counts past a computable horizon; a companion lemma shows softmax attention over k repeated spans becomes O(1/k)-uniform. Empirically, across six checkpoints from three model families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B), scored with a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid an autoregressive judge's own repetition bias, repeated-phrase items show a median count error of -8.3% at k ≥ 6 while length-matched non-repetitive controls stay at 0.0% error up to k = 32, and the effective rank of the generation trajectory grows at roughly half the rate under repetition versus control, with the effect surviving output-monotony controls. The authors conclude the decoder stops acquiring internal states capable of representing the count, rather than merely erring on repeated output.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's title and abstract claim a general "structural limit" of autoregressive TTS, but the evidence base is a narrow, internally heterogeneous slice of that space, and the panel's one non-conforming model partially contradicts rather than merely sidesteps the mechanistic story.
   **Where** — Abstract ("Autoregressive text-to-speech models loop, truncate, and lose count..."); Discussion, "Implications and limits" ("the panel is English-only, and all six checkpoints are codec-language models with softmax attention over text, so the claim concerns that class rather than AR TTS at large"); §4.3 (Lemma 1 "covers the four checkpoints with separable text attention; Qwen3-TTS sums text and acoustic embeddings... the lemma cannot be checked there"); Table 1 (capacity-gain ratio ranges 0.28–0.78, not a uniform 0.50).
   **Why it matters** — The scoping sentence appears only in the last paragraph of the Discussion, after the abstract, introduction, and title have already made an unqualified claim about "AR TTS." Within even that narrow class, the attention-dilution mechanism (the theory's starting point) is verified on 4/6 checkpoints, and the panel's designated counterexample, Qwen3-TTS-1.7B, has zero count deficit yet still shows a capacity-gain ratio of 0.78 — a "gap" by the paper's own criterion. If capacity reduction under repetition occurs even in a model that counts perfectly, the capacity statistic does not cleanly discriminate the theorized attractor-driven failure from a generic, mechanism-agnostic entropy reduction that any model might show under repetitive conditioning. That weakens exactly the generalization the paper is built to support: that this is *the* structural explanation, not *a* correlate present alongside a different, unidentified cause.
   **What would address it** — Scope every headline claim (title included) to the tested class explicitly and early; extend the panel to non-codec AR TTS architectures (e.g., attention-based Tacotron2/Transformer-TTS with monotonic alignment, RNN-T-style AR TTS), additional languages, and multiple speaker/voice conditions; report per-model capacity-ratio dispersion rather than leading with only the pooled 0.50 figure.

2. **Issue** — The count-error metric only tallies how many units a transcript contains, not which words they are, so the reported "perfect" control result may reflect a metric blind spot rather than flawless synthesis.
   **Where** — §3, "Behavioural measurement"; §4.2, Fig. 1(b) (control error "0.0% [0.0, 0.0] over n = 639 generations... at every k up to 32, for every model in the panel").
   **Why it matters** — A model could drop filler word #5 and duplicate filler word #3 and still register a correct total count under this metric. The paper's sharpest test — periodicity versus length — rests entirely on the contrast between a literally zero-variance control and a negative repeated-condition error; if the control metric cannot detect substitution/reordering errors, a competing explanation (garden-variety exposure-bias slippage that happens not to change the total unit count) is not ruled out, and the categorical "the two curves would coincide... they do not" framing overstates what was actually shown.
   **What would address it** — Report a content-alignment score (e.g., word error rate restricted to the k target positions, or a Levenshtein alignment against the intended filler sequence) for at least a sample of control items, alongside the count-only statistic.

3. **Issue** — Assumption 2 (contraction, q < 1) — the actual mechanistic premise of Theorem 1 — is admittedly never measured; the empirical case rests entirely on a coarse proxy (effective rank) that is consistent with, but does not establish, a genuine fixed-point contraction.
   **Where** — §4.4, "Two things we could not establish" ("We could not measure q itself... The capacity measure needs no boundaries"); §5, "What is proved, and what would refute it."
   **Why it matters** — The title advertises a "formally verified attractor theory of repetition hallucination," but only the conditional implication is formally verified; the antecedent (that real decoders contract) is inferred from a rank statistic that could fall under repeated conditioning for reasons unrelated to a Banach-type contraction — e.g., learned duplicate-suppression heuristics, dataset-driven frequency effects on codec tokens, or simple reduced local entropy. The paper is commendably honest that this is an open gap, but the honesty doesn't substitute for a test that would actually distinguish the attractor account from these alternatives.
   **What would address it** — Attempt a genuine falsification: apply the same protocol to an architecture/training variant explicitly designed to resist representational contraction (e.g., a model with an explicit counting mechanism, recurrent state, or anti-repetition training curriculum) and check whether the capacity/count signature disappears as the theory predicts, rather than relying solely on within-panel model-to-model variation to carry the causal weight.

4. **Issue** — The related-work discussion omits directly relevant work on whether and how transformer LMs represent and track internal state, which bears on the validity of the effective-rank/ridge-probe operationalization used here.
   **Where** — §5, "The closest prior result..." paragraph and its citation list ([9], [10]-[12], [13]-[14], [15]).
   **Why it matters** — [2503.02854] ("(How) Do Language Models Track State?", Li, Guo & Andreas) studies exactly this question — whether and how transformer LMs internally track evolving state, using targeted probing/causal methodology on tasks like permutation composition and finite-automaton simulation — and finds state-tracking capability and its representational signature vary substantially with task and training regime. That is a direct caution against generalizing a single capacity-ratio statistic, measured on one narrow model class, into a universal "attractor" account of counting failure; the paper's current related-work framing (representational collapse, self-attention dynamics, expressivity-by-circuit-class) skips the literature closest in method to its own capacity measurement.
   **What would address it** — Engage this literature and consider a more targeted causal-intervention test (e.g., activation patching of the count-relevant subspace) that would more directly establish whether count information is being erased versus merely correlating with a coarse rank statistic.

5. **Issue** — Several statistical claims are reported without enough detail to audit their robustness, and pooling masks substantial cross-model heterogeneity.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("median error of -8.3% [−12.5, −8.3] (n = 622)"); §4.2 ("87% of 54 pairs"); §4.3 ("78% of pairs"); Table 1 (capacity-gain ratio 0.28–0.78 vs. the headline "factor 0.50").
   **Why it matters** — The interval "[−12.5, −8.3]" has its upper bound equal to the point estimate itself, which is not explained (bootstrap CI? IQR? one-sided range?) and reads as either a typo or an unconventional interval either way. The "87% of 54 pairs" and "78% of pairs" claims are bare proportions with a small denominator and no accompanying test statistic, p-value, or CI, and no correction is described for the many implicit comparisons across a 6-model × 10-k-value panel. The pooled 0.50 capacity ratio also obscures that individual models range from 0.28 to 0.78 — nearly a 3× spread — which matters for a claim framed as a shared structural signature.
   **What would address it** — Report the number of independent stimulus templates underlying each bootstrap, the resampling unit and iteration count, exact test statistics/CIs for the pairwise proportions, and per-model (not only pooled) effect sizes with intervals.

### Minor concerns
- The linked repository name (`icassp_antispoofing`) doesn't match the paper's subject (TTS repetition hallucination); worth checking before submission that the correct repo/branch is referenced.
- The formula for N* is written inconsistently between the abstract (`log(µ/2LC)/log q`) and Eq. (1) (`log(µ/(2LC))/log q`); the missing parenthesis in the abstract could mislead readers about operator precedence.
- Reference [5] is a same-author companion paper also from 2026, leaned on for "descriptively identified an attractor regime" without independent replication of that identification in this paper.
- The abstract's single headline number ("-8.3% at k ≥ 6") obscures that Table 1 shows a bimodal split (five models from -8.3% to -16.7%, one at 0.0%); a phrase signaling this is a pooled median over a split distribution would help.
- The repetition-penalty ablation (§5) sweeps only {2, 3, 5, 8} plus disabling on a single model (XTTS-v2); the number of seeds/items used for this specific mini-experiment isn't stated.
- "Autonomous" (in "the map... is approximately autonomous") is a dynamical-systems term of art used without a gloss; a clause defining it would help ICASSP's broader audience.
- Table 1's caption abbreviation `dNeff/d log k` isn't restated with units near the table itself, requiring readers to cross-reference §4.3.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the formal contribution and the periodicity-vs-length control are genuinely interesting, but the generalization claims (title and abstract) outrun a narrow, internally heterogeneous six-checkpoint panel whose own outlier partly undercuts the mechanistic story, the theorem's actual premise is admittedly unmeasured, and several headline statistics need clearer reporting before the causal attribution to a contracting fixed-point map can be accepted as established rather than merely consistent with the data.
