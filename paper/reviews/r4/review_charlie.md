*(Note on method: because I had direct access to the manuscript's linked repository, this review verifies several reproducibility claims against the actual artifact rather than taking the paper's prose at face value — e.g. I ran the Lean build/axiom-audit myself and inspected the release's git history and `.gitignore`. Findings below are grounded in what I observed there, cited by file/path.)*

### Summary

The paper argues that repetition hallucination in autoregressive TTS (looping, truncation, miscounting) is a structural consequence of two facts: softmax attention over *k* near-identical repeated spans becomes near-uniform (Lemma 1), and if the resulting per-repetition decoder update contracts, no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can distinguish repetition counts beyond a computable horizon *N** (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, across six TTS checkpoints (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, XTTS-v2, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B), scored with a CTC recognizer chosen to avoid the autoregressive bias of Whisper, repeated-word/sentence stimuli show a median relative count error of −8.3% at k≥6 while length-matched non-repeating controls score 0.0% at every k up to 32 — offered as evidence that the failure tracks periodicity, not length — and the effective rank of the generation trajectory grows at roughly half the rate under repetition versus control, taken as the directly observable signature of the theorem's consequence.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's blanket data/code-availability claim overstates what is actually released: the raw generations, tokens, and hidden-state activations that the headline statistics are computed *from* are not in the linked repository.
   **Where** — §3, "Code and data": *"Everything — Lean development, stimuli, code, per-item tables, both transcript sets — is at https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing."*
   **Why it matters** — Inspecting the repository directly, `data/generated` (audio, tokens, activations, ASR outputs) is a symlink to a private, off-repo mount (`/home/kirill/mnt/hdd_6tb_1/icassp_tts/`), and `.gitignore` explicitly excludes it along with `data/results/**/*.npz` and `data/results/**/*.wav`. Only derived per-item CSV/JSON summaries and ASR transcripts are shipped. This means a reader cannot listen to a single generated file to sanity-check a CTC transcript, cannot inspect a single hidden-state trajectory behind an effective-rank number, and cannot audit whether "audio-level degeneracy" was scored consistently — they can only trust the authors' aggregation code applied to data nobody else can see. "Everything" is not an accurate description of the release.
   **What would address it** — Either host a representative subset of raw audio/activations (enough to spot-check each model × condition), or soften the claim in §3 to specify precisely what is and is not included, and state the compute/environment cost of regenerating the rest (per the repo's own README, ~74GB of checkpoints across four mutually-incompatible conda environments).

2. **Issue** — Decoding hyperparameters that the paper's own mitigation analysis shows are load-bearing for the headline deficit are never reported in the main text.
   **Where** — §3 "Models"/"Behavioural measurement" (absent); relevant contrast is the Discussion's mitigation sweep, *"sweeping XTTS-v2's repetition penalty ... over {2, 3, 5, 8} ... The deficit moves only from −12.5% to −16.1%."*
   **Why it matters** — The repo's generation logs show XTTS-v2's main-panel run used `temperature=0.75 top_p=0.85 top_k=50 repetition_penalty=5.0`; none of temperature, top-p, top-k, or repetition penalty appear anywhere in the four pages provided. Since the paper itself demonstrates that repetition penalty alone shifts the deficit by several points, a reader cannot judge from the text whether Table 1's numbers are a robust property of "the model" or an artifact of one particular sampling configuration, and cannot replicate the reported numbers without consulting the repository.
   **What would address it** — State per-model decoding settings (even compactly, e.g. as a table row or footnote) in the main text rather than relying entirely on the linked repository/supplement.

3. **Issue** — The CTC judge is validated only against synthetic, concatenative audio with known ground truth, never against genuinely model-generated audio, and the rule for reconciling disagreements between the ASR-derived count and the duration-derived count is not specified in the text provided.
   **Where** — §3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong."
   **Why it matters** — The audit (CTC counts at ratio 1.00 for k≥4 on periodic/distinct-word concatenative ground truth vs. 0.19–0.65 for Whisper) establishes that the CTC judge is unbiased on *clean, evenly-spliced* audio. But the phenomenon under study — looping, truncation, spectral flatness — produces exactly the kind of acoustically anomalous audio that a concatenative splice cannot mimic. The repo's own results table carries `count_a`, `count_b`, `agree`, and `count_final` columns, and the internal project notes describe correctness as decided "conjunctively" between ASR and duration signals — none of which is explained in the text given, so it is not possible to assess from the paper alone whether disagreements are resolved in a way that could systematically favor exactness on regularly-paced control audio over repeated audio.
   **What would address it** — Report the CTC judge's error rate on a held-out sample of genuine (non-concatenative) model outputs against a human-verified count, and state the exact tie-break rule between the ASR and duration signals in the main text.

4. **Issue** — The reported control result — 0.0% median error, [0.0, 0.0], across all 639 generations, at every k up to 32, for every one of six architecturally distinct models — is a literally perfect, zero-variance result reported without any error analysis or manual spot-check.
   **Where** — §4.2, "The limit is periodicity, not length"; Figure 1(b).
   **Why it matters** — An adversarial read of this number should ask what would make it false: any single mis-rendered filler word, mis-transcription, or off-by-one recognizer slip anywhere in 639 independent generations from six different codebases would break "exactly 0.0% at every k." A result this clean, with no dispersion reported beyond a degenerate interval, is at least as consistent with a scoring-pipeline effect (e.g., the duration-based signal dominating the conjunctive rule for non-repetitive, evenly-paced speech, discussed in concern 3) as with genuine perfect counting by the models.
   **What would address it** — Report the raw distribution of per-item control errors (not just the median/interval), and describe any manual verification performed on a sample of control transcripts.

5. **Issue** — The single behavioural counterexample used to argue the theory is falsifiable (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B, 0.0% deficit) is exactly the one model for which the paper's own foundational mechanism, Lemma 1, cannot be checked.
   **Where** — §4.1 ("The theorem is silent about such a model") and §4.3 ("Qwen3-TTS sums text and acoustic embeddings per position, so no attention column belongs to the text and the lemma cannot be checked there").
   **Why it matters** — The Discussion frames the panel split as evidence of a genuinely conditional, falsifiable theorem: *"Our data could have shown any of the three [refuting outcomes]."* But the one model that would be needed to instantiate "a model with the deficit whose capacity gain matches its control," or conversely to probe why the deficit vanishes, is architecturally opaque to the paper's own diagnostic (Lemma 1) for exactly this checkpoint. The capacity-gain ratio for Qwen3-TTS-1.7B (0.78, still <1) is at least directionally consistent with the theory, which is a fair point in the paper's favor, but the behavioural exception itself is supported by n=1 model whose relevant premise is unverifiable in principle, not merely unconfirmed empirically — a materially weaker falsification test than the Discussion's framing implies.
   **What would address it** — Either find or engineer an architecture that separates text attention *and* lacks the deficit (to make Lemma 1 checkable in a true exception case), or explicitly flag in the Discussion that the theory's premise is untestable for the one model that appears to refute its consequence.

6. **Issue** — Several reported intervals across the panel (count-error CI, capacity-gain CI for 6 models, entropy slope, probe-retention ratio, mitigation-sweep slope) are treated as independently confirmatory ("disjoint intervals in all six") with no correction for multiple comparisons, and at least one headline interval's statistical meaning is unlabeled.
   **Where** — Abstract and §4.1: *"a median count error of −8.3% [−12.5, −8.3] at k ≥ 6"*; §4.3: *"disjoint 95% intervals for all six panel checkpoints."*
   **Why it matters** — The pooled figure "[−12.5, −8.3]" is never identified as a bootstrap CI, an IQR, or a range of per-model medians (its upper bound coincides with a per-model point estimate elsewhere in the text, which reads more like a min/max of per-model medians than a CI on the pooled median). Meanwhile, "disjoint intervals in all six" checkpoints, plus a battery of secondary analyses (entropy slope, ridge-probe retention, mitigation sweep, matched-pair naturalness test), collectively constitute many implicit comparisons; no adjustment or acknowledgment of the multiplicity is offered, which inflates the apparent strength of "the effect is present in the other 5 of 6 panel checkpoints."
   **What would address it** — Label every interval explicitly (bootstrap CI vs. IQR vs. range), and either apply a multiplicity correction or explicitly justify treating each secondary analysis as a pre-specified, non-multiplicity-inflating confirmatory test.

### Minor concerns

- The repo's own project-memory file (`findings.md`, which the README explicitly tells anyone extending the work to "read first") still reports Llasa-8B's deficit as −25.0% (n=67, single seed), a figure the git history shows was superseded by the three-seed −16.7% (n=103) folded into the actual paper — but `findings.md` was never updated to strike it, contrary to its own stated convention ("mark superseded claims as struck"). Harmless once traced, but a real trap for a reader who follows the paper's own pointer into the repository.
- No LICENSE file is present in the repository, so the terms under which the "released" code and data may be reused are undefined.
- Reference [1] (Wang et al.) is cited to support "the behaviour is well documented as a stability problem" despite being marked withdrawn (13 Feb 2026) in the bibliography itself — the disclosure is honest, but a withdrawn preprint is a weak anchor for the paper's opening motivating claim.
- HF model checkpoints are loaded via `from_pretrained` without pinned revision hashes in the generation scripts, so exact bit-for-bit reproduction is not guaranteed if upstream weights are updated.
- The theorem is stated to hold "for greedy, sampled and beam decoding alike" (§2), which is true of the math, but the empirical panel exercises only one sampling configuration per model; the claim of decoding-rule invariance is asserted, not empirically stress-tested, and the two could diverge in practice even though the horizon itself does not depend on the decoding rule.
- Once inspected, the Lean-formalized Theorem 1(ii)–(iv) is a short chaining of mathlib's existing Banach fixed-point machinery (reused directly for part (i), per the paper's own text) with the triangle inequality and Lipschitz continuity — correct, but mathematically elementary. This is a legitimate contribution pattern (cf. other single-result Lean formalizations, e.g. [2604.16507]), but the "formally verified" framing in the title/abstract risks reading to non-expert reviewers as a claim of deep mathematical novelty rather than of assurance; a calibrating sentence would help.
- "the number of distinguishable states a generation visits grows with k at a factor 0.50 of the control rate" (Abstract) rounds Table 1's six per-model ratios (0.42–0.78, mean ≈0.51); stating that this is a mean/median of heterogeneous per-model ratios, not a pooled single measurement, would avoid the impression of a single tight estimate.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the formal artifact and core empirical design are genuinely sound (I verified the Lean development builds sorry-free with the claimed axiom closure, and the benchmark/stimuli counts match the paper's descriptions), but the paper overstates its own data/code availability, omits load-bearing hyperparameters from the main text, validates its judge only on conditions easier than the phenomenon it measures, and leans its falsifiability argument on the one model where the underlying mechanism cannot actually be checked — each fixable without new experiments.
