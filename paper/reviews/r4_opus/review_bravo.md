# Review — Counting Collapse: A Formally Verified Attractor Theory of Repetition Hallucination in Autoregressive TTS

### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive TTS models mis-count repeated text. It proposes that this is structural rather than a sampling artifact: softmax attention over *k* identical spans is within O(1/k) of uniform (Lemma 1), so the decoder's boundary-to-boundary update is approximately a single autonomous map *F*; if *F* is contractive with factor *q* < 1, boundary states converge geometrically to a fixed point and no *L*-Lipschitz readout with decision margin µ can separate counts beyond N\* ≤ log(µ/2LC)/log q (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4 over mathlib with an axiom audit. Empirically, six checkpoints from three families are run on a 180-item repetition ladder (k ∈ {1…32}) with length-matched distinct-word controls, scored by a CTC recogniser chosen because Whisper's AR decoder is itself biased against repetition. Headline: pooled median relative count error −8.3% at k ≥ 6 on repeated items versus exactly 0.0% on controls, and the effective-rank "capacity gain" dN_eff/dlog k on repeated text is 0.50× the control rate with disjoint bootstrap intervals in all six checkpoints.

**Steelman.** The strongest form of this paper is genuinely attractive, and I want to state it before attacking it. The authors do not claim to have verified their premise — they say explicitly (§4.4, §5) that they could not measure *q*, and that what they test is the theorem's *consequence*, not its antecedent. They anticipate the circularity objection to the effective-rank measure and address it two ways (covariate adjustment; restriction to correctly-rendered items). They anticipate the "your judge is the confound" objection and disqualify the obvious judge with an audit. They anticipate the unfalsifiability objection and name three falsifiers. They report a checkpoint that does not conform and refuse to spin it. The length-matched control is the right control and the qualitative result — 0.0% error on 32 distinct words versus a deficit on 32 identical ones — is, if the measurement holds, a clean dissociation that length-indexed accounts such as [9] do not predict. That is a well-constructed paper by the standards of this literature.

Pressing from there: the argument has three load-bearing joints, and each fails independently. The measurement instrument was validated on a distribution that systematically excludes its one known failure mode; the empirical panel is three systems, not six, and the sentence carrying the exception is factually wrong about its own Table 1; and the quantitative shape of the reported effect (a *constant relative* deficit) is not what a fixed horizon N\* predicts — it is close to what an off-by-one instrument artifact predicts. The Lean artifact, meanwhile, certifies a statement whose antecedent the authors' own §4.4 shows they cannot check and whose formalised form (exact autonomy, fixed-dimensional state) the system is known not to satisfy.

### Major concerns

**1. The CTC judge was audited on exactly the material that hides its known failure mode, and that failure mode points the same way as the reported effect.**

- **Issue** — CTC greedy decoding collapses adjacent identical labels unless separated by a blank frame; the audit that certifies the judge uses *concatenative* audio, which by construction has clean unit boundaries and silence structure that model output at k = 32 will not have.
- **Where** — §3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong"; the audit is described as run "against concatenative audio with known ground truth," and the Whisper comparison explicitly describes the audit material as having a controlled "silence structure." §4.1's headline and Table 1's `count err.` column both rest on this instrument.
- **Why it matters** — This is a distribution-shift problem in the instrument, and it is fatal in the specific direction the paper needs. The paper disqualifies Whisper on the grounds that its bias "point[s] the same way as the effect we want to measure," then adopts a judge whose canonical, textbook failure mode — repeat-collapse on adjacent identical tokens — also points the same way, and audits it only on material engineered to avoid triggering it. A struggling decoder producing run-together, coarticulated repetitions of *very very very…* is precisely the acoustic regime where CTC blank-collapse drops units; distinct-word controls are immune to the artifact by construction, which would produce *exactly* the reported dissociation (deficit on repeated, 0.0% on controls) with no attractor anywhere. It would also explain the otherwise unexplained one-directionality of the error (concern 9) and the −1/k lattice of the reported values (concern 3). Reporting "ratio 1.00 for k ≥ 4" on the audit set does not transfer to the test set, because the audit set differs from the test set on the one variable that drives the artifact.
- **What would address it** — (a) Audit on *degenerate* audio, not clean audio: synthesise or splice repeated units with progressively reduced inter-unit silence and increasing coarticulation, and report the counted/true ratio as a function of inter-unit gap duration; (b) report the distribution of inter-unit gaps in actual model output at each *k*, and show that it lies inside the region where the judge is unbiased; (c) decode with a blank-aware / frame-level alignment count rather than collapsed greedy output, or count units from CTC frame posteriors and peak-picking; (d) cross-validate a subsample with human listeners, blind to condition, at k ∈ {8, 16, 32} — this is a counting task, so human annotation is cheap and decisive.

**2. "Six checkpoints from three families" is three systems, and the sentence that manages the non-conforming checkpoint contradicts the paper's own table.**

- **Issue** — The panel contains one architecture at three scales (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, shared backbone and X-codec2 tokenizer), one Qwen3-TTS family at two scales, and one XTTS-v2; pooled statistics and "all six" claims treat clustered checkpoints as independent replications, and §4.1 calls Qwen3-TTS-1.7B "the largest checkpoint in the panel" when Table 1 lists Llasa-8B at 8B.
- **Where** — §3 "Models"; §4.1 ¶2; Table 1; abstract ("six checkpoints from three families", "disjoint bootstrap intervals in all six").
- **Why it matters** — Two things break. First, the effective replication count for a claim about "autoregressive TTS" is 3, not 6, and n = 622 pooled generations gives false precision when the units are nested within three training recipes. Second, and worse, the factual error is load-bearing: the narrative reading of the exception is that the biggest model has enough capacity to avoid contraction, and that reading is exactly what the corrected fact destroys. Table 1 shows the deficit is *flat* across Llasa 1B→3B→8B (−16.1, −12.5, −16.7; capacity ratio 0.42, 0.28, 0.43 — non-monotone in both columns) and shrinks only within Qwen3 (−8.3 → 0.0; 0.57 → 0.78). So the escape is family-specific, not scale-general, and "Qwen3-TTS-0.6B has the weakest non-zero deficit and its sibling has none" is more parsimoniously read as *a property of the Qwen3 recipe or its text–acoustic embedding scheme* than as the theorem's premise failing. Compounding this: §4.3 states that Lemma 1 cannot be checked on Qwen3 at all, because it sums text and acoustic embeddings per position. The premise mechanism is therefore verified on 4/6 checkpoints and specifically *not* on the family that contains the counterexample and the anomalous capacity ratio.
- **What would address it** — Correct the factual error. Report all panel-level claims per-family as well as per-checkpoint, and treat family as a random effect (or at minimum report the three family-level medians alongside the pooled one). Add at least one further *independent* family with separable text attention (a VALL-E-style or CosyVoice-style codec LM, both already cited as [2, 4]) so the dissociation rests on more than two conforming architectures. State explicitly in §4.1 that the Qwen3 exception cannot be adjudicated because Lemma 1's premise is unmeasurable there.

**3. A constant relative deficit is not what a fixed horizon predicts, and the reported values sit on the −1/k lattice.**

- **Issue** — Theorem 1(iv) gives an absolute ceiling N\* on countable repetitions, which implies relative error ≈ −(k − N\*)/k *worsening* with k; §4.1 instead reports the deficit as "roughly constant in relative terms rather than growing," and every reported per-model value is at or adjacent to a −1/k lattice point (−16.7 = −1/6, −12.5 = −1/8, −8.3 = −1/12).
- **Where** — §4.1 ("The deficit is roughly constant in relative terms rather than growing — Llasa-8B sits at -16.7% across the whole upper ladder — so a model asked for 32 repetitions does not stop at 6; it stops at about 27"); Table 1; abstract.
- **Why it matters** — The paper presents its central quantitative finding as *confirming* the theory when it is in tension with it. If Llasa-8B has horizon N\* ≈ 27, it must be exact at k = 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 and err only at k = 32 — the observed error at k = 6 is then unexplained. Conversely, if the true behaviour is "drops approximately one unit at every k ≥ 6," the relative error is −1/k, which *decreases* in magnitude with k, and pooling medians across five k-values into one number both hides that and produces exactly the lattice values reported. The "stops at about 27" claim is an extrapolation of a pooled median to a single k at which the per-k error is never reported. Note also that the pooled CI [−12.5, −8.3] has its upper limit equal to the point estimate, which is what a bootstrap on a coarse discrete lattice produces — it is not a meaningful interval. And a distribution concentrated on ĉ = k − 1 is precisely the signature of concern 1.
- **What would address it** — Report ĉ (raw counted units), not only relative error, per model per k, with the full distribution — a table of median and IQR of (ĉ − k) at each k ∈ {6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32}. Fit and report a horizon per model: is the error better described by ĉ = min(k, N\*) or by ĉ = k − c? Model comparison between those two forms is the decisive test of the theorem's operative content, and the data to run it already exist.

**4. The effective-rank measure does not test Theorem 1(ii), and a nonzero constant slope ratio is the opposite of the saturation claimed.**

- **Issue** — Theorem 1(ii) is a statement about mutual distinguishability of *boundary* states s_m; N_eff is computed over the whole per-step generation trajectory, whose length itself grows with k, and Table 1 reports a *positive nonzero* dN_eff/dlog k for all six repeated conditions (15.4–31.3) while Fig. 1(c) is captioned "states saturate."
- **Where** — §3 "State measurement"; §4.3; Table 1 `capacity gain` columns; Fig. 1(c,d) and caption.
- **Why it matters** — Three separate gaps. (i) Under contraction to a fixed point, the boundary states collapse, but a trajectory that traverses a periodic orbit retains full *within-period* rank; the paper measures the latter and attributes it to the former. (ii) The theorem predicts the marginal gain to go to *zero* past N\*, i.e., a plateau; a constant slope of 0.5× the control means states keep becoming distinguishable indefinitely, just more slowly — that is a quantitative difference in degree, not the qualitative saturation the caption asserts, and the caption should not say "saturates" while the table reports a positive fitted slope. (iii) Because trajectory length grows with k (more repetitions → more decoded frames), N_eff can grow from sample count alone, and control trajectories contain more lexical variety by construction, so a 2:1 slope ratio is what one would expect from stimulus content even under a non-contracting decoder. The two circularity rebuttals do not close this: covariate adjustment is reported as a single unchanged number (0.50) with no model specification, coefficients, or n; and "restricting to items each model rendered correctly" conditions on the outcome, which is a collider — at a −16% median deficit, the correctly-rendered subset at k ≥ 6 is a biased and probably low-k-dominated sample, which would mechanically compress the k range the slope is fit over.
- **What would address it** — Compute N_eff over *boundary states only* (the theorem's actual object), or state clearly why that is impossible and what the trajectory statistic can and cannot license. Report N_eff at matched trajectory lengths (subsample steps to equal count across conditions). Report the covariate model in full (specification, coefficients, CIs, n). Report the size and k-distribution of the correctly-rendered subset. Reconcile Fig. 1(c)'s caption with Table 1, and test the plateau hypothesis directly (piecewise fit with a breakpoint) rather than fitting a single log-linear slope. Also state the k-range over which panels (c,d) were fit — the axis appears to span only 2²–2⁴, narrower than the behavioural ladder, in which case no state-level claim reaches k = 32.

**5. The formal artifact certifies an idealisation the system is known to violate, and the decoding-rule-independence claim does not follow.**

- **Issue** — Assumption 1 posits a single exact map with s_{m+1} = F(s_m); the text only argues the update is the same "to within O(δ + 1/k)," and the slack is never propagated into the bound. Separately, the claim that the bound is decoding-rule-independent presupposes a *deterministic autonomous* F, which stochastic decoding violates — and the experiments use three seeds per item, i.e. stochastic decoding.
- **Where** — §2, Assumption 1 and the paragraph following Theorem 1 ("with no property of the decoding rule entering, so the bound holds for greedy, sampled and beam decoding alike"); §3 "Models" ("Each item is generated with three seeds"); §5 ¶1.
- **Why it matters** — (i) Under a perturbed iteration s_{m+1} = F(s_m) + e_m with ‖e_m‖ ≤ η, the orbit is confined to a ball of radius ~η/(1−q) and part (iii) becomes |g(s_m) − g(s_n)| ≤ LC(q^m + q^n) + 2Lη/(1−q); if η is not small relative to µ, *no horizon follows at all*. The robust version is the one the paper's own empirics require, and it is neither proved nor formalised. Verifying the exact-autonomy version in Lean and presenting the artifact as certification of the account conflates "this implication is true" with "this implication is about the system." (ii) The decoding-independence claim is not a theorem, it is an assumption: sampling makes the boundary-to-boundary map random, so Assumption 1 is violated in the experimental setting the paper actually runs. This matters concretely because §5's mitigation argument ("mitigations help only insofar as they raise q or enlarge the readout margin") is a claim *about a decoding rule* derived from a bound that was advertised as blind to decoding rules. (iii) Most fundamentally, a transformer decoder's carried state at boundary m is the whole KV cache, which *grows* with m — it is not a point in a fixed metric space evolving under a self-map. That modelling leap is asserted, never argued, and §4.4's measurement failure is arguably a symptom of it.
- **What would address it** — State and formalise the perturbed/robust variant with explicit η, and report what η would have to be for the bound to be non-vacuous. Justify (or drop) the reduction of a growing KV cache to a fixed-dimensional state — e.g., argue via a bounded-summary or last-layer-residual read, and say what is lost. Restate the decoding-rule sentence as conditional on determinism, and either run the empirics under greedy decoding or explain how the seed-averaged behaviour relates to a deterministic F.

**6. None of the four constants in the headline bound is measured, so N\* is never instantiated and the theory cannot be pinned down.**

- **Issue** — §4.4 concedes *q* is unmeasured; *L*, *C*, and µ are never measured or estimated anywhere; consequently equation (1) yields no numeric prediction for any checkpoint.
- **Where** — §2 eq. (1); §4.4; §5 ¶2; absent throughout Results and Table 1.
- **Why it matters** — The abstract advertises "a quantitative impossibility result … with an explicit horizon," and the contribution list claims "an explicit horizon in terms of the contraction factor, the orbit scale and the readout's decision margin." With four unconstrained free parameters, any observed horizon between 0 and ∞ is consistent with the theory post hoc — including the degenerate regime µ ≥ 2LC, where N\* ≤ 0 and the bound predicts *no counting at all*, which is plainly contradicted by exact performance below k = 6. The paper cannot say which regime any of its models is in. The stated falsifiers do not repair this, because the paper already absorbed one of them: Qwen3-TTS-1.7B has a capacity gap without a deficit, which is arguably the second listed falsifier's mirror image, and §5 rescues it by appeal to an unmeasured µ ("as (1) allows when the readout margin is large"). A theory whose escape hatch is an unmeasured constant is not being tested by these data.
- **What would address it** — Bound µ and L directly from the stop head — these are accessible: µ is a logit-margin at the stop decision and L can be estimated for the readout head (its weight-norm gives an upper bound). Even a crude interval on µ/(2LC) plus a rough *q* would let the paper compute a predicted N\* per model and compare it against a fitted horizon (concern 3). That comparison — predicted versus observed horizon across six checkpoints — is the experiment this paper is missing, and it is the only one that would make the theorem load-bearing rather than decorative.

**7. The verification contribution certifies mathematics that was never in doubt, and the novelty claim is about packaging.**

- **Issue** — Theorem 1(i) is mathlib's Banach fixed-point theorem; (iii) is two applications of the triangle inequality plus Lipschitz-ness; (iv) is algebra; Lemma 1 is elementary softmax bounds and a one-line entropy estimate. The paper claims "to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact."
- **Where** — §1 contribution list; §2 "Formal verification."
- **Why it matters** — Machine-checking buys assurance in proportion to the risk of human error, and here that risk is near zero: any competent reader verifies all of Theorem 1 in five minutes. The claim as phrased ("first … as a formal artifact") is a priority claim about the wrapper, not about a result. Meanwhile the genuinely uncertain content — Assumption 1's approximation slack (concern 5), the fixed-state reduction, whether real decoders contract — is exactly what is *not* formalised. The stated methodological benefit ("formalisation forced the separation between what is proved and what is measured") is a virtue of careful writing, not of Lean. As it stands, a reader could reasonably conclude the formal-methods framing is doing rhetorical rather than epistemic work.
- **What would address it** — Either retitle and re-scope the claim to something defensible ("we release a Lean development of the bound"), or make verification earn its place by formalising the parts that are hard: the robust/perturbed bound with explicit η, the composition of Lemma 1's δ-bound into Assumption 1's approximation error, and the stochastic-map variant. That would be a real formal-methods contribution.

**8. Inferential apparatus is thin: no hypothesis tests, no multiple-comparison control, unstated bootstrap resolution, no pre-registration.**

- **Issue** — Every claim is a median with a bootstrap interval; "disjoint intervals" is used as the inferential standard across six checkpoints × multiple statistics × multiple subgroup analyses with no correction and no stated α.
- **Where** — §4.1–4.3; Table 1; Fig. 1(d) caption; absent: any test statistic, any correction, any power analysis, any pre-registration statement.
- **Why it matters** — Disjoint CIs is a crude criterion (roughly conservative for a two-sample contrast but not calibrated), and the paper runs many contrasts: per-model repeated-vs-control count error, per-model capacity gain, two circularity rebuttals, a probability control, an acoustic control, a probe contrast, and a penalty sweep. With no correction and no pre-registration, the reader cannot distinguish confirmatory from exploratory analysis. Critically, the bootstrap is "over stimulus templates" and the number of templates is *never stated* — with 180 items across ten k-values, several stimulus types, and length-matched controls, the template count could plausibly be single-digit, in which case the bootstrap has very coarse resolution and the intervals in Fig. 1(d) and the "disjoint in all six" claim are not trustworthy. The unequal n (622 repeated vs 639 control) is also unexplained despite the design being described as fully matched, which suggests dropped generations that are never characterised.
- **What would address it** — State the number of stimulus templates and the bootstrap procedure in full. Replace "disjoint intervals" with an explicit paired test on matched repeated/control twins (the design is paired — use it), report effect sizes with CIs, and correct across the family of reported contrasts. State clearly which analyses were planned and which were exploratory. Explain the 622/639 asymmetry and report the audio-degeneracy rate, which §3 promises ("reported as its own rate") and the paper never gives.

**9. The error is strictly one-directional, which a fixed-point account does not predict — and which the instrument artifact does.**

- **Issue** — §4.1 reports models "produce fewer repetitions than requested, never systematically more," while §1 and the abstract motivate the work with looping and run-on; a contraction to a fixed point is agnostic about whether the trapped state emits stop or keeps emitting.
- **Where** — Abstract ("loop, truncate, and lose count"); §1 ("it stops early, runs on, or locks into a loop"); §2 ("Stopping early or running on are then the only behaviours available"); §4.1 ("one-directional").
- **Why it matters** — The theory explicitly permits both signs, and the motivating phenomenology includes both, but the data show only one. That asymmetry is unexplained and it is informative: something outside the attractor account is selecting the sign (a length prior, a duration model, an EOS bias — or, per concern 1, an instrument that can only undercount). The paper does not remark on this. Under a serious reading it is the single strongest internal clue that the mechanism is not the one proposed, and it deserves direct treatment rather than being reported as a robustness feature.
- **What would address it** — Report the full signed distribution of (ĉ − k), not just the median, and state how many generations over-produced. If looping genuinely does not occur in this panel, say so and explain why the attractor account nonetheless motivates itself with looping. Test whether over-production appears under any decoding configuration (high temperature, penalty = 1 — the ablation arm already exists).

**10. Reproducibility and reporting gaps that matter specifically for this paper's claims.**

- **Issue** — Decoding hyperparameters are never reported; probe layer indices, speaker/reference conditioning for XTTS, the "causal LM outside the panel," the 54-pair subset, and the template count are all unspecified; the artifact URL names an institution matching the author affiliation.
- **Where** — §3 "Models," "State measurement," "Code and data"; §4.2 ("under a causal LM outside the panel the control is the less probable member in 87% of 54 pairs"); §4.3 ("probe layers", "deep probe layers").
- **Why it matters** — For a paper whose central theoretical claim is that *decoding rules do not matter*, omitting temperature, top-p/top-k, any CFG scale, beam width, and repetition-penalty defaults for five of six models is not a minor omission — the claim is untestable without them, and §5's penalty sweep shows the authors know these settings move the measured quantity. "Probe layers" and "deep probe layers" without indices makes Table 1's capacity gains irreproducible from the paper. The 54 pairs are inconsistent with the 622/639 generation counts and no selection rule is given. It is also unclear whether Qwen3-TTS is open-weight; if it is API-served, the per-step hidden states in Table 1 could not have been collected, and the paper should say. Finally, the repository is named `icassp_antispoofing` — an unrelated project name for this work — and `lab260ru` matches the author affiliation "lab260," which is a deanonymisation problem if the venue is double-blind, independent of whether the artifact is good.
- **What would address it** — A full hyperparameter table (per model: decoding settings, seeds, reference audio, sampling rate, hardware, library versions); explicit layer indices; a named probability model and a stated pair-selection rule; a statement of weight availability per checkpoint; and either an anonymised artifact link or confirmation the venue permits identified ones.

### Minor concerns

- Arithmetic: §5 reports the penalty sweep as "a 3.6-point span across a fourfold change in the penalty, sloping -0.7 points per unit" — over {2,…,8} that is a 6-unit change, giving ≈ −0.6/unit. Also "fourfold" describes 2→8 but the sweep has four *arms*, which reads ambiguously.
- The abstract's "-8.3%" is the pooled median over a bimodal panel one of whose members is exactly 0.0%; it coincides with the *smallest* nonzero per-model value in Table 1 and is a poor summary. Lead with the per-model range.
- Lemma 1's bounds are rendered ambiguously in the text as "e−δ/k ≤ softmax(z)i ≤ eδ/k" — as literally typeset this reads e^(−δ/k), which is not the bound that yields the stated corollaries. Typeset as e^(−δ)/k and e^(δ)/k unambiguously.
- §4.3's Lemma-1 check reports "profile entropy grows with log k at slope 0.97" — slope of what regression, over which k, with what CI? And "no occurrence … captures more than 1.9/k of the block's mass" is unclear: 1.9/k is nearly twice uniform, which is not obviously "within a few percent of uniform."
- §4.4's probe result (0.97 vs 1.11 retention) is reported with no intervals, no test, and no definition of the probe target in the *control* condition (count of what, when the words are distinct). As stated it supports very little.
- The claim "The repeated unit can be a word, a sentence, or a place-value pattern; all three behave the same way" (§4.1) is given with no numbers or per-type breakdown; §4.2's acoustic control quotes "1.7 repetitions" and "1.50×" without units or CIs.
- Figure 1(c) appears to span only k ≈ 4–16, narrower than panels (a,b), which reach 32; if so, no state-level claim extends to the top of the ladder. Label the fitted range explicitly.
- Reference [1] is annotated as withdrawn but is still used in §1 to support "well documented as a stability problem [1, 2]" and in §5 for "Existing mitigations act late [1, 3]." Restrict its use to the stated purpose or drop it from factual claims.
- References [5], [12], [15] are very recent preprints carrying substantial framing weight (the prior spectral study, the dynamical-attractor positioning, and the competing representations-vs-outputs account). [5] is self-citation described as a companion paper, which also deanonymises.
- No ethics, compute, or licence statement; no discussion of model usage terms for the six checkpoints.
- Typography: "N_eff" and "sufficient" render with broken ligatures throughout; §3 "Models. six checkpoints…" begins a sentence lowercase; spacing artifacts in "( 1)", "[ 6]", "[ 13, 14]".
- "Counting collapse" is strong framing for a median relative deficit of 8–17% with exact performance below k = 6; consider whether the title matches the effect size.

---

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the length-matched control and the honest reporting of a non-conforming checkpoint make this worth pursuing, but the headline dissociation is not yet separable from a known CTC repeat-collapse artifact that the audit design specifically fails to probe, the reported constant-relative deficit is in tension with the horizon the theorem predicts, and the bound's four constants are never measured, so the theory is not tested by these data.
