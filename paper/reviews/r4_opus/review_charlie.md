### Summary

The paper argues that repetition-counting failures in autoregressive TTS (undercounting, looping, early stopping on text like "say *very* sixteen times") are a structural consequence of decoder dynamics rather than a sampling artifact. It models one repetition as a state map `F` on the decoder hidden state, proves via a Banach-fixed-point argument that if `F` is a `q`-contraction then no `L`-Lipschitz readout can separate repetition counts beyond `N* ≤ log(µ/2LC)/log q` (Theorem 1), supports this with an attention-dilution lemma (softmax over `k` identical spans is within `O(1/k)` of uniform), and machine-checks both in Lean 4 / mathlib with a `sorry`-free axiom audit. Empirically, a 180-item repetition ladder (`k ∈ {1…32}`) is run over six checkpoints from three families, scored by a CTC recogniser (chosen because Whisper's AR decoder is itself biased against repetition), yielding a median relative count error of −8.3% at `k ≥ 6` on repeated text versus 0.0% on length-matched distinct-word controls, plus an activation-level claim that the effective rank of the generation trajectory grows with `log k` at ~0.50× the control rate.

The paper is unusually candid about its own gaps (§4.4, §5), which I credit. That candour does not, however, rescue the central inferential chain, and the adversarial reading below is that the headline theory is *inconsistent with the paper's own data*, not merely under-supported by it.

### Major concerns

**1. The "formally verified" contribution is unauditable from the paper, and the formalization gap is never addressed.** *(reproducibility lens)*

- **Issue** — The paper's most distinctive claim ("the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact") rests on a Lean development whose *statements* are never shown, whose toolchain/mathlib version is never pinned, and whose correspondence to the informal Theorem 1 is never argued.
- **Where** — §2 "Formal verification"; §3 "Code and data" (`check_lean.sh`); abstract ("machine-checked in Lean 4 with mathlib, free of `sorry` and of nonstandard axioms").
- **Why it matters** — `sorry`-freeness and an axiom audit certify only that *some* Lean proposition was proved. They say nothing about whether that proposition is Theorem 1. The entire scientific value of formalization here is the guarantee that the informal statement is the verified one, and that link is exactly what is omitted. Meanwhile, a mathlib-backed Banach argument is version-sensitive: `contractingWith` / `edist_le_of_...` API names and hypotheses have moved across mathlib releases, so "reruns the axiom audit" is not reproducible without a commit pin and a `lean-toolchain` reference. A reader today cannot check the one thing the paper says makes it novel.
- **What would address it** — Print the verbatim Lean statement of Theorem 1(i)–(iv) and Lemma 1 in the paper (or a numbered appendix listing), state the mathlib commit and Lean toolchain version, give the archived artifact a DOI (Zenodo/Software Heritage) with a commit hash, and add a paragraph on formalization adequacy: what `S`, `F`, `g`, `µ` are in Lean, and which informal claims are *not* in the artifact (e.g. that `s_m` is a transformer's state at all).

**2. The empirical artifact is under-specified to the point that the reported numbers cannot be regenerated, despite the claim that they can.** *(reproducibility lens)*

- **Issue** — Decoding configuration, sampling hyperparameters, checkpoint identifiers, per-condition sample counts, and exclusion criteria are all absent; two of the six models are neither cited nor identified.
- **Where** — §3 "Models", §3 "Code and data" ("`build.sh` regenerates every number here"); §4.1 (`n = 622`), §4.2 (`n = 639`); Table 1. Qwen3-TTS-0.6B/1.7B appear in Table 1 and throughout §4 with **no reference entry anywhere in §6** (contrast Llasa [6], XTTS-v2 [7]).
- **Why it matters** — (a) The paper's strongest theoretical selling point is that "no property of the decoding rule enter[s]" Eq. (1), yet temperature, top-p/top-k, CFG scale, and default repetition penalties are never disclosed for *any* model; XTTS-v2 is knowingly run with its shipped repetition penalty active while Llasa and Qwen3 configurations are unstated, so the six-model comparison in Table 1 is confounded by unequal decoding settings by construction. (b) `n = 622` for repeated items at `k ≥ 6` is **not divisible by 3**, although §3 states every item is generated with three seeds; the matched control set is 639. Under a matched design these should be equal and both multiples of 3 (639 is, 622 is not). Generations were therefore dropped, or the design is unbalanced — either way the attrition, its cause, and whether it correlates with failure (e.g. non-terminating generations, degenerate audio) is undisclosed, and dropping exactly the pathological generations would bias the −8.3% *toward zero* or away from it depending on direction. (c) §3 promises audio-level degeneracy is "detected separately and reported as its own rate" — that rate never appears in the paper. (d) Qwen3-TTS is the model carrying the paper's most load-bearing anomaly (the sole non-deficit checkpoint, §4.1, §5) and the model where Lemma 1 admittedly cannot be checked (§4.3); it is also the one a reader cannot obtain, version, or verify from the text.
- **What would address it** — A configuration table: per model, the exact HF repo id + revision hash, decoding parameters, any built-in penalties (including whether they were left at library defaults), GPU/driver/library versions, and per-cell `n` for every (model × k × condition). A citation and checkpoint identity for Qwen3-TTS, or its removal. An explicit exclusion/attrition table reconciling 622 vs 639. Release of the generated audio, not only "both transcript sets" — transcripts alone cannot be re-scored under a different judge, which is precisely the audit a skeptical reader needs (see concern 6).

**3. The data contradict the theorem's central prediction: Eq. (1) predicts a hard ceiling, the experiments show a constant *rate*.**

- **Issue** — Theorem 1(iv) gives an absolute horizon `N*` beyond which counts are inseparable; the reported behaviour is a roughly constant *relative* deficit, which implies the absolute achievable count grows without bound in `k`.
- **Where** — §4.1: "The deficit is roughly constant in relative terms rather than growing — Llasa-8B sits at -16.7% across the whole upper ladder — so a model asked for 32 repetitions does not stop at 6; it stops at about 27."
- **Why it matters** — This sentence is presented as a reassuring detail; adversarially it is a refutation. A model governed by a horizon `N*` should saturate: requesting `k = 8, 12, 16, 24, 32` should all yield roughly `N*` units, i.e. a relative error that becomes *more* negative as `k` grows (−0%, −20%, −40%, …). Llasa-8B instead delivers ~27 at `k = 32` and ~5 at `k = 6` — behaviour with **no fixed horizon at all**. Constant relative error is the exact signature of a per-unit dropout process (each repetition independently skipped with probability ~0.08–0.17), a mechanism that is *not* what Theorem 1 describes and that is fully compatible with the competing output-policy account the paper cites at [15]. The paper's own §5 falsification criteria do not include this test, and the data fail it.
- **What would address it** — Report absolute counts `ĉ` against `k` (not only the relative error) per model, and test the horizon prediction directly: does `ĉ(k)` plateau? If it does not, the theory as stated is contradicted and the paper must either (a) reframe the contribution as "periodicity-specific degradation" without the `N*` horizon as the operative claim, or (b) extend the theory to a stochastic per-repetition model that predicts constant relative loss and show that Eq. (1) is not the right consequence to be testing.

**4. "States saturate" is asserted in a figure caption and contradicted by the paper's own table.**

- **Issue** — Theorem 1(ii) implies the capacity gain `dN_eff/d log k` should tend to zero under repetition; Table 1 reports it as strongly positive (15.4–31.3) for every checkpoint.
- **Where** — Fig. 1(c) label "states saturate"; §4.3 "the states a decoder visits stop being mutually distinguishable"; abstract "it stops acquiring states in which the count could be represented"; Table 1, column `rep.`.
- **Why it matters** — A ratio of 0.50 between two positive growth rates is *not* saturation, and it is not what part (ii) predicts. Under genuine contraction, `d(s_m, s_n) → 0`, so additional repetitions buy asymptotically no new distinguishable states and the repeated-text gain should approach 0, not 22.0 (Llasa-1B) or 31.3 (Qwen3-1.7B). Nothing in Theorem 1 predicts the value 0.50, or any ratio; the theorem is therefore not being tested by this measurement, only loosely gestured at. The abstract's "the theorem's consequence is directly observable" overstates what §4.3 shows.
- **What would address it** — Either show curvature evidence that `N_eff(log k)` on repeated text is decelerating toward a plateau (fit a saturating model, report the asymptote and its CI, compare AIC against the linear fit currently used), or drop the saturation framing and present §4.3 honestly as "reduced but positive state accumulation," and correct Fig. 1(c)'s caption.

**5. The effective-rank result has a mechanical length confound that the stated controls do not remove.**

- **Issue** — Undercounting shortens the generation, and the effective rank of a `T × d` trajectory is hard-capped by `T`; the repeated condition is by definition the one with fewer emitted tokens.
- **Where** — §3 "State measurement"; §4.3, including the rebuttal paragraph ("Adding realised output diversity … as covariates leaves the ratio at 0.50; restricting to items each model rendered correctly … leaves it at 0.52").
- **Why it matters** — A rank ceiling is not a linear nuisance; entering length as an additive covariate in a regression does not undo a hard constraint on the statistic's support, so the first control is the wrong instrument. The second control (restrict to correctly rendered items) is better in principle but is confounded in a different way: at high `k` a checkpoint with −16.7% error renders almost nothing correctly, so the restricted repeated sample is truncated toward small `k` while the control sample spans the full ladder — the two slopes are then estimated over different ranges of `log k` and are not comparable. The paper reports neither the restricted `n`, the per-model restricted ratios, the covariate coefficients, the number of bootstrap templates, nor the probe layers ("deep probe layers", Table 1 caption, is never defined). §4.3 also reports "a positive gap in 78% of pairs" — i.e. 22% of matched pairs run the *wrong* way — while claiming per-model intervals are disjoint in all six; that tension is unexplained and no paired test statistic is given.
- **What would address it** — Match trajectories on realised token count by truncating both members of each pair to the shorter length before computing `N_eff` (or subsample to a fixed `T`), and report the ratio under that construction. Report the restricted-analysis `n` per model per `k`, the exact probe layer indices, the number of stimulus templates entering the bootstrap, the covariate model and its coefficients, and a proper paired test (sign/Wilcoxon with CI) for the 78% figure.

**6. The CTC judge is validated on stimuli that do not resemble the material it is used to score, and no human ever listens to a single generated item.**

- **Issue** — The audit that qualifies wav2vec2-CTC and disqualifies Whisper is run on "concatenative audio with known ground truth", i.e. spliced speech with clean boundaries and silence structure — not on continuous, coarticulated TTS output.
- **Where** — §3 "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong": "Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth… on periodic ground truth it counts at ratio 1.00 for `k ≥ 4`".
- **Why it matters** — This is the single point on which the entire headline number depends, and adversarially it is the most likely place for the result to be an artifact. Greedy CTC decoding collapses repeated emissions; the standard blank-separation mechanism that lets CTC represent two adjacent identical units degrades exactly when units are coarticulated and prosodically compressed — which is what a TTS model produces when asked to say the same word sixteen times in a row, and *not* what a concatenative splice produces. So a judge shown to be exact on spliced periodic audio can still undercount on synthesised periodic audio, and its error would be periodicity-specific and one-directional: the same signature the paper attributes to the decoder. That the distinct-word controls score exactly 0.0% is consistent with judge bias, not evidence against it, because distinct words are the easy case for CTC in both the audit and the test. The paper explicitly identified this class of danger for Whisper and then applied a weaker version of the same test to its replacement.
- **What would address it** — A human listening study: sample ~100 generations stratified over model × `k` × condition, have annotators count the rendered units blind to condition, and report judge-vs-human agreement and the CTC error as a function of `k` **on model output**, not on splices. Additionally, report a second independent non-AR judge (e.g. a different CTC acoustic model, or forced alignment against the requested transcript) and show the −8.3% survives. Release the audio so others can re-judge.

**7. Assumption 1 does not describe an autoregressive transformer, and the claimed decoding-rule independence is inconsistent with it.**

- **Issue** — The paper models the decoder as a point in `R^d` evolving under a single autonomous map `F`, but an AR transformer's carried state is the growing KV cache, and under sampling the boundary-to-boundary update is a random map, not a function.
- **Where** — §2, Assumption 1 ("There is a single map `F : S → S` with `s_{m+1} = F(s_m)` for all `m`"); §2 after Eq. (1) ("no property of the decoding rule entering, so the bound holds for greedy, sampled and beam decoding alike").
- **Why it matters** — (a) The state that determines the next repetition includes every previous key/value plus position information, so it is neither fixed-dimensional nor time-invariant; `F` as written does not exist for a transformer except as an approximation whose error is never bounded. This matters concretely because a counter can plausibly be *implemented* in exactly the part of the state the model discards — attention over the model's own prior output (induction-head-style counting) — which grows rather than contracts. The paper never considers this mechanism. (b) Under sampled decoding `s_{m+1}` is a random variable given `s_m`, so Assumption 1 is simply false, and the contraction argument would need an almost-sure Lipschitz condition on the random map (and a corresponding statement about the invariant measure, not a fixed point). The sentence claiming the bound covers sampled and beam decoding is therefore unsupported by the theorem as stated — and, notably, the paper's own mitigation sweep in §5 shows the deficit *moving* with the repetition penalty (−12.5% → −16.1%), i.e. decoding-rule dependence in the data.
- **What would address it** — Restate Assumption 1 honestly as an approximation with a quantified residual (`s_{m+1} = F(s_m) + e_m`, `‖e_m‖ ≤ η`) and propagate `η` into Eq. (1) — this changes the conclusion materially (the horizon becomes a resolution floor `η/(1−q)`, not a geometric collapse). Give a separate stochastic-map statement if the sampled-decoding claim is to be retained, or withdraw that claim. Discuss why a KV-cache-resident counter is excluded.

**8. Because `q` is never measured, regime assignment is post hoc and determined by the outcome it is meant to explain.**

- **Issue** — The theorem's antecedent is unmeasurable by the paper's own admission, so the sole disconfirming checkpoint is reassigned to a "non-contracting regime" on no evidence other than its lack of a deficit.
- **Where** — §4.4 ("We could not measure `q` itself"); §4.1 ("The theorem is silent about such a model — if its per-repetition map does not contract, no horizon follows"); §5 ("What we establish empirically is the theorem's consequence, not its antecedent").
- **Why it matters** — This makes the framework locally unfalsifiable in exactly the way §5 tries to pre-empt. The stated refutation criteria are also weaker than they appear: criterion 3 ("a probe reading the count as well late as early") is *nearly satisfied* by the paper's own data — 0.97 retention on repeated text vs 1.11 on controls (§4.4), with no CIs, no test, and an effect whose entire support is a sign consistency across six non-independent checkpoints (one-sided sign test `p ≈ 0.016`, uncorrected, and the six checkpoints include a 1B/3B/8B ladder from a single family, so the effective independent `n` is nearer 3 than 6). And Qwen3-1.7B is described in §5 as "instructive" (no deficit, capacity gap present) — a pattern that is equally the prediction of "the capacity measure is confounded" (concern 5) as of "capacity loss is necessary but not sufficient".
- **What would address it** — Provide *any* independent estimate of contraction that does not require a monotone read-head: e.g. finite-difference Jacobian norms of the boundary-to-boundary map via autodiff on perturbed hidden states, or empirical Lipschitz estimates `‖F(s+ε) − F(s)‖/‖ε‖` over sampled perturbations, per model. If Qwen3-1.7B is genuinely non-contracting this is testable, and if it contracts like the others the account fails. Also report CIs and a proper test for the 0.97/1.11 probe contrast.

**9. The observed error is one-directional; the proposed mechanism is symmetric.**

- **Issue** — Contraction erases the count, which should produce both premature stopping *and* run-on, but the data show only undercounting.
- **Where** — §1 ("it stops early, runs on, or locks into a loop"; "Stopping early or running on are then the only behaviours available"); §4.1 ("the error is one-directional: models produce fewer repetitions than requested, never systematically more").
- **Why it matters** — An erased counter read by a stop head should fail in whichever direction the head's prior points, and across six checkpoints from three families one would expect at least some to run on. Uniform undercounting is the signature of a *biased readout* — an EOS/stop prior that fires early under monotonous acoustics — which is precisely the "output policy fails" account of [15] that §5 concedes it cannot exclude. The paper's abstract nonetheless asserts "the decoder does not merely err on repeated text; it stops acquiring states in which the count could be represented," which is stronger than the evidence supports.
- **What would address it** — Report the full distribution of `ĉ − k` (not just medians): if there is genuine bidirectional dispersion masked by a median, say so; if not, address why. Directly probe the stop-head logit trajectory as a function of `m` on repeated vs control text, and test whether the early-stop bias is present at fixed decoded state, which would separate readout bias from state collapse.

**10. Lemma 1's empirical verification is internally inconsistent.**

- **Issue** — Two reported attention statistics cannot both be true under Lemma 1's own inequality.
- **Where** — §4.3, final paragraph: "the implied spread is `δ ≤ 0.06` nats across the ladder, and no occurrence — not even the most attended — captures more than `1.9/k` of the block's mass".
- **Why it matters** — Lemma 1 states `softmax(z)_i ≤ e^δ/k`. With `δ = 0.06`, `e^0.06 ≈ 1.06`, so the maximum block mass would be `≤ 1.06/k`. A most-attended occurrence at `1.9/k` implies `δ ≥ log 1.9 ≈ 0.64` nats — an order of magnitude larger, and it means the most-attended copy receives nearly *twice* uniform weight, which is not "within a few percent of uniform" as the same sentence claims. Separately, `δ` inferred from an entropy slope is a weak witness: entropy is insensitive to a small number of concentrated columns, so deriving `δ` from `H ≈ 0.97 log k` and then citing a max-mass statistic that contradicts it suggests the two were computed on different objects (different layers? different `k`? averaged over heads before or after softmax?). Since Assumption 1's plausibility rests entirely on this paragraph, and it is uncheckable for 2 of 6 checkpoints (Qwen3), the premise is effectively unverified.
- **What would address it** — Recompute and reconcile: report `δ` directly as `max_a z_a − min_b z_b` over occurrence columns (per layer, per head, per `k`), alongside the max-mass statistic, and state explicitly which layers/heads are aggregated and in what order. If the true `δ` is ~0.6 nats, propagate that into the claim that the conditioning is "the same to within `O(δ + 1/k)`" — at `δ = 0.64` the `O(δ)` term dominates and Assumption 1 is not well supported.

**11. Attribution to *autoregressive decoder* dynamics lacks the decisive control: a non-AR TTS system.**

- **Issue** — Every checkpoint in the panel is an AR codec language model; there is no non-autoregressive (diffusion/flow-matching/NAR) TTS arm.
- **Where** — §3 "Models"; §5 "Limits" (acknowledges the class restriction but does not treat it as a design gap).
- **Why it matters** — The periodicity-vs-length contrast (Fig. 1b) is the paper's sharpest result, but as run it cannot distinguish "AR decoder state contracts" from "the text encoder / grapheme frontend / codec tokenizer loses count on periodic input" — none of which involve the recursion the theorem is about. A NAR system with no decoder-state recursion is a cheap, decisive control: if it also undercounts periodic text, the attractor account loses its explanatory monopoly. Relatedly, the Llasa scale ladder is non-monotone (1B −16.1%, 3B −12.5%, 8B −16.7%, Table 1), so within-family scale — the one designed comparison in the panel — yields no interpretable trend, and this is not discussed.
- **What would address it** — Add at least one NAR/diffusion TTS system on the identical ladder, and comment on the non-monotone Llasa ladder (is the 3B/8B difference within bootstrap error? no CIs are given for the count-error column in Table 1 at all).

**12. Eq. (1) is never instantiated, so the "explicit constants, not an asymptotic" contribution yields no prediction.**

- **Issue** — `L`, `C`, `µ`, and `q` are unmeasured for every model, so `N*` is computed nowhere in the paper.
- **Where** — §2 (Eq. 1, and "is why (1) carries explicit constants rather than an asymptotic"); §4.4; Table 1 (no `N*` column).
- **Why it matters** — The theorem's advertised advantage over the length-indexed prior work [9] is that "(1) supplies a rate in repetitions that a length-indexed bound does not" (§5). But a rate with four unmeasured constants supplies no rate. The theorem also cannot explain why the deficit onsets at `k = 6` specifically, cannot predict which models have deficits, and cannot predict the magnitude (−8.3%) or the 0.50 capacity ratio. Stripped of instantiation, Theorem 1 is a short corollary of the Banach fixed-point theorem plus Lipschitz composition — correct, but not by itself a research contribution, and the Lean artifact verifies the part of the paper that was never in doubt.
- **What would address it** — Estimate `L` (readout Jacobian norm of the stop head — directly computable), `C` (from `d(s_0, F s_0)` and any `q` estimate per concern 8), and `µ` (stop-logit margin at the decision boundary), then report predicted `N*` per model and compare against observed collapse onset. Even a coarse per-model `N*` with wide error bars would convert this from an unfalsifiable framework into a testable one — and would be the paper's strongest possible result.

**13. Ethics, licensing, and citation hygiene are unaddressed.**

- **Issue** — No ethics statement, no licensing/terms disclosure for the six checkpoints or the released speech, no provenance for XTTS-v2's zero-shot speaker references, and a withdrawn preprint used as framing.
- **Where** — Absent (no ethics/broader-impact section anywhere); §3 "Code and data"; reference [1], annotated "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026; cited for its alignment analysis", relied on in §1 ("well documented as a stability problem [1,2]") and §5 ("Existing mitigations act late [1,3]").
- **Why it matters** — XTTS-v2 is a voice-cloning system requiring a speaker reference; releasing synthesised speech in that voice has consent implications that the paper does not mention, and the repository is not identified as containing audio or not. Model licences (Llasa/Llama-derived, XTTS-v2's non-commercial CPML terms) constrain redistribution of derived artifacts and are unstated. Building the motivation and the mitigation discussion on a *withdrawn* preprint, without saying why the withdrawal does not affect the cited claims, is a real citation-integrity problem — the annotation acknowledges the withdrawal but does not discharge it. Finally, the release URL (`github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing`) names a repository for an apparently unrelated anti-spoofing project, with no commit hash, branch, or subdirectory given.
- **What would address it** — Add an ethics/data statement (speaker reference provenance and consent, audio release policy, model licences); pin and correctly name the artifact repository (or a DOI-archived snapshot); state explicitly which claims from [1] survive its withdrawal, or replace it with a non-withdrawn source.

### Minor concerns

- §4.1 calls **Qwen3-TTS-1.7B "the largest checkpoint in the panel"**, but Table 1 lists Llasa-8B at 8B parameters. Straight factual contradiction, and it appears in the sentence carrying the paper's key anomaly.
- The abstract's headline CI is degenerate: **"median error of -8.3% [−12.5, −8.3]"** — the point estimate sits exactly on the interval boundary. Likewise "0.0% [0.0, 0.0]". Both are lattice artifacts of a median over `(ĉ−k)/k` with `ĉ` integer; report the discreteness explicitly or use a continuous summary, and say which bootstrap (percentile? BCa?) and how many resamples.
- Pooled `n = 622` / `n = 639` treat generations as independent, but they are nested (3 seeds × 6 models × shared stimulus templates). The capacity analysis bootstraps over templates; the count-error analysis apparently does not. Pseudo-replication inflates precision in the headline number.
- **The `k ≥ 6` analysis cut-point is chosen after inspecting Fig. 1(a)** ("From `k = 6` the panel splits") and then every headline number is conditioned on it. No preregistration is claimed. State the cut-point choice as post hoc, or show the result is stable across cut-points.
- No multiple-comparison control is mentioned anywhere despite six checkpoints × several statistics × ten `k` levels, and "disjoint bootstrap intervals in all six" is used as the significance criterion throughout.
- §4.2's alternative-explanation tests are reported as bare percentages with no test or CI: "the control is the less probable member in **87% of 54 pairs**", "differing by **1.7 repetitions** while the token count at collapse differs **1.50×**". The last is hard to parse — 1.7 repetitions of what baseline, and is 1.50× large or small under the acoustic account? These are relegated to "supplement, S8", which reviewers of a 4-page submission may not have.
- Control-side capacity gains span 34.9–55.8 across models (Table 1) — a ~60% spread in the *denominator* of every reported ratio — with no discussion of what drives it.
- Figure 1 has rendering defects: a literal LaTeX escape leaks into the axis label (`count error (\%)`), and the (c) panel legend entries run together (`Llasa-1BLlasa-3BLlasa-8BXTTS-v2`). Ligature corruption in the body text (`Neﬀ`, `suﬀicient`, `eﬀective`).
- Table 1's "capacity gain is `dN_eff/d log k` at **deep probe layers**" — "deep" is never defined; give layer indices per architecture. `N_eff` also needs its construction stated (trajectory matrix orientation, whether singular values are of the raw or mean-centred matrix, `T` used).
- The intro's motivating example — number phrases like "six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six" — is in the stimulus set, but no separate result for the number-phrase subset is ever reported beyond "all three behave the same way" (§4.1). Show it.
- Eq. (1) in the abstract is typeset without disambiguating parentheses (`N* ≤ log(µ/2LC)/log q`); `µ/2LC` reads ambiguously.
- The claim of being "the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact" is hedged into near-vacuity by conjunction (first + hallucination + speech synthesis + formal artifact). Either drop it or support it with a search protocol.
- Prior work on contracting recurrent dynamics forgetting inputs at a geometric rate (the fading-memory / echo-state-property literature) is the direct classical antecedent of Theorem 1 and is not cited; §5 engages only the transformer-specific literature.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the periodicity-vs-length control (Fig. 1b) is a genuinely valuable experiment and the paper is admirably explicit about its unmeasured premise, but the headline framing is not supported: the reported constant *relative* deficit is inconsistent with the fixed horizon `N*` that Theorem 1 predicts (concern 3), the "states saturate" claim is contradicted by the paper's own Table 1 (concern 4), the attention statistics verifying Lemma 1 are mutually inconsistent (concern 10), and the formal and empirical artifacts are both under-specified to the point of being unauditable (concerns 1–2) — all of which require reframing and substantial new measurement rather than editorial repair.
