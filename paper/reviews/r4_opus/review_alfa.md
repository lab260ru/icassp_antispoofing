### Summary

The paper makes three claims: (1) that undercounting of repeated phrases by AR TTS decoders is a *structural* limit provable from periodic conditioning plus contraction, yielding a horizon N\* = log(µ/2LC)/log q; (2) that this is machine-checked in Lean 4 and is the first formally verified hallucination result in speech synthesis; (3) that empirically, six checkpoints undercount repeated units by a median −8.3% at k ≥ 6 while length-matched distinct-word controls are exact, and that trajectory effective rank grows at 0.50× the control rate. The writing is unusually honest in places — §4.4 admits two failed measurements, the mitigation sweep is reported as negative, the Whisper-vs-CTC judge audit is a real piece of methodological work, and the artifact release is above average for this venue. But the paper's core empirical signature — a *constant relative* deficit of ~1 unit in 6 to 12, one-directional, immune to the decoding rule, absent on distinct words — is exactly what a per-unit merge/elision process produces and is *not* what a fixed horizon N\* predicts. The theorem is Banach's fixed-point theorem plus the triangle inequality; its antecedent is never measured; and the paper's own probe number (0.97 retention late-vs-early) implies q ≈ 1, under which the theorem imposes no bound at all on any model in the panel. I read the headline as most likely an artefact of the CTC judge in a regime its audit did not cover, wrapped in a theorem that would not constrain the models even if the measurement were sound.

### Major concerns

**1. The measured error is proportional, not horizoned — this contradicts the theorem it is offered as evidence for. [Evidence — alternative explanations]**
- **Where** — §4.1: "The deficit is roughly constant in relative terms rather than growing — Llasa-8B sits at -16.7% across the whole upper ladder — so a model asked for 32 repetitions does not stop at 6; it stops at about 27." Also Fig. 1(a).
- **Why it matters** — Theorem 1(iv) says counts above N\* are *unreadable* by any Lipschitz readout. If a fixed horizon N\* existed, ĉ would saturate near N\* and relative error would trend toward −(k−N\*)/k → −1 as k grows. Instead ĉ ≈ 0.83k over the entire upper ladder, i.e. the decoder tracks k *proportionally* out to 27 units. That is a multiplicative rate effect: each unit has an approximately independent ~1/6 chance of being dropped. A per-unit dropout rate is the signature of elision or of a segmentation/merge failure, not of state collapse to a fixed point. The paper reports this as a reassuring detail ("does not stop at 6") when it is the strongest disconfirmation of its own model in the data. This single fact governs whether a reader should update at all toward the attractor account.
- **What would address it** — Fit both models to the per-model ĉ(k) curves and report which wins: saturating (ĉ → N\*) vs proportional (ĉ = αk). If proportional wins, say so and reframe the theory, or explain how a contraction produces a constant relative rate. Report per-model N̂\* estimates from the saturation fit and compare them against Eq. (1) instantiated with measured L, C, µ.

**2. The CTC judge is not audited in the regime where the effect lives, and its known failure mode reproduces every qualitative feature of the result. [Evidence — instrument validity / baselines]**
- **Where** — §3, "Behavioural measurement": "put through the same audit that disqualified its predecessor: on periodic ground truth it counts at ratio 1.00 for k ≥ 4."
- **Why it matters** — CTC greedy decoding collapses runs of identical emitted symbols unless separated by a blank. Two adjacent identical words in *fluent, prosodically reduced* speech are precisely the case where the blank may not appear. The audit was run on **concatenative** audio — copies spliced together, with clean boundaries and silence structure by construction — which is the one periodic condition where the merge rule is guaranteed *not* to fire. So the audit certifies the instrument on material that is not the material being measured. And the artefact hypothesis predicts, with no free parameters: undercount only (never overcount); constant *relative* magnitude; exact at low k where boundaries are prosodically marked; onset around k ≈ 6 as the model speeds up; exact on distinct words; and complete immunity to the repetition penalty (§5). That is the entire result. Given the paper's own audit found the alternative judge off by a factor of 5 on this material, instrument bias here is a first-order effect and the reported −8.3% is well inside its plausible range.
- **What would address it** — Audit the CTC judge on *synthesised* repeated speech with known ground truth: have a human (or forced alignment against a known-correct reference) count units in a sample of the model outputs the CTC judge scored as short, and report judge-vs-human agreement stratified by k. Additionally report the CTC frame-level alignment (how many acoustic segments matching the target word are present) rather than the collapsed transcript, and report whether inserting a forced pause between copies in the prompt removes the deficit.

**3. Lemma 1 does not license Assumption 1, and Assumption 1 is false for the architectures studied. [Narrative — precision of claims / Evidence]**
- **Where** — §2: "This is what the following lemma buys: if the decoder cannot distinguish the k spans, its update between consecutive boundaries is the same to within O(δ + 1/k)."
- **Why it matters** — Assumption 1 posits a single autonomous map F on a fixed state space. A transformer decoder has no such map: the state at boundary m+1 is a function of a KV cache that *grows with m*, plus positional encodings, plus the decoder's own generated prefix — which contains m already-rendered copies of the target word. Lemma 1 bounds only the softmax profile over the k repeated **text** spans. It says nothing about attention over the generated acoustic prefix, which is where a count would most naturally live, and nothing about position. Worse, "the same to within O(δ + 1/k)" is not autonomy: a non-autonomous system s\_{m+1} = F\_m(s\_m) with F\_m = F + O(1/k) can encode m exactly, and the O(1/k) term is precisely the term that would carry the count. The gap between "attention is nearly uniform over text" and "the update map is autonomous" is where the entire result lives, and it is bridged by one sentence of prose.
- **What would address it** — Either state Assumption 1 honestly as an unproved idealisation with no lemma support (and drop "This is what the following lemma buys"), or prove a perturbation version: show that for s\_{m+1} = F\_m(s\_m) with ‖F\_m − F‖ ≤ ε, separability is still bounded — noting that the resulting bound will contain an ε/(1−q) term that does not vanish and may dominate. Separately, check attention over the *generated* prefix, not just text.

**4. The paper's own probe number implies Assumption 2 fails for all six models. [Evidence — internal consistency]**
- **Where** — §4.4: the count "is retained at 0.97 of its early level on repeated text against 1.11 on controls."
- **Why it matters** — Theorem 1(iii) makes separability decay as q^m. Retention of 97% of the count signal from the first to the last third of a trajectory spanning up to 32 repetitions is only compatible with q^m ≈ 1 over the observed range, i.e. q ≈ 1, i.e. N\* = log(µ/2LC)/log q → ∞. If the paper's own probe is right, the theorem is silent for **every** checkpoint in the panel, not just Qwen3-TTS-1.7B — and the escape clause "where it fails, the theorem is silent" swallows the whole paper. The abstract's "it stops acquiring states in which the count could be represented" is also in direct tension with a 3% degradation. The paper notices the erasure-vs-degradation distinction and hedges it verbally, but never does the arithmetic that shows the hedge is fatal.
- **What would address it** — Back out the implied q from the probe retention curve (fit retention vs m, report the exponent), then compute N\* per model from Eq. (1) with measured L, C, µ, and check it against the observed collapse point. If the implied N\* is far above 32, say plainly that the theorem's bound is not active in the measured regime.

**5. The theory is protected by two escape hatches that jointly make it unfalsifiable in practice, and the headline threshold is chosen after seeing the data. [Evidence — post-hoc-shaped hypothesis]**
- **Where** — §1 ("the bound applies where its premise holds"), §4.1 ("The theorem is silent about such a model"), §5 ("capacity loss is necessary but not sufficient, as (1) allows when the readout margin is large"), and §4.1 ("Below k = 6 every model is essentially exact. From k = 6 the panel splits") followed by every headline number being computed on the k ≥ 6 subset.
- **Why it matters** — q is never measured (§4.4 says so), so "contraction failed" is assigned exactly when the deficit is absent; µ is never measured, so "margin was large" is assigned exactly when a capacity gap appears without a deficit. Between them, every cell of the 2×2 (deficit × capacity gap) is accommodated post hoc. §5's three stated refutation conditions are therefore weaker than they look: two of the three are pre-blocked. Separately, k ≥ 6 is a threshold read off Fig. 1(a) and then used as the pooling cut for the abstract's number, with no pre-registration statement anywhere in the paper. The theory does not predict a common knee — N\* is per-model — so a single panel-wide k ≥ 6 cut is an analysis choice the theory does not motivate.
- **What would address it** — Report the full k-curve numbers, not the k≥6 pool, and state the analysis plan's provenance explicitly (pre-specified or exploratory). Give at least one *independent* estimate of q or µ so the escape hatches are load-bearing rather than free.

**6. The two empirical numbers offered as verifying Lemma 1's premise contradict each other. [Evidence — statistical rigour]**
- **Where** — §4.3: "the implied spread is δ ≤ 0.06 nats across the ladder, and no occurrence — not even the most attended — captures more than 1.9/k of the block's mass: attention over the repeated spans is within a few percent of uniform."
- **Why it matters** — Lemma 1 gives max\_i softmax(z)\_i ≤ e^δ/k. With δ ≤ 0.06 that is 1.06/k, not 1.9/k. A max mass of 1.9/k requires δ ≥ log 1.9 ≈ 0.64, an order of magnitude above the reported spread. One of the two numbers is wrong, or they are computed over different aggregations (median vs max) and presented in one sentence as if jointly certifying near-uniformity. Also, 1.9/k is 90% above uniform, not "within a few percent." And the entropy-slope evidence (0.97 against log k) does not constrain δ at all — H ≥ log k − δ has unit slope for *any* fixed δ. So the only quantity that matters for Assumption 1, namely δ, is supported by one number that is inconsistent with the other two.
- **What would address it** — State the aggregation for each statistic, report the distribution of δ (not a bound), and drop or correct whichever of the two figures is wrong. Note also that the one checkpoint that breaks the pattern (Qwen3-TTS) is the one where the lemma admittedly cannot be checked — the premise check excludes the informative case.

**7. The abstract omits the disconfirming checkpoint and implies a horizon was computed when none was. [Narrative — overclaiming]**
- **Where** — Abstract: "models render repeated text with a median count error of -8.3% at k ≥ 6 while length-matched sequences of distinct words come out exact (0.0%) at every k up to 32"; "with disjoint bootstrap intervals in all six"; and the contribution list, "an explicit horizon in terms of the contraction factor, the orbit scale and the readout's decision margin."
- **Why it matters** — A reader of the abstract alone learns that all six models undercount and that a horizon was derived. Neither is what the body shows: Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is at 0.0% (§4.1, Table 1), and **no numeric value of N\*, q, L, C, or µ appears anywhere in the paper for any model**. Eq. (1) is never instantiated. "Explicit horizon" is technically hedged by "in terms of," but the phrase does the work of implying a computed number. The juxtaposition of "in all six" (capacity) immediately after the count claim compounds the impression. This is the difference between "we bounded counting in AR TTS" and "we wrote down a formula whose every constant is unmeasured."
- **What would address it** — Say in the abstract that five of six checkpoints show the deficit and one does not, and either instantiate Eq. (1) for at least one model or drop "explicit horizon" in favour of "a horizon expressed in unmeasured constants."

**8. [Primary axis] The mathematical contribution is Banach's theorem restated, the "first formal artifact" claim is a first by construction, and the paper's sharpest empirical test does not distinguish it from the prior work it names as closest. [Novelty — overclaimed novelty / prior art mischaracterised]**
- **Where** — §2 (Theorem 1 and its "Formal verification" paragraph), §1 contributions list ("to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact"), and §5's treatment of representational collapse [9, arXiv:2406.04267].
- **Why it matters** — Three distinct problems compound:
  - *(a) The theorem.* Parts (i)–(iv) are: Banach fixed point (mathlib supplies it, as the paper says); the triangle inequality; Lipschitz composition; and a contrapositive. Lemma 1 is a two-line softmax exercise whose entire content is the hypothesis "the logits are within δ" — I checked both bounds and they are correct and immediate. Nothing here is a research-level mathematical result, which makes the "quantitative impossibility result" framing and the Lean apparatus heavy machinery for a statement a referee can verify by hand in five minutes. Formal verification adds assurance where a proof is long or subtle; here it mainly transfers credibility from the artifact's difficulty to a claim whose difficulty lies entirely in the *unproved, unmeasured* premises (Assumptions 1 and 2), which Lean does not touch. The paper says as much in §5 — but the abstract and contributions do not.
  - *(b) The "first" claim.* "First hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact" is a first over an intersection of three narrow qualifiers, and is true by construction of the reference class. It licenses no belief update about anything.
  - *(c) The prior-art characterisation, which is the serious one.* §5 asserts that representational collapse [9] "predicts that a 32-word control and a 32-word repetition fail alike, since both are equally long." That is a mischaracterisation. Over-squashing/collapse in that line of work is driven by the *similarity of the representations of successive prefixes*, not by raw sequence length: two prefixes differing by a novel distinct word are far apart in representation space, while two prefixes differing by one more copy of the same word are close. Collapse-by-similarity therefore predicts the periodicity-not-length dissociation **directly**. Figure 1(b), which the paper calls "the paper's sharpest test," is a prediction shared by the closest prior work, so it cannot distinguish the two accounts — and the paper's claim that it does rests on attributing to [9] a length-indexed mechanism it does not have. Likewise, the paper's own probe result (§4.4: degradation, not erasure; 0.97 retention) is *more* consistent with the representations-vs-outputs dissociation of [15, arXiv:2605.09239] than with the geometric erasure its theorem predicts, yet [15] is dispatched in one clause ("the two are not exclusive"). Strip (a), (b), and the mis-attributed distinguishing test, and the residual novelty is: a repetition-ladder benchmark with length-matched controls, an honest judge audit, and an N\_eff measurement — a useful but modest empirical contribution, presented as a deep structural result.
- **What would address it** — Rewrite §5's treatment of [9] to state the similarity-based mechanism correctly, and identify a prediction on which the two accounts *actually* diverge (e.g. the contraction account predicts a saturating horizon; a similarity/over-squashing account predicts graded degradation with k — see concern 1, where the data favour the latter). Run a head-to-head experiment on that divergence. Demote the formal-verification framing from a headline contribution to a methodological note, and drop or heavily qualify the "first" claim.

### Minor concerns

- **Unexplained excluded generations.** n = 622 (repeated) and n = 639 (control) at k ≥ 6. With 6 models × 3 seeds = 18 runs per item, neither is divisible by 18 (622/18 = 34.6; 639/18 = 35.5), so generations were dropped, the counts should be equal if every repeated item has a matched control, and no exclusion criterion is stated. If the dropped set is the degenerate-audio set, that exclusion is exactly the one that would bias the count-error estimate.
- **The degeneracy rate is promised and never reported.** §3: "Audio-level degeneracy … is detected separately and reported as its own rate." No such rate appears in the paper. Under the attractor account this is a first-order prediction (s\_m → s\* means the audio should converge to a fixed output), so its absence is conspicuous.
- **"Length-matched" is matched in words, not tokens.** With BPE/subword text encoders, k copies of a word and k distinct fillers do not produce the same text-token count, and the paper reports token counts only for the sentence-vs-word comparison (§4.2, "1.50×"), never for repeated vs control. State the token counts for the matched pairs.
- **Bootstrap resampling unit unstated for the count error.** §4.3 says capacity-gain intervals are bootstrapped over stimulus templates; the count-error interval [−12.5, −8.3] has no stated unit. Three seeds per item and shared carrier templates mean generation-level resampling would be anticonservative. Also note the point estimate sits on the interval boundary, which is what happens when the median lands on a quantisation grid (errors are multiples of 1/k) — worth acknowledging.
- **Six checkpoints, roughly three independent systems.** Llasa 1B/3B/8B share a backbone and tokenizer; Qwen 0.6B/1.7B share a recipe. "Disjoint intervals in all six" counts correlated checkpoints as independent replications. Note also that the deficit is not monotone in scale within Llasa (−16.1, −12.5, −16.7), so the one clean checkpoint is a *family* effect — which points to a training-recipe explanation (data containing repeated phrases, or explicit duration modelling) that the paper never raises.
- **Table 1's two headline quantities do not track each other.** Llasa-3B has the worst capacity ratio (0.28) but not the worst deficit; XTTS-v2 has a middling ratio (0.60) with a large deficit (−15.6). If capacity loss is the mechanism, its magnitude should predict deficit magnitude. n = 6 is too small to test — say that explicitly rather than presenting the two columns as mutually corroborating.
- **The mitigation sweep's direction is over-read.** "Sloping -0.7 points per unit — slightly the wrong way" is a directional claim from four penalty arms with no interval. Report it as null.
- **Generation hyperparameters are absent.** No temperature, top-p, CFG scale, seeds, HF model IDs, or revisions for any of the six checkpoints; no probe-layer indices ("deep probe layers"); no CTC decoding configuration (greedy vs beam, word-boundary handling) — the last of which is load-bearing for the count and is the crux of concern 2. The artifact release is good, but a paper should not require reading `build.sh` to know whether decoding was greedy.
- **No example transcripts anywhere.** Given that the entire measurement rests on how a CTC recogniser renders repeated words, at least a handful of randomly sampled (not hand-picked) transcripts against their prompts belong in the paper or supplement.
- **Figure 1 axis choice buries the effect.** Panels (a) and (b) span −100% to 0% while every reported effect lies in [−17%, 0%]; 85% of the plot area is empty, which makes the repeated and control curves nearly indistinguishable and the panel unreadable at ICASSP column width. Panel (c) has no units on N\_eff and an overlapping legend; panel (d) has no x-tick labels. The caption does not say what, if anything, the shaded bands in (a)/(b) represent.
- **Unit slip.** "δ ≤ 0.06 nats" — δ is a logit difference, not an entropy.
- **Reference hygiene.** [1] is withdrawn, which the paper flags honestly (good), but it is cited twice, including in §5 as support for "existing mitigations act late"; a live citation would be better. The repository name (`icassp_antispoofing`) has nothing to do with the paper's topic, which will confuse anyone following the link.

**Framework checks that produced nothing for this paper:**
- *Cherry-picked qualitative examples* — there are none to cherry-pick; no qualitative examples appear in the paper text at all (this is itself flagged above as a gap, not as cherry-picking).
- *p-value tier* — the paper reports no p-values anywhere, so the `.01 < p < .05` skepticism has no target. Intervals are reported instead, which is the better choice; the concern is the resampling unit, not the tier.
- *Missing ablations of method components* — the method is a measurement protocol, not a multi-component system, and the two ablations that do exist (repetition penalty sweep; XTTS with the penalty disabled, reported separately and never pooled) are appropriate and honestly handled. I have no missing-ablation finding.
- *Reproducibility* — this check largely **passes**. Lean development, stimuli, per-item tables, both transcript sets, an axiom-audit script and a regeneration script are released at a stated URL. The gap is hyperparameters in the paper text (listed under minor), not artifact availability.
- *Limitations honesty* — also largely passes, and above venue norm: §4.4 reports two failed measurements including the one that would have tested the theorem's premise, §5 states refutation conditions, the mitigation result is negative and reported, and the English-only / codec-LM-with-softmax-text-attention scope is stated. The glaring unnamed limitation is judge validity in the fluent-repetition regime (concern 2), and the unacknowledged tension between a proportional deficit and a horizon (concern 1).

### Belief update

Small and mostly not in the paper's direction.

- **"AR TTS models systematically undercount repeated units":** modest positive update, held loosely. The distinct-word control is a genuinely good design and the Whisper audit is real work that most authors would not have done. But I now think the measurement instrument is the leading candidate explanation rather than a solved problem, so I would not quote −8.3% as a property of the models.
- **"The undercounting is caused by contraction of an autonomous per-repetition state map":** slight *negative* update. Before reading, I gave this maybe 15% as the dominant mechanism. The proportional-not-saturating error curve and the 0.97 probe retention are both evidence against it, and both come from the authors' own data. Call it 8% now.
- **"Repetition-specific failure is distinct from length-driven representational collapse":** essentially no update. The control is consistent with the paper's account and equally consistent with [9]'s, so it cannot move me between them.
- **"Formal verification adds evidential value to claims of this kind":** no update. The Lean artifact certifies an implication whose proof is short and whose premises are the entire difficulty. I do not believe the theorem more because it was checked; I did not doubt it.
- **What I would actually reuse:** the repetition ladder with length-matched controls, and the practice of auditing an ASR judge against ground-truth material before using it as an instrument. Those are transferable and I updated positively on both as methodology.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the paper's mathematical and framing contributions are overclaimed (Banach's theorem plus an uninstantiated formula, a first-by-construction artifact claim, and a "sharpest test" that is equally predicted by the representational-collapse work it names as closest prior art), and the empirical core needs a judge audit in the fluent-repetition regime plus a saturating-vs-proportional fit before the attractor account is even the leading explanation of its own data.
