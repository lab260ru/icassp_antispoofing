# Review — alfa

## Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive (and some non-autoregressive) TTS models' well-known tendency to mis-render repeated phrases is driven by input *length* or by *periodicity* per se, and isolates the two with a 180-item "repetition ladder" in which each repeated-word stimulus is paired with a length- and word-count-matched control whose repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus later probes of VITS, F5-TTS and CosyVoice 2, the authors report a large, consistent gap between repeated and control conditions (e.g., 18.2% vs 94.3% exact-count accuracy at k≥6), which survives decoding-rule sweeps, judge-choice sweeps and exclusion-rule ablations. They then formalize a candidate mechanism (a Lean-4-verified contraction bound on a per-repetition state map) and report measuring its premise directly via a Jacobian-based Lipschitz estimate, finding the premise false (q≈38 rather than <1), and use causal patching experiments to argue the count survives in the representation but is not used by the readout.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper labels its central manipulation (§3.1) "Confirmatory," but no pre-registration is reported for the ladder design, exclusion rules, or primary statistic prior to data collection; the only genuinely time-stamped prediction is the single-architecture CosyVoice 2 extrapolation.
   **Where** — §3.1 heading ("Confirmatory: the limit tracks periodicity, not length"); the only explicit pre-registration evidence in the text is "from a fit committed before the checkpoint had been run (S16 dates both)," applied solely to the CosyVoice 2 prediction and to the two analyses explicitly marked "Exploratory" in §3.3.
   **Why it matters** — Calling an analysis "confirmatory" implies its hypotheses, exclusion rules, and primary contrasts were fixed before the data were seen. Without that, "confirmatory" is doing rhetorical work it hasn't earned: the 180-item ladder, the k≥6 threshold, the choice of median count error and exact-match rate as headline statistics, and the family/checkpoint clustering model could all have been selected, even unconsciously, after inspecting early results. The 420-specification robustness check (S11) partially mitigates this for the sign of the effect, but it does not substitute for a registered protocol, and the paper's own framing invites the reader to weight §3.1 more heavily than it should be weighted absent that guarantee.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly whether the ladder design and primary contrasts were frozen before any model was run (a registered-report timestamp analogous to S16), or reword the section as exploratory-but-robust rather than confirmatory.

2. **Issue** — The claim that the deficit is a general property of "periodicity" rather than of any one architecture rests on extrapolation from only three architecture families in the core panel, and the paper's own within-panel data (VITS) already contradicts the pattern it extrapolates.
   **Where** — §3.1 ("Our six checkpoints are only three architecture families... the posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel is 65.3 points [5.9, 93.3]... under the widest prior... that interval does touch zero (−1.6, P=0.956)"); §3.2 ("VITS...shows no dissociation through the same recogniser on the same strings... a gap of -6.7 against the panel's +76.1").
   **Why it matters** — A hierarchical/partial-pooling model built on n=3 clusters cannot generate a trustworthy predictive interval for an unseen architecture family regardless of how the priors are dressed up; the paper's own sensitivity check under a wider prior admits the interval touches zero. More importantly, the paper already has a disconfirming case inside its own experiments — VITS shows *no* dissociation, and the paper's explanation (per-token vs. whole-duration modeling) is a post-hoc account fit after seeing the VITS result, not a prediction made in advance. The one successful advance prediction (CosyVoice 2) is n=1 against one failed-to-generalize baseline (VITS) that the authors did not predict in advance. That is not evidence for a universal periodicity effect; it is evidence for an effect that appears in most, but demonstrably not all, of the architectures tested, with the boundary condition unconfirmed.
   **What would address it** — Either drop language implying near-universality ("no counting horizon," "the phenomenon is not [refuted]") in favor of "present in most decoder designs we tested, absent in at least one," or pre-register and test the duration-modeling hypothesis (per-token vs. global duration) against additional non-autoregressive architectures before claiming it explains the VITS exception.

3. **Issue** — The judge-validation "ground truth" used to certify the CTC recognizer (and disqualify Whisper) is concatenative synthetic audio, not the actual distribution of degenerate outputs (loops, truncations, glitches) the study is trying to score.
   **Where** — §2 ("Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth its counted/true ratio falls to 0.27–0.65...We score instead with a CTC recogniser...put through the same audit — ratio 1.00 on the same ground truth").
   **Why it matters** — Concatenated clean word renderings are acoustically much better-behaved than the actual model outputs being scored (loop failures often involve pitch drift, spectral degradation, or audible discontinuities at loop boundaries that concatenative audio cannot reproduce). A recognizer validated as perfect on clean concatenative ground truth is not thereby shown to be unbiased on the actual, messier failure cases it is asked to count — the paper never demonstrates that CTC blank-collapse (which they identify and try to rule out) or any other recognizer artifact behaves the same way on real generated loops as on synthetic ground truth.
   **What would address it** — Validate the judge against a sample of the *actual* generated stimuli with independently verified transcripts (e.g., from the human-audit protocol the paper sets up but does not report results for — see concern 4), not only against constructed concatenative audio.

4. **Issue** — The paper builds infrastructure for human verification of the automatic judge ("a stratified 165-clip sample...ships with each clip's transcript and scoring decision, so a judgement can be checked by ear") but never reports having run a human listening study or any inter-rater agreement statistic.
   **Where** — §2 ("Code, data, settings"); explicitly reiterated in Limitations ("we ran no listening study").
   **Why it matters** — Every headline number in the paper (18.2% vs 94.3%, the 75.4-point gap, the four-recognizer replication) is downstream of an automatic transcript-matching pipeline that has never been checked against a human ear on the actual stimuli under test, despite the authors themselves flagging judge validity as central enough to warrant its own subsection and a "the obvious judge is wrong" argument. A study whose main methodological contribution is "get the judge right" and whose most novel finding is a judge artifact (Whisper's autoregressive bias) is exactly the case where skipping human validation is riskiest — the paper has demonstrated that automatic judges *can* be badly biased in the direction of the studied phenomenon, but has not ruled out that the CTC judge it settled on carries a smaller, undetected bias of the same kind.
   **What would address it** — Run the human listening study on (at minimum) the 165-clip stratified sample already prepared, report agreement with the CTC judge's scoring decisions, and give an inter-rater/human-vs-machine agreement statistic.

5. **Issue** — The mechanistic refutation of the contraction hypothesis (the paper's most striking negative result, "q=38.1... repetition makes the map more expansive") is measured on a single checkpoint, and the causal patching evidence that "the decoder ignores" the surviving count coordinate is likewise reported from one checkpoint/one small sample, yet both are stated as general conclusions.
   **Where** — §4 ("On Llasa-1B, over 29 repeated items and their length-matched controls, q=38.1... The map is not a contraction"); §5 ("A rank-1 patch...moves the count not at all (0.00 log-count [−0.09, 0.09], n=36)...The count coordinate transplants cleanly and the decoder ignores it (S26)").
   **Why it matters** — The abstract and conclusion state the mechanism is refuted and the count "stays decodable" but is ignored by the readout as if these were general facts about the phenomenon; both are demonstrated only on Llasa-1B, one of six checkpoints and one of at least four architectures shown to exhibit the deficit (including F5-TTS and CosyVoice 2, which are structurally quite different — flow-matching vs. LLM-over-codec). It is entirely possible that contraction (or some other geometry) explains the deficit in Qwen3-TTS or F5-TTS even though it doesn't in Llasa-1B; the paper's own §3.3 notes that "Qwen3-TTS-1.7B inverts" the saturation-scale pattern, i.e., there is already within-paper evidence of architecture-dependent mechanism, which sits uneasily with a single-checkpoint mechanistic refutation being reported as closing the question generally.
   **What would address it** — Either replicate the Jacobian estimate and the rank-1 patch experiment on at least one checkpoint from a second architecture family before stating the mechanism is refuted "for these decoders" generally, or scope the claim explicitly to Llasa-1B in the abstract and discussion.

## Minor concerns

- The F5-TTS duration-injection experiment supporting the "where the count must live" story rests on n=30/n=60 splits within one model (§3.2) — noted by the authors themselves as resting on "one model per strategy," but this caveat belongs closer to the claim rather than only in the general Limitations paragraph.
- The Bayesian partial-pooling numbers (posterior P(gap>0)=0.996, and the "widest prior" sensitivity check) are reported without specifying the prior family or shrinkage structure used for the group-level scale — readers cannot assess how much of the headline confidence is prior-driven with only n=3 clusters.
- Table 1's "cap. ratio" column (0.21–0.80) is not discussed in the main text at the same level of detail as the count-error/exact-match columns; its relationship to the Fig. 1(c) capacity-gain analysis could be made explicit.
- The related-work paragraph cites Barbero et al. (representational collapse) and several transformer-counting-expressivity papers as compatible-but-distinct accounts, but does not discuss any prior TTS-specific literature on repetition/looping failures beyond the two attention-supervision/decoding-rule papers in the introduction ([3],[4]) — a brief note on why no closer TTS-specific periodicity-vs-length study exists (if none does) would strengthen the novelty framing.
- Reference [13] (Venkatesh, "Repeated-token counting reveals a dissociation between representations and outputs") appears highly topically adjacent to this paper's own §5 dissociation claim; the text cites it once in passing ("the live question is where the count fails: erased from the state, or surviving there while the policy that reads it fails [13]") without clarifying whether that paper's finding is being replicated, extended, or is independent prior art — this borders on under-attribution given how central the dissociation claim is to §5.
- "q=38.1 [32.9, 51.4] against 31.8" (§4) is stated without a unit or explicit definition of what the second number (31.8) is (presumably the control's q, but this should be stated explicitly at first use rather than inferred from the surrounding sentence).

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the core periodicity-vs-length dissociation is a genuinely interesting and reasonably well-controlled finding, but the "confirmatory" framing is not backed by demonstrated pre-registration, the universality claims outrun what three architecture families (one of which already breaks the pattern) can support, and the paper's most novel mechanistic claims (contraction refutation, readout-ignores-surviving-count) are generalized from a single checkpoint without replication — each is fixable with either rescoped claims or targeted additional experiments rather than a redesign.
