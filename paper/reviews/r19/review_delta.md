### Summary

The paper asks whether the well-documented tendency of TTS models to miscount, loop, or truncate on repeated text is driven by input *length* or by *periodicity* per se, and separates the two with a 180-item "repetition ladder" in which each repeated-word stimulus is paired with a length-matched, non-periodic control. Across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa, Qwen3-TTS, XTTS-v2), plus two non-autoregressive baselines (VITS, F5-TTS) and a later-acquired fourth family (CosyVoice 2), the authors report that repeated items are rendered exactly 18.2% of the time versus 94.3% for controls at k≥6, a gap they argue survives decoding changes, penalty sweeps, exclusion-rule choices, and the use of four different ASR judges. They then formalize a candidate mechanism — a Lean-4-verified theorem stating that a contracting per-repetition state map bounds how many repetitions a Lipschitz readout can distinguish — measure its premise (the contraction constant q) on Llasa-1B, find q≈38 (expansive, not contracting), and conclude the mechanism is empirically false even though the phenomenon it was meant to explain persists.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's headline generalization ("the gap holds across architecture families," predictive posterior for an unseen architecture) rests on a hierarchical/partial-pooling model with only 3 architecture-family clusters (6 checkpoints), which is too few clusters to estimate between-family variance reliably, yet the paper reports posterior probabilities to three decimals (P=0.996, P=0.956) and a bootstrap CI as narrow as [71.1, 78.9].
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "By family the gap is 75.4 points [71.1, 78.9]... A mixed-effects linear probability model with architecture as a random effect... Partial pooling answers the question three families cannot answer directly: the posterior predictive gap for an architecture outside the panel is 65.3 points [5.9, 93.3]."
   **Why it matters** — With n=3 top-level clusters, group-level variance components are essentially unidentified from the data alone; the posterior predictive interval is dominated by the prior on between-family variance, which the authors themselves half-concede ("under the widest prior... that interval does touch zero (−1.6, P=0.956)"). Reporting three-decimal posterior probabilities alongside an admission that a wider (still defensible) prior flips the conclusion's sign is internally inconsistent — it advertises precision the design cannot support. The subsequent "confirmation" on CosyVoice 2 (single new checkpoint, n=90) is presented as validating the prediction, but a single point estimate falling inside a wide, prior-sensitive interval is weak confirmatory evidence, not the decisive test the framing implies.
   **What would address it** — Report the prior-sensitivity analysis for all headline numbers, not just the ancillary one; downgrade the language around the "predicted 65.3 vs observed 64.4" match from confirmatory to illustrative; and if a truly out-of-panel prediction is the goal, register it and its acceptance criterion before running CosyVoice 2 (the paper does date the fit before the run, which is good practice, but a single family is still not an independent test of a variance-component model fit on 3 families).

2. **Issue** — The extensive robustness battery (420 specifications; four recognisers; decoding, penalty, and exclusion sweeps) is summarized only as "the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points and none reverses its sign," with no correction for multiple comparisons and no accounting for non-independence between specifications that reuse the same underlying generations.
   **Where** — Section 3.1 ("Across 420 specifications... the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points and none reverses its sign (S11)") and Section 3.2 (penalty sweep, greedy-vs-sampled, exclusion-rule-off, four-recogniser re-scoring).
   **Why it matters** — A specification-curve range and a "sign never reverses" claim is a useful sanity check but is not itself a statistical test; many of the 420 specifications share the same base generations and are heavily correlated, so treating "420" as evidence of breadth risks implying more independent replication than exists. No aggregate significance statement (e.g., a joint test across the specification curve, or an explicit vibration-of-effects analysis with a null-permutation baseline) is offered, so a reader cannot tell whether the 34.4-point floor is a genuine worst case or an artifact of correlated sub-analyses.
   **What would address it** — State how many of the 420 specifications are mutually independent (distinct data subsets) versus re-analyses of the same runs, and provide a formal joint test (e.g., permutation test on the full specification set, or a random-effects meta-analysis across truly independent arms) rather than a min–max range.

3. **Issue** — The Lean-verified bound's premise (contraction constant q) is measured on a single checkpoint (Llasa-1B) with n=29 items, yet the conclusion is generalized in the abstract and Section 4 to "these decoders" (plural) and used to declare the mechanism refuted for the whole panel.
   **Where** — Section 4, "On Llasa-1B, over 29 repeated items and their length-matched controls, q = 38.1... The map is not a contraction: q < 1 in 0 of 29 items, in every lag and sub-stack measured," followed by "The proposed mechanism is refuted" in the abstract, unqualified by checkpoint.
   **Why it matters** — The behavioral effect is shown across six checkpoints/three families plus later a fourth, but the mechanistic refutation — the paper's most novel theoretical contribution — is demonstrated on exactly one 1B checkpoint. "Every lag and sub-stack measured" on n=29 also raises an implicit multiple-comparisons question (how many lag/sub-stack combinations were tested, and would any survive correction if q were closer to 1?); this is not reported. Generalizing a single-checkpoint measurement to "these decoders" overstates what was actually tested.
   **What would address it** — Either replicate the Jacobian/q estimation on at least one checkpoint from another architecture family (Qwen3-TTS or XTTS-v2, both already in the panel) or explicitly scope the refutation claim to Llasa-1B and mark the cross-family claim as untested/exploratory.

4. **Issue** — Several "null result" claims that carry real argumentative weight (the rank-1 patch's "moves the count not at all," the mean-answering-predictor comparison, the F5-TTS duration-supply experiment at k≥12) are supported only by a point estimate and CI touching or containing the null, with no power or equivalence analysis to justify treating a non-significant result as evidence of absence.
   **Where** — Section 5: "steering along it gives no dose-response... 0.00 log-count [−0.09, 0.09], n = 36"; "Past the extension horizon the 5 checkpoints reach 1.01, 0.99, 0.86, 0.74, 0.90 of a mean-answering predictor: inconclusive at n = 5"; Section 3.2, F5-TTS "at or above it the gain is 0.0 (n = 60)."
   **Why it matters** — A CI of [−0.09, 0.09] around zero from n=36 patches is consistent with a modest true effect the study was not powered to detect; the paper's own claim that "the decoder ignores [the transplanted count coordinate]" is a strong causal-null claim that needs an equivalence test (e.g., TOST against a pre-specified smallest-effect-of-interest) rather than a CI that merely fails to exclude zero. The n=5 mean-answering-predictor comparison is honestly labeled "inconclusive," which is good practice, but it is nonetheless folded into the Discussion's narrative ("Our evidence points the second way") in a way that risks over-crediting an underpowered comparison.
   **What would address it** — Pre-specify a smallest-effect-of-interest for the rank-1 patch experiment and run a formal equivalence test; increase n for the patch experiment if feasible; and in the Discussion, keep the n=5 mean-answering-predictor result clearly bracketed as non-evidential rather than narratively supportive.

5. **Issue** — The Whisper-vs-CTC judge audit — the paper's own justification for its central methodological choice — reports judge/ground-truth ratios (0.19, 0.27–0.65, 1.00) without stating the sample size or uncertainty interval behind them.
   **Where** — Section 2, "its counted/true ratio falls to 0.27–0.65 over k ≥ 4 and to 0.19 at the top of the ladder, while same-voice distinct-word sequences of identical length score 1.00... ratio 1.00 on the same ground truth."
   **Why it matters** — This audit is what licenses discarding Whisper as "the wrong instrument" and adopting the CTC recogniser as the paper's primary measurement device; if it rests on a handful of concatenative items, the precision implied by figures like "0.19" is unsupported, and the entire measurement pipeline downstream inherits that uncertainty.
   **What would address it** — Report n for the concatenative ground-truth audit and a CI or exact binomial interval around each ratio.

6. **Issue** — Presentation compresses a very large number of statistics (dozens of percentages, CIs, p-values, and ratios) into dense prose with minimal signposting of which analysis is confirmatory versus exploratory versus post hoc, and many numbers (e.g., the headline 75.4 [71.1, 78.9]) are not traceable to a stated estimator without consulting the (unprovided) supplement.
   **Where** — Throughout Section 3, especially 3.1–3.2.
   **Why it matters** — For a paper whose central contribution is methodological rigor around measurement (the judge-choice argument, the Lean-verified bound, the specification-curve robustness check), the write-up itself makes independent verification of any single number difficult within the ICASSP page budget; a reviewer cannot check, e.g., whether the "75.4 points" CI is a percentile bootstrap, a normal-approximation interval, or a model-based interval without the supplement (S-tags are cited dozens of times but not resolvable here).
   **What would address it** — State the estimator/interval-construction method inline for at least the headline numbers, and ensure the public supplement (referenced as available at the repository tag) is unambiguously the source for every S-tagged claim, with a table mapping each headline statistic to its exact estimator.

### Minor concerns

- Reference [13] (Venkatesh, "Repeated-token counting reveals a dissociation between representations and outputs," arXiv:2605.09239, 2026) and [7] (the authors' own companion paper) both carry unusually forward-dated arXiv IDs; given the paper's discussion explicitly adopts [13]'s framing as "the rival the evidence favours," a slightly deeper engagement with how this paper's design differs from or extends [13]'s would help readers judge incremental novelty.
- The counting-expressivity literature cited (refs [15–20]) overlaps closely in spirit with related theoretical work on transformer expressive power and depth (e.g., Merrill & Sabharwal on log-precision/log-depth transformers), which is not cited but addresses a similar "what can transformers count" question; worth a check for completeness.
- PDF-extraction artifacts throughout ("V ALL-E", "F 5-TTS", "d eff/dlog k") should be cleaned up in the camera-ready.
- Table 1's "cap. ratio" column is not explained in the caption in enough detail to know how it maps to the "capacity gain" metric introduced only in Fig. 1(c)'s caption.
- The claim "exact-match scores 30-of-32 and 3-of-32 alike" (Section 2) is a good illustration of a design choice but reads ambiguously on first pass — clarify that this means the binary exact-match metric is insensitive to *how* wrong a miscount is.
- The abstract's "we reject it: q = 38.1" states a conclusion about the mechanism's falsity in the same breath as a number computed from a single checkpoint (see Major #3); abstract phrasing should flag the scope.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the behavioral dissociation (periodicity vs. length) is well-controlled and its robustness checks are extensive, but several of the paper's most decisive-sounding numbers (the partial-pooling posterior probabilities, the single-checkpoint contraction-constant refutation, and the null causal-patch result) are reported with a precision and generalizing force the underlying sample sizes and cluster counts do not support, and this needs to be resolved or explicitly scoped before the claims as stated should stand.
