### Summary

The paper asks why autoregressive (AR) text-to-speech (TTS) models miscount repeated phrases (e.g., "say a word twelve times"), and argues the cause is structural rather than a training artifact: softmax attention over k repeated spans dilutes to weight Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1), which makes the decoder's per-repetition update approximately autonomous; if that update map contracts (Assumption 2), a Banach-fixed-point argument shows any Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — cannot distinguish repetition counts beyond a computable horizon N* (Theorem 1). Both results are machine-checked in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, across six checkpoints (three architectures) evaluated with a CTC recognizer chosen specifically because the "obvious" Whisper judge is itself shown to be repetition-biased, models undercount repeated phrases at k ≥ 6 (median error −8.3%, exact-match 18.2%) while length-matched non-repeating controls are essentially exact (94.3%), establishing that the deficit tracks periodicity rather than length or duration. Within k ≤ 32 the deficit is proportional rather than saturating (contradicting the theorem's qualitative prediction); a post-hoc extension to k = 128 on 4 checkpoints does find saturation, at a substantially lower scale than controls. Critically, the authors report that the theorem's own premise (a measurable contraction factor q) could not be estimated in two attempts, that a direct dose-response test of the attention-dilution lemma came out sign-reversed, and that a linear-probe test of the theorem's conclusion succeeds in only 1 of 2 checkpoints tested.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's central novelty claims (a "conditional impossibility result," Lean formalization billed as "to our knowledge the first hallucination result in speech synthesis carried out as a formal artifact") rest on mathematics that is a near-mechanical corollary of the Banach fixed-point theorem plus a Lipschitz-readout argument, and the related-work discussion does not engage the closest theoretical precedent for "self-attention provably cannot do X past a limit," namely Hahn's theoretical-limitations-of-self-attention line of work [1906.06755] and the broader hard-attention/formal-language-expressivity literature (only gestured at through the survey [18]).
   **Where** — Introduction (contributions list); Section 2 (Theorem 1, "Formal verification" paragraph); Discussion, final paragraph ("Related bounds [15–19] bound one pass; state tracking [20] probes many").
   **Why it matters** — If the theoretical contribution is mostly domain transfer and Lean packaging rather than new mathematical content, then the "first formal artifact" framing overstates what is novel, and the paper risks reviewers/readers crediting it with more theoretical originality than it has. The one sentence that tries to differentiate the bound from prior rank-collapse/state-tracking results ("bound one pass" vs. presumably many) is asserted, not argued, and is undercut by the fact that Assumption 2 (contraction) — the premise that would make "many passes" the operative regime — is never empirically confirmed (see concern 2), so even the claimed axis of novelty (single-pass vs. multi-pass bounding) is untested in practice.
   **What would address it** — Reframe the contribution honestly as an application/formalization exercise; add explicit discussion distinguishing the contraction-based, autoregressive-time bound from Hahn (2019) and hard-attention circuit-complexity results, and from the cited rank-collapse ([14],[15]) and state-tracking ([19],[20]) literature, on grounds other than assertion.

2. **Issue** — The theorem's proposed causal mechanism (contraction, Assumption 2) is not just unconfirmed but partially contradicted by the paper's own direct tests: two attempts to measure the contraction factor q failed, and the dose-response test of the companion dilution lemma is sign-reversed (flatter attention correlates with *better* counting, r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]).
   **Where** — Section 4.4 ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately... its dose-response prediction fails outright"); Section 5 ("two attempts to measure q failed... its linearity control failing (S5)"); Abstract.
   **Why it matters** — Under an adversarial reading, this is the paper's central vulnerability: what remains solidly supported is a correlational fact (errors track periodicity, not length), not a demonstrated causal mechanism. The paper itself names at least three rival explanations it cannot exclude (S20, chiefly a learned dispreference for verbatim repetition), and the one mechanism-specific test that was run (dose-response) points away from the theorem's story. A true theorem about a false or unverified premise explains nothing about the actual system; the Lean certificate guarantees the implication, not that the implication is triggered in real decoders.
   **What would address it** — Either succeed at directly estimating q on at least one checkpoint with a method that doesn't require a monotone read-head, or explicitly downgrade the theorem to "a candidate mechanism, not yet confirmed" throughout the abstract and introduction (the title already signals this; the abstract's ordering still leads with the theorem before the caveats).

3. **Issue** — With n = 6 checkpoints (3 architectures), the paper explicitly states no multiplicity-corrected test clears p < 0.05, yet reports many exploratory forks (judge choice, exclusion-rule variants, penalty sweep, filler-pool regeneration, duration-conditioning test, the post-hoc k = 128 extension) without a global correction or a specification-curve style summary.
   **Where** — Section 4.2 ("The panel is too small for significance testing... no multiplicity correction clears 0.05 (S11)"); S15, S16, S18, S20 (referenced, not shown in main text).
   **Why it matters** — Point-estimate effect sizes built on this many researcher degrees of freedom (even if each was individually well-motivated and dated post hoc via commit log) are fragile; e.g., the k = 128 extension draws on only 4 of 6 checkpoints with per-cell n of 21–26, yet yields a headline "3.5-fold" horizon-lowering claim and tight-looking CIs (ˆKctl = 105 [94, 114]).
   **What would address it** — Present effect sizes explicitly as descriptive/exploratory (the paper already leans this way rhetorically), and report a robustness envelope across the forking choices rather than single preferred numbers presented in the abstract as if precise.

4. **Issue** — The CTC judge's validity is audited on concatenative synthetic ground truth, not on the actual class of degenerate, possibly looping audio produced by miscounting AR-TTS models, and CTC decoders have their own known repetition-related failure mode (blank-token collapse on monotonous acoustic sequences) that is never directly ruled out for the true failure cases.
   **Where** — Section 3 ("Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong"); Section 4.4 ("not merely the monotonous audio a correct rendering produces (S8)"); Section 4.3 ("blank-collapse would have depressed its repeated items too" — inferred from the VITS null, not directly measured).
   **Why it matters** — The entire empirical counting-accuracy pipeline depends on this judge. If CTC blank-collapse systematically undercounts long, acoustically repetitive spans regardless of what the TTS model produced, this could inflate exactly the "undercount at high k" signal the paper interprets as evidence of the theorem.
   **What would address it** — A direct human-adjudicated audit of the CTC judge specifically on highly repetitive/looping synthesized failure audio (not only clean concatenative ground truth), reporting agreement rates in that regime.

5. **Issue** — The paper's motivating premise that an "attractor regime" exists in related architectures is imported from a same-author, same-year, unpublished companion preprint.
   **Where** — Introduction ("The work is the autoregressive-decoder companion to a spectral study of hallucination in encoder–decoder ASR [7], which identified an attractor regime descriptively; here the attractor is what the theorem is about"); reference [7] (arXiv:2604.08591, 2026).
   **Why it matters** — A foundational piece of motivation is not independently verifiable at review time, and self-citation to a concurrent unrefereed preprint for load-bearing framing weakens the paper's standalone evidentiary basis.
   **What would address it** — Summarize the specific evidence from [7] within this paper so it can be evaluated on its own terms, or explicitly flag the attractor framing as an unconfirmed working hypothesis.

### Minor concerns

- Reference [1] is cited as motivation despite being marked "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026"; flagging the withdrawal is good practice, but leaning on its "alignment analysis" without further caveat is questionable.
- Eq. 1 (N*) is never evaluated for any real checkpoint since q, C, µ, L are all unmeasured; this limitation belongs next to the equation, not only in the Discussion.
- Table 1's "capacity gain ratio" column is not self-explanatory from the table alone; the reader must consult §4.4 for the definition.
- The abstract packs in numerous parenthetical caveats (e.g., "n=6, too small to test") that are important enough to warrant placement in the main text rather than as an aside.
- The filler pool of eight, cycled above k = 8 for controls, is acknowledged to introduce a quantified 13.4-point confound (§4.3); better to have used a non-cycled pool from the start given the interpretive cost of correcting for it post hoc.
- Theorem 1 assumes readouts (including "the model's own stop head") are L-Lipschitz with implicitly small L; real logit/softmax heads can have large local Lipschitz constants, and this assumption's plausibility for actual stop-token logits is not discussed.
- Figure 1's k-axis values are hard to parse from the extracted text (e.g., "22 25 k"); ensure axis labeling and scale (log2 k?) are unambiguous in the camera-ready figure.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the empirical periodicity-vs-length benchmark and CTC-judge methodology are a solid, honestly-reported contribution, but the paper's rhetorical lead (a Lean-verified theorem) overclaims explanatory novelty and causal grounding relative to what its own contraction-measurement failures, sign-reversed dose-response test, and 1-of-2 probe replication actually support, and this needs to be resolved in the framing and related-work positioning before the causal story is publishable as headlined.
