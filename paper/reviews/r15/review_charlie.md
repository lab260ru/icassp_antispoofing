# Review (Reviewer: charlie)

### Summary
The paper argues that autoregressive TTS models miscount repeated phrases because softmax attention over k identical spans dilutes to Θ(1/k) (Lemma 1), which drives the per-repetition decoder update toward an approximately autonomous, contracting map; if that map contracts, a Banach-style argument shows no Lipschitz readout — including the model's own stop head — can separate repetition counts beyond a computable horizon N* (Theorem 1). Both results are formalized in Lean 4/mathlib. Empirically, six checkpoints across three TTS architectures are tested on a repetition ladder (k = 1…32, later extended post hoc to 128) with length-matched non-repetitive controls, scored by a CTC recognizer chosen specifically because the more obvious Whisper judge is itself repetition-biased. Repeated items are undercounted (median error ≈ ‑8.3% to ‑12.5% at k ≥ 6) while controls are essentially exact (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match), and the deficit tracks periodicity rather than length or a decoding-rule confound. However, the paper's own attempts to test the theorem's mechanism largely fail or invert: two contraction-factor (q) measurements were inconclusive, the dilution lemma's dose-response prediction is sign-reversed, and a direct post-horizon readout test replicates in only 1 of 2 checkpoints tried.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The experiments designed to test the theorem's own causal mechanism return null, reversed, or barely-replicated results, so the paper's design provides no positive evidence connecting the formal bound to the behavior it is invoked to explain.
   **Where** — §4.4 ("Lemma 1's premise holds only approximately… its dose-response prediction fails outright… r = 0.59 [0.35, 0.75]"); §5 ("two attempts to measure q failed… 7/18, p = 0.12, its linearity control failing"; "the prediction holds in 1 of 2, a failure to replicate").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's entire framing (title, abstract, contribution list) rests on the claim that a *measured* mechanism (dilution → contraction → horizon) explains the behavioral data. But every manipulation check the authors themselves designed to test that mechanism either failed to detect an effect (n = 18 perturbation probe, clearly underpowered for a null this study wants to treat as informative), came out backwards (flatter attention correlates with *better* counting, the opposite of the dilution-lemma prediction), or replicated in only half of a 2-checkpoint sample (which is not a design that can distinguish "the theorem holds" from "coin flip"). A design that tests its central causal claim on n = 2 and calls a 1-of-2 split "a failure to replicate" rather than "uninterpretable" is asking the reader to accept the theoretical apparatus on the strength of its formal derivation alone, decoupled from any empirical test that actually favored it. The alternative account the authors cite — Venkatesh [2605.09239], who shows in general LLMs that linear probes decode the count correctly *even at the layers where the wrong output crystallizes* — is a directly competing explanation (output-policy failure, not representational collapse) that was tested at far larger scale than this paper's n = 2 checkpoint replication of the post-horizon probe test.
   **What would address it** — Either measure q directly for at least a subset of checkpoints with a validated estimator (the paper concedes its two attempts don't work), report the post-horizon probe result on all 6 checkpoints (not 2) with pre-registered analysis code, or retreat the claims: state plainly that the theorem is a conditional (if contraction, then horizon) whose antecedent remains unverified, and that the single behavioral test run so far is as consistent with the Venkatesh dissociation account as with Theorem 1.

2. **Issue** — The outcome measure (CTC recognizer) is calibrated on concatenative (spliced) audio, not on genuinely-generated repeated TTS speech, and CTC decoding's built-in repeat-collapsing mechanism is exactly the kind of bias the paper is trying to rule out.
   **Where** — §3, "Behavioural measurement, and why the obvious judge is wrong"; the CTC audit reports "ratio 1.00 on periodic ground truth" but only against concatenated stimuli.
   **Why it matters** — CTC decoding merges consecutive identical labels by construction (that's the CTC collapse rule). If genuinely-generated repeated speech from a TTS model is acoustically/prosodically less distinct between repeats than concatenated audio splices (which is plausible — the paper itself notes in §4.4 that repeated-text output tends toward "monotonous audio"), the judge could systematically undercount real repetitions in exactly the condition under test, independent of anything the decoder's hidden state is doing. Calibrating against spliced ground truth doesn't test this failure mode because splices preserve full acoustic separation between repeats by construction; the paper never audits the judge against *TTS-generated* repeated audio with an independently-verified true count (e.g., via careful human transcription of a stratified sample, cross-tabulated against the CTC score). The 165-clip "checked by ear" sample is offered as an artifact but no concordance statistic is reported in the text.
   **What would address it** — Report explicit CTC-vs-human agreement (e.g., Cohen's κ or simple accuracy) on the shipped 165-clip stratified sample, broken out by k and by repeated-vs-control arm, so a judge-side confound with the same periodicity signature as the headline effect can be ruled out rather than assumed away.

3. **Issue** — The generalization claims ("in every checkpoint and family") are stated with a design that has an effective family-level n of 3, and the paper's own significance analysis concedes it is underpowered, yet the abstract's rhetorical framing treats the result as broadly established.
   **Where** — Abstract ("the limit tracks periodicity, not length, in every checkpoint and family (n=6, too small to test)"); §4.2 ("The panel is too small for significance testing — the exact signed-rank floor at n = 6 is 0.031 and no multiplicity correction clears 0.05").
   **Why it matters** — Three architecture families with 1–3 scale variants each is a reasonable exploratory panel, but it cannot support claims of universality; the within-family checkpoints are not independent replications (they share tokenizer, training data, and much of the decoder), so the true degrees of freedom for a "does this generalize across architectures" claim are closer to 3 than 6. The one genuine exception found (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no median-error saturation gap at k ≤ 128 and even inverts the ratio) is explained away as "an unexplained exception, not the theorem behaving conditionally" — but with n = 3 families, one clear outlier is a substantial fraction of the evidence base, not a footnote.
   **What would address it** — Either add more architecture families (more than a scale ladder within existing ones) before claiming cross-architecture generality, or soften the claim to "consistent with, but not powered to establish, generality across architectures," and treat the Qwen3-TTS-1.7B exception as a first-class data point requiring its own explanation rather than a caveat.

4. **Issue** — Llasa-3B is silently dropped from the k = 128 extension ladder with no stated reason, even though the paper explicitly explains why XTTS-v2 is excluded.
   **Where** — §4.2: "the 4 checkpoints admitting utterances that long (Llasa-1B, Llasa-8B, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B; XTTS-v2's 602-mel-token ceiling censors every k ≥ 48 item, S10)."
   **Why it matters** — Six checkpoints are in the main panel; only 4 appear in the extension, and only one exclusion (XTTS-v2) is explained. If Llasa-3B's exclusion is for the same reason (token budget), it should say so explicitly and report where its ceiling falls, since that ceiling itself is a data point relevant to the "general length ceiling" argument in §4.2. If it's excluded for a different, unstated reason, that needs disclosure — a design in which sample composition changes across sub-analyses without a documented rule invites the reader to wonder whether the missing checkpoint would have disrupted the fitted saturation-scale story.
   **What would address it** — State the exclusion criterion and Llasa-3B's specific limitation explicitly, or include it with censored/lower-bound data points as is done for XTTS-v2's shorter-ladder items elsewhere.

5. **Issue** — The k = 128 extension shows *both* repeated and control conditions saturating, which is not what Theorem 1 predicts (the theorem's horizon mechanism is specific to periodic conditioning, Assumption 1; non-periodic controls have no reason to hit a contraction-driven horizon at all), yet the paper frames the extension as support for "the theorem's distinctive prediction."
   **Where** — §4.2 ("Tracking stops… Controls stop us reading this as collapse alone: they saturate too… these decoders have a general length ceiling"); §5 ("The theorem's distinctive prediction is a constant N∗, invisible where our ladder stopped; extending to k = 128 found saturation of that kind").
   **Why it matters** — A generic architectural/decoding ceiling (KV-cache limits, duration-model saturation, token budgets) predicts saturation in *both* arms, which is exactly what's observed (K̂_ctl = 105, K̂_rep = 30). That both curves saturate is evidence for a shared non-mechanism-specific ceiling, not particularly strong confirmation of Theorem 1's periodicity-specific contraction story — the theorem doesn't predict the control should saturate at all. The paper is careful to say K̂ is "not the theorem's N∗, a function of the unmeasured q," which is honest, but the discussion still leans on the extension as if it corroborates the theorem's "distinctive prediction," when a simpler shared-ceiling-plus-repetition-penalty account (one of the "three we cannot exclude" in S20, e.g., a learned dispreference for verbatim repetition) fits the same data equally well without invoking contraction dynamics at all.
   **What would address it** — Either derive a testable, quantitative link between the fitted K̂_rep and the theorem's N∗ (which requires the still-unmeasured q, C, µ, L), or explicitly demote the k = 128 finding to "consistent with a horizon existing, but equally consistent with a non-contraction-based capacity ceiling that repetition makes worse" rather than framing it as evidence for the theorem specifically.

6. **Issue** — The headline dissociation statistic (94.3% vs 18.2% exact-match) is computed over stimuli whose controls, above k = 8, draw from a cycled 8-item filler pool — a periodicity confound the paper itself later shows is "worth 13.4 points" — but the correction is only applied to a follow-up subset (k = 12…32), not folded back into the primary reported numbers.
   **Where** — §3 (Stimuli: "cycled above k = 8"); §4.3 ("Re-generating k = 12…32 from a never-cycled 146-word pool leaves the dissociation at 71.2 points… where cycled controls give 84.6: the confound is worth 13.4 points, not the bulk of it").
   **Why it matters** — This is commendably transparent, but it means the paper's own most-quoted number (94.3%/18.2%, and the abstract's headline framing) is computed on a design the authors know contains a partially-confounded control arm, and the de-confounded re-run is reported only as a secondary robustness check on a k-restricted subset, not substituted into or reconciled with the primary statistic.
   **What would address it** — Report the primary exact-match/median-error statistics using the never-cycled pool throughout (or report both side by side as primary, not relegate the cleaner version to a robustness footnote), so the reader isn't left to average two numbers to guess the "real" effect size.

7. **Issue** — Qwen3-TTS-1.7B's dissociation between a null median-error gap and a 60-point exact-rate gap is left as "an unexplained exception," but a plausible alternative explanation — that this checkpoint's markedly higher decoding stochasticity (T = 1.0, top-k 50, penalty 1.05, flagged by the authors themselves as "the most stochastic family") inflates output variance enough to null out a median-based statistic while leaving a strict exact-match statistic intact — is not tested.
   **Where** — §3 ("making Qwen3-TTS the most stochastic family, so its larger checkpoint's null is not an artifact of conservative decoding"); §4.2 ("Qwen3-TTS-1.7B shows no repeated-side saturation… though its exact-rate gap is 60.0 points — the deficit is there, just not in the median").
   **Why it matters** — The authors preemptively rule out one alternative explanation (conservative decoding producing a spurious null) but not the mirror-image one (aggressive stochastic decoding producing a spurious null in a location statistic while a stricter statistic still shows the effect). These are different failure modes and ruling out one doesn't rule out the other; as it stands the "unexplained exception" could simply be a decoding-hyperparameter artifact rather than a genuine architecture-level counter-example to the periodicity account.
   **What would address it** — Rerun Qwen3-TTS-1.7B at matched (lower) temperature/top-k against the other checkpoints, or report the full error distribution (not just the median) for this checkpoint to show whether it's bimodal (some generations exact, others wildly off) versus uniformly shifted.

### Minor concerns
- The abstract leads with the pooled -8.3% [-12.5, -6.2] count-error figure, while the body explicitly says this interval "ignores clustering" and "we lean on the first" (the -12.5% per-checkpoint median) — leading the abstract with the number the authors say they trust less is confusing for a skimming reader.
- Citation [1] (ShiMing Wang et al., arXiv:2509.19852) is noted as "Withdrawn 13 Feb 2026; cited for its alignment analysis" — using a withdrawn preprint substantively without any note on what withdrawal means for the reliability of the cited analysis is a minor citation-hygiene issue.
- The GitHub repo name is flagged as predating the study ("name predates this study") — a distracting aside better handled with a footnote or simply renaming the branch/repo.
- Figure 1's axis tick labels appear to be log-scale exponents (e.g., "2^2 2^5" rendered as "22 25") — likely a text-extraction artifact, but worth double-checking the camera-ready figure renders axis labels legibly.
- §4.4's "effective rank" measure for capacity gain is introduced without specifying the singular-value threshold/criterion used to compute it, or which "deep layers" (S-referenced but not detailed in the main text) are probed — a reproducibility gap adjacent to the main design concerns above.
- The phrase "though perhaps not peculiar to autoregressive decoding: of two non-autoregressive baselines, one is immune and one is not" in the abstract is syntactically awkward and could be tightened.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the behavioral repetition-ladder benchmark with matched controls is a genuinely useful, carefully-controlled contribution, but the paper's own mechanism tests (contraction measurement, dilution dose-response, post-horizon probe replication) are null, reversed, or run on samples too small to interpret, so the connection between the Lean-verified theorem and the observed behavior is asserted more strongly in the framing than the experimental design currently supports; the CTC-judge calibration and filler-pool confounds also need to be resolved on the primary (not secondary) statistics before the headline numbers can be taken at face value.
