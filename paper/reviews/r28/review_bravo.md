### Summary
The paper asks whether TTS models' well-known tendency to mis-render repeated phrases is a repetition-specific failure or simply an artifact of longer inputs, and answers with a parametric "repetition ladder" (180 stimuli, k ∈ {1…32} repetitions of a target word) paired with length-matched controls whose repeated token is swapped for distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints spanning three architecture families (Llasa, Qwen3-TTS, XTTS-v2), plus VITS and F5-TTS as non-autoregressive baselines, the authors report a large and robust exact-rendering gap (18.2% repeated vs. 94.3% control at k≥6) that survives decoding-rule sweeps, judge substitution, and hundreds of specification variants; a companion "period ladder" shows the deficit is graded with periodicity rather than a step function at verbatim adjacency. They also formalize a candidate mechanism — a Lean-4-verified bound stating that if per-repetition state updates contract, no Lipschitz readout can separate counts past a horizon — and then measure the contraction constant empirically, finding it expansive (q ≫ 1) in every checkpoint, thereby refuting their own proposed mechanism.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper cites attention-supervision and alignment-guided decoding as the field's standard fixes for this failure mode but never tests a model that actually implements them, weakening its claim that "the deficit survives the field's standard mitigations."
   **Where** — Introduction ("fixes proposed at the level of attention supervision or decoding rules [3, 4]", citing RALL-E and CosyVoice 2) and Section 3.2 ("Not the decoding rule, the judge, or our exclusions").
   **Why it matters** — RALL-E's chain-of-thought alignment and ELLA-V's alignment-guided reordering are presented as the literature's answer to this class of failure, yet the panel tests only repetition-penalty and greedy/sampling variation — both decoding-time knobs, not architectural alignment fixes. CosyVoice 2 is later invoked in Section 3.1 as a stand-in for "alignment supervision" and still shows a 64.4-point gap, but CosyVoice 2 is never introduced in the Methods model list, so it's unclear whether it was held out by design or added post hoc, and whether it is actually representative of the RALL-E/ELLA-V mechanism the intro cites. As written, the paper's strongest novelty claim — that this is a repetition-specific limit "the field's standard mitigations" don't touch — rests on an indirect proxy rather than a direct test of the cited prior-art fixes.
   **What would address it** — Either include a genuine alignment-supervised or CoT-guided checkpoint (RALL-E-style or an open reimplementation) in the panel, or narrow the claim to "decoding-rule mitigations" and explicitly flag attention-supervision fixes as untested.

2. **Issue** — The Lean-4 contraction lemma is presented as a self-contained novel formalization without being situated against existing formal-expressivity results on self-attention that the paper itself cites for other purposes.
   **Where** — Section 4 ("The candidate mechanism, measured and refuted"); related-work paragraph in Section 5 cites Hahn [18] and Li et al. [20] only for "single-pass" and "state-tracking bounds… properties of the model, invariant to period."
   **Why it matters** — Hahn's theoretical-limitations paper [18] and the state-tracking bounds in [20] already formalize constraints on what self-attention layers can propagate across steps; the paper's own contraction/Lipschitz-readout lemma is a closely related construction (bounding what a readout can recover from an evolving boundary state) but the text never explains how its Lean-verified result relates to, extends, or differs from that existing formal machinery. Without that comparison, a reader cannot tell whether the "novel" theoretical contribution is a genuinely new bound or a re-derivation of known transformer smoothness/expressivity properties dressed in a new (Lean-verified) proof assistant. This matters especially because the paper leans on the formal-verification framing ("free of sorry," "axiom audit") as evidence of rigor and novelty.
   **What would address it** — Add a paragraph explicitly deriving or contrasting the contraction lemma against Hahn [18] / Li et al. [20]'s formal results, and clarify what is new (the Lean encoding? the specific per-repetition contraction framing? the Lipschitz-readout corollary?).

3. **Issue** — No mechanistic account of *why* repeated tokens specifically trigger anomalous dynamics is discussed or cited, even though this is the most natural competing explanation for the phenomenon under study.
   **Where** — Section 5 "Related work" — covers representational collapse [14] and counting-expressivity bounds [15–20], but omits any engagement with circuit-level or induction-style explanations of repeat/copy behavior in autoregressive sequence models.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's entire design hinges on distinguishing "repetition" from "length," but it treats the mechanism space as bounded by {contraction of state, erasure vs. readout failure}. A large body of interpretability work on copying/repeat circuits offers a third candidate mechanism (attention locking onto a recently-attended token rather than a global state collapsing), which would predict effects closer to what Section 3.1's period ladder actually shows (graded degradation with periodicity, half the deficit surviving non-adjacency). Not engaging with this leaves the novelty and completeness of the "candidate mechanism" (Section 4) undermotivated — the paper tests and refutes one hypothesis without acknowledging others already documented in the literature.
   **What would address it** — Add a citation and discussion of copy/repeat-circuit interpretability work as an alternative or complementary account, and explain why the chosen contraction hypothesis was prioritized over it.

4. **Issue** — Several claims stated as unconditional ("no map is a contraction… in any checkpoint," "ruling out the judge-side artifact") rest on validation steps with very small samples.
   **Where** — Whisper/CTC audit "n=12 over 6 donors" and "n=24, mean 0.996" (Section 2); Spanish arm "S9, S27"; CosyVoice 2 and VITS results at n=90; Llasa-8B causal-patch bound "19% at n=36" (Section 5).
   **Why it matters** — The paper's rhetorical structure repeatedly treats these small-n checks as decisive rulings-out of alternative explanations (judge artifact, architecture-specific immunity, causal irrelevance of the probed direction). With n in the tens, confidence intervals on these specific sub-claims are almost certainly wide, and the paper doesn't report them for these cells (unlike the careful interval reporting elsewhere, e.g., the 420-specification curve). This creates an inconsistency: extensive uncertainty quantification for the headline gap, but point-estimate certainty for the sub-analyses doing the "ruling out" work.
   **What would address it** — Report intervals (or exact counts) for the small-n audits and temper language like "ruling out" to reflect the actual precision available.

5. **Issue** — The ordering/composition ablation ("Does the ordering carry it?") is framed as a clean dissociation ("Ordering drives Qwen, not the panel") despite the result itself failing the paper's own pre-registered confirmation band and splitting in opposite directions by architecture.
   **Where** — Section 3.1, "Does the ordering carry it?" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — The reported outcome is: pooled effect excludes the pre-committed confirmation band (P=0.010, i.e., the pre-registered hypothesis was not confirmed), one architecture gains 17.8 points, one loses 30.0, and both intervals exclude zero in opposite directions. This is a genuinely equivocal, low-n (two-checkpoint) result, but the surrounding prose ("what the ladder tracks is composition… not where the recurrences sit") reads as a settled conclusion. Given the paper's own admission elsewhere that "our six checkpoints are only three architecture families, so we model the clustering rather than count checkpoints," presenting a 2-checkpoint split as an architecture-level finding is inconsistent with that stated standard of evidence.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly label this result as inconclusive/architecture-contingent given n=2, rather than asserting a general composition-over-ordering conclusion.

### Minor concerns
- The Methods section lists the model panel (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2, plus VITS/F5-TTS baselines) but CosyVoice 2 appears only in Results (Section 3.1) without being introduced as part of the design — a reporting gap independent of the substantive concern above.
- The mixed-effects estimate ("76.4 points, and all six interval constructions exclude zero") is reported as a stable point estimate elsewhere, while the partial-pooling model with the same three-group structure is explicitly flagged as barely identified ("three groups barely identify a group-level scale") — the two analyses use the same sparse grouping structure but are treated with different epistemic caution.
- Figure 1(b) caption is dense ("hollow markers are the p = k arm, matched to neither in vocabulary nor length") and would benefit from a legend entry rather than caption-only explanation.
- "median count error of -12.5% per checkpoint against 0.0%" (Section 3.1) is only true for 3 of 6 rows in Table 1 (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B is 0.0%); the text does later clarify this checkpoint separately, but the initial phrasing risks implying uniformity across all six checkpoints on first read.
- The repository name "icassp_antispoofing" for a TTS-counting paper is explained as legacy ("name predates this study") but is worth a rename or clearer note for a camera-ready reproducibility package.
- Reference [7] (self-citation, companion paper) and [13] (Venkatesh) are both dated 2026, consistent with the paper's own dateline but worth double-checking at camera-ready that both are genuinely available at the cited arXiv IDs by publication time.

### Verdict
**Major revision** — the empirical robustness program (specification curves, multi-recognizer validation, mechanism refutation) is unusually rigorous for the venue, but the novelty claim that the deficit "survives the field's standard mitigations" is only partly earned since the cited alignment-supervision fixes are never directly tested, the formal contraction bound isn't situated against existing self-attention expressivity results the paper itself cites, and at least one headline sub-finding (ordering vs. composition) is asserted more strongly than its own pre-registered test supports.
