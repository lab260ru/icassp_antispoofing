LENS: Related work and novelty attribution
STANCE: Skeptical-but-fair

### Summary

The paper asks whether autoregressive TTS models' well-documented tendency to miscount, loop, or truncate on repeated text is a length effect or a repetition-specific effect, and isolates the two with a "repetition ladder": items where a word is repeated k times, each paired with a length- and word-count-matched control in which the repeated token is replaced by distinct fillers. Across six checkpoints from three architectures, repeated text is rendered exactly far less often than matched controls (18.2% vs 94.3% at k≥6; a 46.8–75.4 point gap depending on threshold), and a companion "period ladder" shows the deficit is graded with periodicity rather than a step function at verbatim adjacency. The authors show the effect survives decoding-rule sweeps, exclusion-rule ablations, and four different ASR judges (ruling out Whisper-specific measurement artifacts), then test a Lean-4-verified theoretical account (that per-repetition state contraction bounds any readout's ability to separate counts) and report measuring the relevant contraction factor q across five checkpoints — finding q ≫ 1 everywhere, i.e., the proposed mechanism is empirically false even though it motivated the experimental design.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper omits VALL-E 2's Repetition Aware Sampling (RAS), a published, directly on-point mitigation for exactly the repetition-induced instability/looping the paper studies, both as related work and as a candidate mitigation to sweep.
   **Where** — Section 1 ("attributed to exposure bias or to the absence of a monotonic alignment constraint, with fixes proposed at the level of attention supervision or decoding rules [3,4]") and Section 3.2 ("The families ship repetition penalties differing by an order of magnitude... Nor is it sampling: greedy decoding gives 72.2 points against 75.0 sampled").
   **Why it matters** — VALL-E 2 [2406.05370] explicitly targets this failure mode: "Repetition Aware Sampling refines the original nucleus sampling process by accounting for token repetition in the decoding history. It not only stabilizes the decoding but also circumvents the infinite loop issue." This is prior art that (a) already frames repetition, not length, as the operative variable — undercutting the framing that the field has only offered length/exposure-bias-flavored fixes — and (b) is a decoding-rule intervention specifically engineered around repetition history that the paper's "field's standard mitigations" sweep (plain repetition penalty, greedy vs. sampled) does not include. The robustness claim in Section 3.2 ("It survives... an order-of-magnitude penalty sweep") would be considerably stronger, or would need qualifying, if tested against a repetition-history-aware sampler rather than only a repetition-penalty magnitude sweep. As written, the claim that the deficit "survives... the field's standard mitigations" overstates what was actually tried.
   **What would address it** — Cite VALL-E 2 and RAS explicitly as related work, and either run it (or an equivalent repetition-history-aware decoding scheme) as an additional ablation, or explicitly scope the "standard mitigations" claim to penalty-magnitude and greedy/sampled axes only.

2. **Issue** — The "novel" theoretical contribution (a Lean-4-verified contraction bound) is derived and then immediately falsified by the paper's own measurements, but the paper never asks whether a piece of prior art already anticipated this dead end, leaving the reader unable to judge how much the Lean formalization itself adds beyond a page of ordinary analysis.
   **Where** — Section 4, "THE CANDIDATE MECHANISM, MEASURED AND REFUTED."
   **Why it matters** — Formal verification is being used here as a badge of rigor for a mathematical claim (attention-flattening ⇒ boundary-state contraction ⇒ readout indistinguishability) that is elementary given its premises and is not compared against related dynamical-systems or expressivity accounts of transformer counting (the paper does cite [15–20] but only in Discussion, as a parallel "another route," not as prior formalizations of similar contraction/precision arguments the Lean proof might be restating). Given that the antecedent is measured to be false in every checkpoint (q = 21.0–347.7, no contraction anywhere), the reader is left with a mechanism paper whose central mechanism did not need mechanized proof to be worth stating, and whose verification effort is not weighed against what the cited expressivity literature (Hahn 2020/1906.06755; Strobl et al. 2311.00208; state-tracking bounds of Li et al. 2503.02854) already establishes about when transformers can or cannot count. The novelty of "Lean-verified but false" is asserted rather than positioned.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly relate the contraction lemma's assumptions to the existing formal-expressivity results cited in Discussion, and justify why formal (Lean) verification was necessary for this particular lemma versus a standard proof, given the lemma is ultimately reported as inapplicable to any measured decoder.

3. **Issue** — The comparison to "representational collapse" [14] (Barbero et al., over-squashing) is described as the paper's closest related work, but the relationship is asserted rather than argued, and no search was made for TTS-specific or codec-LM-specific prior work on repetition/counting collapse that might pre-empt the "we isolate what drives it" framing.
   **Where** — Section 5, "Related work, and what the control decides": "Closest is representational collapse [14]... distinguishable states grow at 0.46 of the control's rate... a slowing, not the theorem's fixed point."
   **Why it matters** — The paper's headline contribution is billed as isolating repetition from length "by construction" for the first time in TTS ("Ask an autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) model to say six hundred sixty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six... The behaviour is documented as a stability problem [1,2] and usually attributed to exposure bias"). But [1]–[4] are cited only for the phenomenon and generic fixes, not searched against for whether any of them (or unlisted work) already ran a repetition-vs-length control. Given that VALL-E 2 already reasons explicitly in terms of "token repetition in the decoding history" as the operative variable (not length), the paper's implicit claim to be the first to separate these two confounds in TTS is not adequately checked against the literature it does cite, let alone the literature it doesn't.
   **What would address it** — A explicit statement of what, if anything, in [1]–[4] or elsewhere already contrasts repetition against length (even qualitatively), and a sharper delineation of what "isolating" adds beyond what RAS's design already implies operationally.

4. **Issue** — The companion-paper citation [7] and the Venkatesh citation [13] are both self-referential or single-source claims dated 2026 (i.e., contemporaneous with or after this paper), used to support substantive framing and discussion claims, without any independent corroboration.
   **Where** — Section 1 ("The work is a companion to a spectral study of hallucination in encoder–decoder ASR [7]") and Section 5 ("It is not erased: it stays decodable late (R² 0.48; S12), as Venkatesh [13] found in text models").
   **Why it matters** — [7] is a same-author preprint from 2026, and [13] is a single non-peer-reviewed 2026 preprint used to corroborate a nontrivial claim (that counts survive in state even when the policy reading them fails). Leaning on very recent, thinly-vetted, and partly self-authored sources for both companion framing and a key corroborating result is a weak evidentiary base for a claim load-bearing enough to be called "the rival we could not decide" in Discussion.
   **What would address it** — Note the provisional status of [13] explicitly (single study, text-domain, preprint) rather than stating "as Venkatesh found" with the same confidence as an established result, and clarify what independent verification (if any) exists for the companion-paper's ASR findings that this paper's framing leans on.

5. **Issue** — Beyond the assigned lens: the specification-curve and multiverse-style robustness analysis (420 specifications, no correction across them) is presented as principled but risks selection on the outcome given the number of researcher degrees of freedom acknowledged (k-threshold "chosen after seeing where the arms diverge," exclusion rules, aggregation choices).
   **Where** — Section 3.1: "The k ≥ 6 threshold was chosen after seeing where the arms diverge... Across 420 specifications... the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points, median 70.5, and none reverses its sign... we correct across none of them."
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably transparent about this (quoting the unconditioned 46.8-point figure, flagging the post-hoc threshold, disclosing exploratory analyses separately in S8/S10/S17), which mitigates but does not eliminate the concern: a specification curve that never reverses sign is reassuring for direction but says little about the precision of any single point estimate quoted elsewhere in the abstract (e.g., "75.4 at k≥6... a threshold set after seeing the data, so we quote both"). The abstract's practice of quoting two numbers for the same quantity (67.6 vs 76.7 cycled/never-cycled controls) is honest but also makes it harder for a reader to know which number is "the" headline effect size for citation purposes.
   **What would address it** — A single pre-registered primary estimate (or explicit statement that none exists) rather than a family of quoted alternatives, with the specification curve retained as robustness evidence rather than as a partial substitute for it.

### Minor concerns

- The abstract is written in a dense, aphoristic style ("It favours the control in all 6 checkpoints... Whether periodic ordering does that work splits by architecture") that trades clarity for rhetorical compression; several quantitative claims (e.g., "75.4 at k ≥ 6... 67.6 if... cycled") are hard to parse without the body text.
- Table 1's "Qwen3-TTS-1.7B" row (0.0% median count-error gap but still 60.0-point exact-match gap) is flagged as a notable exception in text but is easy to miss in the table itself; a footnote or bolding would help.
- CosyVoice 2 appears in Section 3.1 results (n=90, gap of 64.4) but is not listed among the "six checkpoints from three architectures" panel described in Methods/Abstract — its provenance (why it's held out as a held-out prediction test rather than a seventh panel checkpoint) should be stated explicitly in Methods, not only inferred from Results.
- Reference [7] gives arXiv ID "2604.08591" with year "2026" and reference [13] gives "2605.09239" with year "2026" — both plausible given the stated current date, but worth double-checking the arXiv ID/date pairing at camera-ready, since a mismatched ID-year pair is a common citation error.
- "q = 21.0–347.7" and other inline range notations throughout would benefit from units/definitions restated near first use in each section (q is defined in Section 4 but the abstract uses it before its definition).
- The phrase "the speech analogue of neural text degeneration [5, 6]" in the Introduction asserts a strong analogy to Holtzman et al. without directly engaging the differences (open-ended generation degeneracy vs. TTS's closed-form, transcript-conditioned repetition), which the paper's own results (repetition penalty sweeps not fixing it) suggest may be a weaker analogy than implied.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the empirical core (repetition-vs-length isolation, multi-judge robustness, and the falsification of the paper's own contraction hypothesis) is a genuine and carefully-instrumented contribution, but the related-work framing overstates what "the field's standard mitigations" and prior TTS literature actually address (most notably omitting VALL-E 2's repetition-aware decoding, which reasons about the same variable this paper isolates) and needs to more clearly delineate the paper's novelty from that adjacent prior art before the claims can be assessed as fully supported.
