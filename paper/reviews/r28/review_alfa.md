### Summary

The paper claims three things: (1) autoregressive TTS repetition failure is caused by repetition/periodicity of a token, not by sequence length, established via a length-matched control ladder across six checkpoints from three architecture families; (2) the failure is not an artefact of the standard measurement instrument (Whisper), which is itself biased against detecting it, and survives re-scoring under four recognisers; (3) a Lean-4-verified contraction bound the authors propose as a candidate mechanism is measured directly and rejected — the boundary-to-boundary map is expansive (q ≫ 1) on every checkpoint, on both arms. The paper is unusually self-auditing: it flags its own post-hoc threshold (k ≥ 6), runs a 420-specification robustness sweep, and pre-registers some sub-analyses. That diligence is real and should be credited. But the paper's two broadest claims — that the deficit is "not peculiar to autoregressive decoding" and that it "survives... every exclusion rule" and "the field's standard mitigations" — are carried by comparisons the authors themselves describe as "suggestive rather than isolating": one model per non-autoregressive design, a repetition-penalty sweep on two of six checkpoints, and a single alignment-supervised checkpoint (CosyVoice 2) bolted on after the main panel at n=90. The core length-vs-repetition dissociation is well-supported; the generalisation claims riding on top of it are not.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The "does ordering carry it?" ablation is run on only 4 of the panel's 6 checkpoints, and the per-model signs directly contradict each other (two Qwen checkpoints +17.8, one null, XTTS-v2 −30.0), yet this is distilled into a clean directional claim. `[Evidence — distinguishing test]`
   **Where** — §3.1, "Does the ordering carry it?" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — A test meant to isolate whether *order* (vs. composition) matters cannot support "ordering drives Qwen, not the panel" when it is silent on half the panel (the three Llasa checkpoints aren't mentioned in this sub-analysis at all) and reverses sign within the tested subset. As written, this reads as a description of variance, not a finding.
   **What would address it** — Run the shuffle manipulation on all six checkpoints, or explicitly state why three were excluded and treat the claim as architecture-specific pending that data.

2. **Issue** — No check rules out synthesis-quality degradation (garbled/distorted audio at high repetition) as an alternative to genuine "loses count," since the outcome measure is entirely ASR-mediated. `[Evidence — alternative explanations]`
   **Where** — §2 (Behavioural measurement), Discussion §5 "What the design does not separate."
   **Why it matters** — A CTC recogniser failing on acoustically degraded audio produces the same signature (dropped/garbled counts) as a model that "loses count" cognitively. The WER-based recogniser-robustness check (§3.2) partially addresses this for the judge, but never establishes that repeated-item audio at high k is perceptually well-formed speech rather than noise; the 165-clip listen-by-ear sample checks scoring decisions, not whether audio quality itself confounds the two arms.
   **What would address it** — Report an independent audio-quality metric (e.g., a non-ASR-based intelligibility/naturalness score, or even PESQ/UTMOS) on repeated vs. control audio, or a small human-listener quality rating orthogonal to the count judgment.

3. **Issue** — Several headline "generalises beyond the panel" numbers rest on very small samples with correspondingly wide or barely-informative intervals. `[Statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — §3.1 (partial-pooling posterior "[5.9, 93.3]," touching zero at −1.6 under the widest prior; CosyVoice 2 validation at n=90; §4 rank-1 patch at n=36 for Llasa-8B "never detectable... bounding only 19%").
   **Where else** — §2, Whisper audit n=12, CTC audit n=24.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is candid that these intervals are wide, which is good practice, but then still leans on the point estimates ("nearer its 65.3 mean than an interval that wide entitles us to expect") to claim the extrapolation is validated. With three architecture groups, a hierarchical model's group-level scale is nearly unidentified; one confirmatory checkpoint inside a wide interval is weak evidence for "the panel generalises."
   **What would address it** — Either drop the generalisation claim to a hedge consistent with the interval width, or add at least one more out-of-panel architecture family before asserting the extrapolation is validated.

4. **Issue** — The prose is dense to the point of obscuring rather than clarifying the argument, and the Lean-4 material assumes formal-verification background never established for a speech/audio audience. `[Presentation]`
   **Where** — Throughout §3.1, e.g.: *"by checkpoint 76.7 [68.4, 84.4], positive in all 6 — including the one whose median-error gap is zero, which still renders repeated items exactly 60.0 points less often than its controls."* Also §4's Lean-4 lemma statement, introduced with no gloss for readers unfamiliar with Lipschitz readouts or mathlib.
   **Why it matters** — ICASSP's audience is signal-processing/ML practitioners, not formal-methods researchers. Packing 3–4 statistics into single sentences with no topic-sentence scaffolding makes the paper hard to audit on a first read, which is ironic for a paper this focused on rigor and self-checking.
   **What would address it** — One plain-English sentence per key result before the numeric parenthetical; one sentence orienting non-formal-methods readers to what the Lean bound claims before stating it symbolically.

5. **Issue** — The paper's two broadest claims ("not peculiar to autoregressive decoding," "survives... the field's standard mitigations") are built on comparisons the authors themselves flag as underpowered, and the most directly relevant non-autoregressive baseline for their own hypothesis was never run. `[Evidence — baselines]` **(AXIS)**
   **Where** — §2 ("VITS and F5-TTS run as non-autoregressive baselines... neither group is pooled into the panel"); §3.2 ("of two non-autoregressive baselines one is immune, one is not, though at one model per strategy that is suggestive rather than isolating" — their own words); §3.2 penalty sweep ("Swept on two architectures the deficit stays put... XTTS-v2... Qwen3-TTS-0.6B"); §3.1 CosyVoice-2 ("n=90," added post-hoc as a single confirmatory checkpoint, not integrated into the panel).
   **Why it matters** — This is the widest gap between what the abstract asserts and what the evidence supports. The abstract states flatly: "Nor is the failure peculiar to autoregressive decoding: of two non-autoregressive baselines one is immune, one is not" — with no caveat that this is n=1 per design. Worse, the paper's own mechanistic story in §3.2 ("What plausibly separates the two is where the count must live: VITS gives each token its own duration, F5-TTS estimates one total") makes an immediate, cheap, testable prediction: any TTS architecture with explicit per-token/phoneme duration prediction and monotonic alignment should be immune, regardless of autoregression. Glow-TTS [2005.11129] is exactly this design — non-autoregressive, monotonic-alignment-search, explicit duration modeling, specifically built to prevent the skip/repeat failures this paper studies — and it was not tested, despite being a standard, freely available baseline. Similarly, the introduction cites RALL-E, ELLA-V and CosyVoice's alignment-supervision approach as "the" field's proposed fixes for exactly this failure mode, but only CosyVoice 2 gets an experimental slot, and even that arrives as an n=90 add-on rather than a panel member with the same treatment (seeds, penalty sweep, ordering ablation) as the six primary checkpoints. As it stands, "the deficit survives the field's standard mitigations" is asserted about mitigations that were, in the main, never run.
   **What would address it** — Add Glow-TTS or FastSpeech2 (explicit duration, non-autoregressive) as a second immune-by-hypothesis baseline; run the repetition-penalty sweep across all six panel checkpoints, not two; give CosyVoice 2 (or another alignment-supervised model) the full panel treatment rather than a single confirmatory measurement; and caveat the abstract's non-autoregressive claim with the same "suggestive rather than isolating" language used in the body.

### Minor concerns

- Fig. 1(a) caption says "one pair per checkpoint" but Fig. 1(b)'s legend lists only four of the six checkpoints (Llasa-3B/8B absent) — unclear which subset panel (a) actually plots.
- "exact-match scores 30-of-32 and 3-of-32 alike" (§2) is a confusing way to state that exact-match is binary regardless of magnitude of miss; a clearer one-clause explanation would help.
- The single p-value reported ("excluding the pre-committed confirmation band (P=0.010)", §3.1) sits oddly next to the paper's explicit stance that "our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values (S11)" — either drop it or explain why this one comparison gets a p-value when nothing else does.
- No axis units/scale given in-text for Fig. 1 beyond percentages — fine, but "count error (%)" in panel (a) should clarify sign convention (negative = undercount) inline rather than only via caption + prose.
- The GitHub repo is named `icassp_antispoofing`, explicitly flagged as predating this study — harmless, but worth a one-line footnote explaining the mismatch rather than a parenthetical aside, since reviewers will otherwise wonder if the artefact link is wrong.
- Spanish-arm numbers ("mean 0.93, S9, S27") are quoted without the corresponding n in the main text, unlike the English audit which gives n=12/n=24.

### Belief update

Genuine, if narrow, update: I now put more weight on the proposition that TTS repetition failure is driven by token/phrase repetition and periodicity specifically, not merely by sequence length — the length-matched control design in §3.1 is a real distinguishing test and the effect size is large and consistent across three architecture families. I also update (moderately) toward the specific contraction-map mechanism being false as an explanation, since the authors measured their own proposed mechanism directly and it failed on every checkpoint (q ≫ 1) — that's a well-executed refutation of their own hypothesis, which is more credible than refuting someone else's. I do **not** update on "the failure generalises across decoding strategies" or "survives the field's standard mitigations" — those claims are carried by one-model-per-condition comparisons that the authors themselves undercut in the body text.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the central length-vs-repetition dissociation and the mechanism refutation are solid, but the paper's claims about generality across decoding strategies and robustness to "the field's standard mitigations" outrun the evidence: one non-autoregressive model per design class, a duration-based baseline (Glow-TTS-style) that directly tests the authors' own stated hypothesis but was never run, and a penalty sweep covering two of six checkpoints.
