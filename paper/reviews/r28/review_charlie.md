### Summary

The paper asks whether text-to-speech (TTS) models' well-known tendency to miscount, loop, or truncate repeated phrases is a length effect (long inputs are hard) or a repetition effect proper. Using a 180-item "repetition ladder" (target word repeated k times, k ∈ {1,2,...,32}) with length-matched, non-repeating controls, the authors measure exact-count accuracy across six checkpoints from three TTS architectures (Llasa 1B/3B/8B, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, XTTS-v2), plus F5-TTS and VITS as non-autoregressive baselines. They report a large, consistent repeated-vs-control accuracy gap (46.8 points overall, 75.4 at k≥6) that survives greedy decoding, repetition-penalty sweeps, exclusion-rule ablations, and rescoring with four different ASR judges. A second "period ladder" separates periodicity from verbatim identity, showing the deficit is graded rather than a step function. The paper's second contribution is methodological: it shows the default evaluation instrument (Whisper) is itself autoregressive and biased toward under-counting repeated audio, and substitutes a CTC recognizer validated against concatenative ground truth. Its third contribution is a Lean-4-verified theoretical bound (state-contraction ⇒ counting collapses past a horizon) which the authors then measure directly and refute — the boundary-to-boundary Jacobian is expansive (q > 1) on every checkpoint they test, so the proposed mechanism does not explain the empirical effect.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The paper's sole named reproducibility artifact — a specific git tag — does not exist in the linked repository, either locally or on the remote, so the pointer a reader is given resolves to nothing.
   **Where** — Section 2, "Code, data, settings": *"Lean development, stimuli, code, per-item tables, the full supplement and both transcript sets are public at tag v1.3-isolating of https://github.com/lab260ru/icassp_antispoofing."*
   **Why it matters** — I have direct access to this repository (it is the working directory of this review session). Running `git tag -l` and `git ls-remote --tags origin` both return only `h9pcr-d1`, `v1.0-submission`, `v1.1-icassp`, and `v1.2-locating` — no `v1.3-isolating`, locally or on the remote. Every other verifiability claim in the paper (the "checkable rather than asserted" CosyVoice2 pre-registration commit, the S1–S28 supplement structure, the Lean proof files, `build.sh`'s number-regeneration pipeline) routes through this same tag. If it isn't cut, none of those claims are checkable by a reader following the paper's own citation, which directly undercuts the "checkable rather than asserted" rhetoric the paper repeatedly leans on (e.g., the CosyVoice2 pre-registration argument in §3.1).
   **What would address it** — Cut and push the `v1.3-isolating` tag before submission, and have `build.sh` (or CI) assert the tag named in `main.tex` exists on the remote as a pre-submission check, so this class of drift is caught automatically rather than by a reader.

2. **Issue** — Nearly all methodological detail — sampling hyperparameters, decoding settings, the audio-degeneracy detector definitions, the mixed-effects model specification, the Jacobian power-iteration validation gates — is offloaded to an unseen supplement (S1–S28), leaving the main text un-self-contained.
   **Where** — Throughout; explicit examples: "Sampling uses each system's shipped defaults, listed in S19" (§2); "the layer chosen by leave-one-template-out R² (S6)" (§2); "It passes three validation gates on every checkpoint first (S5)" (§4).
   **Why it matters** — For an ICASSP-length paper this offloading is somewhat expected, but the density of S-pointers (over 20 distinct references) means a reader cannot evaluate central claims — e.g., what "shipped defaults" actually means numerically for each of six checkpoints, or what the three Jacobian validation gates check — without the supplement, which is itself only reachable through the broken tag in Concern 1. Reproducibility here is not "harder without extra work," it is currently blocked.
   **What would address it** — At minimum, put the sampling hyperparameters (temperature, top-p/top-k, repetition penalty per model) in a compact table in the main text or a properly attached appendix, not solely behind a citation to S19.

3. **Issue** — The judge-validation audits that the entire measurement methodology rests on use very small samples.
   **Where** — §2: "Audited against concatenative audio with known ground truth it [Whisper] transcribes the repeated word exactly once in every trial that returns anything (0.19 of the true count, n=12 over 6 donors)... ratio 1.00 (n=24, mean 0.996)" for the CTC recognizer.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's second headline contribution — "the obvious judge shown disqualified" — is built on n=12 and n=24 audit trials. The direction of the effect (Whisper undercounts, CTC doesn't) is plausible and mechanistically motivated, but with these sample sizes the specific point estimates (0.19, 1.00, mean 0.996) carry very wide uncertainty that is never reported (no CI given for the audit itself, only for downstream quantities). A single donor or template idiosyncrasy could shift 0.19 substantially.
   **What would address it** — Report confidence intervals or exact counts for the audit numbers themselves, and scale up n where feasible (concatenative audio generation is cheap and doesn't require running TTS models).

4. **Issue** — Multiple-comparisons correction is explicitly and deliberately not applied across a very large number of derived statistical tests.
   **Where** — §3.1: "Across 420 specifications spanning every forking choice we made... the gap runs 34.4–86.4 points, median 70.5, and none reverses its sign. These are not independent tests and we correct across none of them... our claims rest on effect sizes, not p-values." Also the period-ladder pre-committed tests (§3.1) and the ordering-effect P=0.010 test in the same section.
   **Why it matters** — The specification-curve framing (report the whole distribution rather than a single p-value) is a defensible and increasingly standard practice, and the authors are transparent about it — this is a genuine strength relative to typical papers in the area. But the paper still reports isolated p-values in places (P=0.010 for the ordering-recovery test) without correction, inconsistently mixing the "effect-size only" philosophy with occasional single-test significance claims. This selective use makes it hard for a reader to know which numbers in the paper are meant to be read as inferential claims and which are purely descriptive.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the isolated p-value entirely in favor of the effect-size-with-interval framing used elsewhere, or state explicitly which specific claims in the paper are treated as confirmatory (and therefore held to a stricter, corrected standard) versus exploratory.

5. **Issue** — Several key sample sizes for ablations that carry load-bearing conclusions are small enough that the reported effects could be dominated by a handful of items.
   **Where** — CosyVoice2 validation "n=90"; VITS dissociation check "n=90"; F5-TTS duration-conditioning ablation "n=60, gains above 8.3 points excluded"; rank-1 patch experiment "bounding only 19% at n=36" (§4/§5).
   **Why it matters** — The F5-TTS ablation in particular applies an unexplained trimming rule ("gains above 8.3 points excluded") to n=60 items with no stated rationale for the threshold or how many items it removed — this reads as a post-hoc filter chosen after seeing the outcome distribution, which is exactly the kind of researcher-degree-of-freedom the paper's own specification-curve discipline (Concern 4) is designed to guard against elsewhere, but does not visibly guard against here.
   **What would address it** — State how many items the 8.3-point exclusion rule drops and why that cutoff (not an arbitrary round number) was chosen, ideally pre-registered as the other exclusion rules claim to be (S16).

### Minor concerns

- The abstract is written in a dense, almost telegraphic style packing in ~15 distinct numeric results with minimal connective explanation (e.g., "It favours the control in all 6 checkpoints, where models render repeated text exactly in 18.2% of generations against 94.3%.") — this hurts accessibility for a reader deciding whether to read further, independent of the paper's substance.
- Table 1 caption says "k ≥ 6," but the table itself doesn't state which k-values were pooled or how many generations back each cell — worth a footnote given how much weight this table carries.
- Figure 1(b) caption references "hollow markers" for the p = k arm but the described plot elements are hard to parse from the caption alone without seeing rendered color/marker distinctions.
- Reference [7], the companion paper, is dated "2026" with arXiv ID arXiv:2604.08591 — self-citation to a companion piece is fine, but its exact relationship to this paper's claims (shared data, shared authors — yes, overlapping) could be stated more explicitly given how much interpretive weight is placed on it ("the work is a companion to...").
- "name predates this study" for the repository name (icassp_antispoofing, for a paper about TTS counting) is a minor but real friction point for anyone trying to locate the correct repository from the paper alone.
- The Jacobian estimator section (§4) states results as lower bounds ("Teacher-forcing removes the token channel, so these are lower bounds") but never gives an estimated correction magnitude — if the true q could plausibly be much larger, the "no contraction anywhere" conclusion is even more robust, but if the correction could push some borderline cells below 1, that should be shown rather than only asserted directionally.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core empirical design (matched repetition/length controls, judge-bias audit, mechanism refutation) is unusually rigorous for the venue, but the paper's own reproducibility promise is currently broken (the cited release tag does not exist in the repository, verified directly), several load-bearing statistical claims rest on very small audit samples without uncertainty reporting, and at least one ablation applies an unexplained post-hoc exclusion rule — all fixable without new experiments.
